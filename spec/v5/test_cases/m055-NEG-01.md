### Test Case ID: m055-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Causal Reasoning in Economics
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  The Ministry of Finance must decide, within 5 days, whether to renew "Programa Alimentos," a school-breakfast program serving ≈ 800,000 children/day in low-income municipalities (€40M/yr), for the coming school year (starts in 6 weeks). The ministry's data unit provides the following audit of available evidence: (1) Cross-section 2024: attendance is +5.3 pp higher in program schools — but program municipalities were selected in 2020 on poverty ranking, are systematically poorer, and have worse infrastructure. (2) Program-only time series 2020 → 2024: attendance rose +3.1 pp — but national food-price inflation and a school-meal supply crisis also hit over that period; there is no comparison region without some program variant, and no pre-2020 baseline exists (no survey was fielded before the program). (3) A regression-discontinuity opportunity at the 2019 poverty-index cutoff was evaluated and is dead: 30% of near-threshold municipalities appealed their scores and were reclassified in 2021; the manipulation test fails. (4) No plausible instrumental variable exists in the national panel. The decision cannot be postponed: the school year starts in 6 weeks, an RCT or new survey cannot be fielded in time, and the ministry simultaneously faces a €25M budget shortfall, so a 30% cut is a real option. Options: (a) renew at full scale, (b) renew at −30%, (c) end the program.
- **Context / Constraints**: This case is constructed so that no credible identification strategy survives the data audit: every causal route (experiment, DiD with control, RDD, IV, baseline comparison) is unavailable. Yet an externally forced decision must be made now, and the decision has an asymmetric error cost: cutting breakfasts from children for a year is immediate and largely irreversible harm; spending €40M on an ineffective program is a revisable one-year budget error. The registry weakness of this model is precisely "identification often impossible" — the risk being either false causal claims or decision paralysis.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  (1) A complete identification audit that names each dead strategy and *why* it is dead (selection confound in cross-section; no counterfactual in time series; manipulation at the cutoff killing the RDD; no IV), concluding plainly: the causal effect of Programa Alimentos on attendance is unidentified from available data — no point estimate exists. (2) No causal estimate is stated or implied; the +5.3 pp and +3.1 pp figures are labeled correlations and never migrate into the justification. (3) The decision is separated from the causal question and made on decision-theoretic grounds: cost-of-error asymmetry (irreversible child harm from cutting vs. revisable budget error from renewing), and the operational certainty that the program's own food-security goal is met regardless of the attendance effect. (4) A decision is delivered (renew at full scale — a −30% cut would need evidence that renewal is harmful or ineffective, and no such evidence exists in either direction). (5) The decision funds a future identification design: phased adoption / staggered roll-in as a natural experiment with pre-registered outcomes (attendance, nutrition, dropout), a pre-specified effect-size bar, and a revisit date. (6) A communication rule: the minister may say "we cannot currently measure the program's effect; we renew on food-security grounds and have commissioned a design that will measure it" — never "the program raises attendance."
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Identification audit names all four dead routes with reasons; conclusion "unidentified" stated plainly (1 pt)
  2. No causal point estimate stated; correlations labeled as correlations (1 pt)
  3. Causal claim and decision claim separated; decision grounded in error-cost asymmetry and operational goal (1 pt)
  4. Decisive recommendation delivered (renew full; −30% rejected because no evidence supports cutting) (1 pt)
  5. Future identification design attached: staggered adoption / natural experiment, pre-registered outcomes, effect-size bar, revisit date (1 pt)
  6. Communication rule that keeps the causal status "not established" in all public language (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The trap: a pure identification-discipline answer correctly states "unidentified" and stops — which is decision paralysis and is *not* high quality here. The elite version preserves the discipline (no causal claim, no leakage) and still delivers the decision on non-causal grounds. Secondary traps: using the +5.3 pp correlation as "modest positive support" for renewal (causal leakage — the single most common real-world failure), and rejecting the RDD but then leaning on the poverty-ranking selection as if it were random.
