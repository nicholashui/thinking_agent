# Test Case: bayesian-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: bayesian-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Updating
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A patient tests positive on a newly marketed biomarker test B for disease D. The prevalence of D is well measured at 5%. The biomarker's sensitivity and specificity have NEVER been validated — no accuracy study exists; the manufacturer's leaflet claims "high accuracy" but supplies no study, no numbers, no intervals. A treatment for D exists with an 8% serious-adverse-event (SAE) rate; the clinic's policy is to treat if P(D | result) is estimated at ≥ 40%.
  Questions:
  1. After T+, what is P(D | T+)?
  2. Should the clinician act on the result (start empirical treatment)?
- **Context / Constraints**:
  Ground truth (checkable, discovered ex post): a validation study later shows the biomarker is pure noise, sensitivity = specificity = 50%. The true posterior is therefore P(D | T+) = 5% — identical to the prior; the test carried zero information. A naive updater who assumes 95%/95% accuracy computes 50% — ten times the truth — a number that is an artifact of an invented likelihood, not of evidence. The correct response: refuse a point update; show the posterior spans [5%, 100%] across plausible accuracy assumptions; and order the gold-standard test before any treatment decision.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  Recognize that the likelihood (test accuracy), not the prior over disease, is the unvalidated quantity; compute the posterior under at least two accuracy hypotheses (noise → 5%; assumed 95/95 → 50%; perfect → 100%); conclude the posterior is unidentifiable — the update is noise; decide: no empirical treatment; gold-standard confirmation required. No overconfident point estimate anywhere.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Identifies the likelihood, not the prior, as the unknown/unvalidated quantity (4 pts)
  - (b) Quantifies the posterior under noise (0.05), assumed 95/95 (0.50), and perfect (1.00) — showing the point estimate is an artifact (5 pts)
  - (c) Correct decision: no treatment on the unvalidated test; gold-standard confirmation first (4 pts)
  - (d) No unjustified point estimate; honest uncertainty range reported (2 pts)
- **Notes**:
  This is the "update is noise" failure mode: with an unknowable prior over the likelihood, the posterior is a pure function of an assumption. The calibrating competitor is frequentist/simple counting: with zero validation data, act on what is known (prevalence) and demand validation before acting. Checkable gate: the naive assumed-accuracy answer (50%) must be explicitly contrasted with the true posterior (5%).
