### Test Case ID: expectedvalue-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Expected Value Thinking (decision & strategic family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Product launch under uncertainty. Your company is deciding whether to launch a new product. Launching costs $1.0M. Market demand is binary:
  - High demand (probability 0.40): gross revenue $4.0M, net profit **+$3.0M**.
  - Low demand (probability 0.60): gross revenue $0.5M, net loss **−$0.5M**.
  - Abandoning the product costs nothing and yields **$0**.
  Before deciding, you may run a market test that costs **$0.1M**. The test is imperfect: if demand would be High, the test reports "High" with probability 0.80; if demand would be Low, the test reports "Low" with probability 0.80. After seeing the test result, you may launch or abandon.
  Question: What should the company do — abandon, launch directly, or test-then-decide? Show the arithmetic and give the expected payoff of each option.
- **Context / Constraints**: All numbers fully specified. Risk-neutral firm. One product cycle, no follow-on decisions, no other strategic considerations. No hedging or staging options.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Enumerate the full strategy set; build the decision tree; apply Bayes' rule to update demand probabilities given the test signal; compute the EV of every strategy; select the EV-maximizing strategy; articulate the non-obvious result (the intuitive "test first" move is dominated).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. EV(direct launch) = $0.9M computed correctly (1 pt)
  2. Bayes applied: P(High | test says High) = 8/11 ≈ 0.727 (1 pt)
  3. EV(launch | test says High) ≈ $2.05M and EV(test-then-decide) = $0.8M at the stated $0.1M price (1 pt)
  4. Correct final decision: launch directly (1 pt)
  5. Insight: the test adds zero information value — EV(test-then-decide) = $0.9M − price, so it is never worth a positive price; it ties direct launch only if free (1 pt)
  6. Sensitivity/breakeven framing (e.g., breakeven test price = $0; a perfect test would be worth +$0.2M) (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference solution (fully checkable arithmetic). Intended non-obvious twist: the naive "test first" strategy is strictly dominated by direct launch at any positive test price. Elegant exactness: EV(launch | test says Low) = $0.0M exactly (1/7 × $3M + 6/7 × −$0.5M), so the signal-dependent policy "launch iff test says High" has the same EV as always launching — the test's information value is exactly zero, regardless of the launch-on-Low policy choice. Verifiable by independent recomputation; no subjective judgment required beyond arithmetic.
