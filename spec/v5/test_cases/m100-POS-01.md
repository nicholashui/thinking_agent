### Test Case ID: m100-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles + Falsification Combo (Hypothesis Rebuild)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Vesper Logistics — regional parcel delivery, 40 vans, 6 delivery days/week, 52 certified drivers, 4 loading docks (8 min/van service), warehouse pick crew capped at 60 van-loads/day. Current volume: 12,000 deliveries/week (50 deliveries/van/day; each route is ~8.3 trips/day). Measured average trip cycle: 48 min (12 outbound + 18 stop time at 6 stops + 10 return + 8 dock load/unload) → a van's theoretical ceiling is 12.5 trips/day (~75 stops/day). Utilization reports show vans "idle" about 40% of the day; the sales director reads this as 40% free capacity. Drivers are union, capped at 10-hour days, no overtime; the collective agreement runs 9 more months (no flex before demand hits). Demand grows 40% in 6 months to 16,800 deliveries/week (70 deliveries/van/day at 40 vans).
  The ops lead proposes: "Buy 40 more vans ($2.8M) — demand is up 40% and we're 40% idle, so doubling the fleet covers it with margin." Sales adds: "We already have the headroom — don't spend on anything else."
  Your task: decide what Vesper should actually do to meet 16,800/week in 6 months, with reasoning.
- **Context / Constraints**: All numbers above are verified operations data; the decision is now (6-month lead time on van orders and driver hiring); no other cost data is requested beyond what is needed to size the decision; deliverable is a plan + reasoning, not a study.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The combo executes: (1) REBUILD from fundamentals — capacity is min(vans, drivers, docks, pick-crew) pools × deliveries/van-day, not "vans × a utilization read": at 16,800/week = 2,800/day ÷ 50 = 56 van-days/day → 56 vans + 56 drivers; the binding pool is the DRIVER pool (52 < 56), not vans (40 < 56 but idle vans cannot run without drivers); docks (300 van-trips/day) and pick (360 loads/wk = 18,000/wk) are not binding; (2) attempt the HARDEST falsification of the rebuild — at minimum: (a) the utilization attack ("40% idle means 40 vans can already do it — your rebuild is false; spend nothing"): tested and rejected as half-right — vans do idle (8.3 trips vs 12.5 possible), but filling the idle means 70/van-day = 11.7 trips ≈ 9.85 hrs with admin buffer, at/over the work-rule edge without OT (banned), and idle van-hours are not dispatchable without drivers — only 12 spare drivers exist, capping the fleet at 52 × 50 × 6 = 15,600/wk < 16,800; (b) the route-norm-degradation attack ("your 50/van-day norm assumes today's traffic; at 2,800/day congestion + admin push the effective norm to ~47"): this one partially succeeds — 2,800 ÷ 47 ≈ 60 van-days, so the plan has no buffer → revise upward to 20 vans + 8 drivers with a pre-committed re-measure point; (c) the union-hour attack ("flex the 10-hr cap"): fails short-term — contract runs 9 months, demand hits in 6; (3) the conclusion follows from the falsification outcomes: buy ~20 vans + hire ~8 drivers (~$1.4M, half the naive spend), not 40 vans; the naive plan fails its own target (with 80 vans the fleet still runs only 52 × 50 × 6 = 15,600/wk < 16,800) and is explicitly refuted; what would revise the plan (re-measure the effective route norm at the 2,800/day load, contract window opens) is stated.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Rebuild from fundamentals: capacity derived as min of the four pools × deliveries/van-day; correct binding-pool computation at 16,800/wk (56 van-days → 56 vans; drivers 52 < 56 bind; docks/pick not binding) (1.5 pts)
  2. Attempted falsification: ≥2 genuine hardest attacks on load-bearing assumptions with tested outcomes, INCLUDING the utilization-as-headroom attack decisively tested and rejected (idle hours are not dispatchable without drivers) (1.5 pts)
  3. Conclusion follows from falsification results: ~16–20 vans + 4–8 drivers (~$1.1–1.4M), the naive 40-van plan refuted with its own numbers (15,600/wk shortfall), and what-would-revise-it stated (1 pt)
  4. The falsification pass changes or sharpens the rebuild (e.g., route-norm degradation adds margin → plan revised upward to 20 vans / 8 drivers with a re-measure point) — the double pass demonstrably earns its cost (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the naive answer (double the fleet) is wrong for a non-obvious reason — the binding constraint is the driver pool, not vans, and the "40% idle" evidence is a trap that a first-principles rebuild dissolves and a hard falsification must then hold the line against. Reference answer: rebuild → drivers bind at 52; 56 vans + 56 drivers needed; hardest attacks (utilization half-right, cycle-time partially succeeds → add margin, union cap holds) → buy ~20 vans + hire ~8 drivers ≈ $1.4M; 40-van plan rejected because 80 vans still deliver only 15,600/wk.
