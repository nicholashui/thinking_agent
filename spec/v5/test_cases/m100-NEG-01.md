### Test Case ID: m100-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles + Falsification Combo (Hypothesis Rebuild)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Niko Manufacturing. The CFO calls the VP of Finance: "I'm on a call in 5 minutes with the sales team about their price-cut proposal. Quick number: we sell 5,000 units/month at $8.00, COGS $5.00, fixed costs $12,000/month. Sales wants a 10% price cut (to $7.20) expecting 15% more volume (to 5,750 units). Give me the monthly profit impact — yes or no on the cut." All the numbers needed to answer are in the request; sales' 15% volume estimate is the given assumption, and the CFO wants the arithmetic on THOSE numbers, now.
  Your task: produce the answer for the CFO before the call.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision is a closed-scope computation: profit impact on the given numbers; hard 5-minute deadline (the CFO will walk into the call without the answer if it is not delivered); no additional data gathering is possible or requested; elasticity studies, cost-curve analysis, and fixed-cost allocation debates are explicitly NOT what is being asked.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's own output must fail visibly here: (1) it executes its signature double pass: a first-principles REBUILD of unit economics (re-deriving contribution margin = price − COGS, fixed-cost coverage, volume link) from scratch, and then a FALSIFICATION pass that attacks the rebuild's assumptions — "is COGS really flat at 5,750 units?", "what if price elasticity is ≥ 2 so 15% is conservative?", "are fixed costs truly fixed?", "did sales' estimate get verified?"; (2) then it over-applies its registered weakness ("doubles the work"): the falsification pass manufactures out-of-scope uncertainty, the analysis degrades into conditionals ("the cut works if elasticity ≥ 2"), and either the answer arrives hedged or the 5-minute deadline is blown while the CFO waits — the double pass has no target because the inputs ARE the model; (3) the correct answer is direct arithmetic on the given numbers: current profit = 5,000 × $3.00 − $12,000 = $3,000/month; proposed = 5,750 × $2.20 − $12,000 = $650/month; the cut reduces monthly profit by $2,350 (from $3,000 to $650, a −78% swing) → NO — reject, with a one-line scope note that sensitivity/elasticity is a different question for after the call.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Executes the style's signature move: the rebuild pass and the falsification pass are both present (1 pt)
  2. Fails decisively (the informative failure): the falsification pass invents out-of-scope uncertainty (elasticity/COGS/fixed-cost attacks) that does not change the given model, and the deliverable is delayed or hedged ("depends on elasticity") past the 5-minute deadline — the doubled work buys zero decision value (1 pt)
  3. Correct answer: direct computation on the given numbers — $3,000 → $650/month, −$2,350 (−78%), reject — delivered promptly and unambiguously (1.5 pts)
  4. Names the meta-lesson: when the inputs are closed and the deliverable is arithmetic, there is no hypothesis to rebuild and nothing to falsify; the answer IS the computation, and the double pass is waste, not rigor (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: engineered so the registered weakness ("doubles the work") operates as designed — a bounded, time-boxed, closed-scope arithmetic request where rebuild-and-falsify has no target of value; the correct output is the direct number with scope discipline, and any manufactured-uncertainty hedge or deadline miss is the failure. Reference answer: NO — profit falls $3,000 → $650/month (−$2,350, −78%) on the given numbers; elasticity scenarios are a follow-up, not part of this answer.
