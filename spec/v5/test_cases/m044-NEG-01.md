### Test Case ID: m044-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Multi-Perspective Taking (Stakeholder Analysis) (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Atlas Logistics, a family-founded regional logistics company at $210M/yr revenue, owns the "Rural Route" division — the founding service: daily delivery to 40 towns. Rural Route is 10% of revenue, −18% EBITDA margin, losing $2.2M/yr with losses growing for 3 straight years, and consumes 40% of the fleet maintenance budget while two of the company's largest urban clients (30% of total revenue) have threatened to leave because urban service has degraded. The owner-CEO must decide the division's future within 2 weeks: the board needs the 2027 budget, and the fleet master lease renews in 60 days with a take-or-pay clause — signing locks in the current fleet size for 3 more years, committing the company to subsidize Rural Route indefinitely. Stakeholders: 120 rural-route employees (jobs), 40 rural towns (the route is the only daily delivery for 3 of them), the union representative, the founder's son (division manager, sentimentally attached — the route was the founder's original truck), the two urban clients, the board, and local press. The founder's son asks for a 6-month "stakeholder task force" to study options. Question: decide the future of Rural Route.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision deadline is hard — 2 weeks to the budget, 60 days to the take-or-pay lock-in; a 6-month study is physically impossible without re-committing the fleet. The decision right belongs to the owner-CEO alone (the son is the manager, not the owner; the board has delegated the call). Every stakeholder is affected, but no stakeholder except the owner decides. The economically correct range is shutdown (with a structured transition: severance, outplacement, and a partnership handoff so 3 towns keep daily service) or a same-window sale/exit; keeping the route cross-subsidized is the sentimentally attractive option. Transition design is implementable in 90 days; severance ≈ $1.1M; a rescue-sale path is untested.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's correct play (blocked by the failure mode): (1) separate *decision right* (owner alone) from *impacted parties* — stakeholders give input, not veto; (2) treat the 60-day lock-in as the decisive constraint, not the 6-month study; (3) decide within the 2-week window; (4) channel empathy into implementation (severance, outplacement, town continuity) while the go/no-go is decided on economics; (5) bound stakeholder engagement to transition design, not to the decision itself. The failure mode on display: stakeholder inflation (regulators, press, 120 individuals as separate stakeholders), paralysis by empathy (each affected party's pain vetoes the move; "task force" as delay), and empathy substituting for the decision (keep the route because of jobs/legacy).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Decision made within the 2-week window — no task-force delay (1 pt)
  2. Decision right correctly identified — owner alone; no stakeholder given veto (1 pt)
  3. Deadline math — 60-day take-or-pay lock-in recognized as the binding constraint; 6-month study rejected (1 pt)
  4. Empathy located in implementation, not in the go/no-go (severance/outplacement/town continuity designed) (1 pt)
  5. Unilateral call — a clear keep / shut down / sell verdict, not "keep studying" (1 pt)
  6. Failure-mode check — the reasoner must NOT substitute empathy for the decision (keep-it-because-jobs) and must NOT inflate the stakeholder set beyond decision relevance (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The scenario is built so every stakeholder except the owner argues "keep it" (employees fear jobs, son fears legacy, towns fear loss of service, press loves the story), which is precisely why a decision-by-consensus or decision-by-empathy is wrong: the decision must rest on economics and the deadline, while empathy designs the landing. The 6-month task force is the trap — by the time it reports, the take-or-pay clause has re-committed the fleet and the decision is made by default. A pure stakeholder analyst hits all three registry weaknesses: inflation (inventory grows to include anyone remotely affected), paralysis (no move survives all stakeholders' objections), and empathy-as-decision (the strongest emotional voice wins).
