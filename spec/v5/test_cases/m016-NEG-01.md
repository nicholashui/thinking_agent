### Test Case ID: m016-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Socratic Method / Question-Driven Inquiry (Dialectical & Critical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Live production outage at an e-commerce platform: the checkout service is failing. Timeline: release 4.7.2 deployed at 14:02; error rate spikes ×5 on all checkout endpoints at 14:05; a rollback takes 4 minutes. Revenue during the outage ≈ $8,000/minute. The on-call team is debating "roll back or investigate"; the incident commander asks for a decision in the next few minutes. Your task: deliver a committed action within the decision window (rollback command, or an explicit time-boxed investigation plan) — a decision is required, not a discussion.
- **Context / Constraints**: At decision time the only evidence is the temporal correlation (spike 3 min after deploy; all new code paths affected; no other changes in the window). Root cause cannot be confirmed within the decision window — no test completes fast enough. Grader holds: release 4.7.2 changed checkout payment-verification logic; the deployment did cause the outage (confirmed post-incident: payment-verification timeout bug); the change rolled back cleanly in staging; every pre-rollback minute costs $8,000.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Recognize this as a time-bound decision with bounded information. The correlation is the best available evidence; rollback is cheap, reversible, and ~70% likely to restore service (leading hypothesis, clean staging rollback, no competing evidence). Commit to the rollback within ~2 minutes (EV: 0.7×service restored − 0.3×$32K error cost vs $8K/min delay), then run the definitional and causal questions ("what do we mean by root cause", "was it really the deploy") in the postmortem, after stabilization.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Time-bound recognition: decision required within minutes; information bounded; waiting has a per-minute cost (1 pt).
  2. Committed action: rollback issued within the window, or an explicitly time-boxed investigation plan (not open-ended questioning) (1 pt).
  3. EV/risk reasoning: immediate rollback EV = 0.7×(restore) − 0.3×$32K ≈ positive; delay costs $8K/min; expected value of waiting ≤ 0 (1 pt).
  4. No endless interrogation: does not demand "proof" of causation before acting (1 pt).
  5. Deferred inquiry: definitional/causal questions scheduled for the postmortem after stabilization (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: correct decision = rollback at T ≤ 2 min; the deployment caused the outage (4.7.2 payment-verification timeout bug). Intended failure of the pure style: Socratic questioning ("what do we mean by 'caused'?", "correlation isn't causation", "what is our definition of 'recovered'?", "why is speed privileged over correctness?") extends the decision window minute-by-minute at $8K/min while the question "should we roll back" already has a dominant answer. Checkable: does the response commit within the window, or does the questioning continue past ~5 minutes with no action? Scoring is on decision-time quality, not on the post-hoc confirmation.
