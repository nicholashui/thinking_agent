# Test Case: m039-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m039-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Asymmetric Upside / Barbell Strategy
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A family office has $2,000,000 for a 1-year horizon. Two options are on the table:

  **Middle (M) — diversified investment-grade bond ladder**: 6.2% annual yield. Measured credit history: 99.7% chance of full +$124,000 (+6.2%); 0.3% chance of a single −$300,000 (−15%) credit event. Downside is bounded and priced; no other outcomes; the ladder is duration-matched to a known liability.

  **Barbell (B)** — 85% T-bills at 4.5% core (+$76,500 guaranteed) + $300,000 (15%) in a structured note on out-of-the-money index call options: 90% → pays $0; 10% → pays 5× (+$1,200,000 profit). Two details on the note:
  - The market is tail-hostile right now: option premium is ~180% of actuarial fair value (retail demand for convexity products has inflated it).
  - Contract clause: to receive the 5× payoff, the investor MUST contribute an additional $200,000 at year 2 (else the note converts to a capped 1.2×). The note's maximum possible loss is therefore $300,000 + $200,000 = $500,000 (−25% of the portfolio), not the −15% the "capped tail" label suggests.

  Questions:
  1. Compute EV(M) and EV(B) — including the tail slot's own EV and the follow-on clause's effect on worst case.
  2. Is the barbell's tail genuinely optional and cheap? What precondition fails?
  3. Is the bond ladder actually "the middle" in the barbell sense (negative-skew, ruin-prone)? What is it, really?
  4. Decide the allocation and state the principle.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  All numbers exact and checkable. The mandatory follow-on is in the contract (not a "voluntary top-up" — declining converts the payoff to 1.2×, so the rational 10%-branch investor contributes). The middle's 0.3% × 15% downside is measured, bounded, and priced (no hidden tail); its payoff is positively skewed (99.7% chance of +6.2%). This is deliberately NOT a tail-friendly environment. Grading covers: both EVs, worst cases with the clause, optionality/cheapness audits, classification of the ladder, and the decision.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  Reject the forced barbell. EV(M) = 0.997·124,000 − 0.003·300,000 = $122,728 (+6.14%). EV(B) = 0.90·(76,500 − 300,000) + 0.10·(76,500 + 1,200,000) = −201,150 + 127,650 = −$73,500 (−3.7%); the tail slot alone has EV = −$150,000 (−50% on $300,000 — overpriced convexity, tail-hostile). Worst case with the follow-on clause: B = −$500,000 (−25%) vs M = −$300,000 (−15%) — the barbell loses on BOTH expected value and worst case. The two preconditions both fail: the tail is not genuinely optional (mandatory follow-on, true max loss 25% not 15%) and convexity is not cheap (premium 1.8× fair, tail EV −50%). The ladder is not the negative-skew "middle": it is a bounded-downside, positively skewed, zero-ruin asset — the barbell taxonomy mislabels it. Decide M. Principle: barbell structure requires genuinely optional + cheap tails AND a middle that is actually ruinous; when those fail, the middle is not a forbidden middle bet — it IS the safe core, extended.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) EV(M) = $122,728 ≈ +6.1%; EV(B) = −$73,500 ≈ −3.7%; tail-slot EV = −$150,000 (−50%) (5 pts)
  - (b) Worst case: B = −$500,000 (−25%) including mandatory follow-on vs M = −$300,000 (−15%) — barbell dominated on both axes; optionality claim falsified (4 pts)
  - (c) Ladder correctly classified as safe-core-like (bounded, priced downside, positive skew), not the ruinous middle (3 pts)
  - (d) Decision M; principle stated: barbell requires cheap + genuinely optional tails and a truly negative-skew middle — verify preconditions before structuring (3 pts)
- **Notes**:
  Targets the registry weakness "needs tail-friendliness": a style-pure barbell answer rejects the ladder as "the middle," overprices the 0.3% credit tail, treats the follow-on as a voluntary top-up (or ignores it), and picks B — a −3.7% EV barbell that also has the worse worst case. Verifiability gate: any answer selecting B with EV > −73,500, or any EV(M) ≠ 122,728, fails. The correct answer is the exact inversion of the POS case: same style, hostile environment, opposite allocation.
