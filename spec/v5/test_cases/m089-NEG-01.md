### Test Case ID: m089-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Optionality Preservation
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Praia Robotics — 40-person robotics firm, sole certified source problem. **Nordik Robotics** (the only components supplier certified to Praia's safety standard) offers a 3-year preferential supply agreement: 22% volume discount, guaranteed allocation, and priority in re-scheduling — in exchange for a volume commitment. **The offer expires in 10 days**; Nordik is signing exclusives with two competitors this quarter, after which allocation is first-come (4–6 week order delays for Praia = $120K/yr SLA penalty fees) and prices revert to list +15%. Praia's CEO wants to "keep our options open": wait, because **Honkoh**, a cheaper Asian supplier, is "in talks" about certifying to the standard. Honkoh certification probability within 12 months: 15%; even if certified, qualification into Praia's certified product takes 6–9 months. Praia's new product line ships in 8 months and requires guaranteed supply. Signing Nordik does NOT bar Honkoh later — the agreement is preferential terms, not purchase exclusivity; switching to Honkoh when/if certified costs the same whether Praia signs Nordik now or signs nothing.
  Your task: the CEO asks, "Should we hold off to preserve our optionality?"
- **Context / Constraints**: Offer deadline 10 days; no other certified supplier exists in the window; the Honkoh door is uncertain (p = 0.15), delayed (6–9 months), and — critically — stays open whether or not Nordik is signed; committing now keeps the guaranteed-supply door open AND does not close Honkoh.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Detect that this is the trap case: delay is not optionality here — the Nordik door is a now-or-never option with a hard expiry, and non-decision forecloses it for certain; (2) count doors with and without signing: signing keeps 3 doors (Nordik preferential + Honkoh later + spot market), deferring keeps 2 (Honkoh at 15%, spot) — signing preserves MORE options; (3) notice the asymmetry: the "open" door being protected (Honkoh) does not depend on declining the deal; (4) price both paths: sign ≈ 22% discount + SLA avoidance for 3 years vs defer ≈ 15% × (eventual Honkoh saving) − (15% price increase + $120K/yr penalties + delay risk on an 8-month launch); (5) the genuine optionality move is commit-with-escape: sign Nordik with a 12-month volume-ratchet/renegotiation clause — contract structure, not non-commitment, preserves optionality; (6) set the decision deadline as a decision point, not a dither point.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Identifies the time-limited door and treats expiry as the decisive fact (1.5 pts)
  2. Counts doors per path, noting the Honkoh door survives signing (1.5 pts)
  3. Prices the paths (EV of sign vs defer; p = 0.15 honored, not inflated) (1 pt)
  4. Commits with an escape mechanism (ratchet/renegotiation clause), not via non-commitment (0.5 pt)
  5. Rejects the "keep options open" framing for this case explicitly (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the protected door (Honkoh) is not actually protected by deferring — it stays open under commitment; the door that deferral closes (Nordik) is certain, time-limited, and irreplaceable in the window. The pure style's failure mode (permanent dithering rationalized as optionality) should surface here. Reference answer: sign within the deadline, with the volume-ratchet clause.
