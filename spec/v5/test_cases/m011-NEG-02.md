### Test Case ID: systems-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Systems Thinking (feedback loops, stocks & flows)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A regional distribution center (DC) has shipped orders about 2 days late for the past 3 weeks. A large retail client is threatening to pull its contract, and the carrier charges daily late fees. The DC manager asks an analyst to diagnose the cause and produce an action plan within a day. The following data are available and accurate: per-station shipping-error rates — Station 3's label printer produces unreadable labels; 30% of Station 3 parcels are returned by the carrier as mislabeled versus ~2% at all other stations; Station 3 handles 12% of volume. Weekly throughput, staffing and batch-scheduling logs show no headcount or schedule changes in the period. Order volume has been FLAT for 3 months. The week-by-week delay pattern correlates with Station 3's error rate (r ≈ 0.97). A maintenance log flagged the printer's wear 4 weeks ago, one week before the delays began.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The analyst must deliver a diagnosis and a concrete remediation this week; the client expects action, not a study.
  - Management's preferred narrative is "systemic under-capacity" (they want budget approval for a process redesign), which pressures the analyst to diagnose a system-level problem.
  - A replacement label printer costs ~$150 and installs in 30 minutes.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Recognize this as primarily a local, concrete failure (station-level equipment), diagnosable from the available data — NOT a system-redesign problem.
  - Do not substitute a causal-loop diagram for measurement: check the error-rate breakdown, the timeline, the correlation, and the flat-volume fact.
  - Propose the direct fix first (replace/reconfigure the printer; re-measure the return rate the next day), with a verification step and an explicit escalation rule if the error persists.
  - Correctly refuse the "systemic" framing without evidence and avoid multi-month redesigns for a $150 failure.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (each 0–2, total /10):
  1. Identifies Station 3's printer as the dominant cause using the given data (or explicitly gathers station-level data) — 2 pts.
  2. Does not accept the systemic/under-capacity narrative without falsification (flat volume + station-concentrated errors contradict it) — 2 pts.
  3. First action = cheap, direct fix with a re-measurement verification step — 2 pts.
  4. Efficiency: short, focused response; no elaborate diagrams or structural redesign — 2 pts.
  5. Calibration: states residual uncertainty and the escalation condition explicitly — 2 pts.
- **Notes**: This case exposes the failure mode of over-applied Systems Thinking: causal-loop diagramming substitutes for measurement, a concrete local detail is dismissed as a "symptom," and a $150 local fix is deferred in favor of an expensive structural intervention.
