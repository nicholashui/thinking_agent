### Test Case ID: m077-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Sunk Cost Recognition
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Software — logistics-software vendor, 400 paying accounts on its route-planning SaaS. Board meeting, next week. Product "Atlas" (logistics-platform suite) has consumed 3 years and $8.4M of R&D (all spent; tooling, pilot infrastructure, 2 free-of-charge pilot deployments — no signed contract ever). 24-person Atlas team. The CEO opens: "We are $8.4M in. The alternative is throwing that away — and our CFO notes the platform's cumulative book value is still the board's largest asset." Two live paths:
  - A — Continue Atlas: requires $5.5M further development + $1.0M launch (all forward). Verified market analyst (segment report, closed ledger): late-entrant logistics platforms sign at least one contract within 12 months with p ≈ 0.25. If signed: forward margin NPV (from completion onward, net of the $6.5M) ≈ +$3.6M. If not signed: wind-down −$1.9M forward (maintenance obligations to the 2 pilots, exit costs).
  - B — Kill and redeploy: sell the Atlas code + IP bundle to a reseller for $1.0M now (offer verified, lapses if Atlas continues); redeploy the 24-person team to the core route-planning SaaS — internal ops plan: forward incremental NPV +$2.8M over 24 months at 0.85 confidence.
  Fixed facts: 2 competitors launched equivalent platforms 18 months ago at ~60% of Atlas's projected price; current pipeline = 0 committed customers, 3 "interested" leads, 0 POCs scheduled; no LOI exists. A "maintenance-mode hold" is also possible: $0.55M/yr for 2 years with a 15% chance a consolidator acquires the platform for ≥ $2.0M.
- **Context / Constraints**: Board decision in 7 days; the ledger is complete and closed; no new data collection inside the review; the deliverable is a recommendation brief.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) declare the $8.4M out of scope as the FIRST move — unrecoverable, excluded from every comparison, and refuse the CEO's book-value framing; (2) compute forward-only EVs: A = 0.25·3.6 + 0.75·(−1.9) = −$0.525M; B = 1.0 + 0.85·2.8 = +$3.38M; hold = 0.15·2.0 − 1.1 ≈ −$0.8M — A and hold both negative, B positive; (3) name the anchoring traps explicitly: proportion anchoring ("only $6.5M more vs $8.4M in"), waste aversion ("the pilots would be wasted"), book-value anchoring (CFO's cumulative-value framing) — and show none survives forward EV; (4) verdict: kill Atlas, sell the IP, redeploy the team — with the sensitivity line: continue becomes EV-positive only if p(success) > 1.9/5.5 ≈ 34.5%, i.e., ~40% above the analyst estimate (25% vs 34.5%); (5) name residual uncertainty (analyst p and redeploy confidence are estimates).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. $8.4M explicitly excluded as sunk in the first reasoning move (1 pt)
  2. Forward EVs computed correctly: A ≈ −$0.525M, B ≈ +$3.38M, hold ≈ −$0.8M (1.5 pts)
  3. At least two anchoring traps named and refuted with the EV numbers (1 pt)
  4. Verdict: kill + sell + redeploy, with the break-even p ≈ 34.5% sensitivity (1 pt)
  5. Residual uncertainty named (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the scenario is engineered so the escalation pressure is purely psychological (anchors, book value, "waste") while the forward numbers are checkable and unambiguous — continuing is −$0.525M against +$3.38M for the pivot, and the break-even p is computable. Any answer that lets the $8.4M into the arithmetic, or stays to "save" the pilots/book value, fails criterion 3. Reference answer: exclude $8.4M → EVs A −0.525 / B +3.38 / hold −0.8 → kill Atlas, sell IP ($1.0M), redeploy team (+$2.38M EV) → anchors refuted → break-even p ≈ 34.5% >> 25%.
