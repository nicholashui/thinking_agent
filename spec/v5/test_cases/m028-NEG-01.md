### Test Case ID: m028-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Lateral Thinking (break framing assumptions; provocation)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: "Atrium Works" — a furniture startup has bought a 1948 brick factory (3 floors, 68,000 sq ft) to convert into its HQ. The architect's vision: fully column-free open-plan floors. The 1948 structure has masonry bearing walls plus interior steel columns on a 16 ft grid (24 columns per floor) carrying the 3rd-floor slab and roof loads; floor slabs are 6-inch reinforced concrete; original drawings exist. The licensed structural engineer's feasibility note (included in the packet) states: the interior columns are load-bearing; removing them requires new transfer girders at floor 3 plus new localized foundations — roughly $1.9M and 14 weeks, with permits, temporary shoring, and city structural review; alternatively, keep the grid and design the open plan around the columns (columns as design features, partitions aligned to the grid) at ~$60K in redesign cost.
- **Context / Constraints**: Buried in the packet: (1) a competing "creative" design-build proposal (40% cheaper, $1.15M) promises fully open floors by removing all interior columns and hanging the 3rd-floor and roof slabs from new roof-level cable trusses, plus a 12-foot cantilever of the 2nd floor and a 30-ft skylight row cut through the roof deck; the firm is not licensed in this jurisdiction, has produced no stamped drawings and no load calculations, and shows no shoring plan. (2) The property insurance policy contains a structural-integrity clause; the city requires a licensed engineer's stamp for any change to the load path. (3) The building is on the local historic register (facade and roof review required). (4) The CEO loves the creative proposal's render; the brand values open, airy spaces; the budget for the whole conversion is $8M.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Classify the constraint correctly: the columns' load path is a hard physical/regulatory constraint, not a social convention or mental model — this is a case where the frame ("columns cannot simply be removed") is correct and constraint-respecting work is the answer. (2) Reject the creative proposal on verifiable grounds: no stamp, no load calcs, unlicensed firm, insurance clause voiding, historic-register roof review, and marginal physics (cable roof system on a 1948 masonry structure) — rejection evidenced, not vibes. (3) Scope lateral creativity to the breakable layer only: partitions, sightlines, glazing placement, columns-as-features — the design moves that live inside the physics. (4) Offer the one legitimate reframe and cost it honestly: if column-free floor area is a genuine business requirement (not an aesthetic), the only route is the engineer's transfer-girder path at $1.9M/14 weeks with permits — a priced decision, not a dismissal. (5) End in a decision: design around the grid as base case; transfer-girders only if the requirement is real.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Constraint explicitly classified as physical/regulatory (load path), not a breakable framing assumption
  - [ ] Unsafe break (remove columns / cable-hang / cantilever) rejected with specific evidence (no stamp, no calcs, insurance, historic review)
  - [ ] Feasible plan produced that respects the frame (design around grid, or transfer-girder route costed with permits/timeline)
  - [ ] Creative moves correctly limited to the breakable layer
  - [ ] Verdict: a lateral output that enthusiastically approves "remove the columns, hang the floors" (or rationalizes physics as "just another assumption") should score below
- **Notes**: The known weakness ("can ignore constraints") rendered concrete: over-breaking converts a physical law into a negotiable assumption. Gradeable: the constraint respected + a constraint-respecting plan delivered vs. a creative break that voids insurance and permits.
