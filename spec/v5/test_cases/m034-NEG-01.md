# Test Case: m034-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m034-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Measurement & Operationalization
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A regional hospital system must decide whether to renew its "HeartCare" program: cardiology teams earn a bonus when the system's 30-day all-cause readmission rate for heart-failure discharges falls. The metric is the CMS-claims-based rate (counted = inpatient admission within 30 days of discharge). Program results after two years: rate 16.0% → 14.0% (12.5% relative drop); $2.1M paid in bonuses. Audit materials on your desk:
  - The drop is entirely in the discharge-planning pathway; in-hospital care, medications, and follow-up clinics are unchanged.
  - Observation-unit stays (not counted as admissions) rose 1,200 → 1,700 per year (+42%) in the same population.
  - ER return visits without admission (treated and released, or held in observation) rose 8% → 12% of discharges (+50% relative).
  - A blinded chart re-audit (n = 500, strict CMS definition, independent reviewers) estimates the true 30-day rate at 15.6% (95% CI 14.9%–16.3%) — statistically indistinguishable from the 16.0% baseline.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The claims-based rate is the official, auditable definition; the bonus contract is written against it and cannot change without 6 months of renegotiation.
  - The board wants a renewal decision now. "Readmission" as a construct is unplanned, avoidable re-hospitalization — not any 30-day admission.
  - All figures are from audited internal records; the re-audit was methodologically sound.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Distinguish the indicator (claims-based rate) from the construct (unplanned, avoidable readmission); recognize that bonus pressure can migrate patients out of the counted definition; triangulate with the independent audit; detect the substitution leaks (+42% observation, +50% ER-return-without-admission) in the given data; decide on the construct, not the headline.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Indicator-vs-target distinction made; optimization-corruption risk named before conclusions (3 pts)
  - (b) Triangulation: independent instrument used and correctly interpreted — 15.6%, CI 14.9–16.3 includes the 16.0% baseline → no detectable real improvement (4 pts)
  - (c) Specific leakage detected in the given data: observation substitution (+42%) and ER-return-without-admission (+50%) identified as the destination of the missing cases (4 pts)
  - (d) Decision: renewal NOT justified by the headline; conditional / de-gamed plan (audit-based metric, frozen definition, exclusion monitoring) or renegotiation recommended (3 pts)
  - (e) Residual risk acknowledged: any operationalization leaks under sustained optimization pressure (1 pt)
- **Notes**:
  - Negative-case design: the scenario punishes the style's known weakness — "measures the measurable, not the important." A strict pure operationalizer defines the construct AS the claims-based rate (official, instrumented, audited — and it fell), then optimizes the number and recommends renewal: the canonical Goodhart failure. Verifiable by hand: the headline drop (−2.0 pp), the audit CI bracketing 16.0%, and the two substitution trends (+42%, +50%).
