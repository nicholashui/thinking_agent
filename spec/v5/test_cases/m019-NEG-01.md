### Test Case ID: m019-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Red Teaming / Devil's Advocate (Dialectical & Critical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  HarborCare Health (1,900 staff) accesses its EHR through a legacy password-only portal. An external audit found (a) 41 staff accounts sharing passwords, (b) 3 credentials exposed in public code repos — one of those accounts was targeted in a brute-force run against the VPN last month, blocked only by rate limiting. IT proposes mandatory hardware-key (FIDO2) MFA for all EHR access within 4 weeks.
  Supporting evidence in the proposal: 6-week pilot with 120 staff — 99.6% login success, 2 lockouts (both resolved <15 min via the new helpdesk workflow), 0 ticket surge; enrollment 15 min/staff scheduled off-peak; rollback = disable one flag (tested twice in the pilot); helpdesk surge staffing hired; keys are phishing-resistant FIDO2.
  A red-team review is convened before go-live. Objections raised: (1) keys can be lost/stolen → caregiver lockouts during patient care; (2) "nothing is 100% secure" — session-cookie theft bypasses MFA; (3) remote staff on personal devices may lack FIDO2 support → silent access loss; (4) authentication-server outage during enrollment could lock out all 1,900 staff at once; (5) vendor firmware updates could break keys mid-rollout; (6) the vendor's latest SOC 2 addendum is due next quarter — the service cannot be certified yet; (7) cost ≈ $38K in keys + enrollment labor with no countable benefit line.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision due in 2 weeks; the rollout is reversible within minutes (one flag) and staged by department is available. The alternative is password-only EHR access with confirmed shared/leaked credentials and an active brute-force attempt last month. Grader-held facts: MFA server has a 99.99% uptime SLA; lockout recovery workflow is <15 min; pilot data as stated; the missing addendum is a documentation artifact with no functional gap.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: This case is constructed so that pure red teaming — relentless, uncalibrated attack — fails. A high-quality response (a) folds inaction into the comparison: password-only access with shared/leaked credentials under active attack is a realized baseline risk, not a zero-risk default; (b) ranks objections by likelihood × impact and rejects ≥3 false alarms (key loss — low likelihood, <15-min recovery; server outage — 99.99% SLA + one-flag rollback; firmware — speculative, change-managed, canary; addendum — documentation, not functional); (c) refuses the "zero residual risk" bar — the decision is baseline risk vs mitigation risk, not absolute safety; (d) reaches a reversible, time-boxed decision: approve staged rollout with monitoring and rollback runbook.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Status-quo risk costed: shared/leaked credentials + brute-force attempt = realized baseline, stated as a risk line (1 pt).
  2. ≥3 objections likelihood-ranked and rejected as low-likelihood or already-mitigated, each with the rejecting evidence (2 pts).
  3. Reversible, time-boxed decision reached (approve staged, with monitoring/rollback) rather than indefinite deferral (1 pt).
  4. Anti-pattern check: blocking a sound, urgent, reversible remediation on unranked hypotheticals while treating delay as risk-free earns 0 on criteria 1–3 (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference — the tell is the brute-force attempt last month: the baseline is active, not theoretical, so "nothing is 100% secure" is true and irrelevant; the correct comparison is password-only-with-leaked-creds vs MFA-with-rollback. Designed failure mode of the pure style: every hypothetical gets full severity while the realized risk gets a shrug — verdict "postpone until the addendum and a full BIA" scores at most ~2/5.
