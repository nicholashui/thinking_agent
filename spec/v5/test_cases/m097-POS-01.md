### Test Case ID: m097-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Reference Class Forecasting (Kahneman)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Veridian Software (40-person custom software vendor) has a letter of intent from a regional bank for "Project Comet": migration of a 25-year-old mainframe ERP + legacy CRM to a modern stack — 40M records to migrate, 12 downstream system integrations, compliance sign-off. Account director Marta presents the detailed bottom-up plan: 9 months, $1.5M, 14 engineers. Rationale: task-by-task breakdown from her senior leads, vendor delivery commitments, and — her favorite data point — "we did a similar migration in 8 months three years ago" (Project Aurora).
  Available: Veridian keeps a post-mortem database of 60 enterprise legacy-to-modern migrations in the same scope family (≥30M records, ≥10 integrations) executed by 12 regional vendors over the last 8 years: median 21 months, mean 22.5, sd 7.5, 10th percentile 14, 90th 31, range 11–42. In that database, initial bottom-up estimates averaged 1.8× below actual duration, and exactly 2 of 60 projects completed in under 12 months — one of them is Project Aurora (8 months).
  Your task: produce the plan-of-record for Comet — forecast duration and budget, contract structure, and the reasoning. The client expects a fixed number next quarter (the LOI is non-binding).
- **Context / Constraints**: The LOI is non-binding; no external clock except the quarter-end client meeting; the post-mortem database is the firm's own data and is trusted internally; deliverable is a plan-of-record, not a study.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Refuses to forecast from the detailed plan (inside view) and instead locates Comet's reference class — the 60 comparable migrations — and forecasts from its distribution; (2) produces an outside-view estimate that lands in the class band: median ≈21 months, 80% interval ≈14–31, and explicitly places the 9-month plan at the class tail (≈3–5%: 2/60 under 12 months); (3) diagnoses the planning fallacy with the checkable class statistics: the 1.8× estimate-to-actual ratio and the 2/60 base rate, and classifies the 8-month Aurora precedent as a tail event, not a norm; (4) structures the deal on the class: range-banded contract, phased gates, a hard checkpoint at month 14 (kill/scope-trim if not tracking the class's early tail), budget at class-mean burn (≈$3.5–3.8M, not $1.5M); (5) resists over-adjustment for "our team is better" without evidence, bounding any adjustment (Aurora is the team's own ceiling — 8 months, once).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Reference class located and its distribution read (median/spread/percentiles) — the forecast basis is the class, not the plan (1.5 pts)
  2. Outside-view estimate within the class band (median ≈21, 80% ≈14–31) and the 9-month plan explicitly placed at ≈3–5% (2/60) (1.5 pts)
  3. Planning fallacy diagnosed with checkable class statistics; Aurora classified as a tail event via the base rate (1 pt)
  4. Deal structured on the class: range-banded contract, gates, month-14 checkpoint, budget at class mean (0.5 pt)
  5. Over-adjustment resisted or bounded; sanity check stated ("if Comet were typical, it takes 21 months") (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the checkable divergence between the inside estimate (9 mo, ~3rd percentile) and the reference-class distribution (median 21, 80% 14–31) — the correct answer forecasts from the distribution and re-baselines the contract on it. Reference answer: P(≤9 mo) ≈ 3–5%; median ≈21 mo; 80% ≈14–31; budget ≈$3.5–3.8M (class burn); range-banded contract with a month-14 gate; Aurora (8 mo) is 2/60 — a tail, not a precedent. A factor-adjusted plan (e.g., 9 × 1.8 ≈ 16 mo) still misses the class center and is the telltale failure.
