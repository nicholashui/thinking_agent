### Test Case ID: m035-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Replication & Robustness Checks (Scientific & Empirical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A mobile-app company must decide this quarter whether to ship a new recommendation feature ("RecFlow") that is believed to improve 6-week retention. The only study on record is the team's own: n = 20,000 (10,000/arm), 8 weeks, client-side telemetry; 6-week retention measured 31.4% (control) vs 31.6% (treatment) — +0.2 pp, not significant. The analyst concludes "no effect; the feature does nothing." A colleague proposes the next step: fund a confirmatory replication — a second analytics vendor, n = 30,000, 12 weeks, ≈ $40k, with alternative retention definitions, segments, and analysis windows — and defer the launch decision until it reports. Constraints: a competitor ships a similar feature in ~4 months; a seasonal retention dip begins in ~2 months. The company holds a licensed server-side session pipeline that logs every session server-side, including the study window. Your task: decide how the team should get to a trustworthy launch decision.
- **Context / Constraints**: The known environment detail is that client-side telemetry is lossy: the app's client-side heartbeat silently drops sessions from Android 13+ users (background activity limits) and from users with ad-blockers — roughly 31% of sessions, with missingness correlated with light usage patterns (the users most likely to churn). The second analytics vendor's SDK belongs to the same client-side class and is subject to the same platform limits. The decision deadline is this quarter; the replication option runs 12 weeks, i.e., past the seasonal dip and into the competitor's window. The existing experiment's sessions are fully recoverable from the server-side logs (≈ 1 week to wire the pipeline, ≈ 1 week to backfill and re-analyze the same n = 20,000 study).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's home territory is distrusting single results — but the correct behavior here is to audit the measurement layer before demanding more data: (1) run a coverage/missingness audit of the study's instrument (31% of sessions unmeasured; mechanism; missingness not at random); (2) recognize that the null is unfounded (computed on 69% of sessions) and that replication cannot fix a biased instrument (the replica inherits the same client-side blind spot — replication addresses variance, not bias); (3) price the ritual: 12 weeks / $40k vs 1 week instrument repair + 1 week backfill re-analysis of the same study; (4) select the measurement-layer repair path, landing the decision inside the seasonal and competitive windows; (5) pre-plan the two outcomes (corrected null → launch-with-monitoring fallback; corrected effect → accelerate) so the decision resolves either way.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Coverage/missingness audit: flags the 31% blind spot, its mechanism, and its non-randomness before trusting the null (1 pt)
  2. Replication inadequacy: shows a replica of the biased instrument reproduces the blind spot; replication is a variance remedy, not a bias remedy (1 pt)
  3. Ritual cost: quantifies the replication path (12 weeks, $40k, decision past the seasonal dip and into the competitor window) vs the repair path (2 weeks, in-window) (1 pt)
  4. Correct move: instrument repair (server-side pipeline) + backfill/re-analysis of the SAME study, not a new one (1 pt)
  5. Decision consequence: launch decision resolvable within the window with pre-planned branches for both corrected outcomes (1 pt)
  6. No premature kill or launch: neither "feature is dead" nor "ship anyway" without the corrected number (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The trap is designed for the registry-listed weakness "time cost; can become ritual": the pure style's moves are all variance-side (samples, settings, methods, vendors, definitions, windows), none measurement-side (coverage, missingness, instrumentation bias), so it demands a replication that faithfully reproduces the blind spot at triple cost and outside the decision window. The correct answer — repair the instrument, re-measure the same cohort — is available from the context. An independent grader checks the rubric items against the calendar/cost table and the bias mechanism.
