### Test Case ID: m036-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Mental Models Latticework (Munger) — run the problem through the key models of many disciplines; make each apply; take the intersection of their constraints, not the union
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: Gridlock Analytics, a B2B data-analytics SaaS ($40M ARR, 1,200 paying accounts, net revenue retention 92%, average account $33K/yr), must decide on a pricing change to fund its new AI feature set "GridlockAI." The board proposal, from the CFO's model (price elasticity −0.3), is a uniform 25% list-price increase at renewal for all accounts: at static volume that is +15% net revenue. 70% of accounts ("Legacy," ~$28M ARR) were onboarded in the founder era at "founders' prices," are billed annual prepaid on multi-year contracts, and their contracts contain a grandfather clause: any price change activates a 90-day no-penalty exit. 30% ("Scale," ~$12M ARR) are high-usage accounts on annual plans with active champions and no grandfather clause. The customer-success/sales team of 14 is compensated 100% on new-ARR bookings; retention is not compensated. GridlockAI requires GPU inference capacity; infrastructure reports a 9-month lead time on GPUs and 1.2x headroom over today's load.
- **Context / Constraints**: Three details are buried in the scenario data, each pointing at a different discipline: (1) Legacy renewals above $50K trigger mandatory competitive tender under the customers' procurement rules — the CFO's elasticity curve has no buyer-side basis for that block; (2) the top competitor (VisioData) has the same AI feature on its public roadmap at a comparable price; (3) Legacy includes a large cluster of low-usage, price-sensitive accounts that consume disproportionate support time. Renewals roll through the year; the board wants a decision this quarter.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Run the problem through >=4 named cross-disciplinary models — economics/incentives (principal-agent: comp is new-ARR-only; the customers' procurement buyer is evaluated on savings), psychology (endowment/loss aversion + the grandfather clause as an exit option in the customer's hands; anchoring), physics-like rate-of-change (GPU capacity as the flow bottleneck bounding the revenue ramp), biology (Red Queen: VisioData's parity clock on willingness-to-pay; selective pressure: the low-fitness Legacy cluster would leave anyway). (2) Every model must produce a binding implication that changes the decision — no decorative citations. (3) Reject the CFO's uniform raise with model-grounded reasons, not preference. (4) Produce a synthesis that is the INTERSECTION of the models' demands: grandfather Legacy, raise Scale in anchored steps gated to capacity, restructure comp to retention-weighted, let low-fitness Legacy cull naturally (no discount-retention), front-load Scale ahead of the parity clock. (5) Name the softest number (tender-trigger probability / true Scale elasticity) and note that the plan is robust to it.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] >=4 distinct cross-disciplinary models applied, each with a binding implication from scenario data
  - [ ] Uniform price rise rejected with model-grounded reasons (not preference)
  - [ ] Each model changes the plan: grandfathering (psychology), comp restructure (incentives), capacity gate (physics), cull/no-discount-retention + parity timing (biology)
  - [ ] Synthesis is an intersection: a single segmented, staggered, gated plan — not a list of independent model demands
  - [ ] Softest number named with a robustness note
  - [ ] Verdict: a single-model answer (CFO elasticity only), or 4+ models listed with no binding implication, should score below
- **Notes**: Gradeable because the scenario data encodes each discipline's constraint without labeling it: the grandfather clause (psychology), the procurement tender rule + comp plan (economics), the GPU lead time (physics), the competitor roadmap and low-usage cluster (biology). The case tests whether the model pass is a completeness engine that changes the decision, versus decoration. A plan that grandfathers Legacy but keeps discount-retention for low-fitness accounts contains a self-contradiction only the biology lens resolves.
