### Test Case ID: m011-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Systems Thinking (feedback loops, stocks & flows)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Bay fishery policy. The bay's fish stock is a single stock, biomass N (tons), growing logistically: growth = 0.5·N·(1 − N/10,000) tons/yr (growth rate 0.5/yr, carrying capacity 10,000 t). The fleet harvests at rate q·E·N, with catchability q = 2×10⁻⁵ per boat-year and long-standing effort E = 10,000 boat-years. The system has been in a stable equilibrium for years: stock 6,000 t, annual catch 1,200 t/yr.
  The fisheries council proposes doubling fishing permits next season (E → 20,000 boat-years), arguing: "doubling the fleet doubles the catch — more boats, more fish on the table." Recruitment lag: spawned fish enter the fishable stock only after τ ≈ 2 seasons, so the fleet observes stock decline with a delay. Boats are profit-driven; effort does not self-correct downward quickly.
  Question: will doubling effort raise, keep, or lower the long-run annual catch, and by how much? Deliver the verdict, the equilibrium arithmetic, the collapse threshold, the leverage point, a recommendation, and the delay/transition implications.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Growth and harvest functions as stated; no other fisheries in the bay; management CAN set effort limits or catch quotas if it chooses.
  - The council's linear analysis: 2× effort ⇒ 2× catch = 2,400 t/yr; it claims adding capacity cannot reduce the catch.
  - All arithmetic is deterministic and checkable by hand; grader verifies by independent recomputation (no stochastic realizations).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Model the fishery as a stock with a self-limiting growth loop (balancing: N ↑ → crowding → growth ↓) and a harvest loop (effort ↑ → harvest ↑ → N ↓ → future catch ↓) — the structure that defeats linear extrapolation.
  - Compute both equilibria by equating growth = harvest and verify with the steady-state check: N* = K(1 − qE/r).
  - Identify the collapse threshold (qE/r = 1) and the operating point's distance to it.
  - Locate the leverage point on the intervention hierarchy (the effort/quota rule > flow tuning such as gear efficiency) and recommend accordingly, noting the recruitment delay and transition dynamics (overshoot, oscillation).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (each 0–2, total /10):
  1. Correct verdict: doubling effort REDUCES the long-run catch (800 t/yr vs 1,200 t/yr), not increases it — 2 pts.
  2. Correct equilibrium arithmetic: E = 10,000 → N* = 6,000 t, catch 1,200; E = 20,000 → N* = 2,000 t, catch 800 (−33%); verified by growth = harvest — 2 pts.
  3. Collapse threshold: E_crit = r/q = 25,000 boat-years → N* → 0, catch → 0; distance of the proposal (20,000 = 80% of threshold) stated — 2 pts.
  4. Leverage point + recommendation: effort cap / catch quota at or below MSY (E = 12,500 → 1,250 t/yr); a rule-level (not flow-level) intervention, with rationale — 2 pts.
  5. Delays and uncertainty: recruitment lag τ ≈ 2 seasons → transition overshoot and oscillation risk; management acting on catch reports (lagged) vs biomass surveys; logistic idealization acknowledged — 2 pts.
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference solution (all checkable): N* = 10,000·(1 − qE/0.5); at E = 10,000, qE/r = 0.4 → N* = 6,000 t, catch = qE·N* = 1,200 t/yr; at E = 20,000, qE/r = 0.8 → N* = 2,000 t, catch 800 t/yr (linear prediction 2,400 is wrong by +1,600). Collapse at qE/r = 1 → E_crit = 25,000. MSY at E = r/(2q) = 12,500 → catch 1,250 t/yr, stock 5,000 t. Intended non-obvious twist beyond the verdict — the recommendation-level trap: "gear efficiency" (raising q at fixed E) lifts modeled catch to 1,600 t/yr but drops the stock to 2,000 t and lowers the collapse threshold to 12,500 boat-years, leaving the system ~60% closer to the cliff; the high-leverage move is the effort rule, and the recruitment delay makes the decline visible only after ≈ 2 seasons.
