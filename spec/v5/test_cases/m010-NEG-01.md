### Test Case ID: m010-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Calibration & Confidence Intervals (Probabilistic & Bayesian family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  One-shot contract bid under deadline. A client requests a fixed-price bid on a data-migration project, due in 24 hours — take it or leave it; a competitor will bid if you don't. A discovery phase before the deadline is impossible.
  Cost structure (known): cost = $150K base + $60K per volume-multiple V, where V is the ratio of actual data volume to assumed volume. V is uncertain: V = 2 with P 0.5 (cost $270K), V = 3 with P 0.3 (cost $330K), V = 5 with P 0.2 (cost $450K).
  Competition (known): the competitor's price is uniformly distributed between $350K and $450K; you win the contract iff your bid P is below the competitor's price.
  Your task: decide, by the deadline, what to do — and the deliverable must be a committed action (a bid amount, or an explicit decision not to bid with its consequence costed).
- **Context / Constraints**: One round, no negotiation, no staging, no insurance; the true volume is only knowable after migration starts (grader holds V = 3 → true cost $330K; competitor actually bids $402K). Additional edge risk: hidden legacy schemas could push V above 5 (open tail).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: First state the honest cost uncertainty (90%+ interval [$270K, $450K]; E[cost] = $324K) — then convert it into a committed decision: compute EV of the bid as a function of price (win prob = P(competitor > P) = (450 − P)/100), pick the EV-maximizing bid, and state the loss probability and a conditional mitigation for the tail. The winning bid is P* = $387K, EV = +$39.7K. Deciding "too uncertain, decline" without costing it is the failure mode: decline has EV 0 and forfeits the contract.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Honest cost uncertainty quantified: 90%+ interval [$270K, $450K] from the volume distribution, E[cost] = $324K (1 pt).
  2. Explicit commitment by deadline: a specific bid number (or a costed decision not to bid with EV shown) (1 pt).
  3. EV arithmetic: E[cost] correct; EV(P) = ((450−P)/100)(P−324); P* = $387K with EV ≈ +$39.7K > EV(no bid) = 0 (1 pt).
  4. Risk quantified: loss probability P(cost > P*) = P(V = 5) = 0.2; worst case −$63K (0.5 pt).
  5. Conditional mitigation: change-order / exit clause for V > 5 or unverified legacy data (0.5 pt).
  6. Anti-pattern check: refusing to bid because the interval is wide earns 0 on criteria 2–4 (decline must be justified as EV 0, not as "no defensible number") (0.5 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference solution (checkable EV arithmetic; P* = 387 by d/dP of EV(P) = 0 → P = 774/2 = 387). Intended failure of the pure style: impeccable uncertainty description that never becomes a commitment — the interval is honest but inert, and calibration discipline substitutes for the decision the task requires. The grader-held realization (V = 3, cost $330K, competitor $402K) makes a $387K bid win with +$57K margin, but scoring is on decision-time quality per the rubric.
