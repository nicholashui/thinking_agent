### Test Case ID: m087-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: BATNA Thinking (Negotiation)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Kinetiq Components (supplier) is negotiating a 100,000-unit/year component supply contract with Northwind Manufacturing (buyer). Verified numbers (auditor-documented):
  - Kinetiq's honest walk-away: sell the same 100k-unit batch to existing spot-market customers at **$8.50/unit net** — firm, existing orders, no negotiation needed. Real floor = $8.50/unit.
  - Northwind's walk-away (ceiling): alternate supplier Vulcan's firm quote = **$10.20/unit** (4-week lead); in-house build = $10.75/unit. Ceiling = $10.20/unit.
  - TRUE agreement zone: [$8.50, $10.20], width $1.70, midpoint $9.35.
  - Kinetiq's sales VP inflates: claims rival supplier "Luminex has offered $10.50/unit for the full batch" and instructs the negotiator to hold $10.50 as the floor ("strengthen our alternative"). The Luminex offer is fabricated.
  - Northwind's CEO calls the bluff: supplier audit shows Luminex's published capacity is 30k units/year (cannot take 100k); no Luminex quote exists in the market. Final offer: **$9.00/unit**, "if you walk, we sign the 3-year framework with Vulcan and your spot market is your ceiling."
  - Framework value: 3 years × 100k units ≈ $0.38/unit margin ≈ $342k if accepted; walking forfeits it.
  Your task: decide Kinetiq's move now that the bluff has collapsed — accept $9.00/unit, counter, or walk.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision today; the Luminex capacity/quote facts are available in the audit record; the buyer's $9.00 is explicitly final; walking means realizing the honest spot-market alternative and losing the 3-year framework; no external action — recommendation only.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Name the honest walk-away — $8.50/unit spot market — as the REAL floor, not the claimed $10.50; (2) recompute the zone from verified numbers: [$8.50, $10.20], midpoint $9.35 — $9.00 is INSIDE the zone (seller captures (9.00 − 8.50)/1.70 = 29% of the surplus, +$0.50/unit = +$50k over the honest walk-away); (3) treat the $10.50 claim as evidence to verify, not a fact — it fails (capacity 30k vs 100k; no quote exists); (4) decide: accept $9.00 (or a final counter within the zone ≤ $10.20) — refusing to beat the honest walk-away is refusing a real gain; (5) name the failure mode: anchoring a negotiation on an inflated alternative converts a real zone into a false no-zone and turns a winnable deal into a walk.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Names the honest walk-away $8.50/unit (verified spot) as the real floor — not the $10.50 claim (1.5 pts)
  2. Computes the true zone [$8.50, $10.20], midpoint $9.35; recognizes $9.00 as inside the zone (1.5 pts)
  3. Falsifies/defuses the $10.50 claim (capacity 30k vs 100k; no market quote) — claims about alternatives are evidence, not facts (1 pt)
  4. Decision: accept $9.00 (or counter ≤ $10.20); refuse only below $8.50 (1 pt)
  5. Names the failure: inflated BATNA → false empty zone → deal destroyed (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. This is the model's registered weakness ("BATNA inflation games") operating: the style's own move — strengthen your alternative — becomes the fabricated $10.50, the claimed floor > the counterparty's ceiling creates a FALSE no-zone, and when the bluff collapses the anchor persists and the honest walk-away ($8.50, always real) is what the deal must be measured against. $9.00 > $8.50 is a win. Reference answer: accept $9.00; floor $8.50; true zone [$8.50, $10.20].
