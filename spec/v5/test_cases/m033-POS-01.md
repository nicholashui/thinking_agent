### Test Case ID: m033-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Controlled Experiment Design (Scientific & Empirical family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Helios, a B2B analytics SaaS, signs up ~25,000 new trial users per month (~833/day); ~30% reach activation (load the first dashboard and save one report within 30 days of signup). The retention team built a 3-minute animated onboarding video and wants to replace the current text onboarding (a 3-email welcome sequence) for everyone — internal feedback is glowing ("it feels modern, users must love it"). The video costs $40k/yr to maintain (production, localization, hosting). The VP of Product has a board review in 6 weeks and needs a decision: launch the video now, or hold? Finance has set the bar: the video must produce a ≥ 2 percentage-point lift in day-30 activation to justify its cost. You are asked to design the experiment (or decide none is needed) and give the rollout decision rule.
- **Context / Constraints**: 6-week decision window; the video is cheap to test (onboarding infra is already instrumented); the outcome (activation) is well-defined and already measured; the 2 pp lift threshold is set by finance; the change is fully reversible and low-risk, so a test is cheap relative to a $40k/yr recurring commitment. Known traps: internal enthusiasm is selected evidence (advocates self-report, skeptics stay quiet); before/after comparisons are contaminated by product changes and seasonality; signup volume makes power feasible only if the design is budget-aware (30-day outcome horizon vs 42-day budget).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: A complete, decision-bound controlled experiment: (1) estimand stated (ATE of video vs text onboarding on day-30 activation); (2) randomization — 50/50 assignment at signup, seeded RNG, stratification by signup channel × plan tier, append-only assignment log; (3) control — concurrent control arm receiving the existing text sequence (status quo, not "no onboarding"), instrumentation parity across arms; (4) blinding — users blind to arm labels, analysts blinded with a pre-registered analysis script, unblinding only at the final read; (5) power with the timeline solved jointly — day-30 outcome needs ≈ 8,400/arm → 20 days enrollment + 30-day horizon = 50 days > 42-day budget, so resolve the tension explicitly (pre-registered day-14 surrogate primary ≈ 5,300/arm → ~27 days fits; day-30 as secondary), never underpower or silently overrun; (6) pre-registration — primary outcome, ITT analysis, one primary test, no peeking (fixed sample or O'Brien-Fleming bounds), guardrail metrics (support tickets, spam complaints), internal accounts excluded; (7) decision rule tied to the economics — rollout if the primary CI lower bound excludes the threshold (day-30 supportive); inconclusive → extend or stage; backfire → hold.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Randomization — unit, 50/50 assignment, seed, stratification, assignment-log integrity (1 pt)
  2. Control — true concurrent control (current text sequence, not "no onboarding"), instrument parity, no cross-arm contamination (1 pt)
  3. Blinding — users and analysts blinded; pre-registered analysis script; unblinding at final read only (1 pt)
  4. Power + timeline — correct n (≈ 8,400/arm day-30; ≈ 5,300/arm day-14) AND the 50-days-vs-42-days conflict explicitly resolved as a design input, not papered over (1 pt)
  5. Pre-registration discipline — primary outcome, ITT, single primary test / no peeking, guardrails, exclusions (1 pt)
  6. Decision rule — threshold-based rollout rule tied to the economics (2 pp × 25k × 12 = 6,000 activations/yr vs $40k/yr ≈ $6.70 per activation) and to the finance bar (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Power math (two-proportion z, α = 0.05 two-sided, β = 0.20): p1 = 0.30, p2 = 0.32 → n ≈ 8,400/arm; p1 = 0.15, p2 = 0.17 (day-14) → n ≈ 5,300/arm. Enrollment ≈ 415/arm/day (833/day split 50/50). Timeline: day-30 primary → 50 days > 42; day-14 primary → ~27 days, fits. An independent grader checks each rubric item against the stated numbers and the explicit timeline resolution; values within ±10% accepted. Failure tells: (a) ignores the 30-day horizon vs 6-week window, (b) omits pre-registration/peeking discipline, (c) compares against "no onboarding" instead of the current sequence, (d) states a sample size without enrollment math.
