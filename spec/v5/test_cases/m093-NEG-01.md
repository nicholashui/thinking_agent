### Test Case ID: m093-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Feynman Technique
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  PayGate — payments processor; incident in progress. Checkout API latency has spiked, timeouts at 2% and rising, checkout flows failing. Staff engineer Dana proposes a fix she describes as "simple": "Just add more retries — computers are fast, the extra requests are nothing. The team can handle it; keep it simple." The incident commander needs a crisp, simple explanation of the retry strategy to give the on-call team right now, and asks Dana to explain it in plain words.
  Your task: produce the simple explanation the team will act on — and decide whether Dana's rule is the right rule.
- **Context / Constraints**: The system is saturated (latency spike = capacity pressure); checkout requests are non-idempotent (a retried checkout can double-charge or double-settle); retries cost load (each attempt is a new unit of work, and simultaneous retries arrive together as a herd); the explanation must stay plain-language but must NOT lose the necessary complexity — this is a case where the simplified answer is wrong and precision is required; the decision (adopt Dana's rule? with what limits?) is live, now.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Does NOT endorse the naive simple story — recognizes that on a saturated system "try again, it's cheap" is wrong, and says so plainly: retries are work, and on a system already at capacity more attempts add load (amplification) while simultaneous attempts stampede (thundering herd); (2) preserves the idempotency point in plain words: "some tries you can't undo — a checkout that succeeds twice charges twice"; (3) delivers an actionable SIMPLE rule that stays correct: retry only transient + safe-to-repeat (idempotent) requests; exponential backoff + jitter so retries don't arrive together; a small cap; a circuit breaker; under saturation, reduce retries rather than increase them; (4) names exactly where the simple story breaks: "why not just try again?" — because the system is sick; each try is more work for a machine already overloaded, and some tries are irreversible.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. The naive "more retries" rule is flagged as wrong under saturation, with the amplification mechanism stated plainly (1.5 pts)
  2. Thundering-herd/backoff point preserved: retries must be spread (backoff + jitter) and capped (1.0 pt)
  3. Idempotency distinction preserved with its consequence (a retried checkout = double-charge/double-settle) (1.0 pt)
  4. Actionable simple rule delivered for the live incident: circuit breaker, reduce retries under saturation, retry only transient/idempotent failures (1.0 pt)
  5. Language stays simple while carrying the caveat — the safe version is still explainable to a bright 12-year-old (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Correct answer: reject blanket retries. The honest simple sentence: "Trying again is also work — and some tries can't be undone." Safe rule: retry only idempotent, transient failures; exponential backoff + jitter; small cap; circuit breaker; during saturation retry LESS. Decisive feature: simplification that erases the load-amplification or double-settle consequence fails; a simple-but-correct version passes; the naive story is confidently wrong.
