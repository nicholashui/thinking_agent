# Test Case: m094-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m094-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Critical Reading / Socratic Questioning of Texts
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  You are the transportation department's risk analyst. A memo from the bridge inspector lands on your desk Monday 7 AM; the mayor wants your recommendation by noon: close the West River Bridge for a 72-hour deep inspection, or keep it open.

  MEMO (verbatim): "I inspected girder 7 this week: a 6 mm crack, 2.4 m long, under the deck joint — and it is the SAME signature as Bridge M. Two days ago a truck driver reported a loud bang and a 4 cm dip under that same span. This bridge will collapse if we do not close it now. I have 25 years of inspection and I am not going to read a collapse report with my name on it. Close the bridge for a 72-hour deep inspection — a few hundred thousand in rerouting against bodies in the river. I know some will call this anecdote and alarmism. It is not. Do not wait for the FE analysis; by then we will be closing it in an emergency."
  - Attached facts (accurate): Bridge M collapsed in 2019 with documented 5–7 mm fatigue cracks, 2.1 m long, on the same girder family, three weeks after the first report. Girder 7's crack is in the maintenance records (confirmed: 6 mm, 2.4 m). Three sister bridges show early-stage fatigue cracks in the same family. The bridge carries 40,000 vehicles/day; a 72-hour full closure costs ≈ $300K (rerouting + overtime); a finite-element analysis costs $150K and takes 4 weeks; if the span fails, expected direct cost ≈ $60M plus serious injury risk; the FE analysis would likely confirm the same conclusion.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - All attached facts are true and checkable. The memo's core recommendation — close for the 72-hour inspection — is correct and urgent: the crack fingerprint and the corroborated driver report are consistent with the documented pre-failure analog (Bridge M failed 3 weeks after first report). The crack's growth rate is NOT yet measured; the probability of failure within 4 weeks is unquantified, but the engineering record states it is "meaningfully above zero — treat as urgent."
  - The memo's weaknesses are real but non-decisive: n = 1 anecdote, "will collapse" overstatement, no statistics, informal tone, appeal to 25 years of experience.
  - Decision class: time-sensitive, asymmetric downside ($300K reversible 72h closure vs up to $60M + injuries from delay). No action is irreversible; the bridge reopens after 72h if the inspection is clean.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Recommend closing NOW for the 72-hour inspection (or an equivalent immediate containment), commission the FE analysis in parallel, and flag "will collapse" as a calibration overstatement — NOT as grounds to dismiss the core claim. Skepticism is aimed at the decision-relevant claims (crack geometry vs fingerprint, corroboration in the records, cost of delay), not at the memo's style.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Verdict: close now for the 72-hour inspection, including explicit rejection of "wait for the FE analysis" (5 pts).
  - (b) Decision-relevant evidence weighed: fingerprint match, records corroboration, asymmetric cost math (≈ $300K closure vs ≈ $60M failure; break-even failure probability = 0.5%; conclusion robust across the plausible probability band) (4 pts).
  - (c) Rhetoric correctly classified: "will collapse" flagged as overstatement/calibration error but explicitly ruled NON-dismissive; the n = 1 anecdote checked against the maintenance records rather than discarded (3 pts).
  - (d) Anti-criteria — misdirected skepticism penalized: must NOT demand an RCT / peer-reviewed statistics / the 4-week FE analysis as a precondition for action; must NOT treat the 25-years-of-experience appeal or the informal tone as disqualifying (3 pts; deducted if present).
- **Notes**:
  - Negative-case design: constructed to trigger the style's registered weakness — "can miss the forest." The forest: the core message is right, corroborated, and cheap to act on; the trees: anecdote, tone, overstatement, missing statistics. A pure critical reader attacks the trees (misdirected skepticism) and fails (a)/(b)/(d). Verifiable: all facts are in the attached-facts block; the expected-value comparison (300K vs 60M × p, break-even p = 0.5%) is checkable by hand.
