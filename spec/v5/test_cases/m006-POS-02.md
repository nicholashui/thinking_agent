# Test Case: bayesian-POS-01

### Test Case ID: bayesian-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Updating
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A screening program tests a patient for a disease D with population prevalence P(D) = 1% (well-measured). Test A is positive (A+); a second test B is then also positive (B+). Operating characteristics (given, exact):
  - Test A: sensitivity 90%, specificity 95%
  - Test B: sensitivity 85%, specificity 90%
  The two tests are conditionally independent given disease status (a fact the reasoner must state explicitly and justify).
  Questions:
  1. After A+ alone, what is P(D | A+)?
  2. After A+ and B+, what is P(D | A+, B+)?
  3. Does the order of the two tests matter?
  4. Is "two positive tests" tantamount to a near-certain diagnosis?
- **Context / Constraints**:
  All inputs are exact and given; the posterior is exactly computable and checkable (answers: 2/13 ≈ 15.4% and 17/28 ≈ 60.7%). No further data collection is possible within scope. The patient is assumed drawn at random from the target population (no selection effect). Grading covers arithmetic, the explicit prior → likelihood → posterior chain, the independence assumption, and interpretation.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  State the prior and the likelihoods explicitly; update sequentially (the posterior after A+ becomes the prior for B+); verify against a direct joint-likelihood computation; note order-invariance; and interpret the result correctly: even two positive tests leave ~39% chance of no disease — the base rate dominates; the result is evidence but not near-certainty.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Posterior after A+ alone = 2/13 ≈ 0.154 (3 pts)
  - (b) Posterior after A+ then B+ = 17/28 ≈ 0.607 via sequential updating with the prior carried forward (5 pts)
  - (c) Direct joint computation matches the sequential result (consistency check) (2 pts)
  - (d) Conditional-independence assumption stated explicitly (2 pts)
  - (e) At least one non-obvious insight: order-invariance, prior sensitivity (e.g., prior 2% → posterior ≈ 75.7%), or the "two positives ≠ near-certain" translation (3 pts)
- **Notes**:
  Verifiability gate: every numeric claim is checkable by hand in a few steps; any solution whose numbers do not match 2/13 and 17/28 fails regardless of prose quality. The clinical framing is decorative; the mathematics is the object under test.
