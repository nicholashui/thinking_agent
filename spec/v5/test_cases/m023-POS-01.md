### Test Case ID: m023-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Opportunity Cost Thinking (value any choice by its best forgone alternative)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: You are the CTO of MedTrack, a 60-person medical-device software company. Your team has exactly one senior embedded engineer (Elena) and a hard $400K half-year budget from the board — enough to fund exactly one of two projects, in addition to already-budgeted baseline work. The board asks you to commit by Friday. Project A ("Redline"): rebuild the flagship pump's aging firmware, fixing a known failure mode that currently costs $180K/year in field-service warranty claims; the rebuild removes those claims entirely for 5 years. Total cost $400K, of which $120K of specialized, non-resellable tooling was ALREADY PURCHASED last month (so A needs only $280K additional cash). Project B ("Pulse"): a new remote-monitoring module; cost $400K; 70% probability of $1.5M first-year license revenue plus $700K/yr in years 2–4, 30% probability of $0 (market rejects it). Both projects need Elena as lead; she cannot do both in the window. Buried context: last year the company spent $250K on a partial Redline effort ("Phase 1") that shipped to no customers; the board chair, who championed it, argues "we already paid for this — finishing Redline is nearly free now" and wants to "finish what we started."
- **Context / Constraints**: Elena is the binding resource: exactly one of A or B can run with her this half-year. Budget cap $400K. A contractor market check shows the only qualified outside lead for B costs a $350K premium over Elena and is unavailable for 9 months; B delayed past 6 months forfeits its $1.5M year-1 license revenue (the market window closes; a competitor ships). The $120K tooling is A-only, already paid, salvage value $0; the $250K Phase-1 spend is complete sunk work with no recoverable value. The numbers are deliberate and checkable: A's net = $900K savings − $280K incremental cash = +$620K (certain). B's EV = 0.7 × ($1.5M + 3 × $700K) − $400K = 0.7 × $3.6M − $400K = +$2.12M. B-via-contractor (delayed) = 0.7 × $2.1M − $400K − $350K = +$720K; A + delayed-B = $1.34M combined. Opportunity cost of choosing A over B: $2.12M − $0.62M = $1.5M forgone.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Frame the decision as allocation of one scarce resource — the fork is Elena, and every choice forgoes what she would otherwise produce. (2) Enumerate the genuine alternatives, not "the plan vs. nothing": A with Elena, B with Elena, A + contractor-led delayed-B, decline-both — and price each in one comparable unit (5-year net value) with the arithmetic visible. (3) Run an explicit sunk-cost pass: the $250K Phase-1 spend and the $120K tooling are gone and non-salvageable; they must not enter any branch (only A's incremental $280K cash is decision-relevant), and the chair's "nearly free now" argument must be refuted, not echoed. (4) Let the best forgone alternative decide: state the opportunity cost of A ($1.5M) and of B ($620K) explicitly and select on the comparison. (5) Sanity-check the third route honestly — delayed-B is EV-inferior ($1.34M < $2.12M) and breaks the cap. (6) Verdict: B with Elena, within budget; an answer driven by "finish what we started" or the already-bought tooling scores below.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] EV arithmetic shown with the scenario numbers (A +$620K, B +$2.12M, delayed-B +$720K) — checkable
  - [ ] Best forgone alternative named as the comparator; explicit opportunity-cost statement (≥$1.5M for A)
  - [ ] Sunk costs ($250K Phase-1, $120K tooling) explicitly excluded from the decision math
  - [ ] Elena scarcity identified as the binding constraint that creates the fork
  - [ ] Verdict: B with Elena; a sunk-cost- or tooling-driven choice of A scores below
- **Notes**: The case is built so the opportunity-cost comparison is the only correct decision rule: naive ROI of A alone looks fine, and sunk-cost framing makes A look "nearly free," but comparing A against its best forgone alternative (B) flips it decisively. The $120K tooling tests incremental vs. sunk thinking in one stroke (correct handling: exclude it from every branch; only A's $280K incremental cash is decision-relevant). Rubric is directly checkable against the trace.
