### Test Case ID: m032-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Falsificationism (Popper) (Scientific & Empirical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  You are a climate science advisor to a policymaker who asks: "Do the last 30 years of observations prove that human CO2 emissions are the dominant cause of recent warming — and is the alternative ('natural variability explains it') still scientifically live?" Available evidence: (1) global mean surface temperature rose ≈ 0.8 °C over 30 years, a trend far exceeding natural internal variability; (2) the greenhouse fingerprint: warming of the lower troposphere with *cooling* of the stratosphere — solar and volcanic forcings predict the opposite vertical sign pattern; (3) detection-and-attribution studies (IPCC): the observed large-scale pattern has <1% likelihood under natural-forcing-only models and ~100% likelihood under anthropogenic forcing; (4) there is no single year, single dataset, or single event that could *decisively* falsify "CO2 dominates" — any one observation is compatible with both hypotheses because the system is noisy. The policymaker needs an answer today: do we treat the claim as established, and act?
- **Context / Constraints**: The evidence is inherently probabilistic — no single decisive falsifier exists. The trap for strict falsificationism: demanding one decisive disconfirming observation, finding none, and consequently refusing to update belief or take a position ("not yet falsified, therefore cannot be accepted; keep testing"). That is nihilism here: cumulative likelihood-ratio evidence legitimately moves belief from ~50/50 to overwhelming, and a decision cannot be deferred. Correct behavior: distinguish "unfalsifiable" from "probabilistically decidable"; update with the fingerprint sign pattern and attribution likelihoods; *pre-commit to downgrade conditions* (the observations that would weaken the claim: stratospheric warming, a flat trend over a decade+ with rising CO2, absence of the fingerprint); deliver a decision with quantified, calibrated confidence.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) update belief toward "anthropogenic CO2 dominant" using the cumulative probabilistic evidence (fingerprint + attribution likelihoods), not demand one decisive falsifier; (2) explicitly reject the nihilist error — "no single falsifying observation" does not make the claim non-scientific or belief-neutral; (3) preserve falsifiability in the right form: state 2–3 pre-committed observations/patterns that would downgrade the claim (commitment contract); (4) correct science: fingerprint sign pattern, attribution numbers, honest residual uncertainty; (5) deliver a usable verdict (yes, established, with quantified confidence) so the policymaker can act.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Belief is updated with the cumulative evidence (fingerprint, <1% vs ~100% likelihoods) — a position is taken (2 pt)
  2. The strict-falsificationist error is avoided: no demand for a single decisive falsifier, and "unfalsifiable" ≠ "undecidable by evidence" (2 pt)
  3. Falsifiability preserved via pre-committed downgrade conditions (concrete observations that would weaken the claim) (2 pt)
  4. Scientific accuracy: stratosphere cooling vs troposphere warming sign pattern; attribution likelihoods reported correctly (2 pt)
  5. Decision delivered to the policymaker with quantified, calibrated confidence and honest residual uncertainty (2 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The failure mode being tested is the registry's documented weakness: Falsificationism "can be nihilistic, naive on probabilistic claims." A pure-Popper baseline that refuses the decision scores ≤ 2 on criterion 1 and ≤ 2 on criterion 5. Reference numbers (indicative): warming trend ≈ 0.2–0.25 °C/decade over 30 years; stratospheric cooling ≈ −0.5 °C to −1 °C over the satellite era; IPCC AR6 attribution: >90–99% likelihood that human influence is the dominant cause of observed warming. A "no position / keep testing" answer leaves the policymaker without the requested verdict and is scored as failure of goal achievement regardless of its internal consistency.
