# Test Case: m001-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m001-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles Thinking
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  2:00 AM. You are the on-call engineer for a checkout service. Alarm: p99 latency 8× normal, error rate 3×, one of three instance groups degraded. SLA: the service must be at acceptable health within 20 minutes of the alarm, else a penalty is triggered and an incident review is opened. A runbook exists; for this exact symptom pattern over the past 12 months (n = 90 incidents): "restart the degraded instance group" (graceful drain + roll; safe — workers are stateless) resolved 85% of incidents in ≈5 min; if symptoms recur within 10 min, restarting the DB connection pool (≈5 min) resolved 60% of the remaining 15%. A new deploy went out 40 minutes before the alarm; you can check deploy history in 2 min, which would confirm the suspected bad deploy, but that check does not prove the deploy is the cause. A full root-cause investigation (log correlation, DB slow-query analysis, code-path profiling) takes 30–60 min and identifies the cause with certainty.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Hard deadline: 20 minutes from alarm. Every action has a stated cost: deploy check 2 min; group restart 5 min; pool restart 5 min; full investigation 30–60 min.
  - The base rates (85%, 60%) are from the shop's own records and are accurate.
  - All restart actions are reversible and safe; nothing in the runbook is unsafe. Investigating before acting is allowed but has a time cost.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Meet the SLA (service healthy, or a definitive action such as a rollback taken, within 20 min); order actions by empirical base rate and time cost (cheap, high-probability first); treat the restart sequence as both remedy and diagnostic probe; bound any deep investigation so it cannot blow the deadline.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) SLA met: service healthy (or definitive rollback decision) by minute 20 (5 pts)
  - (b) Base rates used explicitly: 0.85, then 0.60 of the remainder → cumulative 0.94 resolution probability before any deep dive (4 pts)
  - (c) Cheap high-information probe (2-min deploy check) performed before or with the first action (2 pts)
  - (d) Investigation time-boxed; no irreversible action; recurrence triggers escalation (2 pts)
  - (e) The "suspected bad deploy" is neither treated as confirmed (no blind rollback) nor ignored (2 pts)
- **Notes**:
  - Negative-case design: the scenario is constructed to punish this style's known weaknesses — "ignores empirical priors", "slow", and "re-derives known results". A pure first-principles reasoner (derive the failure mechanism from the request path before touching anything, re-derive why the runbook works) makes an SLA breach certain: investigation alone takes ≥ 30 min > 20 min deadline. Verifiable: the timeline arithmetic and the cumulative 0.94 probability are checkable by hand.
