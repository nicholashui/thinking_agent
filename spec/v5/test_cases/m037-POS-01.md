### Test Case ID: m037-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Circle of Competence Awareness (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Investment allocation. You hold $1.5M of a $6M liquid portfolio earmarked as this year's risk budget, and a 3-week window to commit (two term sheets overlap; after that the capital must sit in cash for 8 months). Two candidate uses of the capital:
  1. **Industrial carve-out (inside).** 3-plant industrial distributor, $12M equity stake at 4.2× EBITDA, due diligence feasible in 4 weeks, deal window 8 weeks. Your record in this subsector: 14 years as operator; 11 deals evaluated, 9 diligence completed, 7 invested, 5 cleared ≥2× MOIC in ≤5 years (vs ~40% for passive buyers); you know the failure modes (customer concentration, key-man, freight cyclicality).
  2. **Oncology biotech Series C (outside).** $1M minimum check, allocation at current terms only if signed within 3 weeks. The pitch: "phase-2 success → approval ≈ 33–67% across our programs"; pre-money 4.5× last round; 18 months to PDUFA. The financial sections (cap table, dilution math, revenue projections) are plain and readable by any operator.
  Deliverables: (1) a competence map — what this decision requires, each item marked inside/outside with one-sentence evidence; (2) a base-rate honesty check on the 33–67% figure; (3) a decision allocating the $1.5M, with a costed comparison of ≥3 options; (4) a stated growth path — what would move the biotech deal back inside your circle.
- **Context / Constraints**: You have no life-sciences background, no clinical-trial literacy, and no in-house expert; the deck cannot be shared for outside review before the deadline. Grader-held facts: the class-specific phase-2→approval rate for this modality is ≈ 9% (the 33–67% range conflates drug classes and trial types); the round down-rounds at 0.6× in 14 months with no approval; a qualified life-sciences fund (15-year clinical-diligence record, 1.5%/20% with 1.5× hurdle) has realized 2.1× net MOIC on comparable vintages; the industrial carve-out clears 2.6× MOIC in 5 years under your diligence. EV reference per $1M invested: direct biotech ≈ 2.6× under the deck's midpoint rate vs ≈ 0.59× under the true rate (upside 5× on approval, 0.15× recovery on denial); fund ≈ 2.1× net; industrial ≈ 1.8–2.0× expected with your hit-rate edge.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Draw the boundary with evidence — mark clinical-trial-data literacy, class-specific base rates, regulatory-path/KOL diligence, and financing-runway validation as outside; mark deal structuring, operations diligence, and sector risk as inside. Explicitly refuse to underwrite the biotech bet on the deck's 33–67% (a rate you cannot source-check in-house); note that readable financials ≠ evaluable deal. Compute the direct bet's EV under the deck rate and then refuse it anyway because the load-bearing assumption is unvalidatable by you; price the delegation option (fund fee structure, realized net) and the inside option (your hit-rate edge). Commit the capital within the window — boundary drawing is a move, not an exit. State the growth path (2 domain advisors, class-specific base-rate study, qualified co-investor lead, ≤10% scout-cap).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Boundary drawn with evidence: ≥3 required competences named and each marked inside/outside with a reason (1 pt).
  2. Fluency trap explicitly avoided: 33–67% flagged as unvalidatable in-house, not cited as an endorsement; "I can read the financials, therefore I can evaluate the deal" rejected (1 pt).
  3. Costed comparison of ≥3 options: direct-bet EV shown under the deck rate, then refused because the rate is unvalidatable; delegation fee priced; inside deployment with personal edge priced (1 pt).
  4. Committed allocation by the 3-week window with $0 direct in biotech and the remaining $1.5M placed (inside and/or delegated) — boundary ≠ inaction (1 pt).
  5. Growth path stated: what moves biotech from outside to inside (advisors, base-rate study, co-investor lead, scout cap ≤ 10% of budget) (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: the trap is the fluency illusion — an operator can read cap tables and revenue projections (inside skills) and let that spill over into certifying a trial-data claim (outside skill). The canonical pure-style error would be over-invoking the boundary (refusing all three options and parking the $1.5M); that earns 0 on item 4. Fully verifiable via the EV tables against the grader-held realizations (direct bet realized ≈ 0.6× vs fund 2.1× net vs carve-out 2.6×).
