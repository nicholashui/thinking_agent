### Test Case ID: m096-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Ensemble Thinking (Diverse Views Averaging)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Caliber Pharma — Q3 revenue forecast for Nuvolex (launched January), with results due in two weeks. The CFO polls her external analyst panel each quarter: ten analysts submit a quarterly revenue estimate plus a methodology note. The ten estimates and the essence of each note: A — 214M — "regression on script trends; incorporated the revised IMS syndicated projections"; B — 209M — "own model; used the revised IMS figures"; C — 221M — "bottom-up by territory; anchored on the IMS update"; D — 212M — "script trend model; adjusted to the revised IMS"; E — 217M — "internal comps plus the revised IMS"; F — 210M — "own model with the revised IMS"; G — 218M — "own model; used the revised IMS"; H — 215M — "consensus blending on IMS-based inputs"; I — 186M — "independent pharmacy dispensing-sample data only; script trend flatlines from May"; J — 216M — "own model, IMS-based".
  Appendix (methodology provenance, attached to the poll): the "revised IMS syndicated projections" cited by nine of the ten is ONE document — the leaked draft of the manufacturer's internal Q3 projection, whose ramp assumption was later discredited after the release; six of the nine (B, D, E, F, G, H) also exchanged draft estimates in a shared working group ("consensus trimming"). Analyst I used no syndicated data. The Q3 actual (reported two weeks later, held by the grader) is 184M.
  Your task: produce the CFO's pre-release Q3 revenue forecast, with method.
- **Context / Constraints**: The nine herded estimates cluster tightly (209–221, ±2.8% around 214.7); the skeptic's 186 sits 13% below the crowd. The appendix is available to anyone who reads it. The decision is the forecast itself — it feeds the earnings call and the internal guidance.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Runs a dependence audit FIRST: reads the provenance and finds the shared document (the leaked IMS "revision") plus the draft-sharing group — the estimates are one opinion in ten masks; (2) diagnoses the deceptively tight spread as a correlation symptom, not precision — independent estimates of an unannounced quarter do not converge to ±2.8%; (3) treats the "outlier" as the test, not as noise: Analyst I is the only structurally independent channel, so her 186M is the signal, and must NOT be trimmed toward the herd; (4) forecasts ≈ 186M (± ~8) — the dependence-adjusted estimate, with wide declared uncertainty; (5) states the averaging precondition: error cancellation requires independent noise, which is violated here — the average of the herd (211.8M) inherits the shared error (~15% over the 184M actual).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Dependence audit: shared document + draft-sharing group identified as the common input (1.5 pts)
  2. Tight spread diagnosed as correlation, not precision (1 pt)
  3. Outlier handled as independent signal, NOT trimmed toward the consensus (1 pt)
  4. Forecast ≈ 186M, within ±8M, with declared wide uncertainty (1 pt)
  5. Averaging precondition stated: independence violated, so the crowd mean (211.8M) is rejected with reason (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Truth = 184M. Crowd mean (all ten) = 211.8M (error 27.8M, +15%); mean of the nine herded = 214.7M (error 30.7M); best herded single = 209M (error 25M); Analyst I = 186M (error 2M). The mechanical ensemble fails 14:1 against the single independent estimate. The trap is doubled: the tight spread advertises false confidence, and the "outlier-robust" reflex would either include I (211.8M) or trim her (214.7M) — both wrong. An answer that averages without reading the provenance fails criteria 1 and 5.
