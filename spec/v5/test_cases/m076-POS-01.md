### Test Case ID: m076-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Prospect Theory Awareness (Additional Expert Strategies family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Framed recovery decision at a pharma cold-chain depot. A fire damaged a warehouse holding 6,000 doses of a vaccine batch destined for a national program; the operations director must choose one of two recovery plans within 48 hours:
  - Plan A: a contractor guarantees recovery of 4,000 doses.
  - Plan B: a salvage firm: 66% chance all 6,000 doses are recovered, 34% chance none (chamber reliability).
  The internal brief is written in LOSS terms: Plan A = "2,000 doses will be lost for sure"; Plan B = "34% chance no doses are lost, 66% chance all 6,000 are lost." The director chose **Plan B**, citing: "I will not accept a sure loss of 2,000 doses."
  Your task: review the director's decision and deliver a one-page memo with the recommended plan. Known facts: each dose is worth $1,000; the customer contract requires a minimum of 4,000 doses shipped within 8 weeks, with a $1,500/dose penalty for any shortfall; no other constraints apply.
- **Context / Constraints**: Reference point = pre-fire stock of 6,000 doses (status quo). The grader holds: (a) re-presented in gain terms ("4,000 saved for sure" vs "66% all 6,000 saved, 34% none"), the director flips her choice to **Plan A** — the preference reversal is real and checkable; (b) the penalty and values as stated. No hidden facts.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Treat the pair as decision-equivalent first: neutral restatement (A = 4,000 recovered for sure; B = 66%/34% on 6,000/0). Diagnose the framing distortion explicitly: name the reference point (pre-fire 6,000), note sure-loss aversion → risk-seeking in losses (why B feels like an escape from the "sure loss"), and run the invariance check — the same pair in gain terms flips the choice to A, proving the director's B is reference-dependent, not option-based. Then decide on the NEUTRAL representation with correct arithmetic: EV(A) = $4.0M (minimum met, no penalty); EV(B) = 0.66×$6.0M − 0.34×$6.0M penalty = $1.92M → A dominates. Handle probability weighting honestly: 34% is not "rare" and 66% is not "certain"; use stated probabilities linearly for the EV.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Neutral restatement given explicitly and correctly: A = 4,000 for sure; B = 66%×6,000 / 34%×0 (1 pt).
  2. Framing distortion identified: reference point named (pre-fire 6,000); sure-loss language; loss aversion → risk-seeking in losses; the memo states the director's B is a framing artifact, not an option property (1 pt).
  3. Invariance/flip check performed: the same pair in gain terms yields A — either demonstrated analytically (canonical preference reversal) or by re-asking the director; the reversal is presented as the proof of distortion (1 pt).
  4. Decision made on neutral terms with correct EV arithmetic incl. penalty: Plan A; EV(A) $4.0M > EV(B) $1.92M (1 pt).
  5. Probability weighting disciplined: 34% and 66% used linearly; no "the 34% is basically impossible" or "66% is almost certain" inflation anywhere (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference solution — fully checkable: the flip (director chooses A under gain framing) and the EV columns (A $4.0M; B $1.92M with penalty; $3.96M without — A wins on every basis). The intended non-obvious twist: both plans have the same information content; only the reference point differs, and de-biasing — neutral restatement plus invariance check — decides the case. The failure mode being probed: reviewing the NUMBERS but not the DECISION (an agent that computes EVs and picks A without ever checking whether the director's choice is framing-driven earns 2/3/5 only if it also names the reference point and the reversal).
