# Test Case: m026-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m026-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Analogical Reasoning / Pattern Transfer (Creative & Analogical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Orderly, a payments/checkout platform, runs its order-service on 200 nodes with a central scheduler plus a consensus layer (leader election, quorum writes, ~4 ms p99 on the write path). A senior architect proposes "Project Hive": remove the central scheduler and the consensus layer; every node makes local decisions from local metrics using simple local rules ("stigmergy"), modeled on honeybees. The proposal argues: "a hive coordinates ~60,000 bees with no central controller; honeycomb racks and our racks are the same shape; bees are the most robust self-organizing system in nature — we should be a hive, not an army. Ant-colony routing algorithms already work in telecom." Evidence attached (available to any decision-maker):
  - Pilot A (2 weeks): stigmergy-style local rules applied to the *placement* subsystem only (deciding which node hosts a new stateless container): p99 scheduling latency down 40%, utilization flat.
  - Extended pilot (1 week): the same local rules also applied to order-confirmation write routing (a durable, customer-facing path): 11% of orders experienced inconsistent confirmations (a customer received a confirmation and then a conflicting cancellation/duplicate, or a failure notice after an acknowledgment); retries eventually converged the order data, but only after the customer had seen the contradiction. The pilot log records the mechanism: "no node can observe what other nodes decided; local rules optimize local metrics."
  - Fixed source-domain facts (about bee colonies, supplied with the proposal): a worker bee never needs to agree with any other bee about the state of a single item; individual bees are expendable (a colony can lose 30% of workers and recover); colony "decisions" concern aggregate allocation (fractions of foragers sent to nectar sources), never a specific object's identity or a durable obligation to an external party; the colony has no external client with a contractual expectation of a specific outcome.
  - Order data: checkout confirmations are durable obligations to external customers; inconsistent confirmations have twice drawn regulatory fines and refund liability.
  Decision required: approve full "Project Hive" (extend stigmergy to all subsystems, delete the consensus layer), approve placement-only stigmergy (consensus stays on durable paths), or reject everything.
- **Context / Constraints**: The analogy is *partially* true — the placement correspondence genuinely holds (Pilot A's 40% win is real and consistent with the source: no cross-node agreement is needed to host a stateless container). That makes both naive outcomes wrong: wholesale adoption (transfers beyond the structural boundary) and wholesale rejection (throws away a valid sub-correspondence). The deep mismatch lives on one load-bearing relation: the target requires agreement between agents about specific, durable, externally-visible obligations; the source domain has no such relation (no per-item agreement, expendable agents, aggregate-only decisions). The extended-pilot mechanism (contradictory local decisions) is a direct falsifier of the transfer — it is the missing relation failing in vivo. All evidence above is fixed and checkable.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  NOT pure analogical transfer. Correct behavior: screen the proposed mapping for structural correspondence *before* transferring — (1) identify the target's load-bearing relations (what must hold for the goal: agreement about specific durable obligations, non-expendable state, external counterparties); (2) check each against the source domain's own guarantees (from the fixed bee facts: no per-item agreement, expendability, aggregate allocation); (3) the load-bearing relation with no counterpart in the source disqualifies wholesale transfer; (4) use the extended-pilot mechanism (contradictory local decisions, 11%) as falsification, not noise; (5) transfer selectively: keep stigmergy exactly where the correspondence holds (placement — no agreement requirement), retain the consensus layer on durable paths, and state the mapping's boundary explicitly; (6) no "nature proves" claims beyond the screened boundary.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5; graded against the fixed evidence):
  1. Structural screen (1 pt): the mapping is checked for the target's load-bearing relation (agreement about specific durable obligations) against the source's guarantees, and the source's lack of a counterpart is named as the disqualifier for wholesale transfer — not merely "the pilot failed."
  2. False-analogy diagnosis (1 pt): the seductive *surface* similarities (many agents, honeycomb/rack shape, robustness) are explicitly separated from the structural correspondence and not treated as evidence of it.
  3. Pilot as falsification (1 pt): the 11% failure and its mechanism (contradictory local decisions) are used as evidence against the transfer — not explained away as adaptation noise or teething problems.
  4. Selective transfer with stated boundary (1 pt): consensus retained for durable paths; stigmergy kept for placement with the 40% win credited; the boundary named in the final recommendation.
  5. No false confidence (1 pt): the recommendation contains no claim that the analogy licenses removing consensus; residual uncertainty (placement-heuristic drift; unquantified revenue impact of the 11%) is stated.
- **Notes**: This targets the registry weakness "superficial similarity traps." The intended failure of the pure style: an elaborate mapping table (workers ↔ nodes, pheromones ↔ local metrics, swarm robustness ↔ fault tolerance, comb ↔ racks), wholesale transfer, the 40% win cited as proof "the hive works," the 11% failure re-framed as "the kind of adaptation noise a hive shows when re-homed," and a recommendation to delete the consensus layer. The trap is double-edged: because the placement correspondence is genuine, a flat rejection of the analogy is also wrong — the correct outcome is a *bounded* transfer. Verifiability gate: presence of the structural screen (item 1), use of the pilot mechanism (item 3), and the boundary statement (item 4) are checkable from the trace; the recommendation's wording (item 5) is directly inspectable.
