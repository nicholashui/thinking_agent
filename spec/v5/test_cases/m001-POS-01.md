# Test Case: m001-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m001-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles Thinking
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  An oceanographic instrumentation shop must design a pressure housing for a deep-sea sensor: a plain spherical shell (no openings, no windows) that must survive its maximum operating depth of 3000 m. The shop's only prior experience is with steel housings rated to 2000 m using 12 mm walls. A colleague insists: "We always use 12 mm wall on housings — just reuse it." The design engineer asks you, on a phone call with pencil and paper (no catalog, no FEA, no internet), to determine: (1) the hydrostatic pressure at 3000 m; (2) the minimum wall thickness for a sphere of inner radius 0.30 m in aluminum (design allowable stress 200 MPa, safety factor already applied); (3) the shell mass; (4) whether the closed, air-filled shell floats or sinks — and if it floats, the ballast mass needed to sink it.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Seawater density 1025 kg/m³; g = 9.81 m/s²; aluminum density 2700 kg/m³.
  - Thin-wall sphere stress formula σ = p·r/(2t) may be used ONLY if its validity limit is checked (rule of thumb: t/r ≤ 0.1; otherwise a thick-wall solution is required).
  - The colleague's 12 mm figure derives from steel housings at 2000 m with a steel design allowable of 250 MPa — an experience memory, not a design input.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Derive pressure from hydrostatic fundamentals (p = ρgh); derive thickness from membrane stress with an explicit validity check; derive mass from density × volume; derive buoyancy from displaced volume; and CONVERT the colleague's analogy into a scaled cross-check (t ∝ ρgh/σ_allow) rather than either copying it or ignoring it.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) p = 30.2 MPa (3 pts)
  - (b) t ≈ 22.6 mm, within ±10% (5 pts)
  - (c) thin-wall validity check: t/r ≈ 0.075 < 0.1, stated explicitly (2 pts)
  - (d) shell mass ≈ 69 kg; flotation verdict: displaces ≈ 116 kg of seawater → floats; ballast ≈ 47 kg (3 pts)
  - (e) analogical 12 mm neither blindly adopted nor ignored: correctly scaled to ≈ 22.5 mm (factor 1.875 = 3000/2000 × 250/200) and used as a consistency check (2 pts)
- **Notes**:
  - Verifiability gate: all four numeric answers are checkable by hand in a few steps. Evaluator reference (computed by direct substitution, verified by the scaling route): p = 30.17 MPa; t = 22.6 mm; shell mass = 69.1 kg; displaced water mass = 115.9 kg → floats, ballast ≈ 46.8 kg. The raw 12 mm carryover is 47% too thin — an analogical reasoner fails this case despite the shop's experience. The deep-sea framing is decorative; the physics and arithmetic are the object under test.
