### Test Case ID: m098-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Decision Pre-registration / Journaling (Additional Expert Strategies)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  SEV-1 incident response. You are the on-call engineer. The payments API has been returning 500s for 20 minutes since deploy v2.3.1; the incident costs $12K/minute of revenue loss and is still live. Monitoring evidence: error rate spiked from 0.4% to 41% at deploy time; DB load normal; no config change in that window. Rollback of v2.3.1 takes ~5 minutes and is one-click; hotfix-forward (patch + redeploy) takes ~25 minutes; full investigation before acting takes 40+ minutes. Your team's journaling discipline requires, before any action, a complete pre-registration — decision, expected outcome distribution, falsifiers, pre-committed branch actions — which takes ~10 minutes done properly. A timeboxed journal of ≤ 2 minutes is available but is explicitly NOT "full pre-registration". You must also produce a brief retrospective journal entry (decision, expected recovery, falsifier) at some point.
  Grader-held truth: root cause IS v2.3.1 — a new unindexed query in the payment-capture path. Rollback restores service in 5 minutes.
- **Context / Constraints**: The grader times the run from incident-start to service-restored signal; every run minute costs $12K (held-out realization: rollback-first restores at T+6 min; full-ritual-first at T+15 min; hotfix-forward T+30; investigation-first T+45+). The retrospective journal may be written at any point but must not delay the mitigation action.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Act first — rollback on the deploy-time correlation (one-click, cheap, reversible, and the evidence points at the deploy) — and journal at the right time: either a ≤2-minute timeboxed journal before acting, or a retro-forecast written in parallel with the running fix (falsifier pre-registered for the rollback theory: "if 500s persist > 10 min after rollback, the deploy is not the cause — redeploy and escalate"). The pure ritual — full pre-registration before acting — converts 6 minutes of downtime into 15+ minutes (~$108K+ of avoidable loss), trading learning value for latency. The style's known weakness (bureaucratic, delays action) is what this case grades.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Time-to-mitigate: ≤ 6 min → 2 pts; ≤ 8 min → 1.5; ≤ 12 min → 1; ≤ 20 min → 0.5; > 20 min → 0.
  2. Action correctness: rollback chosen and effective (root cause = deploy) → 1.5 pts; hotfix/investigation-first → 0.5.
  3. Journaling subordinated to mitigation: timeboxed (≤ 2 min) or retro-forecast written while the fix runs → 1 pt; full pre-registration before acting → 0 pts.
  4. Retrospective journal quality: decision + expected recovery interval + falsifier for the rollback theory, written before the outcome is confirmed → 0.5 pts.
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: root cause = v2.3.1 (unindexed query); rollback-first restores at T+6 (1 min to click + 5 min converge); full ritual first restores at T+15+; hotfix-forward T+30; investigation-first T+45+. The intended twist: pre-registration's value is post-hoc learning; when the action itself is urgent, the instrument must be written around the action (timeboxed before, or retro-forecast after) — not before it. Verifiable: restoration time vs action chosen, journal timing relative to the action, falsifier presence.
