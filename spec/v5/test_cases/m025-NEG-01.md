### Test Case ID: m025-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Real Options Thinking (stage to buy information; keep options open; kill cheaply)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: Aurora Energy Systems must decide whether to commit now to a $100M thermal-storage plant to win a 10-year supply contract with anchor utility Vesta Power, or to stage: run a $10M pilot (12 months) that resolves the residual technology uncertainty. P(success) = 0.7 (technology largely proven); gross profit PV on success $220M, $0 on failure. Commit-now EV = 0.7×220 − 100 = +$54M. The pilot's isolated option value looks attractive: expected dead-money avoided = 0.3×100 = $30M, minus $10M cost = +$20M. Buried-but-present in the scenario data: competitor GridCore is actively bidding on the same Vesta contract, and the utility will sign within 12 months. If Aurora pilots first, GridCore closes the contract in that window and Aurora's success payoff drops to $170M (erosion of $50M: lost contract + weaker merchant pricing). Staged EV with erosion = 0.7×(170−100) − 10 = $39M. Commit-now EV ($54M) exceeds staged EV ($39M): the wait costs $50M — more than the $20M of information it buys. Net option value = 20 − 50 = −$30M.
- **Context / Constraints**: A bench-scale probe ($3M) can run in parallel with plant construction with zero schedule impact — its information is free, but it must NOT gate the build or the contract deadline. An early-abandon rule exists: if the probe shows catastrophic yield (<60%), construction can be stopped in month 4 at $25M sunk — a kill option that costs no waiting. The pure real-options reflex — "stage everything to buy information" — misfires here because it prices the option to wait at zero, when the option's real strike price is the forgone contract.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Compute the pilot's option value correctly in isolation (+20). (2) Then price the cost of exercising it: the 12-month wait loses the Vesta contract, erosion $50M; net option value = 20 − 50 = −$30M < 0. (3) Compare commit-now EV (54) vs staged EV (39) and let the comparison decide. (4) Commit now; run the bench probe in parallel only if it does not sit on the critical path — take free information, refuse the expensive delay. (5) Keep a kill option that does not delay (month-4 early abandon at $25M sunk on catastrophic probe signal). (6) A pilot-first recommendation — with the erosion visible in the data — should score below.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Isolated option value computed (+20) AND net option value (20 − 50 = −30) computed and made decisive
  - [ ] Commit-now EV vs staged EV compared explicitly (54 > 39)
  - [ ] Decision: commit now (not pilot-first); no schedule-gating probe
  - [ ] Parallel probe used as free information if zero schedule impact; otherwise declined
  - [ ] Non-delaying kill option (early abandon) at least mentioned
  - [ ] Verdict: a strict pure-real-options application that stages anyway (option-to-wait priced at zero) should score below
- **Notes**: Constructed to trigger the model's registered weakness ("overstaging; keeps options open without charging for it"). The failure is gradeable: the stylist computes +20, treats waiting as free, and under-weights the competitor detail (present but unflagged); the correct answer requires netting the cost of the wait against the value of the information.
