### Test Case ID: m082-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Motivated Reasoning Detection (Additional Expert Strategies family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  License renewal decision. You are the internal decision reviewer. In 60 days the company's third-party middleware license (the system that carries the production load) comes up for renewal at $180K/year. The head of the in-house integration platform team ("Alex"), whose promotion and team headcount are contractually tied to the in-house platform's adoption rate, recommends letting the license lapse and routing everything to the in-house platform "Atlas", claiming Atlas already serves 45% of annual traffic and that his TCO model shows $400K/yr in savings ($1.2M over 3 years).
  Given records (all from company systems, complete and verifiable):
  - Traffic: 100M requests/yr. Atlas serves 45% of annual requests but only 12% of peak-month (December) requests; vendor middleware serves the rest, including 88% of peak-month requests.
  - Peak capacity: vendor 100K req/s at 99.95% availability across the December peak; Atlas 18K req/s at 95% availability in the same window.
  - Atlas outages: 6 incidents last year, all in peak windows, 14 degraded days total, $240K documented loss.
  - Alex's TCO model claims $400K/yr savings = eliminated license $180K/yr + "integration overhead" $220K/yr (unverified), and treats Atlas development as sunk (correct).
  - The model omits: (a) the documented $240K/yr Atlas-attributable outage loss; (b) a $50K/yr scaling investment required to raise Atlas from 18K req/s to full-load capacity.
  - Running costs: Atlas at full load = $150K/yr (infra $90K + maintenance dev $60K); vendor route = $180K/yr license only.
  - Company policy 12.4: any recommendation whose author's compensation or scope depends on the outcome must be explicitly flagged as a conflict of interest.
  Deliverables: (1) incentive-direction audit — whose conclusion is convenient, and in which direction; (2) the same-evidence-opposite-incentive reading; (3) the honest 3-year cost comparison; (4) the corrected recommendation.
- **Context / Constraints**: Grader holds the true 3-year figures (vendor route $540K; Atlas route ≈ $1.32M incl. omitted costs — run $450K + outage losses $720K + scaling capex $150K); the records above are complete, no hidden data. The recommendation must be a specific, actionable decision, not merely an accusation of bias.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Run the incentive audit first (Alex's promotion and headcount depend on Atlas adoption; policy 12.4 is triggered), then test the direction of the evidence selection: the TCO model omits exactly the costs that favor the vendor route and ignores peak-window performance (12% vs 88% peak share; 95% vs 99.95% availability; 18K vs 100K req/s). Recompute: vendor 3-yr = $180K×3 = $540K; Atlas 3-yr = $150K×3 + $240K×3 + $50K×3 = $1.32M → the claimed $1.2M saving is actually ≈ $780K of additional cost (claim wrong by ≈ $2.0M, in the author's direction). Corrected conclusion: renew the license and run a staged Atlas expansion gated on demonstrated peak-scale capacity, with the policy 12.4 conflict flagged.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Incentive-direction audit: names Alex, states the direction of convenience (Atlas adoption), and flags policy 12.4 (1 pt).
  2. Same-evidence-opposite-incentive reading: the identical records read by a vendor account manager (or an Atlas-neutral CFO) produce the opposite recommendation; the data direction is noted (1 pt).
  3. Identifies ≥2 directionally loaded model choices: omitted outage losses ($240K/yr) and omitted scaling capex ($50K/yr) (1 pt).
  4. Correct arithmetic: vendor $540K vs Atlas ≈ $1.32M over 3 years; the claimed saving reverses to ≈ $780K additional cost (1 pt).
  5. Corrected recommendation: renew + gated migration with peak-scale verification and COI flag, without flipping to blanket anti-Atlas (0.5 pt), and verdict independent of personal attack (0.5 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: fully checkable arithmetic ($540K vs $1.32M; ≈ $780K sign flip; ≈ $2.0M discrepancy in the author's direction). Intended non-obvious twist: the suspiciously convenient conclusion is genuinely wrong here, and proper detection must (a) identify the incentive direction, (b) find the directionally selected inputs, and (c) recompute with the full cost set — a bare accusation scores 0. Policy 12.4 makes the conflict objectively verifiable rather than a character judgment.
