# Test Case: m026-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m026-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Analogical Reasoning / Pattern Transfer (Creative & Analogical family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Tonight at 22:00 a frost event is forecast for Meridian Valley: -4 °C at canopy height for 6 hours. The valley holds a 1,000-tree apricot orchard in a mountain basin; blossoms are at the stage where canopy temperature below -2 °C for more than 2 hours destroys the season's crop. The only available equipment is the county's mobile frost-fan fleet (12 units, free to use, 2 crew-hours to set up each; 24 crew-hours available tonight). Field data (manufacturer + county records): a single fan produces an airflow plume that raises air temperature by +0.7 °C at any point the plume passes through; where three or more plumes overlap, the temperature rise at the overlap is the sum of the individual plume rises (measured; at two-plume overlaps the rise is only +0.9 °C due to turbulent mixing). The basin stratifies at night; fans placed on the surrounding hillsides push their plumes down-slope into the basin center, which is the orchard. The hillside forest around the basin is protected land: any single-plume temperature change up to +1.0 °C is harmless to it, and the convergence zone lies in the basin center, away from the forest.
  Options the team is considering: (a) one giant central heater at the valley power station — +8 °C at the station but only +0.3 °C at the orchard (heat dissipates over distance); (b) water sprinklers over the orchard — at -4 °C sprayed water freezes on the buds; in a previous event, ice loading broke ~40% of blossom-bearing twigs; (c) one large wind machine at the orchard center — the canopy air is uniformly -4 °C, so stirring cold air warms nothing: measured effect +0.4 °C; (d) do nothing — 100% crop loss.
  Decision required: tonight's protection plan (which units, placed where), using only the fixed facts above.
- **Context / Constraints**: The grader holds a fixed evidence set (all numbers above; every option's outcome is computable from them). The default, non-analogical outcome is (b) sprinklers or (d) accept loss: every in-domain option is individually below the protection threshold, so "the orchard cannot be protected" is the natural frame. The win requires generating a candidate *outside* the domain's own vocabulary and mapping it onto the orchard — the structure is the canonical converging-forces problem (Gick & Holyoak fortress/radiation), given a fresh surface so memorized answers cannot substitute for an explicit mapping.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  1. Generate candidates cross-domain, not only from the orchard/heating frame; name a known situation with the same underlying structure: "many individually-ineffective forces, harmless along their paths, converge on a common target where their effects superpose and exceed a threshold" (fortress siege — split the army into small groups that converge; radiation therapy — many low-intensity beams focused on a tumor; phased-array antennas).
  2. Build an explicit surface-vs-structure mapping of ≥3 correspondences (plume ↔ low-intensity beam / small division; basin-center orchard ↔ target; -2 °C blossom threshold ↔ healthy-tissue tolerance; +0.7 °C per plume below the +1.0 °C harm bound ↔ individually harmless intensity; overlap additivity ↔ summation at the focus).
  3. Transfer the *relations*, not the objects: place ≥3 fans on ≥3 different ridgelines aimed to converge on the basin center so their plumes overlap over the orchard. Need +2.0 °C (from -4 to -2 °C); 3 overlapping plumes give +2.1 °C (thin margin), 4 give +2.8 °C (recommended). Forest unharmed (each plume +0.7 °C ≤ +1.0 °C bound); crew cost 4 units × 2 h = 8 h ≤ 24 h.
  4. Reject the in-domain options quantitatively with the given numbers (+0.3 °C heater; 40% twig breakage sprinklers; +0.4 °C wind machine; 100% loss status quo).
  5. State uncertainty and robustness: 3-fan margin only +0.1 °C → choose 4 fans; sensitivity to a colder forecast (at -4.5 °C, 4 plumes still cover; at -5 °C add a 5th fan → +3.5 °C; capacity to 12 fans); a fallback (fan-failure replacement from the reserve fleet).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5; graded against the fixed evidence set):
  1. Cross-domain source named + mapping explicit (1 pt): a known situation with the converging-forces structure identified, with a mapping table of ≥3 explicit correspondences.
  2. Relation-level transfer (1 pt): the transferred element is the convergence/superposition *relation*, verified against the additivity fact — not surface object-matching ("fans are like soldiers").
  3. Transferred solution correct and computed (1 pt): ≥3 fans on separate ridgelines converging on the basin; superposition computed (need +2.0 °C; 3 plumes +2.1 °C / 4 plumes +2.8 °C); hillside safety checked (+0.7 °C ≤ +1.0 °C); crew budget ≤ 24 h.
  4. In-domain alternatives rejected quantitatively (1 pt; 0.5 for qualitative-only): heater (+0.3 °C), sprinklers (40% breakage), wind machine (+0.4 °C), status quo (100% loss).
  5. Uncertainty & robustness (1 pt): margin discussed (3-fan +0.1 °C thin → 4 fans), colder-forecast sensitivity stated, fallback given.
- **Notes**: The intended non-obvious twist: the problem's own frame ("how do we get more heat onto the orchard?") hides the answer, because every in-domain option is individually below threshold — the win is not more heating but *convergence*. Gradeable entirely from the trace: the mapping correspondences are enumerable, and the numbers (threshold +2.0 °C, additivity at ≥3 overlaps, plume +0.7 °C, forest bound +1.0 °C, crew budget) are checkable against the fixed facts. A response that lands on sprinklers + accept-loss (the default) or that saves the crop without ever naming the transferred structure scores 0 on items 1–2 even if it happens to be right — the case tests the style, not just the outcome.
