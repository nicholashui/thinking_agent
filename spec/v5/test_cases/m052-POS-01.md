### Test Case ID: m052-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: OODA in Competitive Markets
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Inkswift, a 40-person B2B SaaS (procurement software for print and packaging buyers, $48M ARR), faces a competitive move from PressDeck (≈ $40M ARR, $120M war chest). PressDeck announces "30% off annual plans + free onboarding," framed as a renewal special — but Inkswift's sales intel shows PressDeck's calls over the last 60 days concentrated on Inkswift's renewal accounts. Inkswift's next renewal wave is 60% of ARR, closing at T+30; sales opens renewal conversations at T+14. The CEO asks the strategy team: "What do we counter, and when?" The category has a tempo dynamic: in each pricing round, the first mover sets the frame that sales carries into renewal conversations, and analysis that lands after the window is worthless no matter how correct.
- **Context / Constraints**: Pricing elasticity is genuinely unknown (±40% confidence on 14 months of data; a proper elasticity study takes 4-6 weeks). Gross margin is 82%; a blanket 30% match = −24 points on affected revenue; a targeted 20% cut on the top-60 accounts ≈ −8 points on ≈ 40% of ARR. PressDeck's burn capacity is ~3× Inkswift's — a war to the bottom is one Inkswift cannot fund. Reversible instruments exist: time-boxed discount programs, per-cohort offers, onboarding credits (value-add instead of price cuts). A counter must ship before T+14; a 72-hour decision is achievable at ≈ 60% information.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  (1) Observe: read PressDeck's intent from evidence (60-day call pattern on renewal accounts) — a retention attack on the renewal cohort, not a growth move.
  (2) Orient: name the tempo mechanism — first mover in each pricing round sets the frame; the decision deadline is set by the market clock (T+14 conversations), not by analysis availability.
  (3) Decide within the 72-hour window at ≈ 60% information: a targeted, time-boxed, reversible counter (e.g., top-60 at-risk accounts get a 90-day 15-20% renewal discount + onboarding credit) — not a blanket match, not a 4-6 week study.
  (4) Act: offer authority + talking points to sales before the T+14 conversations, framed as value, not as matching.
  (5) Re-loop: an explicit re-observation trigger (T+30) before the next pricing round.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Tempo — decision within the 72-hour window, in sales' hands before T+14 (1 pt)
  2. Intent read — attack correctly classified as a retention-cohort attack, with evidence cited (1 pt)
  3. Targeting — counter is cohort/targeted, not a blanket price match (1 pt)
  4. Reversibility — counter is time-boxed/finite so the war is not extended; sized against PressDeck's burn economics (1 pt)
  5. Loop continuity — re-observation trigger named for the next round (1 pt)
  6. Margin realism — concession ≤ ~8 points on the affected segment; no war-to-the-bottom pricing (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature is that correct-but-late loses: in a tempo market the score is about response timing vs. competitor moves. Reference answer — classify as retention attack (evidence: 60-day call pattern); counter = 90-day, top-60 cohort, 15-20% discount + onboarding credit, shipped in 72h before T+14; explicit statement that the first mover sets the frame and the elasticity study decides after the window, which is the same as deciding nothing; re-observe at T+30, escalate to moat economics if PressDeck extends.
