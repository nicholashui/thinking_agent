### Test Case ID: m023-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Opportunity Cost Thinking (value any choice by its best forgone alternative)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: You are a senior ML engineer. In a single week — a convergence that has never happened to you before — you receive three offers, each with a strict 2-week accept-or-expire window: (1) your current employer's counteroffer: promotion to staff engineer, $180K base, 20-minute commute, a team and codebase you know, no equity change; (2) "Halcyon Bio," a 40-person startup: $160K base plus 1.5% equity (30% chance the company fails), 45-minute commute, and it is the ONLY offer in the disease domain you have spent five years studying — your stated dream assignment; (3) "Vertex Cloud": $200K base plus $80K sign-on, 60-minute commute, large-team matrix bureaucracy, high-visibility growth work. All three are in the same metro region; your parent, in declining health, lives 15 minutes from your current office (20 minutes from Offer 2's office, 25 from Offer 3's).
- **Context / Constraints**: A careful one-pass pricing puts all three within noise of each other: 5-year total comp ≈ $0.95–1.06M (Offer 3), ≈ $0.90–1.0M (Offer 1), ≈ $0.85–1.2M including equity upside (Offer 2) — the bands overlap on every pairwise comparison. Every input you refine (tax bracket, equity strike and dilution, vesting cliff, commute cost, promotion odds, health-insurance delta) shifts the ranking by less than the error band. The windows are hard; the counteroffer is policy-limited and lapses 14 days after issue if unaccepted. Job-market cycles run ~18 months at your seniority, so every option is re-obtainable later at bounded cost. The non-fungible values — the disease-domain work (available nowhere else) and proximity to your parent — cannot be priced in dollars without arbitrary assumptions.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Recognize the class before computing: this is a near-tie under any defensible model — optimizing is unavailable, so further analysis has value-of-information ≈ 0. (2) Bound the analysis: ONE pricing pass with explicit error bars; treat all ranking differences inside the band as ties; refuse the re-computation loop. (3) Invoke the opportunity-cost discipline against itself: the costliest forgone alternative now is the decision itself — every additional pricing pass forgoes the 14-day windows. (4) Switch to a rule: commit within 48 hours on a pre-stated tie-breaker; let the non-fungible signal (the unique mission domain) break the tie rather than a fabricated dollar price. (5) Note reversibility (an ~18-month cycle) as the reason regret is bounded. (6) Verdict: a committed acceptance before any window expires; a trace that re-computes until all three windows lapse should score at the bottom.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Near-tie recognized within stated error bands (no false "winner" claimed on noise)
  - [ ] VOI of further comparison stated as ≈ 0 → stop condition invoked
  - [ ] One-pass pricing with bands; no unbounded re-computation loop in the trace
  - [ ] ≥1 non-fungible value identified and used as tie-breaker, not monetized
  - [ ] Verdict: committed choice inside the windows; paralysis (all windows expire) scores below
- **Notes**: The case isolates the model's registry-listed weakness ("counting everything can freeze action") plus its companion failure — monetizing what is non-fungible. The math is checkable only as a band (every comparison falls inside it); the gradeable outcome is whether the trace terminates in a commitment inside the windows or loops until they lapse.
