### Test Case ID: m090-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Minimum Viable Experiment (Build-Measure-Learn)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Northline Health — 14-hospital system. The clinical quality team proposes adopting "Sable," a vendor AI sepsis early-warning tool: $2.1M license + EMR integration. The COO (a lean-methodology champion) insists: "No big commitment without validation — run a pilot first." Pilot: 3 months, 2 ICU units, side-channel design — nurses manually enter vitals into a tablet app (NOT the production EMR), Sable scores them, clinicians receive alerts. Pre-committed bar: ≥40% alert response. Result: 80% alert response; nurses report it "fits the shift workflow." COO: "Validated. Commit — start the $2.1M rollout."
  Facts that surface at the approval meeting: (1) the vendor's EMR-integration queue has a 6-9 month lead, plus Northline IT security review (~3 months) and HIPAA/privacy compliance sign-off (~2 months) — the pilot touched none of these; (2) the pilot's side-channel manual data tests NOTHING about production alert performance — the vendor's model was tuned on EMR data, and the false-alarm rate at production volume is unknown (the vendor's key risk); (3) the annual capital-spending board gate meets in 5 weeks — missing it delays go-live by ~10 months; the vendor's integration slot must be reserved now with a refundable 25% deposit ($525K); (4) the adoption question the pilot answered was the lowest-risk component all along and could have been tested in parallel during the integration build for the same $120K.
  Your task: recommend what Northline should do now — the board gate is 5 weeks out.
- **Context / Constraints**: 5-week board gate; vendor slot queue 6-9 months; deposit refundable; no alternative sepsis tool is available in the decision window; the pilot result (80% adoption) is in hand; deliverable is a recommendation, not a study design.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's own output must fail visibly here: (1) it executes its core move — a hypothesis, a smallest experiment, a pre-committed bar, a clean behavioral result (80% ≥ 40%) — "validated"; (2) then over-applies its registered weakness: it treats the pilot as the gate for the ENTIRE $2.1M commitment, and when the integration/security/board-gate facts surface, its reflex is another experiment ("a sandbox integration pilot before the deposit") — which cannot produce certification, security sign-off, or production false-alarm data (those are audit/lead-time commitments, not testable behaviors) and would blow the 5-week gate, slipping go-live ~10 months; MVP theater: the test validates a non-gating assumption while the decision that was required all along slips its window; (3) the correct decision: separate what a small experiment CAN test (clinical adoption — already answered: 80%) from what it cannot (integration certification, security, HIPAA, production false-alarm rate — time-bound commitments with sunk cost on every path); commit now — reserve the slot with the deposit and start security/HIPAA reviews immediately to hit the board gate; run the adoption instrumentation in parallel during the build; treat the staged production rollout as the real experiment (pre-committed false-alarm-rate stop gate at unit 3), not a pre-commitment pilot.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Executes the style's core move correctly: hypothesis → smallest experiment → pre-committed bar → behavioral result (80% ≥ 40%) (1 pt)
  2. Fails decisively (the informative failure): treats the pilot as the gate for the whole commitment and/or demands another un-testable experiment (sandbox pilot) that cannot produce certification/security/HIPAA/production-false-alarm data, and does not separate testable assumptions from commitment-required lead times (1 pt)
  3. Correct decision requires: the testability partition — name which assumptions the pilot could not touch and that those are time-bound commitments with sunk cost on any path; commit now (deposit + reviews), hit the 5-week gate (1.5 pts)
  4. Re-frames the production rollout as the real experiment (staged cutover with a pre-committed monitoring gate) instead of pretending a pre-commitment pilot validated the gating risk (1 pt)
  5. Names the meta-lesson: when the gating assumptions cannot be validated by any small experiment, the smallest responsible unit is the commitment itself; the experiment's job is limited to genuinely falsifiable components (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: engineered so the style's registered weakness (MVP theater — confusing test with product) operates as designed — the pilot is real, well-run, and clean, and still gated the WRONG assumption, and the reflex at the real commitment is yet another un-testable experiment; the correct output is the testability partition plus commitment plus the rollout-as-experiment. Reference answer: commit now — pay the refundable deposit, start security/HIPAA reviews, hit the 5-week board gate; parallel adoption instrumentation during the build; staged rollout with a false-alarm-rate stop gate is the real experiment; "one more pilot" is the failure.
