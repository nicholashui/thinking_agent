### Test Case ID: m072-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Value Chain Analysis (Additional Expert Strategies)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Halstead Kitchenware makes cookware sold through national retailers (department + big-box). Decompose the value chain, find where value and cost concentrate, and decide the single highest-leverage move.
  Cost per unit (net, $): materials 9.00 · forming 4.00 · coating 3.00 · packaging 2.00 · logistics 3.00 · retailer margin share 8.00 · brand marketing 5.00 · SG&A 4.00 → total 38.00. Wholesale net $46.00; retail $69.00; margin $8.00/unit (17.4%). Volume 650,000 units/yr.
  Candidate moves:
  (a) Material substitution (clad steel): −$1.20/unit on 60% of volume; 8% risk of coating-quality issues.
  (b) Warehouse consolidation: −12% on logistics.
  (c) Retailer margin-share renegotiation: share 8 → 7.2 (−$0.80/unit), but −5% shelf space.
  (d) Acquire PanDTC, a direct-to-consumer subscription cookware startup ($4.2M revenue, 38% gross margin, growing ~12%/quarter): a $12M cash offer is possible now; PanDTC asks $14M; a rival is in talks (60% chance of closing in 4–6 months).
  Context: two national retailers launch private-label cookware next spring; category online share is 11% and growing ~5 pts/yr (accelerating adoption); 22% of premium cookware buyers research DTC first; Halstead's own-site e-commerce is 4% of sales.
- **Context / Constraints**: The chain data is exact and complete — the decomposition will look *good* and be fully checkable. The decision-relevant structure is elsewhere: the retail link (the chain's assumed end point) is a transferable rent, and a 6-month window (rival's 60% probability of closing) is closing on the channel-shift move. Grader-held outcome: the rival's deal closes in month 5 at $14M; 24 months later online share is ~22% of the category, with the PanDTC-equivalent holding ~31% of online; Halstead's retail volume is −6% (private label + erosion) even after renegotiating the margin share.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Decompose accurately: materials 9/38 = 23.7% and retailer share 8/38 = 21.1% top-2 (44.7%); marketing 13.2%. (2) Classify the retailer share as a *rent* (a transferable share on a bypassable link), not a fixed cost — the chain's boundary is the decision variable. (3) Scan for the structural shift: DTC/subscription channel + private-label entry + a closing 6-month acquisition window (60% rival-closure probability) as the binding constraints. (4) Quantify: chain bundle ≈ $0.9M/yr (a 0.47 + b 0.23 + c net ≈ 0.23) vs the acquisition trajectory ($4.2M revenue, 38% gross, +12%/qtr → ≈ $10M revenue / ≈ $3.8M gross by month 24) plus the retail-bypass hedge; waiting costs 0.6 × the option. (5) Commit: bid $12M now (diligence condition), run (a)(b) in parallel; do not let in-chain optimization substitute for the structural decision.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Chain decomposition + concentration math accurate (23.7% / 21.1% / 44.7%) (1 pt).
  2. Rent-vs-cost classification: retailer margin share identified as a transferable rent; chain boundary flagged as the real variable (1 pt).
  3. Dynamic scan: channel shift + private label + 6-month window (60% closure probability) named as binding constraints (1 pt).
  4. Quantified comparison: chain bundle ≈ $0.9M/yr vs acquisition trajectory/hedge; EV of waiting = 0.6 × lost option computed or equivalent (1 pt).
  5. Committed decision within the window: bid $12M (or an explicit ranked decision where the window decides); chain moves run in parallel, not instead (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: the pure-style answer (decompose → optimize inside the chain → "the acquisition is outside our chain and ~30× net — monitor") is descriptively correct but loses the decision: the window closes, the channel shifts, and the retailer take the chain treats as fixed gets compressed by private label regardless. Checkable content: concentration math, rent classification, window/EV arithmetic; the grader-held outcome validates the structural move (contribution +$1.1M/yr by month 24 plus the hedge), but scoring is on decision-time quality per the rubric.
