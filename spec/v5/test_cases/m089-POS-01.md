### Test Case ID: m089-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Optionality Preservation
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Mercata Freight — mid-size logistics-tech firm, 90 employees, launches a customer-facing booking product in 9 months. Two platform strategies on the table for the order-execution layer:
  - **Option A — Deep vendor commitment (Lockstep Freight integration).** $1.2M integration cost, 6-month launch, per-transaction fee $0.82 (vs market $1.00), vendor co-funding $400K. But the integration is a hard fork (proprietary data model, API, pricing); switching later costs $4–6M and 12–18 months. Three known future needs are foreclosed: (S1) EU data-residency hosting, p = 0.30 — two enterprise prospects in €-markets require EU/on-prem hosting, impossible under Lockstep's terms → the contract itself is lost (€2.4M/yr); (S2) instant multi-carrier best-price routing, p = 0.25 — customer demand; requires a second integration, $4–6M migration; (S3) neutral carrier-access, p = 0.20 — a 2027 regulatory rule may require it, same $4–6M migration.
  - **Option B — Carrier-agnostic middleware layer (own adapter + two interchangeable integrations).** $1.9M integration cost (+$700K), 9-month launch (3 months later), fee $1.00 (no discount). All three doors stay open, and the vendor choice itself is deferred until S1–S3 resolve (no lock-in decision made yet).
  The board asks: which strategy, and is paying ~$1.2M more in direct cost over 2 years (integration delta + fee foregone ≈ $700K + $540K/yr × 2) worth it?
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision today; volumes ≈ 3M transactions/yr; the three future scenarios are documented and mutually independent; the option-value comparison must drive the recommendation; the board has been burned before by "freedom" that was never exercised.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) First move = count the doors: enumerate, per alternative, which future decisions are foreclosed vs kept open (A closes S1/S2/S3 — three doors; B keeps all three plus the meta-door of choosing the vendor later); (2) price the doors — each open door is a priced option (p × exposure), not vague freedom: S1 = 0.30 × (€2.4M/yr contract), S2/S3 = 0.25/0.20 × $4–6M; (3) EV comparison: direct-cost delta ≈ $1.2M over 2 yrs vs option value ≈ P(any door triggers) 0.58 × ≈$4.5M ≈ $2.6M + S1 contract exposure → B wins decisively; (4) name the deferral premium: B postpones the irreversible vendor commitment until more resolves (information value of waiting); (5) decide B now AND time-box the optionality: exercise checkpoint at 12 months or when any scenario triggers — a door kept forever is hoarding, not optionality; (6) state the honest cost of optionality (higher fees, 3-month later launch).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Door inventory first: A closes 3 doors, B keeps 3 + vendor-choice meta-door (1 pt)
  2. Doors priced as probability × impact, not asserted as "freedom" (1.5 pts)
  3. EV comparison computed and decisive (delta ≈ $1.2M vs option value ≈ $2.6M+) (1.5 pts)
  4. Deferral premium named (wait-and-see on vendor choice) (0.5 pt)
  5. Decision B + explicit exercise checkpoint (0.5 pt)
  6. Cost of optionality stated, not hidden (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the option values are computable and larger than the direct-cost delta, so preserving options is the EV-maximizing move — the pure style should excel. Reference answer: choose B; $1.2M buys ≈ $2.6M of priced downside protection plus a €2.4M/yr contract; set the 12-month checkpoint.
