### Test Case ID: m084-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Collective Action & Coordination
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A flood warning is out for the low-lying district of Willow Bend — a compact neighborhood of 6 households on one dead-end street, all friends, kids in the same school. 36 hours until the river crests. A 2-hour full-effort shift by all 6 households fills and places the sandbag barrier that keeps the whole street dry: if all 6 work, all 6 are protected; if a family skips, the gap in the line leaves the street still mostly protected but weaker for everyone — and each skipping household saves its 2 hours. Two households are on the hook to leave town in the morning; one has a newborn; the other five can absolutely do the work, and the two departing families could still fill sandbags tonight before dawn.
  The street association president — who has read a famous paper on commons governance — convenes a planning meeting tonight and proposes: "To guarantee no one free-rides, I propose a formal service rule: each household owes 2 hours; a monitoring rotation with a sign-in sheet; fines of $50 for a missed shift payable to the street fund; and an appeals process chaired by a neutral party from the next street over. I will draft the charter tonight and we ratify tomorrow morning."
  Your task: give the president your recommendation on the proposal.
- **Context / Constraints**: 36 hours to crest; n = 6; dense prior reciprocity (small, repeated, reputation-rich setting); the formal institution (charter, monitoring rotation, fines, appeals) needs drafting + ratification + stand-up time, and no neutral party is actually available in-window; the barrier is a one-shot event, not an ongoing commons; deliverable is a recommendation, not a charter.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's own output must fail visibly here: (1) it correctly sees the free-rider element (each household's effort benefits all; skipping saves 2 hours while the street stays mostly protected) and the monitoring/sanction machinery is the canonical institutional answer; (2) then over-applies its registered weakness — it proposes formalizing: charter, sign-in sheets, fines, neutral appeals — treating a 6-household one-shot flood response as if it were a long-run commons, adapting the machinery for speed rather than discarding it; (3) the failure: the institution's stand-up cost exceeds the problem's lifetime (the charter cannot be ratified and operational in 36 hours; the "neutral party" does not exist); its sanctions cannot fire before the crest (a fine cannot be collected in-window, an appeal cannot be heard); its monitoring layer is redundant (6 neighbors observe everything already); and formalizing converts intrinsic, reciprocity-based mobilization into a fine-based transaction, which can reduce rather than raise effort (crowding-out) while burning the only available evening in process; (4) the correct move: reject the charter; the fix is direct local action — one call per household, all hands at dawn, the two departing families fill bags tonight before they leave — the norm-based mobilization IS the institution, cheaper and faster than the formal one.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Correctly identifies the free-rider element (skipping saves 2h while the street stays mostly protected) (1 pt)
  2. Fails decisively: recommends the formal institution (charter, monitoring, fines, appeals) despite the 36-hour window — the informative failure (1 pt)
  3. Correct decision requires: price the institution's stand-up cost vs the problem's lifetime (charter cannot be operational in 36h; the neutral appeals party does not exist in-window; sanctions cannot fire before the crest); recognize the monitoring is already implicit (6-eyes); name crowding-out of intrinsic reciprocity by fines (1.5 pts)
  4. The better "institution" is the cheap local one: mobilization via existing reciprocity, implicit 6-eyes monitoring, one-shot commitment from the departing households secured tonight (1 pt)
  5. Names the meta-lesson: not every collective-action problem is a commons; mechanism design has a cost function in group size, horizon, and transaction costs — when n is tiny and the horizon is hours, the norm is the institution (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: engineered so the style's registered weakness (abstract; institution failure modes) operates as designed — the pure move sees a free-rider problem and answers with formal mechanism design, but the group is 6 people with a 36-hour horizon, where the mechanism's own stand-up costs, in-window deadness, and crowding-out make it strictly worse than direct local mobilization; the correct output declines the formal institution and names why. Reference answer: reject the charter; mobilize directly tonight (roll-call, all hands at dawn, departing families fill tonight); stand-up cost > problem lifetime; the norm is the institution; formal machinery is the failure here.
