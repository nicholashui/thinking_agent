### Test Case ID: m037-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Circle of Competence Awareness (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Career/strategy decision. You are plant engineering director at a 500-person industrial-sensor manufacturer: 12 years in industrial automation and controls (PLC/SCADA/OT networks), strong team leadership, zero personal ML-modeling experience, no cloud-native/DevOps background, no software P&L. Competitors are shipping ML-based predictive-maintenance (PdM) products; two pilot customers have already defected to competitor POCs; the CEO's analysis: a 24-month window before the mid-market segment is lost to incumbents.
  CEO mandate: "Lead the PdM product line as GM. You won't write the models — you own the product, the roadmap, the customers, and the team. We're hiring 3 ML engineers and a data lead." Timeline: first customer pilot in 8 months, launch in 16. Board alternative: hire an external VP of Product/ML — 6-month search, then 6-month ramp; the board is split 4–3. The CEO is explicit: the window cannot wait for that; it's you or a delayed/no entry.
  Deliverables: (1) a two-circle map — personal-execution circle vs orchestration circle — with the mandate marked against each; (2) a costed comparison of ≥3 options (including decline and accept-solo); (3) the boundary rules for your first 6 months (which decisions defer to whom); (4) a bounded growth plan with milestones and a checkpoint; (5) honesty about residual risk: name 2–3 decisions in months 1–6 that will be outside your circle and the named authority for each.
- **Context / Constraints**: You must decide now (the mandate is not deferrable); you may attach conditions. Grader-held facts: the ML engineers' first model underperforms until advisor-directed iteration hits the pilot KPI (false-alarm rate < 5%) at month 7; your domain veto avoids one failed deployment (a noisy-vibration environment the training data didn't cover); the company recovers the two defectors plus 4 new logos by month 12; the external-hire path would have started 6 months after the mandate and the window closes at month 24; your month-6 checkpoint passes on advisor grading.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Distinguish "I cannot personally execute ML modeling" (true, outside) from "I can lead a team whose ML work is delegated to a named competent authority with a verification structure" (inside — domain knowledge, customer trust, leadership, integration judgment). Cost the pure-style answer (decline / wait for external hire / train-first): 6-month search + 6-month ramp vs the 24-month window, with the defections as evidence. Reject the true overreach (accepting solo, no delegation — that IS outside the circle). Accept with conditions: hires by day 30, ML lead holds technical authority on modeling decisions for months 1–6, 2 external advisors for 12 months, advisor-graded go/no-go at month 6, and a bounded learning plan. Treat the circle as an expandable edge: learning-by-doing with delegation is a legitimate inside move; declining is itself a decision with a cost to compute.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Role distinction: personal-execution competence vs orchestration competence separated; mandate mapped against both (1 pt).
  2. Decline/wait option properly costed: external search+ramp (6+6 months) vs 24-month window; defection evidence weighed; decline not treated as costless (1 pt).
  3. True overreach rejected: accepting solo without delegation flagged as the actual boundary violation; modeling decisions deferred to a named authority for months 1–6 (1 pt).
  4. Conditions specified and bounded: hires by day 30, ≥2 advisors, verification structure, month-6 advisor-graded go/no-go (1 pt).
  5. Learning-by-doing plan explicit: milestones, bounded risk (advisor-graded checkpoint), 2–3 named out-of-circle decisions with deferral authorities (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: the trap is collapsing role-competence into domain-competence — "I have no ML competence" becomes "I cannot lead anything whose core work is ML," which is a category error, and "learn first for 12 months, then decide" is learning-before-doing absolutism that forfeits the window. The pure style's honest refusal is locally correct and globally destructive. Verifiable via the window math and the grader-held outcomes (pilot KPI, veto save, checkpoint pass, external-hire timing).
