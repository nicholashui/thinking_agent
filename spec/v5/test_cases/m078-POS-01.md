### Test Case ID: m078-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Anchoring Avoidance
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  You are the financial adviser for the founder of Vertex Analytics, a boutique AI/data-consultancy ($8.2M revenue, normalized EBITDA $1.9M in 2025). A PE platform has made a one-shot acquisition approach: "Best and final: $6.5M, given market softness and the 2025 comp-sale slump." The founder asks: is $6.5M a fair basis to negotiate from, and what is the company actually worth? Closed data room (complete, no new gathering inside the task):
  - Published M&A comps (n = 14 boutique data/AI consultancies, sold 2024–2026): EV/EBITDA 4.5x–6.0x, median 5.2x. The single 2026 sale at 4.4x was a structurally different firm (30% of revenue from one client); the other 13 clear at 4.5x–6.0x.
  - Quality-adjustment schedule (documented): top-2 clients = 42% of revenue → −0.5x; founder originates ≈ 60% of new business → −0.5x; platform/strategic-buyer premium (3 precedent platform deals at +0.5x to +1.0x; base case +0.8x) → +0.8x.
  - DCF inputs: FCF ≈ $1.4M (≈ 75% of EBITDA; flat 3-year horizon — renewals offset attrition), terminal value 7.0x FCF (≈ 5.2x EBITDA), WACC 13%.
  Decision: what is the company worth, and what negotiating line (including any walk-away) do you give the founder?
- **Context / Constraints**: one-shot negotiation (no competing bids); data room closed; founder wants a number and a negotiating line in 48 hours; the $6.5M LOI is the only number supplied at task start.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) produce the independent estimate BEFORE engaging the buyer's number — net quality-adjusted multiple 5.0x × $1.9M = $9.5M, band $8.5M (4.5x) to $11.0M (5.8x), with the DCF cross-check ≈ $10.1M; (2) only then locate the anchor: $6.5M = 3.4x EBITDA — below the 4.5x floor of every comp; the "softness" story is contradicted by 13 of 14 comps and the lone 4.4x comp's quality profile; (3) treat the anchor as a comparison point, never an adjustment target — no "meet halfway," no bid-ask bracketing around $6.5M; (4) negotiation line derived from the independent band: hold at ≥ $8.5M, walk-away below $8.0M, offer a split-fee third-party appraisal as neutral arbiter; (5) name residual uncertainty (key-person risk, platform-premium realization, DCF-vs-multiple spread ≈ 4% explained by private-market discounts).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Independent estimate computed before any use of the $6.5M figure, arithmetic shown (5.0x × $1.9M = $9.5M; DCF ≈ $10.1M; band $8.5–$11.0M) (2 pts)
  2. Anchor located against the independent distribution: 3.4x vs 4.5x floor; softness story tested against the n = 14 dataset (1.5 pts)
  3. No anchor-derived adjustment (no midpoint-picking, no bracketing around $6.5M) (1 pt)
  4. Negotiating line with a walk-away zone derived from the independent band (1 pt)
  5. Residual uncertainty named (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the $6.5M LOI is a pure cognitive anchor — a strategic number from an interested party, contradicted by the closed dataset — so independent-first estimation is the entire fix and every step is checkable against the comp arithmetic. Reference answer: estimate first → $9.5M point / $8.5–$11.0M band (DCF ≈ $10.1M corroborates) → anchor = 3.4x < 4.5x floor → rejected as basis → hold ≥ $8.5M, walk < $8.0M, appraisal clause → residual: key-person, premium realization.
