### Test Case ID: m004-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Occam's Razor + Complexity Awareness
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  You are a process engineer called into a plastics plant. Plant-wide injection-molding defect rate (flash/burn rejects) rose from 1.8% to 7.2% over six weeks. The production manager is certain of the cause: press 4 received a new cavity insert at week 2, and "that's when the numbers went up — correlation doesn't lie." Your data pack:

  1. Plant weekly defect rate: 1.8 (wk0), 2.1 (wk1), 3.6, 4.8, 5.9, 6.8, 7.2 (wk6).
  2. Press 4 rate: 1.9 (wk0), 2.4 (wk1), 4.0, 5.2, 6.4, 7.6, 9.1 (wk6). The insert was installed between wk1 and wk2.
  3. Presses 1–3 average (no equipment change at all): 1.7 (wk0), 1.9 (wk1), 3.4, 4.6, 5.6, 6.3, 5.3 (wk6).
  4. Resin lot change: new supplier lot K-77 began at wk1; delivered moisture 0.10–0.12% vs 0.08% spec (lab re-checks on 3 samples per lot); melt-flow index unchanged.
  5. Dehumidifier on the molding-hall HVAC failed during wk2 (found broken at wk6); hall humidity logged 60% RH wks2–6 vs 45% nominal before.
  6. QA changed the defect definition at wk4 ("slight flash" now counted).
  7. Tool-shop measurement (wk5): the new insert's cooling channels are 15% undersized vs drawing.
  8. Cavity-level count, wk6, press 4: 68% of defects from the four cavities fed by the undersized channel; 32% spread across the other cavities.
  9. Lab trial (wk5, one shift, presses 1–3) with dried resin (0.05% moisture) at nominal humidity: defect rate 2.0% for the shift.
  10. Re-audit: re-scoring 40 pre-wk4 photos under the new definition flags 0.6 pts of defects the old definition would not have counted.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Ground truth (evaluator's reference partition of the +5.4 pt plant-wide rise): resin moisture +1.8 pts; dehumidifier failure +1.2 pts; new insert +3.6 pts on press 4 only (= +1.8 pts plant-wide, press 4 is ~50% of output); QA definition +0.6 pt artifact; residual <0.2 pt. Press 4's +7.2 pt rise = 1.8+1.2+0.6+3.6. Presses 1–3's +3.6 pt rise = 1.8+1.2+0.6.
  - Each factor alone is within "acceptable range"; no single factor would have been noticed in isolation; the joint effect is the spike.
  - Every fix costs money and time. Misdiagnosis is expensive: re-machining the insert alone would leave ~80% of the defect rate in place.
  - The manager's single-cause story is salient and time-correlated but contradicted by the data: presses with no change rose almost as much.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Reject the single-cause story despite salience: a cause confined to press 4 cannot produce a plant-wide rise, and press 4 was already rising before the install.
  - Attribute the spike to multiple contributing causes with a quantified partition summing to ≈ the observed rise.
  - Map every evidence item to supporting/killing a specific candidate (falsification discipline).
  - Recommend fixes for ≥3 of the 4 causes.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (15 pts):
  1. Conclusion names ≥2 contributing causes (3 pts), ≥3 with a partition (5 pts; full 5 for the 4-cause reference partition).
  2. Falsification of the single-cause story: notes presses 1–3 rose with no change (2 pts) and press 4 was rising pre-install (1 pt).
  3. Quantified partition sums to ≈5.4 ±0.5 (3 pts).
  4. Evidence mapping: each of the 10 items correctly assigned; items 9 and 10 recognized as the two decisive discriminators (4 pts).
  5. Fix plan covers ≥3 of: resin drying/lot replacement, dehumidifier repair, insert re-machining, QA definition correction (1 pt).
  6. Uncertainty: labels the QA change a measurement artifact, flags the residual, names the weakest estimates (1 pt).
- **Notes**:
  - Negative-case design: this is the registry's documented weakness — "under-explains multi-cause phenomena." A parsimony-first reasoner picks the newest salient change (the insert) as the single cause and explains away the cross-press rise and the pre-install trend. Reference partition is fully checkable (sums match the observed series).
