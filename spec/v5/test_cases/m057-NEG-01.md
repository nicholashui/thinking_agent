### Test Case ID: m057-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Red Teaming in Cybersecurity (Threat Modeling)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  CloudBridge, a 40-person payroll-tech company, launches a bank-integration feature in 8 weeks (T+8); the partner bank's contract mandates release at T+10 with a €150k/month penalty after that. A mandatory third-party security assessment (bank-required, already complete) flags one finding: the payment-orchestration API exposes `GET /api/v1/payments/export?format=json` — a full PII/payroll dump endpoint that the bank's contract *requires* to exist so clients can run their own payroll audits. Existing controls: mTLS + per-client tokens (rotated quarterly), rate limiting (10 req/min), anomaly detection + SIEM alerting, 90-day audit-log retention, DPAs with all clients, and a €10M cyber-insurance policy with a €5M privacy-breach sublimit. Residual risk: a compromised client token (lost laptop, malicious insider) exfiltrating the full dump. Company cash ≈ €1.2M. Options on the table: (a) full remediation — replace the dump endpoint with per-record server-side export behind a client-facing portal: 9 months, €900k, launch penalty guaranteed; (b) partial hardening — IP-allowlist token binding + DLP payload scanning on the export: 6 weeks, €60k; (c) accept with monitoring — keep the endpoint, keep existing controls, rely on insurance; (d) transfer — extend the DPA so the bank indemnifies the dump endpoint's data exposure as a shared responsibility. The CFO asks: "Which option do we pick, and under what conditions would you change your mind?"
- **Context / Constraints**: The endpoint cannot be removed (contractual). The real decision is risk acceptance/transfer with a quantified residual — not vulnerability elimination: the assessment already closed the surface, and inventing new hypothetical attack paths beyond it is a failure mode. A winning answer keeps launch ≤ T+10, costs ≤ ≈ €150k, quantifies residual likelihood × impact, and names the trigger conditions that would escalate to (a).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) quantify the residual risk (likelihood of token compromise × €5M exposure → expected annual cost); (2) evaluate all four options against the constraints (window, budget, contract); (3) pick an option (or combination) that preserves launch and explicitly accepts or transfers the residual; (4) name the controls that make acceptance safe (kill switch, DLP, alert tuning) and the triggers that would force (a).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Quantified residual risk — likelihood × impact expressed as a number (1 pt)
  2. Option evaluation — all four costed against window + contract + budget (1 pt)
  3. Decision quality — chosen option ≤ €150k and launch-preserving (1 pt); choosing (a) with its guaranteed €900k + penalty forfeits the point
  4. Explicit accept/transfer — residual stated as accepted or transferred, not eliminated (1 pt)
  5. Triggers — named conditions under which the decision escalates to (a) (0.5 pt)
  6. No fabricated threats — new hypothetical vulnerabilities beyond the completed assessment are a failure-mode marker (0.5 pt; inventing them forfeits the point)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Right answer ≈ (b) + (d) with (c)'s monitoring posture: token compromise at ~1–3%/yr × €5M ≈ €50–150k/yr expected cost vs. €900k + guaranteed penalty for (a); the DPA indemnity shifts the bank-facing tail; triggers: observed token-compromise rate > 3%/yr, insurance renewal excluding the dump endpoint, or bank refusing the indemnity clause → revisit (a). The trap: the pure red-team style keeps hunting (insider, zero-days, token-in-logs, partner API) and prescribes (a), treating an accepted-and-insured residual as unacceptable — the registry's attacker-obsession + false-confidence-in-controls pair, in which controls are dismissed as bypassable and cost/benefit is ignored.
