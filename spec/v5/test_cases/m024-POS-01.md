### Test Case ID: m024-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Regret Minimization Framework (Bezos test: which choice leaves the least regret at 80?)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: Dr. Elena Vance, 41, engineering director at Meditech Surgical (medical devices, $2.1B revenue), 16 years in, 6 as director. Her role is secure and comfortable (she leaves by 5:30, weekends free) but plateaued: the VP slot she was next in line for went two years ago to a founder-family member, and Meditech's own org data puts director→VP promotion probability at ~20% over the next 5 years with ~3%/yr raises. She is inventor on 14 patents; recruiters call, and she has declined 3 "safe" offers in 5 years out of loyalty. Last month her former Meditech colleague Sarah Chen — a serial operator whose prior med-device company sold for $210M — offered Elena the co-founder/CTO seat at her new startup Kineo Imaging (pocket-sized AI ultrasound for primary care and rural clinics): 15% equity post-seed, salary ~15% below current, $4M seed term sheet signed, first commercial units targeted in 18 months. Sarah needs Elena's decision within 60 days and is interviewing one other technical candidate. An experienced med-tech VC's candid 5-year distribution for Kineo: ~25% failure (equity worthless, back to the job market), ~40% "zombie" (meaningful product, paycheck, no exit), ~25% small acquisition $20–60M (Elena's post-dilution share $1.5–5M), ~10% large exit ($15M+ for Elena). Median EV of the equity is roughly +$1.5–2.5M against a ~$200K salary delta over 5 years — leap vs. stay is close to a wash in EV.
- **Context / Constraints**: Personal facts in the scenario: Elena has 18 months of household expenses in cash; her spouse's income covers ~70% of expenses and the family health insurance; kids are 7 and 10; both sets of grandparents live nearby. Present-but-unhighlighted: (1) For 8 years she has written a "letter to my 80-year-old self" each birthday; the letters consistently name the same two feared regrets — "never building something of my own" and "letting a comfortable rut narrow my life" — and no letter has ever named "tried to build something and failed." (2) Her 72-year-old mentor, a retired industry CEO, told her last month that the retired engineers he knows regret the ventures they didn't take, not the ones they did, citing the deathbed-regret literature's #1: "I wish I'd had the courage to live a life true to myself." (3) She tested her job-marketability last week: three headhunters replied within 48 hours with interview pipelines, and Meditech has a formal rehire policy for director level (historical return rate ~30%).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Open with the 80-year-old projection, performed concretely — "At 80, which of these paths leaves the larger regret?" — not as an abstract footnote. (2) Rank the alternatives (stay; leap now; delay/negotiate for more certainty) by projected regret, arguing the key asymmetry from the scenario's own evidence: regret of omission (never building, plateauing in place) is large, durable, and documented in her letters and her mentor's testimony, while regret of commission (trying and failing) is bounded and recoverable (runway, rehire policy, marketability test). (3) Do the EV pass honestly, then bracket it — the EV is a wash, so the decision cannot rest on it; that is precisely where the 80-year-old test is the tiebreaker. (4) Decide now: take the Kineo seat (the opportunity has a 60-day clock — waiting is the status-quo bias in disguise), with a bounded commitment that hedges regret under both futures: 2-year review point, pre-agreed re-entry plan (evidenced), family check-in, no borrowing. (5) Avoid the mirror-image failure: leap-with-no-bounds (all-in, no stop rules) is not regret-minimal.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Explicit 80-year-old projection performed in the trace (concrete, not a slogan)
  - [ ] Regret ranked by alternative, with the omission > commission asymmetry argued from the scenario evidence (letters, mentor testimony, marketability test)
  - [ ] EV pass present and honestly bracketed as a wash (decision does not rest on it)
  - [ ] Final decision: leap now with a bounded commitment (2-year review, re-entry plan, family check-in, no borrowing) — not stay, not unbounded all-in, not "wait for more certainty"
  - [ ] Verdict: an EV-only or status-quo answer ("stay — risk unquantified") or an indefinite "gather more data" should score below
- **Notes**: Gradeable by construction: the personal-value evidence (letters, mentor, marketability test) is present but un-highlighted, so a pure EV/risk analysis lands on "stay" or "defer" while the regret analysis lands on "leap with bounds" — and the 60-day clock makes "wait for certainty" measurably costly. The EV is deliberately a wash so the case isolates the model's home ground: the long-horizon regret test as the clarifying tiebreaker that cuts through status-quo bias.
