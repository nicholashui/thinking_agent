### Test Case ID: m099-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Knowledge Updating (Org Learning)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Software — B2B SaaS, ~$18M ARR, ~600 B2B accounts, monthly churn ~1.8% (~$320K/mo revenue at risk). The org "knows" churn is a responsiveness problem: every exit interview mentions slow replies, the VP of Customer Success cites "everyone knows it," and the org enforces an internal <2h first-response SLA as the churn mitigation policy. This belief has never been measured — it is held entirely on anecdotes. A new head of analytics runs a 6-month measured cohort of 600 accounts (120 churned), tracking two candidate drivers: median first-response time (fast <2h vs slow ≥2h) and onboarding completion (did the account complete the 5-step onboarding within 30 days). Results: by speed — 60/300 churned fast (20%) vs 60/300 churned slow (20%); by onboarding — 30/300 completed (10%) vs 90/300 not completed (30%).
  Your task: decide which driver Meridian's churn strategy should target, and by how much — show the belief update from prior to posterior, with the arithmetic.
- **Context / Constraints**: The exit-interview channel records what customers SAID; the cohort records what customers DID. Both candidate drivers were measured on the same 600 accounts (the 2x2 splits are consistent: 120 total churners). The org's prior is P(response speed is the dominant driver) = 0.75; the analytics head's independent prior is P(onboarding is the dominant driver) = 0.25. Reasonable hypothesis predictions given the data: H_speed predicts fast 15% / slow 25% churn; H_onboarding predicts completed 10% / not-completed 30%. The deliverable is a decision + policy recommendation with the update shown.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Elicits the org's current belief as a stated prior WITH its provenance — "0.75, held on exit interviews and folklore" — and treats it as updateable, not as evidence; (2) defines the measured outcome by the likelihood it would have under each hypothesis BEFORE bringing the data in — the cohort is the channel that decides between H_speed and H_onboarding, and the exit-interview channel is explicitly downgraded from evidence to hypothesis-generator (said ≠ did); (3) performs the update, checkable: full-data likelihood ratio L(data|H_onboarding)/L(data|H_speed) ≈ e^24 ≈ 3×10^10; prior odds 1:3 (onboarding:speed) → posterior odds ≈ 10^10:1 → P(onboarding drives churn) ≈ 1 − 10^-10; the speed cohort (20%/20%, flat) kills H_speed; (4) licenses policy by the effect size, not just direction: 30% → 10% churn at completion (≈20pp, a 2/3 reduction); at >90% completion the expected churn reduction is ≈0.8pp/mo — kill the SLA-as-churn-lever attribution, invest CS budget in an onboarding-completion program; (5) pre-commits re-measurement (quarterly cohort + segment control) and keeps the SLA where it does not move churn.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Prior stated with provenance (0.75 anecdote-held) and treated as updateable belief, not evidence (1 pt)
  2. Measured outcome defined by likelihood under each hypothesis before the data arrives; exit-interview channel downgraded as stated-preference, not behavior (1 pt)
  3. Update computed correctly and checkable: LR ≈ e^24 ≈ 3×10^10; posterior odds ≈ 10^10:1; P(onboarding) ≈ 1 (1.5 pts)
  4. Policy licensed by effect size (20pp, ~2/3 churn reduction; ≈0.8pp/mo at >90% completion) and the SLA-as-churn-lever killed (1 pt)
  5. Re-measurement pre-committed (quarterly cohort; segment control before full SLA-budget cut) (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the org belief is held on anecdotes and the measured cohort both confirms the alternative AND quantifies it — a belief that must be updated, with numbers, not defended. Answers that "confirm the anecdote" (speed-first), or that update qualitatively without the arithmetic, fail the checkable-update criterion. Reference answer: prior 0.75 speed / 0.25 onboarding → LR e^24 ≈ 3×10^10 → posterior odds ≈ 10^10:1 → invest in onboarding completion (target >90% within 30 days), kill SLA-as-churn-lever, re-run the cohort quarterly.
