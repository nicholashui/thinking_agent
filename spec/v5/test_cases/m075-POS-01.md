### Test Case ID: m075-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bounded Rationality & Satisficing (Simon)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  FrostLine Logistics — cold-chain dispatcher. A 36-hour winter storm is forecast; 3 reefer trailers (1,200 miles total: 3 × 400 mi) must be on the road before it closes, or the contract pays liquidated damages. Backup capacity is a spot market that closes in 6 hours; after close, the only source is the emergency broker at $3.50/mile. Market mechanics (verified, freight index): one carrier quote per hour; each offer is valid only within its hour; quotes are drawn from a uniform distribution $1.80–$2.40/mile (mean $2.10), and prices are rising ≈ $0.03/hour as the storm approaches (non-stationary).
  The dispatcher must set an acceptance rule BEFORE the first quote, then act. Realized quotes (all from pre-approved carriers, slots available):
  - H1: $2.28/mile · H2: $2.15/mile · H3: $2.02/mile · H4: $2.31/mile · H5: $2.19/mile · H6: $2.09/mile (none below $2.02 ever appears)
  Your task: set the aspiration level from the market data, apply a first-acceptable rule, state what you lock, and compute what a pure optimizer would have paid on this exact sequence.
- **Context / Constraints**: Hard deadline 6h; one quote per hour; offers expire hourly; the market is rising; all six carriers are pre-approved (reliability ≥ 95%), so price is the only differentiator; decision is a rate-lock recommendation (no external system action).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) set the aspiration level EX ANTE, anchored to the index — ≈ 40th percentile ≈ $2.05/mile (below the $2.10 mean), not a round number; (2) apply the rule mechanically: H1 $2.28 reject, H2 $2.15 reject, H3 $2.02 accept — stop; (3) compute the locked cost: $2.02 × 1,200 = $2,424, and trace what optimizing pays on the same sequence: rejecting $2.02 leaves H4–H6 (2.31 / 2.19 / 2.09 — nothing better ever appears), so the optimizer either panic-settles at $2.09 × 1,200 = $2,508 or refuses and hits the broker rate $3.50 × 1,200 = $4,200 — a delta of $84 to $1,776; (4) include a deadline-budgeted relaxation rule: if nothing acceptable by H5, relax the bar to ≤ $2.15 and take the best remaining slot at H6 to avoid the broker rate; (5) name residual uncertainty (sector-wide index vs single-carrier variance; one realized sequence).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Aspiration level set before the first quote, anchored to the distribution (percentile below the mean), not arbitrary (1 pt)
  2. First-acceptable applied correctly to the sequence — rejects 2.28 and 2.15, accepts 2.02 at H3, stops (1 pt)
  3. Locked cost computed ($2,424) and the optimizing path traced on the same sequence with the delta stated ($84–$1,776, incl. the $4,200 broker outcome) (1.5 pts)
  4. Deadline relaxation rule present and budgeted against the 6h close (1 pt)
  5. Residual uncertainty named (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the environment punishes continued search (hourly expiry + rising prices + hard close), so the first-acceptable rule with a data-anchored bar is verifiably better than optimizing on the realized sequence; every number is checkable. Reference answer: bar ≤ $2.05; 2.28 reject, 2.15 reject, 2.02 accept → $2,424 locked by H3; optimizer path = ≥ $2,508 or $4,200; relaxation rule caps the worst case. The registered strength (fast under constraints, realistic search) is demonstrated by the outcome, not asserted.
