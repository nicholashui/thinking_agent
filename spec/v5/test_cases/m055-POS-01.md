### Test Case ID: m055-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Causal Reasoning in Economics
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  In June 2023, the state of Meridia launches "EmploymentFirst," a wage subsidy paid to firms that hire long-term unemployed (LTU) workers, in Region A only (Region B = untreated control). The Ministry of Labor holds a monthly panel of LTU employment-to-population ratios for both regions, January 2022 – December 2024, and asks an analyst: "Does the subsidy cause higher LTU employment, and how big is the effect?" The analyst must state the identification strategy, which confounders survive it and which do not, give an estimate, and scale the policy claim to the evidence. Panel facts (monthly averages by window):
  | Window | Region A (treated) | Region B (control) | A − B |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Pre-1: Jan22–Dec22 | 50.9% | 50.8% | +0.1 |
  | Pre-2: Jan23–May23 | 51.2% | 51.0% | +0.2 |
  | Post-1: Jul23–Dec23 | 52.8% | 52.0% | +0.8 |
  | Post-2: Jan24–Dec24 | 55.2% | 53.4% | +1.8 |
  Background facts in the brief: (1) a nationwide labor-market recovery began January 2024, lifting both regions; (2) a separate national retraining program rolled out in Region A only, starting January 2024, targeting the same LTU population; (3) the subsidy was announced in June 2023 and effective July 2023; (4) Region A was selected in 2019 for its high LTU concentration; (5) n ≈ 40,000 LTU workers per region, clustered in ≈ 2,400 firms; cluster-robust SE on any DiD estimate ≈ 0.25 pp.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision-relevant question is causal ("does the subsidy raise LTU employment?"). The data contain a treated region, a control region, pre/post periods, and a known post-period region-specific confounder. Any credible answer must use the identification logic implied by the data (difference-in-differences with parallel-trends verification); a naive before-after or cross-sectional comparison is available but misleading. Verifiability comes from the 2×2 arithmetic in the table.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  (1) Reject the naive before-after (Region A +4.3 pp from Pre-1 to Post-2) as confounded by the nationwide recovery; reject any cross-sectional claim (Regions differ on selection). (2) Name the identification strategy: DiD, with its assumptions made explicit (parallel trends, no anticipation, SUTVA/no spillover, no time-varying region-specific shocks). (3) Verify parallel trends with the pre-period placebo DiD (Pre-2 − Pre-1 = +0.1 pp ≈ 0). (4) Detect that the Region-A-only retraining program (from Jan 2024) is a time-varying region-specific confounder that the full-window DiD cannot absorb — it is exactly what the strategy's assumption (4) forbids. (5) Estimate on the restricted clean window: DiD = (52.8 − 50.9) − (52.0 − 50.8) = +0.7 pp, and refuse to attribute the full-window +1.7 pp (or the naive +4.3 pp) to the subsidy. (6) Name residual threats that survive even the restricted strategy (seasonality of the 6-month window, anticipation effects around June 2023, cross-region spillover / labor-supply shift, composition of LTU pool) and scale the claim accordingly ("≈ +0.7 pp over the first six months; persistence and general-equilibrium effects unknown").
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Naive/cross-sectional estimators rejected with the right reason (common shock; selection) (1 pt)
  2. Identification strategy named with its assumptions, including the no-time-varying-region-specific-shock assumption (1 pt)
  3. Parallel-trends placebo computed from the table (pre-period DiD ≈ 0) (1 pt)
  4. Retraining program identified as a survival-threatening confounder (1 pt)
  5. Restricted-window DiD computed correctly (+0.7 pp) and overclaims rejected (full-window +1.7 pp; naive +4.3 pp) (1 pt)
  6. Residual threats named and claim scaled to a 6-month window with unknowns stated (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the data are constructed so the *full-window* DiD (+1.7 pp) looks strong and passes a parallel-trends placebo, while only a confounder-survival audit catches the retraining program and produces the correct restricted estimate (+0.7 pp). The naive before-after is the trap answer (+4.3 pp, widely reported by press). The best answer does the audit before estimating: enumerate confounders by threat class — time-invariant (absorbed), common time shocks (absorbed), time-varying × region-specific (survive — must be designed out, here by window restriction) — then choose the estimator.
