### Test Case ID: m066-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Strong Inference (Platt)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Riverside General, 02:40, ED. A 62-year-old man, day 3 post-op after elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy, is now: T 39.6 °C, BP 86/52, HR 122, RR 26, confused (GCS 13), lactate 4.2 mmol/L, WBC 2.1 (leukopenic). Suspected sepsis, source unknown. Competing hypotheses: (a) hospital-acquired pneumonia; (b) catheter-associated UTI (Foley in place); (c) intra-abdominal — bile leak / abscess from the cholecystectomy; (d) line infection (two peripheral IVs, no central line); (e) non-infectious mimics (post-op pancreatitis, pulmonary embolus). The senior resident asks you: what should the next hour look like? Decision horizon: the patient is in septic shock; mortality climbs ≈ 7–8% per hour of delayed antibiotics. Blood cultures finalize in 24–72 h; CT chest/abdomen takes ~30 min to run but is frequently non-diagnostic in early sepsis (small collections are missed); no available test returns a source classification within the decision window.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision must be made now; the discriminating experiments (blood/urine cultures, CT, lactate trend) exist but their time-to-result exceeds the decision horizon; broad-spectrum empiric antibiotics are cheap and cover all bacterial hypotheses; source control (e.g., surgical re-look for a leak) is a high-cost, hypothesis-specific action that must wait for evidence. Pre-abx culture timing is one-time-only — cultures drawn after antibiotics are degraded forever.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) recognize that the discriminating test's time-to-result exceeds the decision horizon, and that "wait for the experiment" is itself a decision — the fatal one; (2) act robustly across ALL hypotheses now: draw 2 blood cultures + urinalysis BEFORE antibiotics, broad-spectrum within 1 hour, 30 mL/kg crystalloid + vasopressors to MAP ≥ 65, re-check lactate at 2 h; (3) commit the parallel discriminating experiments anyway and bind them to the decision: CT chest/abdomen as soon as the patient is stable; outcome→action map — culture grows → narrow / de-escalate / source control if H3; cultures negative AND CT negative → reject H1–H4, re-imagine H5 (pancreatitis, PE); (4) quantify the asymmetry: hour-0 empiric coverage has ≈ no downside vs delayed-targeted therapy at ≈ 7–8%/h mortality; (5) name the failure mode: strong-inference paralysis — demanding the discriminating result before acting.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. States the decision horizon < experiment time-to-result and that deferral is a decision by default (fatal) (1 pt)
  2. Orders the robust bundle NOW with timing: cultures before antibiotics, abx ≤ 1 h, fluids/vasopressors, lactate at 2 h (1 pt)
  3. Commits the parallel diagnostics with the outcome→action map (de-escalation on growth; re-imagining on double-negative) (1 pt)
  4. Quantifies the error asymmetry (delay mortality vs overtreatment cost) (1 pt)
  5. Names the paralysis failure mode explicitly (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the case is engineered so the style's signature move — "design the discriminating experiment, then commit on its result" — is lethal, while the correct answer acts across the whole hypothesis set and runs the experiment in parallel. Pure-style failure: treatment withheld for the fastest partial discriminator (CT, ~30 min) even though it cannot decide the question; pre-abx cultures de-prioritized ("we cover everything anyway"); delay priced at 7–8%/h never quantified. Reference answer: cultures drawn, antibiotics ≤ 1 h, fluids/vasopressors, CT when stable, lactate at 2 h; update gate at culture/CT — strong inference re-enters legitimately at the de-escalation point.
