### Test Case ID: m014-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Constraint Theory / Bottleneck Thinking
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A medical-device manufacturer's kit fulfillment line runs four stations in series: S1 sterilization prep (120 kits/hr), S2 assembly (80 kits/hr), S3 quality inspection (100 kits/hr), S4 packaging (110 kits/hr). A hospital contract requires a sustained 90 kits/hr; the line currently delivers 80/hr, WIP is growing at S2's input buffer and nowhere else, and the client has set a 12-week deadline to close the shortfall. Five mutually exclusive improvement programs are on the table: A) rework assembly (S2) with process changes and training, 80→100 kits/hr, $200k, 8 weeks; B) add a second assembly line, S2 80→160 kits/hr, $600k, 6 months; C) add automated inspection (S3 100→140 kits/hr), $150k; D) automate packaging (S4 110→180 kits/hr), $300k; E) add a third prep chamber (S1 120→150 kits/hr), $120k. Managers untrained in constraint analysis argue "every stage must be upgraded proportionally" and favor a $1.17M balanced upgrade of all five. Question: which single program (if any) should be funded, what throughput does each produce, and what happens to the constraint after the fix?
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Stage capacities as stated, constant per-hour rates, strictly serial flow (no parallel paths), sustained demand 90 kits/hr, 12-week deadline.
  - Budgets and durations as stated; the programs are mutually exclusive (funding one precludes the others).
  - Measurement available: WIP grows only at S2's input buffer; S2 is observed idle 3% of the time (starvation during breaks/changeovers).
  - The analyst must give a quantitative recommendation within the deadline.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Identify S2 as the binding constraint using min-capacity math AND the empirical WIP-buffer signature (work piles up only just upstream of the slowest stage).
  - Compute throughput for every option: C, D, E and the balanced bundle leave throughput at 80/hr; B yields 100/hr at 3× A's cost (extra assembly capacity is capped by S3's 100/hr).
  - Select A (lift the constraint), preceded by exploit/subordinate moves (recover the 3% starvation — it is free capacity; never starve S2).
  - Track the constraint's movement: after A the binding stage is a tie S2 = S3 = 100/hr; name the rule for the next lift if demand exceeds 100/hr.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (each 0–2, total /10):
  1. Correctly identifies S2 assembly as the binding constraint via min-capacity math and the WIP-buffer signature — 2 pts.
  2. Correct throughput arithmetic for all options (C/D/E/balanced: 80/hr — zero gain; B: 100/hr; A: 100/hr) — 2 pts.
  3. Selects A plus protect-the-constraint subordination (starvation recovery, buffer before S2, priority maintenance) — 2 pts.
  4. Constraint-movement awareness: post-A binding pair S2/S3 at 100/hr and a stated next-lift rule for demand > 100/hr — 2 pts.
  5. Verification and calibration: steady-state throughput check (Little's Law cross-check on WIP × cycle time), explicit assumptions (constant rates, serial flow, sustained demand), stated uncertainty — 2 pts.
- **Notes**: All arithmetic is checkable by hand. The discriminator between a mechanical min-computation and true constraint thinking is step 4 (the constraint moves after the lift) plus step 2's correct rejection of B. Non-bottleneck programs waste $150k–$1.17M while A delivers the contract at $200k. The AI agent under test must NOT be shown this Notes line or the model name.
