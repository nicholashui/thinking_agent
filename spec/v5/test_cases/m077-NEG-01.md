### Test Case ID: m077-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Sunk Cost Recognition
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Halcyon Aero — aerospace components supplier. 22 months ago Halcyon spent $14M (all unrecoverable: custom tooling, no resale) co-building a titanium casting line together with its key customer Aerion Airframes; Aerion co-designed the line, co-invested staff time, and in return granted Halcyon a 5-year preferred-supplier window covering 3 programs — 1 in production now, 2 future programs (both scheduled, per the signed framework agreement). Renegotiation, today: Aerion's procurement demands a 12% price cut on the current program's remaining 3 years. Two live paths:
  - A — Stay under the cut: forward margin on the current program at −12% ≈ +$1.8M over 3 years (thin but positive). Window stays live.
  - B — Walk: a competitor (Veyron Forge) offers a casting contract worth EV ≈ +$4.2M forward (different, easier product family; requires Halcyon to exit Aerion). The $14M line's specialized tooling has no salvage value either way.
  Fixed facts (verified): Aerion's procurement VP, in the room: "You built this line with us — we know you're not going anywhere." Industry reports: 3 other OEMs currently list Halcyon in sourcing evaluations explicitly citing the co-built Aerion line as evidence of long-horizon reliability; sourcing evaluations are re-run on major supplier exits. Halcyon's strategy roadmap calls for 2 more co-built co-investment lines with other OEMs over the next 3 years; aerospace co-investment deal terms historically price in the partner's demonstrated record of honoring sunk co-investments (documented supplier-survey: firms with a visible co-investment exit pay 8–15% worse terms on the next two deals). Aerion's own switching costs are also high (their design freeze is built on the line's tolerances; a switch costs them ~$5M and 9 months).
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision due end of quarter (6 weeks); the ledger is closed; deliverable = negotiation-position brief. The negotiation is two-sided: Aerion also has skin in the game.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The pure style's own output must fail visibly here — it will (1) strip the $14M correctly and (2) compute the clean single-agent EV: walk (+$4.2M) > stay (+$1.8M) — but (3) misread the OEM's "you're not going anywhere" as a manipulation trick and dismiss the commitment channels as sunk-cost rationalization: the window programs (2 future programs, forward value ≈ +$9M, ~50% contingent ≈ +$4.5M EV of staying), other-OEM reputation (visible exit → 2 contracts re-bid, ≈ −$2.5M EV of walking), and co-investment credibility (exit record → worse terms on the next 2 co-investment deals, ≈ −$2.5M EV of walking). Pure style concludes "walk" on +4.2 vs +1.8 and misses ≈ $11M of forward value that is contingent on the sunk investment's visibility. The correct answer: stay — exclude the $14M from the arithmetic (correct discipline retained) but include the forward commitment value the sunk investment purchased (window ≈ +$4.5M, reputation ≈ +$2.5M, co-investment credibility ≈ +$2.5M ⇒ stay ≈ +$11.3M vs walk ≈ −$9.3M), and use Aerion's own co-investment lock-in as counter-leverage to negotiate the cut down to a 5–8% band; name the meta-lesson: sunk costs are informationally inert in single-agent EV but are signals that change other players' behavior in multi-agent games.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. $14M excluded from the arithmetic (still required even here) (1 pt)
  2. At least two of the three commitment channels identified and valued (window / reputation / co-investment credibility) (1.5 pts)
  3. Comparison including channels computed → stay (≈ +$11.3M) beats walk (≈ −$9.3M); verdict with a renegotiation band (5–8%) using Aerion's lock-in (1 pt)
  4. Forward commitment value cleanly separated from sunk-cost sentiment — no rationalization (1 pt)
  5. Meta-lesson named: past matters only through what it changes about others' future actions (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the case is engineered so that pure forward-EV reasoning (the model's strength) is exactly what produces the wrong verdict — the counterparty quote and the framework-agreement facts put the commitment value in the ledger, so the failure is a framing failure, not a data failure. The registered weakness ("ignores reputation/commitment effects") fires: the $14M itself is never counted, but the pure model refuses to count what the $14M purchased. Reference answer: strip $14M → single-agent EV favors walk (+4.2 vs +1.8) → then add the three forward commitment channels (+4.5 window, +2.5 reputation, +2.5 co-investment) → stay + renegotiate to 5–8% → meta-lesson on multi-agent sunk signals.
