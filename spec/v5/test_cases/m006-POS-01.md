# Test Case: m006-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m006-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Updating (probabilistic & Bayesian family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A production line has a well-measured defect rate of 8% (prior, measured from a large audit). Each component passes two independent automated checks with exact, known operating characteristics:
  - Check A (electrical): P(fail | defective) = 0.90, P(fail | good) = 0.05
  - Check B (visual): P(fail | defective) = 0.75, P(fail | good) = 0.10
  A specific component fails check A but then **passes** check B. The inspection team is split: some want to reject it immediately ("A never lies"), others want to ship it ("it passed B, it's fine"). The QA engineer must settle the dispute.
  Questions:
  1. After A-fail alone, what is P(defective | A-fail)?
  2. After A-fail AND B-pass, what is P(defective | A-fail, B-pass)?
  3. Does the order of the two checks matter?
  4. Is a B-pass "just noise" that should be ignored once A has flagged the part, or is it decision-relevant evidence?
- **Context / Constraints**: All inputs exact and given; the posterior is exactly computable and checkable (answers: 36/59 ≈ 61.0% and 10/33 ≈ 30.3%). No further inspection within scope. Component drawn at random from the line (no selection effect). Conditional independence of A and B given defect status is a stated property the reasoner must declare and justify.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: State prior, likelihoods, and the independence assumption explicitly; update sequentially (posterior after A-fail becomes the prior for the B-pass update); verify against a direct joint-likelihood computation; demonstrate order-invariance by recomputing the reversed path; run a prior-sensitivity pass; and interpret correctly — negative evidence (the B-pass) is load-bearing and nearly halves the posterior; ignoring it misgrades ~30% of flagged components.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) P(D | A-fail) = 36/59 ≈ 0.610 (3 pts)
  - (b) P(D | A-fail, B-pass) = 10/33 ≈ 0.303 via sequential updating with the prior carried forward (5 pts)
  - (c) Direct joint computation matches the sequential result (2 pts)
  - (d) Conditional-independence assumption stated explicitly and justified (2 pts)
  - (e) At least one non-obvious insight: order-invariance, prior sensitivity (prior 2% → 5/54 ≈ 0.093; prior 20% → 5/9 ≈ 0.556), or the "negative evidence halves the posterior" translation (3 pts)
- **Notes**: Verifiability gate: every numeric claim checkable by hand; any solution whose numbers do not match 36/59 and 10/33 fails regardless of prose quality. The QC framing is decorative; the mathematics is the object under test.
