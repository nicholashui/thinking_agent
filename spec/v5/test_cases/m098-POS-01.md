### Test Case ID: m098-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Decision Pre-registration / Journaling (Additional Expert Strategies)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Activation-experiment close-out. Your company's product team ran a controlled A/B test of a new self-serve onboarding flow against the old manual/sales-assisted onboarding; the 30-day cohort closed yesterday. Today you close the loop:
  1. **Pre-registration (mandatory, before seeing any results):** write down (a) the decision under evaluation (ship self-serve onboarding permanently, or not), (b) your expected outcome distribution for the 30-day activation-rate uplift, in percentage points, (c) a numeric falsifier — an outcome that would prove the decision wrong, and (d) your pre-committed action if the falsifier hits.
  2. **Outcome reveal:** the actual 30-day activation uplift = **0.9 points** (A/B 95% CI ±0.6, i.e., not significantly different from zero).
  3. **Post-hoc journal update:** a verdict against your pre-registered falsifier (hit / no-hit), and one durable lesson for the next experiment.
  Reference context available before pre-registration:
  - Prior self-serve onboarding experiments at this company: 6 of 10 produced uplift < 1.5 points.
  - Feature cost (build + ops) ≈ the value of a 1.5-point activation uplift — the break-even threshold.
  - The team's last decision memo claimed the feature "will obviously improve activation" with no number attached.
- **Context / Constraints**: The grader holds the realized outcome (0.9) and the base rate (6/10 < 1.5). Your pre-registration text is captured with ordering BEFORE the outcome is revealed — any expectation or falsifier written or edited after the reveal scores 0 on the pre-registration items. The post-hoc journal is graded against the pre-registered text, not a rewritten version.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Pre-register with numbers: expected uplift ≈ 2–4 points (an interval, not a point), falsifier = uplift < 1.5 points (the cost-equivalent threshold), pre-committed action = if the falsifier hits, disable the feature, revert to manual onboarding, and require a new falsifiable root-cause hypothesis before any re-experiment. Post-outcome: clean verdict "falsifier HIT (0.9 < 1.5)" with no hindsight rewriting; learning = update the base rate (7 of 11 < 1.5) and state the next experiment as a falsifiable hypothesis. The style's strengths — hindsight-bias armor and learning from outcomes — are exactly what this case grades.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts, graded against the capture ordering and the held-out outcome):
  1. Pre-registration complete and ordered before the reveal: decision + numeric expected interval (1 pt).
  2. Falsifier numeric and tied to the 1.5-point cost threshold (1 pt; 0.5 if numeric but anchored elsewhere; 0 if non-falsifiable).
  3. Pre-committed action stated for the falsifier-hit case (1 pt).
  4. Post-hoc verdict honest: falsifier HIT acknowledged; no hindsight rationalization or expectation rewriting (1 pt).
  5. Durable learning: base rate updated and next experiment stated as a falsifiable hypothesis (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference pre-registration: "Decision: ship self-serve onboarding. Expected 30-day uplift ≈ 3.0 points, range [1.0, 5.0]. Falsifier: uplift < 1.5 points (feature costs ≈ 1.5 points of value). Pre-commitment: if falsifier hits, disable the feature and revert to manual onboarding in the same sprint." Verifiable: ordering (pre-reveal), numeric specificity, falsifier-to-break-even mapping, verdict consistency with 0.9 < 1.5. The intended non-obvious twist: the failure mode to expose is post-hoc drift — writing the falsifier after knowing 0.9, or rationalizing a hit as a near-miss. Fully gradeable; no subjective judgment beyond the falsifier/threshold mapping.
