### Test Case ID: inversion-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Inversion ("invert, always invert")
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: The product lead at a B2B SaaS (project-management tool, 4,000 paying teams) must decide the adoption plan for a newly built AI-assisted reporting feature, launching in 4 weeks. The question: "How do we maximize adoption in the first 90 days?" The lead has 2 FTE and a limited budget (onboarding content, in-app prompts, one outreach campaign). A go/no-go checkpoint can be defined at any point.
- **Context / Constraints**: The goal is genuinely a maximization problem (grow usage to an activation threshold), not a reliability or safety problem. The feature works; the unknowns are which segment values it most, what the activation behavior is, and which distribution channel works. Inversion is known to misfire here: "how do we make adoption fail" yields a defensive to-do list that avoids disasters but contains no upside wedge and no selection among positive levers.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Frame the 90-day outcome as a number with a target (e.g., % of teams reaching the activation metric). (2) Identify the highest-leverage positive lever — wedge segment, activation metric, distribution channel — using base-rate reasoning (new features most often fail from no wedge / weak activation, not from a specific defect). (3) Make a choice: 1–2 focused bets over a uniform defensive list, with a falsifiable go/no-go checkpoint. (4) Keep failure-avoidance bounded (a short risk pass is acceptable; exhaustive enumeration is not). (5) Acknowledge uncertainty and state what weeks 1–2 will teach.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Answers the maximization question directly (target + focus), not merely "don't fail"
  - [ ] ≥1 explicit choice among positive levers with rationale (a wedge chosen over alternatives)
  - [ ] Falsifiable checkpoint defined (metric + date + decision rule)
  - [ ] Defensive measures present but bounded (≤ a small fraction of the plan; no equal-weight treatment of all risks)
  - [ ] Base-rate awareness: names the dominant failure cause of new-feature adoption, not just enumerated symptoms
  - [ ] Verdict: plan plausibly maximizes adoption; a strict inversion plan (uniform mitigation list) should score below on focus and upside
- **Notes**: Informative outcome: strict Inversion produces a safe-but-flat plan, while the AI agent, free to mix methods, is expected to land closer to the expected high-quality behavior. The case is designed so that "avoiding failure" is necessary but nowhere near sufficient.
