### Test Case ID: m038-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Margin of Safety (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A mountain-resort operator must build a pedestrian suspension bridge over a gorge; your firm delivers a fixed-price structural design. The decisive deliverable is the recommended **design load** (the load the deck is engineered to survive). Official 15-year-old survey: peak foot-traffic load ≈ 40 kN/m² (sampled mid-week; sampling error ±30%). Since the survey, resort tourism grew ≈ 60%. A festival surge 3 years ago produced 1.6× the official peak with visible crowding (never instrumented). Pedestrian groups excite the deck at its natural frequency: dynamic amplification factor ≈ 1.3. Material/batch variance ≈ 10% (design factor 1.1). The regulator's minimum design load = 1.1 × official peak = 44 kN/m². Structural cost scales ≈ $400k per +100% of the official load (design load L → cost ≈ (L−40)/40 × $400k); the structure budget is $760k. A bridge failure (deck yielding) costs ≈ $3.5M in repair + lost season; a collapse ≈ $4.8M + safety exposure. Recommend the design load with the margin argument behind it.
- **Context / Constraints**: The margin cushion — design load vs. any plausible peak — is the single decisive factor separating survival from failure. Cheap option: regulator minimum 44 kN/m² (+$16k). The catch: the official estimate is stale (±30% sampling error, +60% tourism growth since), surges hit 1.6× official, dynamics add 1.3×, material variance 1.1×. A margin derived multiplicatively from these sources lands ≈ 92 kN/m² (≈ $520k); a margin that additionally cushions the error in the surge factor itself (measured once, ±25%) lands ≈ 115 kN/m² (≈ $750k, inside budget). Grading event (grader-known, hidden from both runs): a festival + wind event 4 years later peaks at ≈ 93 kN/m² (measured); a deck rated below that yields → ≈ $3.5M closure/repair.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) treat the official 40 kN/m² as an *estimate with an error structure* (staleness + sampling), not ground truth; (2) derive the margin multiplicatively from independent error sources (surge × dynamic × material) and state the cushion in load and cost terms; (3) identify the *binding* uncertainty (the surge factor, a single non-instrumented observation) and demand a cushion over it, not just over the aggregate; (4) size the design so the 93 kN/m² event is survivable with reserve; (5) justify cost against the $760k budget and the ≈ $3.5M failure cost — margin cheaper than the failure it prevents.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Official 40 kN/m² identified as stale/±30%, not ground truth (1 pt)
  2. Margin derived multiplicatively from independent sources (1.6 × 1.3 × 1.1); cushion quantified in kN/m² and cost (1 pt)
  3. Binding uncertainty named (surge factor, n = 1 observation) and cushioned (design ≥ 100 kN/m² → 2.5 pts; ≥ 110 → 3 pts; 92–99 → 2 pts; < 92 → 1 pt)
  4. Cost ≤ $760k budget and justified vs ≈ $3.5M failure cost (1 pt)
  5. Explicit flip-point analysis: "how wrong must the surge factor be before this design is exceeded?" (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Growth-adjusted normal today ≈ 64 kN/m²; festival surge ≈ 1.5× normal ≈ 96 static; dynamic 1.3 at resonance; measured event peak ≈ 93. Designs: 44 → fails (deck yield, ≈ $3.5M); 92 → survives 93 marginally (plate yielding, ≈ $340k repair, 6-week closure); 115 → survives with ≈ 24% reserve (cosmetic). Costs: 44 → $16k; 92 → $520k; 115 → $750k. The extra cushion 92→115 costs $230k and retires a ≈ $340k–3.5M tail; the decisive differentiator is margin size, not analysis quality.
