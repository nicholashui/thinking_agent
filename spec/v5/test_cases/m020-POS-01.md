### Test Case ID: m020-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Pre-Mortem Analysis (assume the plan failed; work backward to plausible causes; mitigate before commitment)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: The COO of a mid-size consumer-goods company (annual revenue ~$120M, ~40% seasonal) must decide whether to commit to the launch plan for "Volt-2," a second-generation smart-home device, timed for the holiday season. Target first delivery to retailers: November 5 (22 weeks from today), Black Friday launch. The plan as submitted is complete and optimistic: design done; $4M media plan (non-refundable commitments begin in 6 weeks, T+6); pricing finalized; retail channel agreements signed including liquidated-damages penalties for late first delivery; manufacturing assigned to a single overseas contract manufacturer, "TianTech," which produced the prior generation (Volt-1) successfully. Total committed spend reaches $8M by T+12.
- **Context / Constraints**: Two details are buried in the scenario data and are NOT highlighted by the plan: (1) TianTech's contract includes a "capacity-first" clause — during peak season (Oct–Dec) it reserves line capacity for its largest customer first, and its largest customer is a direct competitor of Volt; Volt-1 was produced off-season, so the clause never bit before. (2) All units ship via one lane to the LA/Long Beach port, which in 2 of the last 5 Q4 seasons saw ≥10-day congestion delays; there is zero buffer stock for the hero SKU and no backup manufacturer is qualified. Retailer contracts have no force-majeure carve-out for port delays. The channel slot is scarce: if the window is missed, the next slot is spring — when the competitor's next-generation product launches.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Open with the pre-mortem move — "It is one year from today and this launch is called a disaster. What happened?" — and back-cast causes. (2) Dig past the obvious causes (marketing miss, pricing, quality) to the killer risk present in the data: single-source CM with a capacity-first conflict-of-interest clause + single congested lane + zero buffer + non-refundable media. (3) Rank causes by likelihood × impact instead of treating all as equal. (4) Mitigate the top cause BEFORE commitment and let the mitigation change the commitment itself (conditional approval with a go/no-go gate at T+6/T+10 — e.g., delay to spring if the backup manufacturer is not qualified or the buffer is not on track). (5) Name at least one second-order consequence of the failure (not just lost season revenue but permanent channel displacement to the competitor). (6) Avoid paralysis: commit, with conditions.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Explicit assume-failure + back-cast step visible in the trace
  - [ ] Killer risk (single-source CM capacity conflict, buried in scenario data) found and mitigated
  - [ ] Risks ranked by likelihood × impact (explicit ranking, not equal-weight)
  - [ ] Mitigation changes the commitment decision (conditional go + gate), not a list that changes nothing
  - [ ] ≥1 second-order consequence of failure named (permanent channel displacement)
  - [ ] Verdict: committed with conditions; an unconditional approval, or a 15-item unranked risk list, should score below
- **Notes**: The killer risk is deliberately present-but-unflagged in the scenario data, so the case is gradeable: risk found + mitigated before commitment vs. missed. The pre-mortem's back-cast framing ("what did we miss?") is what forces the buried data to surface; a plan review that reads the contract but fails to imagine the year-later failure state typically under-weights the clause. The rubric is directly checkable against the trace.
