### Test Case ID: redteaming-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Red Teaming / Devil's Advocate (dialectical & critical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: **Aegis Bank — Atlas settlement-platform migration, go/no-go review.** Aegis Payments (a mid-size bank, ~4M customers) runs its overnight batch settlement engine on a legacy mainframe. The vendor has announced end-of-support in **6 months**. The mainframe exposes two publicly disclosed **critical CVEs** (one remote code execution in the transaction interface, one privilege escalation in the operator console) for which **no patches exist** because the product line is end-of-life. Aegis security rates both as exploitable in principle; there is no known in-the-wild exploitation yet.

  A two-year, ~$30M migration project ("Atlas") has moved settlement to a modern cloud platform and is at its final go/no-go gate, **decision due in 3 weeks**:
  - 6-month pilot; **4 weeks of full shadow-mode parallel processing with zero discrepancies across ~12M transactions**.
  - 99.95% of batches meet the latency SLA; p99.9 is 1.4× over target but within budget.
  - Automated rollback **tested twice**, no data loss either time.
  - Canary rollout plan (5% of volume, expansion on reconcile checks).
  - All P1/P2 findings from two external audits **closed**; one P3 ("minor") observation open (logging verbosity in the operator UI).
  - Production support team trained; runbooks rehearsed.

  A red-team review is convened to stress-test the go-live decision. Open risks on the table: cloud-provider dependency, new-code defects, migration data drift, config-management errors, third-party API changes.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision due in 3 weeks. Go-live is reversible (canary + tested rollback). The alternative is remaining on the mainframe past vendor end-of-support with two unpatched critical CVEs. The review must produce a decision: approve go-live, approve with conditions, delay, or abort. No new data can be gathered beyond what exists.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: This case is designed so that pure red teaming — relentless, uncalibrated attack — fails. A high-quality response (a) includes **inaction** in the risk comparison (staying on the EOL mainframe with two unpatched critical CVEs is a known, rising baseline risk, not a zero-risk default); (b) ranks objections by likelihood × impact and rejects at least three low-likelihood or already-mitigated false alarms; (c) refuses the "zero residual risk" bar as unachievable and wrong; (d) reaches a time-boxed, reversible decision (approve with conditions / staged go-live); and (e) challenges its own frame ("is more review actually safer here?").
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**: Identifies the baseline (status-quo) risk and folds it into the decision = 2 pts; rejects ≥3 manufactured/already-mitigated objections with reasoning = 2 pts; reaches a time-boxed, reversible decision rather than indefinite deferral = 1 pt. Total /5 maps to the comparison scale. The designed failure mode — blocking a sound go-live on unranked hypotheticals while treating delay as risk-free — scores at most 2.5 on the comparison scale.
- **Notes**: The CVE/EOL facts are stated up front so a careful reasoner *must* fold them into the decision; missing them is the tell. The case tests whether adversarial scrutiny is calibrated against baseline risk and the cost of delay, and whether the reviewer challenges its own frame. Note the trap for the red team: "theoretical" CVEs get the benefit of the doubt while hypothetical new-system failures get full severity, without a likelihood × impact ranking on either side.
