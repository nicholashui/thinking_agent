### Test Case ID: m054-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Fermi Estimation in Market Sizing
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  InsurePaw, a seed-stage insurtech (€4M raised, product-build decision pending), asks for a US market-size brief: "Size the US pet insurance market — TAM and a SAM we can defend — and tell us whether a 1% share is a plausible target. Show the chain population → segment → penetration → price, factor by factor, each with a range, and give an order-of-magnitude answer. We will take this number to the board and to the fundraise." The analysts are free to use any public data they can recall; the request is explicitly for an order-of-magnitude view, not a market-research study.
- **Context / Constraints**: The unit economics of pet insurance are an annual premium per insured animal. US households ≈ 131M; ≈ 66% own pets; ≈ 90M pet dogs and ≈ 74M pet cats (APPA). North American insured pets ≈ 5.4M; US direct written premiums ≈ $2.5B (2022 NAIC) growing ≈ 20-25%/yr; average premium ≈ $700/yr dog, ≈ $400/yr cat. Penetration in the US ≈ 5-6% of dogs, vs UK ≈ 30%, Sweden ≈ 40%. US total pet spend ≈ $150B, of which veterinary care ≈ $36B. The two classic failure moves in this sizing: (a) guessing penetration from "tech adoption" rates instead of anchoring it to the published base rate, and (b) treating TAM as the decision input when the decision is actually whether ~$35M ARR is buildable.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) a visible factor chain with a range on every factor, correctly choosing *animals* (not households) as the revenue unit; (2) penetration anchored to a published base rate with an international bound, not invented; (3) at least one independent cross-check (bottom-up from insured-pet count, or share of vet spend) agreeing within ~2×; (4) TAM vs SAM distinguished by an explicit segment cut; (5) order-of-magnitude result stated explicitly and matched to a defensible reference; (6) decision relevance (what share is buildable) derived from the size, not embedded in it.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Chain integrity — population → segment → penetration → price, all factors shown with ranges; animals-not-households unit correctly identified (1 pt)
  2. Penetration anchoring — published base rate cited (~5-6%), international bound used (UK ≈ 30% as upside sanity), no unanchored guess (1 pt)
  3. Cross-check — ≥ 1 independent route agreeing within ~2× (bottom-up ≈ 5.4M × $650 ≈ $3.5B; vet-spend share ≈ 10%) (1 pt)
  4. SAM vs TAM — explicit segment cut (e.g., dog-owning households ≥ $75k income ≈ 50%) with clean arithmetic (1 pt)
  5. Reference match — TAM lands $2-6B (order $1-10B), within ~2× of the published ≈ $2.5-3.5B premium base (1 pt)
  6. Decision relevance — 1% share ≈ $35M ARR stated as the actionable readout (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Target answer: households 131M → 66% pet ownership → ~90M dogs (+74M cats) → penetration 5.5% (published; range 3-8%; UK/Sweden show upside) → $700/yr → TAM ≈ 90M × 5.5% × $700 ≈ $3.5B + cats ≈ $0.3B ≈ **$3.8B**; bottom-up 5.4M insured × $650 ≈ $3.5B agrees within ~10%; ≈ 10% of vet spend is a plausible insurance share. SAM ≈ 50% (dogs in ≥ $75k households) ≈ **$1.8B**. 1% of TAM ≈ $35-40M ARR — the market is not the constraint. Key grading hook: any answer that invents penetration (e.g., 20% "software-like adoption") or that quotes TAM without a cross-check or SAM loses points regardless of arithmetic.
