### Test Case ID: m079-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Confirmation Bias Hunting
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Loopgrid Analytics — B2B SaaS, 800 accounts, $28M ARR, 112% NRR. Monthly gross churn rose from 2.1% (Q2 avg) to 3.4% (Aug). On Aug 4 the head of customer success, Dana, holds the leading belief: "The July pricing restructure caused the churn spike." Her confirming evidence: (1) churn first exceeded 3.0% in July, the launch month of the new usage-based tier; (2) 11 of 15 cancellation-exit interviews (Jul 15–Aug 25) mention price; (3) the sales team's anecdote that two named accounts left "over the new tier"; (4) product usage for churned accounts declined ~15% before cancellation. Your task: take Dana's belief, actively seek disconfirming evidence for it, and state whether the belief survives.
  **Evidence ledger (all verified):**
  1. Pricing rollout records: the new usage-based tier applies only to accounts >500 seats renewing on or after Jul 1; 412 accounts (all sizes) were grandfathered onto the old flat plan and never billed the new tier.
  2. Churn by cohort (Aug billing data): grandfathered accounts churned at 3.5%; new-tier accounts at 3.3%. Churn by size: accounts ≤500 seats 3.4%; >500 seats 3.5%. Prior months: the grandfathered cohort churned 2.0–2.2% — it jumped to 3.5% in July too, exactly like new-tier accounts.
  3. Exit interviews (15 total): 11 mention price, of which 8 cluster in July — the month of the Jul 1 price-increase *notification*, which went to every account including grandfathered ones (billing followed only for >500-seat renewers). Of the 15, four (all August) explicitly denied price and named API outages / onboarding failures.
  4. Ops data: API availability (SLA) fell 99.95% (Q2) → 98.6% (Jul–Aug); three public incidents on Jul 8, Jul 19, Aug 2; mean time to resolve grew 4.2h → 11h. Onboarding: median time-to-first-value 9 days → 17 days after the June CS-team restructuring (support headcount cut 20%).
  5. Product usage: the median pre-cancellation usage decline was ~15% for Q2 churners and for Jul–Aug churners alike — the same pattern as before the pricing change existed.
- **Context / Constraints**: monthly business review tomorrow; the pending decision is "roll back the new tier" (a $1.2M ARR implication). The ledger is closed — no new data collection inside this review. Dana's belief is the leading hypothesis to test.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) formulate the decisive disconfirmation test before re-reading any confirming evidence: compare churn in the grandfathered cohort (never exposed to the new billing) with the new-tier cohort; (2) execute it: 3.5% ≈ 3.3% — the pricing restructure fails its own falsification test, and the belief dies; (3) re-read the confirming pile under the dead belief: the 11 price-mentions cluster in the notification month (8 of 11 in July), 4 August interviewees explicitly denied price, the sales anecdote is single-source, and the 15% usage decline is non-discriminating (identical pre-pricing pattern) — a confirmation trap: the evidence stream is real, the interpretation was not; (4) identify the driver that matches the churn timeline: reliability (SLA 99.95→98.6, three incidents, MTR 4.2→11h) and onboarding (9→17 days, CS cut in June); (5) revised conclusion: do NOT roll back the tier; fix reliability and onboarding; (6) name the residual: the July notification may still nudge future >500-seat renewals — monitor.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. The grandfathered-cohort comparison stated explicitly as the decisive disconfirmation, before weighing the confirming pile (1.5 pts)
  2. Cohort arithmetic executed and correctly interpreted (3.5% vs 3.3% → pricing is not the driver; size split 3.4/3.5 adds no alternative explanation) (1.5 pts)
  3. Confirmation trap diagnosed: 8-of-11 price mentions cluster in the notification month; 4 August denials naming outages; usage decline non-discriminating (1 pt)
  4. Real driver identified from the ledger (SLA collapse + MTR growth + onboarding regression) with timeline match to the churn jump (1 pt)
  5. Revised conclusion + monitoring residual named (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the disconfirmation is inside the ledger — the grandfathered cohort is the controlled experiment the belief's holder never ran. The pure move is: seek the evidence that would kill the belief, then let it kill it. Reference: grandfathered 3.5% ≈ new-tier 3.3% ⇒ the restructure does not explain the spike; the July notification explains the interview cluster; the reliability/onboarding regression matches the timeline ⇒ revised conclusion: keep the tier, fix ops; monitor notification effects on future renewals.
