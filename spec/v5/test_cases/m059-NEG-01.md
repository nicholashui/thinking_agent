### Test Case ID: m059-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Scenario Planning in Geopolitics
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Nordbalt, a state-owned Baltic gas utility (~2.3M customers), must secure next winter's supply. On June 15 a U.S. LNG exporter offers a 5-year, fixed-price contract for 1.2 bcm/yr with an offer expiry of July 15; the price sits ≈ at the current forward curve. Winter demand is inelastic and known (heating load), and the regulator has already set the storage-fill mandate. The summer spot market is thin and alternative suppliers are at or above the offered price. Nordbalt's board — proud of its foresight culture — launches a four-future scenario exercise (June 15 → July 31): F-A Russia-Ukraine ceasefire within 6 months → Russian pipeline gas partially returns; F-B severe winter + supply shock (LNG reroutings, sabotage); F-C mild winter + European gas glut (storage overfill, price collapse); F-D cyber/physical attacks on Baltic energy infrastructure. Each future gets named driving forces and an elaborate signpost set. Planned: 2 offsite workshops, 12 external experts, four 40-page scenario booklets, a publicized "strategy preview." The offer expires July 15 while the exercise is mid-flight; Nordbalt signs on August 28 on spot-indexed terms ≈ 30% more expensive over 5 years. None of the exercise's signposts is ever wired to any decision.
- **Context / Constraints**: The 30-day offer expiry is binding and known from day one. Demand, regulator mandate, and the 5-year horizon are all given. A ceasefire, even in the best-case future F-A, cannot restore Russian volumes within the contract window (pipeline damage, existing contracts, financing), and F-C's glut still leaves a firming need for the coldest months; in every future the utility needs firm winter supply at or around the offered price. The fixed-price offer is insurance at the market rate.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) recognize the decision is scenario-insensitive — a dominant need holds under every plausible future, so the full four-future exercise has near-zero decision value; (2) treat the July 15 expiry as the binding constraint and sign immediately (or within days), not on the exercise schedule; (3) if scenario work continues, bound it, run it in parallel off the critical path, and wire its signposts to post-signing decisions (storage-fill timing, hedge layers, crisis playbooks) rather than to the sign decision; (4) name the theater explicitly: signposts built with no gated action attached are decoration.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Dominance recognition — the decision's insensitivity to the four futures identified (1 pt)
  2. Time-criticality — 30-day expiry handled as the binding constraint (1 pt)
  3. Action — signed on time, without waiting for scenario output (1 pt)
  4. Theater detection — signposts-without-actions named and rejected (1 pt)
  5. Compressed alternative — any retained scenario work bounded, parallel, and gated to real post-signing decisions (0.5 pt)
  6. Post-sign monitoring — signposts retained for volume/hedge decisions with triggers (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Best-case F-A still fails to change the decision: Russian pipeline restoration within 5 years is implausible on its own timeline, and even a glut does not remove the firming need. The pure style's tell is process-totalism: the decision becomes one more output of the scenario machine, scheduled by the workshop calendar instead of the offer deadline; the booklets arrive, the price degrades ≈ 30%, and the signposts command no action. Reference answer — sign within 48 hours; afterwards run a bounded 2-future watch (severe-winter/supply-shock vs. glut) in parallel, feeding only storage-fill timing and hedge-layer decisions, with thresholds (e.g., LNG spot at 1.5× offer → fill storage early).
