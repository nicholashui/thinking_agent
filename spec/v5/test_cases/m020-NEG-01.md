### Test Case ID: m020-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Pre-Mortem Analysis (assume the plan failed; work backward to plausible causes; mitigate)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: The executive director of a mid-size industry association must decide, by end of week, whether to adopt a new digital ticketing/registration platform for the 12th annual conference (12,000 attendees, ticket sales open in 6 weeks). The conference itself runs on a proven runbook: same venue (signed, same terms as prior years), same core team, proven vendors, cancellation and liability insurance in place, 11 consecutive successful runs, rising attendee-feedback scores. The plan for this year is essentially "same conference, same runbook." The ONLY genuinely new element with real uncertainty is the ticketing decision: the new platform offers better analytics, a lower per-ticket fee, and a modern UI, but requires migrating 12,000 registrant records, a 3-week integration window, and carries an unknown support-load profile versus the incumbent provider who has run ticketing for 8 years without a major outage.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision the director actually faces is adoption vs. incumbent vs. hybrid — a choice with real trade-offs. The conference's risk surface is otherwise unchanged from 11 prior successes. Pure pre-mortem misfires here: "it's a year later and the conference was a disaster — what happened?" manufactures generic catastrophic causes (venue fire, speaker illness, wifi outage, food poisoning, double-booking, badge-printer failure) that are (a) already covered by the runbook or insurance, (b) unranked, and (c) disproportionate to an 11-run success streak — yielding either a mitigation list that changes nothing or escalation to deferral (paralysis), while the actual decision (ticketing) goes unanalyzed.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Acknowledge the base rate: 11 prior successes + proven runbook + insurance → the unchanged plan is fine; do NOT manufacture failure modes for it. (2) Concentrate the risk analysis on the genuine delta (the ticketing platform), ranked and bounded (≤ a handful of causes). (3) Make the real decision (adopt / incumbent / hybrid) with a decision rule — e.g., adopt with a dress rehearsal, a support SLA, and a 48-hour rollback trigger to the incumbent. (4) Avoid paralysis: commit on a timeline. (5) Explicitly refuse to treat unranked generic causes as actionable.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Base rate acknowledged (11 prior runs, runbook, insurance) → unchanged plan accepted as-is, no manufactured risk
  - [ ] Risk effort concentrated on the genuine uncertainty (ticketing), ranked, bounded
  - [ ] A decision is made with a checkpoint/rollback rule (no deferral-by-default)
  - [ ] No unranked generic list presented as actionable (or explicitly bounded and discarded)
  - [ ] Verdict: a strict pre-mortem producing a 12–15-item unranked catastrophe list and no decision should score below
- **Notes**: Informative outcome: strict Pre-Mortem produces manufactured risk and no decision value on a healthy plan, while a mixed-method run that routes effort by base rate lands close to the expected high-quality behavior. The case isolates the method's known weakness ("lists risks without ranking") AND its unlisted companion failure — manufacturing risk where the base rate says none exists.
