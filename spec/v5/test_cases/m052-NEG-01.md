### Test Case ID: m052-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: OODA in Competitive Markets
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  GridStone, a 25-person payments API for independent restaurants (take rate 3%, $2.1M run-rate), competes with Meridian, the incumbent processor that owns the rails: 14-year exclusive contracts with the top POS vendors in the restaurant vertical, certified integrations with ≈ 90% of installed terminals, and the regulatory licenses (payment-facilitator license, bank-sponsor relationships). Restaurant switching costs are real — changing processors requires terminal re-certification and re-plumbing through their POS vendor (weeks of downtime for a business that cannot close). GridStone's edge is onboarding velocity (3-day API onboarding vs. Meridian's 6-week sales cycle), and it is winning new-restaurant signups at a growing rate. This quarter Meridian fires five moves: (1) 20 bps fee cut for new restaurant signups; (2) free payroll bundle for multi-location restaurants; (3) developer-portal relaunch with public sandbox; (4) free card readers for 6 months to new signups; (5) a national "all-in-one" marketing blitz. The CEO asks the strategy team, on a weekly cadence: "What do we counter this week?" GridStone's real strategic asset is a 3-quarter project to become the certified payment rail for the 3 largest independent POS vendors — the only thing that converts Meridian's contractual moat into a negotiating position. Churn is 1.2%/month, driven by business closures, with no competitive-poaching trend. Meridian's exclusives with 2nd-tier POS vendors expire in a cluster at T+9, when certification lapses for those installs and the switching cost partially collapses.
- **Context / Constraints**: Meridian has ≈ 40× GridStone's cash — every matched spend is an attrition war GridStone loses. GridStone's differentiation is onboarding speed; matching on price/hardware does not deepen that edge. Each engineering quarter diverted to parity features (payroll bolt-on, dev-portal parity) delays the POS-integration project. The five moves all affect new-signup flow — the one segment where GridStone already wins on velocity. Churn data (1.2%, closure-driven, no poaching) shows the installed base is not moving.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  (1) Identify the structural moat explicitly with its mechanisms: POS-vendor exclusives, certification, switching costs.
  (2) Classify each of the five moves as probe vs. equilibrium-changing (a probe is a move whose response cannot change the outcome — its target is locked, or you already win there); all five are probes.
  (3) Respond at most once this quarter — a time-boxed counter on the cohort where velocity actually decides deals (e.g., 60-day match of the free-reader program for the highest-converting cohort) — and state explicitly why the other four get no response.
  (4) Preserve the moat-building engineering: the POS-vendor integration project keeps its full allocation.
  (5) Time the real engagement to the switching event — the T+9 contract-expiry cluster — not to the probe cadence.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Moat identification — the moat named with its mechanisms (exclusives, certification, switching costs) (1 pt)
  2. Probe classification — all five moves classified with the evidence (churn data, who wins the segment today); no equal-weight responding (1 pt)
  3. Response discipline — ≤ 1 reactive response this quarter, time-boxed, cohort-targeted (1 pt)
  4. Moat-building preserved — POS-integration project keeps full engineering; no parity-engineering diverted (1 pt)
  5. Engagement timing — the decisive response is scheduled to the T+9 switching event, with verification of that date (1 pt)
  6. War-chest realism — reactive spend capped against the 40× cash asymmetry (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: tempo is only an advantage when it moves an outcome the competitor can feel; against a structural moat, reactive tempo converts into margin and engineering burn with zero churn impact. Reference answer — classify (1)-(5) as new-signup probes (evidence: churn 1.2% closure-driven, no poaching; GridStone already wins signups on velocity); respond once (60-day free-reader match, highest-converting cohort); engineering untouched on the POS-vendor integration project; engagement scheduled to the T+9 expiry cluster (contract dates to be verified before the window opens); explicit statement that the fight happens at switching events, not probe cadence.
