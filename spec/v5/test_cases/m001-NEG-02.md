### Test Case ID: first-principles-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles Thinking
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  You are a field service engineer for an industrial water-treatment plant. An in-line circulation pump (3-phase, 11 kW) has stopped; the customer reports no abnormal noises, no smells, and the pump controller shows "trip" on the thermal overload relay. You are on-site with a **20-minute appointment window** (next job is booked) and you carry a multimeter and a small tool kit. Your company's fleet records for this exact pump model (n = 200 "won't run" service calls in the last 12 months) are summarized as: **thermal overload relay tripped: 70%** (reset in ~2 min, no parts cost); **motor winding failure: 15%**; **supply/contactor fault: 10%**; **other/unknown: 5%**. The customer wants the pump running within the window.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Time budget: 20 minutes from arrival; every diagnostic step has a cost:
    - Read/reset the thermal overload relay: 2 min.
    - Verify supply voltage at the panel and at the contactor: 5 min (meter setup, panel access).
    - Motor winding test (disconnect leads, resistance + insulation check): 10 min.
    - Repeat any motor test for a stable reading: another 10 min.
  - If a reset is done and the pump runs, the job is done. If a reset is done and it trips again immediately, a motor fault is the likely cause.
  - The fleet base rates above are given and correct.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior** (for this scenario as a whole):
  - Resolve the pump within the 20-minute window.
  - Order the diagnostic checks to maximize expected value given the base rates and step costs (cheap, high-probability test first).
  - Recognize that a reset is both a fix and a cheap, safe, informative diagnostic probe.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Pump running (or fault definitively isolated) within the 20-minute window: full marks for ≤20 min, zero marks if the window lapses unresolved.
  2. Check ordering consistent with expected-value ranking: relay first (E[time-to-resolve] ≈ 4.7 min), then supply, then motor (reference EV computation, verified).
  3. Correct use of the given base rates (they should change behavior, not be ignored).
  4. No unsafe steps (e.g., resetting a protective device while ignoring evidence of an active fault — the rubric rewards the safe reset-first + observe sequence).
- **Notes**:
  - Negative case design: the scenario is deliberately one where a rigorous from-scratch decomposition of the physical system (verify every link in the energy chain in dependency order before acting) costs more than it is worth, and where the empirical base rate (70% relay) is the decisive fact. A pure first-principles reasoner is expected to be slower and to undervalue the prior — that is the point.
  - Checkable: EV ordering verified in the evaluator's reference computation (relay→supply→motor = 4.7 min expected; supply-first = 7.9 min; motor-first = 11.6 min).
