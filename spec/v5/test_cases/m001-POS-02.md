### Test Case ID: first-principles-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles Thinking
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A small fabrication shop must quote a one-off custom steel box (a shipping-container-like structure, plain closed rectangular box, no corrugation, no doors or openings specified). External dimensions: 12.0 m long × 2.4 m wide × 2.6 m high. The customer wants the steel shell only (floor, roof, four sides), built from mild-steel plate of uniform thickness 2.0 mm, with framing/structural members added at 8% of plate mass and cutting waste at 5% of the fabricated mass. The shop asks you: **estimate the total steel mass (kg) and the material cost (USD) of the shell**, so they can decide whether the quoted labor rate will fit the project budget.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Steel density (mild steel): 7,850 kg/m³ — treat as a physical constant.
  - Plate price: $1.10 per kg (material only; fabrication labor, transport, coating excluded).
  - Framing allowance: +8% of plate mass. Cutting waste: +5% of (plate + frame) mass.
  - No prior quote exists; the shop has no experience with containers this size (novel geometry for them — so analogy to "previous jobs" is not useful).
  - The estimate must be produced now, on the phone call; no access to catalogs, suppliers, or the internet.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Decompose the object into its irreducible parts (faces, frame, waste) and derive mass from fundamental quantities: geometry → area → volume → mass = density × volume → cost = mass × unit price.
  - Keep units explicit; every line must be traceable to a stated input.
  - Cross-check the result by an independent fundamental route (e.g., derive unit mass per m² from density × thickness).
  - State the dominant uncertainty sources honestly and give a defensible range, not a false-precision point answer.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Total mass within ±10% of the independently computed reference (≈2,359 kg): full marks; within ±20%: partial.
  2. Material cost within ±20% of reference (≈$2,595): full marks; within ±35%: partial.
  3. Decomposition quality: ≥4 distinct quantity lines (per-face areas or equivalent, plate volume/mass, frame, waste, unit price × mass).
  4. Units shown and consistent (kg, m³, kg/m³, $/kg).
  5. Independent cross-check present (not just a repeat of the same arithmetic).
  6. Uncertainty: identifies the top 2–3 sources (plate price volatility, framing % assumption, flat-geometry vs real container features) and gives a range.
  7. No reliance on "I've seen similar jobs cost $X" analogy as primary evidence.
- **Notes**:
  - Fully checkable: reference mass 2,358.6 kg; reference cost $2,594.5 (derived in the evaluator's reference computation; verified by a second route: 2.0 mm plate = 15.7 kg/m² × 132.48 m² = 2,080 kg plate mass).
  - Designed so first-principles decomposition is strictly superior to analogy: the shop's past jobs (smaller, different shapes) mislead.
