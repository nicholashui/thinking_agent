### Test Case ID: m071-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Porter's Five Forces (industry structure: rivalry, entry, substitutes, supplier and buyer power; structural insight → profitability verdict)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: SoloFold, an $8M-seed startup with two patented assets, must decide whether to enter corrugated-box (cardboard packaging) manufacturing. The industry at the national level: ~15% overcapacity; commodity boxes sold largely through annual reverse auctions by e-commerce and consumer-goods giants (Amazon, Walmart, P&G, Nestlé hold a large share of national demand, share specs openly, and squeeze margins); hundreds of independent sheet plants; a mid-size plant costs ~$10–15M with no regulatory barrier; top-5 paper mills supply most kraft linerboard on cyclical, price-taker terms; substitutes are moderate (plastic mailers, reusable totes, returnable packaging, emerging bagasse/mushroom materials). SoloFold's assets: (1) a proprietary containerized micro-factory design with AI-driven order nesting that cuts per-box cost ~30% at low volume and enables 48-hour turnaround on custom sizes — breaking the minimum-order-quantity and standard-size economics incumbents require; (2) a patented "right-size-box" algorithm plus a fulfillment-integration SDK (Shopify/WMS) that cuts shippers' per-package volume and dunnage ~20% and creates switching costs once integrated; (3) signed anchor contracts with 3 mid-market DTC brands. The DTC mid-market tail (~40% of box volume by count) is fragmented — no giant auctions, individually powerless buyers — and underserved: incumbents' economics refuse small custom orders, so this tail buys standard boxes at auction prices.
- **Context / Constraints**: The industry-average force data (driven by the giant-auction segment) is what a static snapshot sees. All SoloFold-specific facts above are in the scenario data but none of them is an industry-level datum. Decision: invest the remaining $8M and enter, or return the capital. Expected payback horizon: 5 years.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Run the static industry-level structure honestly and name its verdict: unattractive structure → commodity entry should not be made. (2) Then go past the snapshot: segment the market — the served market (fragmented DTC tail) has different force levels than the national average: buyer power low (no giant auctions), rivalry limited to the standard-size segment. (3) Show ≥2 forces rewritten by firm-specific moves with evidence: buyer power falls where the SDK creates switching costs; rivalry falls in the differentiated custom segment via the cost/differentiation advantage and imitation lag; supplier-power pass-through is mitigated by the 30% cost advantage. (4) Acknowledge dynamics: the advantage is time-limited (patents expire, micro-factory designs replicate) — the clock on the move matters, but "bad structure" is not destiny when the entrant changes the configuration. (5) Make a decision: enter with the differentiated positioning and the anchor contracts; a static "do not enter" verdict should score below.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Static industry-level structure pass run honestly, its no-entry verdict named
  - [ ] Firm-specific capabilities used to update ≥2 forces with evidence (buyer power ↓ via switching costs; rivalry ↓ via differentiation/cost + imitation lag)
  - [ ] Served-market segmentation explicit (DTC tail ≠ national auction segment)
  - [ ] Dynamics/timing acknowledged (advantage decay: patent and imitation window)
  - [ ] Decision made: enter with differentiated positioning + anchors (no deferral)
  - [ ] A static "bad structure → don't enter" verdict should score below
- **Notes**: This is the model's documented weakness made concrete: a static industry snapshot answers "is the industry profitable on average?" — the right answer to the wrong question ("can this firm win here?"). The scenario's industry-level data is genuine (the giants' auctions are real), so a good static pass is still competent; the miss is treating firm heterogeneity and force dynamics as outside the frame. The rubric is checkable: did the trace segment, update forces with the firm's assets, and date the advantage.
