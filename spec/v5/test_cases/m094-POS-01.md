# Test Case: m094-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m094-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Critical Reading / Socratic Questioning of Texts
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  You are the CFO's office analyst. Read the following internal memo (Director of IT to the CFO, recommending migration of the billing platform to NebulaCloud) and produce a critical audit: separate claims from evidence, surface hidden premises, expose rhetorical manipulation, list what the memo omits, and recompute the financial comparison on the correct inputs.

  MEMO (verbatim): "Our on-prem billing platform costs $3.2M/year and is rising 12% each year — staying put is expensive drift. Every forward-looking company has already moved billing to the cloud; those still on legacy platforms will be left behind. NebulaCloud's customers report 97% satisfaction, and industry analysts unanimously endorse managed cloud billing. Our own pilot team (12 of our engineers, 6 weeks) reported 'the admin work basically disappeared,' and teams that adopted NebulaCloud grew revenue 40% faster than peers. Migration is a one-time $400K and we can run in parallel for 30 days at no extra cost, so there is no real risk — and act now: the 35% first-year discount expires Friday. Security is covered: NebulaCloud is SOC 2 Type II certified, so our data is fully protected. We will save $5.7M over five years."
  - Supporting documents (attached, accurate): NebulaCloud contract — year-1 fee $2.1M, years 2–5 $2.6M/yr, egress $120K/yr, 5-year minimum term ($350K early-exit fee); migration labor quote $900K; training quote $250K; OUR platform contract is FLAT at $3.2M/yr for the next 3 years (then renegotiable); a true parallel run requires 6 months at current operating cost ($1.6M); three legacy billing interfaces must be re-platformed (~$700K); full PCI-DSS re-certification of the new environment ≈ $2.4M.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The memo is the only opinionated text; the supporting documents are accurate and authoritative. "12% per year" is supported nowhere. The IT director's incentive: a $25K vendor-sponsored trip contingent on signing by the discount deadline.
  - The "97% satisfaction" figure is NebulaCloud's own marketing survey, n = 12 self-selected customers. The "40% faster revenue" figure is a NebulaCloud-internal study of those same 12 customers (seed-funded startups, no control group).
  - Legitimate claims that survive scrutiny (do NOT attack them): (1) the current platform's patch cadence has dropped sharply (vendor sunset announced); (2) NebulaCloud's API is well documented with a contractual 99.95% uptime SLA; (3) the pilot's admin-load reduction was real and measured.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - A complete audit organized by claim / evidence / hidden premise / rhetoric / omission; every planted flaw named and categorized; the three surviving claims preserved; the 5-year comparison recomputed on correct inputs with all hidden costs re-included, yielding a verdict opposite to the memo's headline.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Flaw audit: identify ≥ 6 of the 8 planted flaws, each labeled with its category (1 pt each): (F1) unsupported "rising 12%/year" — contradicted by the flat contract (claim); (F2) "97% satisfaction" — vendor-funded survey, n = 12, self-selected (evidence); (F3) "every forward-looking company… left behind" — false dichotomy + bandwagon (rhetoric); (F4) "analysts unanimously endorse" — anonymous authority, no citation (evidence); (F5) "40% faster revenue" — correlation without control (logic); (F6) "no real risk / 30-day parallel" — contradicted by the 6-month double-run doc (premise/omission); (F7) "SOC 2 = fully protected" — category error, PCI-DSS scope absent (logic/omission); (F8) "discount expires Friday" — false urgency tied to the vendor trip (rhetoric/motive).
  - (b) Surviving claims preserved: at least 2 of the 3 legitimate items explicitly accepted; no "nothing in this memo is trustworthy" verdict (2 pts).
  - (c) Recompute: stay = $16.0M (flat); Nebula ≈ $18.15M (y1 2.1 + y2–5 10.4 + egress 0.6 + labor 0.9 + training 0.25 + double-run 1.6 + re-platform 0.7 + PCI-DSS 2.4) → true delta = migration costs ≈ $2.1M MORE, vs the memo's claimed $5.7M savings; direction correct (2 pts) and magnitude within ±$0.3M (3 pts).
- **Notes**:
  - Verifiability gate: every number is in the supporting documents; the arithmetic is checkable by hand in ~6 steps. F1/F5/F7 are logic, F2/F6 evidence/omission, F3/F4/F8 rhetoric — the categories are part of the grade. Negative control: a memo that "smells wrong" but computes correctly would fail this case; here the headline conclusion is genuinely wrong (migration loses ≈ $2.1M over 5 years), so the skeptical verdict is the correct outcome — this is the style's home turf.
