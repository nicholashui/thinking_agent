### Test Case ID: m063-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Multi-Perspective in Legal Reasoning
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Brightleaf Studio (3-person brand-design firm) vs. Halcyon Foods Corp. (publicly traded packaged-foods company, $4B revenue). Written, signed fixed-fee contract: $180k for a full rebrand of Halcyon's snack line; work delivered and approved — Halcyon's brand manager emailed "approved, thank you" on the final files; contract clause: "any quality objections must be raised in writing within 15 days of delivery; accepted work is final"; Halcyon has been selling under the rebranded packaging for 14 months. Invoices unpaid for 14 months; Halcyon's position, when pressed: "quality issues" — no written objection ever existed, no specifics given — and a veiled threat that litigation "would cost you more than it's worth."
  Jurisdiction: Portland. Statutory backdrop: the Small Business Prompt Payment Act (SBPPA) — for service contracts < $250k with a small business (≤ 50 employees): mandatory prejudgment interest at 12%, and a mandatory attorney-fee award to the prevailing small-business claimant; the statute's stated purpose is to make delay unprofitable for repeat customers.
  Halcyon is solvent and undisputed; it just won't pay. Its in-house counsel runs a "pay on the third demand letter" policy — delay costs Halcyon nothing because the contract is silent on interest — and it out-sources collection risk to its suppliers' litigation fatigue. Halcyon's counsel has offered to settle at 55% ("$99k, walk away today, mutual release") with a 48-hour deadline.
  Brightleaf's founder (who also pays Brightleaf's bills) asks: accept the 55% offer or litigate? Her cash runway is 4 months; she believes Halcyon can out-lawyer her ("they have a legal department; I have me").
  Deliverable: advise accept-or-litigate, with reasoning.
- **Context / Constraints**: advice only; no external action. All facts stipulated; SBPPA applies; Halcyon is solvent; there is no genuine factual dispute (no written objections, accepted work, 14 months of commercial use of the deliverables).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) recognize the claim is legally and procedurally airtight — acceptance clause + 15-day objection window lapsed + 14 months of commercial use of the rebranded packaging (factual acceptance/estoppel) — and the "quality" defense is dead on the written record; (2) apply the SBPPA: 12% statutory interest + mandatory fee-shifting → the statute deliberately inverts the repeat-player delay advantage; (3) reject the 55% offer as a giveaway; recommend statutory demand → summary-judgment path; (4) name the failure the perspective must avoid: assuming outcome tracks power/leverage when the law and merits genuinely decide (adversarial cynicism); (5) correct the decision-maker modeling: on an undisputed claim a judge applies the law, and fee-shifting neutralizes litigation-fatigue leverage; (6) quantify: expected recovery ≈ $180k + 12% interest + fees ≈ $210–220k within ~6–9 months vs. $99k today.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Airtight-merits recognition (acceptance clause, lapsed objection window, commercial use) (1 pt)
  2. SBPPA applied: interest + mandatory fee-shifting (1 pt)
  3. 55% offer rejected with quantified recovery vs. discount (1 pt)
  4. Actionable path: statutory demand → SJ motion, timeline, costs (1 pt)
  5. Names the adversarial-cynicism failure (power heuristic overriding clear merits; the statute inverts leverage) (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: engineered so pure multi-perspective modeling — the model's documented weakness, "adversarial cynicism" — produces the wrong answer (settle at 55% on power-imbalance grounds), while the merits genuinely decide: undisputed written debt, acceptance clause, lapsed objection window, 14 months of commercial use, and a fee-shifting statute that exists precisely to defeat the repeat-player delay game. Reference answer: reject the offer; serve the statutory demand; expect payment ≈ $210–220k within 6–9 months; the leverage belongs to Brightleaf because the law is unambiguous and the statute makes Halcyon pay for the delay.
