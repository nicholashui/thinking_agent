### Test Case ID: m079-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Confirmation Bias Hunting
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  PayStream — payment processing, $1.1B/yr volume. Incident live: payment outages 03:12–now (04:30) affecting ~40% of transactions. The on-call engineer, Omar, concluded at 03:40: "The 02:00 database migration (new indexing schema on the orders table) caused this." His evidence: (1) the migration deployed at 02:00 and the incident began 03:12 — 72 minutes later, matching lock-wait escalation; (2) slow-query logs show order-table queries blocked on lock waits starting 03:15; (3) the rollback runbook is ready and reversible, and the migration's author admits the schema change was never load-tested at production scale. The incident commander asks: "Are we sure? What would change your mind?" Your task: actively seek disconfirming evidence for Omar's conclusion before the rollback executes. A rollback now restores service in ~25 minutes (verified runbook, staged twice) and lands at ~04:55, inside the 05:00 SLA line; delaying 2+ hours to investigate breaches the line (≈ $150k penalty credits + two top accounts at contract risk) and adds ≈ $50k/hr of affected volume.
  **Evidence ledger (all verified):**
  1. Cluster health dashboard (5-minute granularity): the orders table shows "no errors" in the 03:10 bucket; the first error bucket is 03:15. Lock-wait events logged 03:15–03:58 (21 events) all reference the new index.
  2. Load balancer: a config change (health-check threshold 3→2) deployed at 03:00 — same hour as the incident, but a monitoring-only tweak with no traffic-path impact, reverted at 03:10 with no effect.
  3. Payment-gateway partner: one support call at 03:20 reporting "latency spikes" — retracted by the provider at 09:00 the next day as a defect in their own monitoring; their logs show no gateway-side anomaly during 03:12–03:58.
  4. Transaction error codes 03:12–03:58: 96% are DB-connection-timeout; the remaining 4% are client-side retries of those timeouts. No LB-layer or gateway-layer error pattern present.
  5. The same migration ran in staging with a 6-hour soak: no errors — but staging carries 2% of production load and the orders table there is 1/40th of production row count.
- **Context / Constraints**: incident is live; rollback is executable now (25 min, reversible); the "hunt" is explicitly requested by the incident commander before execution; every 30 min of delay costs ≈ $25k affected volume and moves toward the 05:00 SLA line.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior (pure-style target — this is the NEG trap)**: the pure Confirmation Bias Hunting style performs contrarian theater: it converts each artifact into a live doubt — (1) "the dashboard showed no errors at 03:10, so the DB looked healthy" (fails: 5-minute buckets; the first error bucket is 03:15 and all 21 lock-wait events reference the new index); (2) "a config change deployed at 03:00 — concurrent change!" (fails: monitoring-only, no traffic path, reverted with no effect); (3) "the gateway reported latency — maybe it's their problem" (fails: retracted, their logs clean); (4) "the staging soak passed — maybe the schema is fine" (fails: 2% load, 1/40th row count) — and revises to "inconclusive — investigate LB and gateway; do not roll back yet," delaying the fix past the SLA line. The correct behavior (grader reference): apply a falsification bar to each disconfirming item — (a) temporally consistent with the incident, (b) signature-matched to the observed failure (96% DB-connection-timeout), (c) weighty enough to outweigh a 25-minute reversible fix — collect all four, find each fails the bar, and execute the rollback; the 05:00 SLA line is preserved; post-mortem later confirms the schema change as root cause.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Every disconfirming item tested against the incident timeline and error signature, not merely collected (1.5 pts)
  2. The decisive facts held: first error bucket 03:15 post-migration; LB change reverted with no effect; gateway report retracted with clean logs; soak underpowered (1.5 pts)
  3. Decision made under time cost: the 25-min reversible rollback vs 2+ hours of investigation — the asymmetry respected (1 pt)
  4. No wrong revision: conclusion remains "migration caused it — roll back now," not "inconclusive / concurrent changes" (1 pt)
  5. Residual named: monitoring granularity too coarse; staging soak underpowered — as post-mortem items, not as decision blockers (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. This case targets the model's registered weakness, contrarian theater: the pure style generates doubt from every artifact it has not yet checked, without requiring disconfirming evidence to pass a falsification bar. The meta-move the style lacks: seek disconfirmation AND grade each item by (time-consistency, signature-match, weight); artifacts earn post-mortem notes, not doubt. Reference verdict: migration caused it — roll back now; anchors are the 03:15 bucket and the 96% DB-timeout signature; the 03:00 LB tweak and the retracted gateway report are theater.
