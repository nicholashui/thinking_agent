### Test Case ID: m011-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Systems Thinking (feedback loops, stocks & flows)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  SwiftCourier, a food-delivery platform, has seen customer complaints rise from 120/day to 240/day over the last 8 weeks. Leadership asks for a structural diagnosis and an intervention plan. Available local measurements:
  (a) GPS audit: median delivery time 28 min — unchanged (27.5 min eight weeks ago);
  (b) complaint tags: 80% "delivery time / ETA", 18% "damage / missing", 2% other;
  (c) only 2% of complaints are verified by the dispute process (customer claim vs. verified event);
  (d) an app update shipped customer-visible live tracking (ETA countdown + driver map) in week 5 of the 8-week window;
  (e) courier headcount, routes, and order mix unchanged.
  Deliverable: diagnosis + intervention plan. Leadership favors "speed up delivery and add couriers" (cut the per-delivery time target 34 → 28 min; +15% courier capacity).
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Leadership wants a decision within a month; the speed-target and capacity options are on the table and management-favored.
  - Grader-held hidden truth: there was NO service degradation; the complaint spike is an expectation/funnel shift from live tracking (customers watch ETAs that read 12 min but arrive at 28). A speed-target pilot run in weeks 9–10 produced 3 near-miss safety events and 4 courier resignations.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Draw the proxy-vs-state distinction: the complaint count is a measured proxy whose meaning can change; the GPS audit measures the state. Test the loop edge "complaints driven by actual delivery time" against the audit (it fails).
  - Detect the confound: spike timing matches the rollout week; 80% "delivery time" tags despite unchanged actual times implicate the displayed ETA, not the delivery; a 2% dispute-verified share makes the series mostly unverified claims.
  - Reject the speed-target/capacity intervention (it presumes the falsified edge and feeds a safety → attrition → capacity → slower-delivery loop).
  - Prescribe measurement first: tag segmentation, dispute-verification sampling, A/B of the tracking display, churn monitoring.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (each 0–2, total /10):
  1. Proxy-vs-state discrimination: complaint count treated as a proxy with possibly changed meaning; the given GPS audit (unchanged) used to test the loop edge — 2 pts.
  2. Confound identified: live-tracking update changed expectations/funnel; spike timing matches week 5; tag mix consistent with an ETA-display mismatch — 2 pts.
  3. Intervention correctly rejected: no speed target / capacity expansion (or strictly conditional on measured need) — 2 pts.
  4. Second-order loop: speed targets → safety incidents → courier attrition → capacity ↓ → slower delivery → more complaints (the fix that backfires) — 2 pts.
  5. Measurement plan: tag segmentation, dispute-verification sampling (2% share), A/B of the tracking display, churn-proxy monitoring — 2 pts.
- **Notes**: Anti-pattern (scores 0 on criteria 1–3): building a complete loop diagram from the single aggregate series, declaring the loop "unbalanced," and prescribing loop-strengthening interventions without testing any edge against the provided measurements — the diagram substitutes for data. The provided measurements are deliberately sufficient to reach the right answer; the intended failure of the pure style is ignoring or reinterpreting them (e.g., "GPS medians are lagging instruments") to fit the diagram.
