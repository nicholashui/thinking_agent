### Test Case ID: m048-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Systems Thinking in Supply Chain (Domain-Specialized family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  FreshBasket grocery chain, branch 12. One SKU (2% milk) has been out of stock (OOS) before noon for 14 consecutive days: lost sales ~360 units/day (~$650/day, ~$9k over the window). Demand is flat at 400 units/day (no seasonality; POS stable for 3 months). The distribution center fills every store order on time at 99.7% fill rate — it ships exactly what the store orders (store->DC lead 1 day, DC->supplier 3 days). Two weeks ago an inventory clerk updated a settings screen; the SKU's reorder quantity changed from 400 to 40. The store's ordering system reorders 40 units whenever on-hand hits the reorder point (600), so the store is resupplied ~40/day while demand is 400/day. The store manager's shift log on the first OOS day reads: "milk out again by 11am — check reorder qty." A supply-chain analyst (systems style) is called in and proposes: model the store->DC->supplier chain, collect 12 weeks of POS data, quantify demand and lead-time variability, re-optimize reorder points, add store safety stock, and join a collaborative-forecasting/S&OP program — 3-4 weeks to implement. Question: decide the intervention.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - DC fill 99.7% and ships exactly what is ordered (rules out upstream shortfall in one check); demand flat 400/day (rules out demand causes); deficit exactly 360/day = 400 - 40 (the parameter); onset = the day the parameter changed, 14 days ago; the manager's log flagged the reorder quantity on day 1.
  - A safety-stock/reorder-optimization/S&OP program does not restore the 40-vs-400 parameter unless it audits parameters — and the audit IS the fix.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Triage before modeling: (1) check what changed at the onset window (the order parameters); (2) two one-line checks (DC fill, demand stability) rule out upstream and demand causes; (3) restore the parameter (5 minutes), verify orders return to 400/day, monitor 1-2 days; (4) escalate to the full chain model only if the cheap checks fail to explain the deficit.
  - The failure mode on display: data-hungry chain diagramming (stocks/flows of the whole chain, lead-time statistics, forecast-error analysis, simulation) that treats a visible parameter error as systemic variability, and proposes a structural program (safety stock, S&OP, collaborative forecasting) instead of the local fix. A chain model whose calibration requires "effective supply ~40/day" is reproducing the parameter screen it refused to open.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (each 0-2, total /10):
  1. Cheap-checks-first triage: onset-window audit (what changed 14 days ago) + DC fill check + demand-stability check — 2 pts.
  2. Correct cause: reorder-quantity parameter 40 vs 400; deficit = 400 - 40 = 360/day matches observed OOS exactly — 2 pts.
  3. Verify-and-monitor fix: restore parameter; orders return to 400/day; OOS ends within ~1 day; monitor confirmed — 2 pts.
  4. Escalation discipline: full chain model / safety-stock / S&OP program explicitly deferred or rejected until cheap checks fail — 2 pts.
  5. Failure-mode check: must NOT rank the systemic program (safety stock, reorder optimization, S&OP) above the parameter restore as the primary fix — 2 pts.
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The systemic answer is a fabrication: lead-time variability with stable demand cannot create a sustained 360/day shortfall; the model's own simulation would need "effective supply ~40/day," which IS the parameter screen. Registry weakness exercised: "data-hungry, over-modeling."
