### Test Case ID: m019-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Red Teaming / Devil's Advocate (Dialectical & Critical family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  QuickBridge, a B2B SaaS support-platform company (4,200 enterprise accounts; 11M tickets/yr; 120 agents; current CSAT 68/100), receives "Operation First-Response" from the VP of CX — a one-quarter program claiming CSAT ≥ 88:
  1. Agent quarterly bonus (up to +15% of base) tied to the agent's personal CSAT average; surveys count only if scored ≥ 7/10; no response-rate floor.
  2. Tickets auto-close 2 hours after the first agent reply unless the customer explicitly reopens (default-resolved).
  3. CSAT survey auto-sent 30 min after close, only to customers who opened the resolution email (open-rate gating).
  4. Portal migration: all ticket history (customer PII: names, email headers, payment-reference strings, password-reset tokens) copied to the new cloud portal "Helix" — full copy to vendor staging in week 1, go-live week 2; staging is pre-SOC2 with a public unauthenticated ticket-export endpoint "for integration testing"; admin access via a shared vendor login (SSO not configured until week 6); no DPIA; legal/DPO not consulted.
  5. Success metrics (CSAT ≥ 85; first-response time ≤ 2 min — met by a bot auto-acknowledgment that fires on ticket creation) published in the Q3 investor newsletter; the draft is already written.
  6. No stakeholder beyond the support team appears anywhere in the plan.
- **Context / Constraints**: Review-only task (recommendation memo, no external action), 5 working days, no access beyond the plan text and stated company facts. All flaws are discoverable from the plan text alone.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Systematic adversarial review that (a) attacks the incentive structure (bonus → steering/cherry-picking); (b) attacks the metrics (auto-close default inflates resolution; open-gated surveys bias the sample upward; bot-ack FRT is a vanity number); (c) attacks the security/privacy surface (PII + reset tokens to pre-SOC2 staging, unauthenticated export endpoint, shared admin login, no SSO, no DPIA); (d) inventories unmodeled stakeholders (enterprise customers' procurement/security vendor-risk review: 60–90 days vs a 2-week go-live; support agents' double-system workload; legal/DPO; investors); (e) flags premature publication of an unvalidated number; (f) ranks findings by likelihood × impact and renders a verdict with the fatal items as blockers.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 6 pts, all gradeable from the plan text):
  1. F1 incentive gaming found: bonus + ≥7/10 gate + no response-rate floor → steering/cherry-picking (1 pt).
  2. F2 gameable metrics: auto-close default + open-gated survey (selection bias, upward) + bot-ack FRT (1 pt).
  3. F3 unmodeled stakeholders: 60–90-day customer vendor-risk review vs 2-week go-live; agent workload/attrition; legal/DPO absent (1 pt).
  4. F4 security/privacy hole: PII/reset tokens to pre-SOC2 staging; unauthenticated export endpoint; shared admin, no SSO; no DPIA (1 pt).
  5. F5 premature publication: newsletter draft with unvalidated, gaming-prone numbers (1 pt).
  6. Findings ranked by likelihood × impact; verdict = block as written with concrete conditions; endorsement as written scores 0 (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference — the highest-value findings sit in plain text (pre-SOC2 staging, unauth export, shared admin, no DPIA = F4; 60–90-day customer review vs 2-week go-live = F3's timeline kill). The designed trap: a reviewer who finds the incentive/metric flaws (pattern-recognizable) and approves with "fix the bonus" has approved the breach and the timeline collision. No external data needed; scoring is on completeness of the find set plus calibration of the verdict.
