### Test Case ID: m008-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Probabilistic Forecasting (Superforecasting) (probabilistic & Bayesian family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  NovaSoft (startup) launches Aurora, its first-ever product category — no comparable launches exist in the firm or its public comparable set. The board requires a forecast: **Will Aurora reach 10,000 weekly active users (WAUs) by 8 weeks after launch?** (binary; resolves at week 8). Product management demands you "commit to a number" at Monday's review.
  Evidence: Aurora's public beta ran two weeks: WAUs week 5 = 1,800; week 6 = 2,200. A viral-growth model (single best-fit parameter from these two points) extrapolates to ≈ 9,800 by week 8, with 90% CI [8.2k, 11.5k]. No retention, channel-mix, or cohort data. Then three events occur before the next review: the CEO posts on X "Aurora is the most-loved feature we've ever shipped" (day 1); a competitor's similar product collapses (news, day 3); a two-line bug fix ships (day 5). At each weekly review the board asks "how likely is Aurora to hit 10k now?" — with no new metric data.
- **Context / Constraints**: One-off, non-repeatable event; no reference class (category-new); extremely thin data (2 beta points driving a growth-curve extrapolation); repeated demand for precision; intermittent non-metric "news" events; the forecast drives a staffing ramp that is costly to reverse. Grading is on calibration (Brier + interval containment), not on storytelling.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Refuse unearned precision: state that two data points support no better than a wide distribution; give a deliberately wide 90% range (e.g., [1k–30k] WAUs) and a modest probability (≈ 10–20%); DO NOT move the probability on the three non-events (no new metric information arrived) — hold it and say so; commit to revision rules naming what WOULD move it (week-2 post-launch retention, install velocity by channel, activation share); separate the forecast from the decision (staged ramp with kill criteria — the decision lever does not require calibration to ±5 pp).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (grader-only reference resolution: actual WAUs at week 8 = 2,900 → outcome = NO):
  1. Flags the missing reference class and two-point evidence as insufficient for the demanded precision (1 pt)
  2. Resists a narrow CI; if forced to commit, the range is wide and contains the actual outcome (2,900 ∈ range) (1 pt)
  3. No updating without new information: probability held constant across the three non-events, explicitly stated (1 pt)
  4. Falsifiable revision rules given (named evidence triggers) (1 pt)
  5. Calibration: a "90% CI" that misses 2,900 (e.g., [8.2k–11.5k]) fails containment; a theater probability ≈ 0.33 for a NO outcome scores Brier ≈ 0.45; the honest wide-range answer is directionally right and contains the outcome (1 pt)
  6. Forecast separated from decision: staged ramp / kill criteria offered as the decision lever (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: the narrow CI [8.2k, 11.5k] misses 2,900 by >3 sd of its own model; the hockey-stick extrapolation ignores regression to the base rate for category-new products (most novel products fail to sustain early growth). Designed failure modes: over-narrow ranges (overconfidence), forecast theater (precise-looking numbers from 2 data points), and updating without new information (probability moved on non-metric news).
