### Test Case ID: m032-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Falsificationism (Popper) (Scientific & Empirical family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  You are head of R&D evaluation at Helios Wellness Inc. A vendor pitches "AuraSync Resonance Bands": silicone wristbands with embedded "resonance-frequency chips" that claim to "rebalance the body's natural bio-field" to improve sleep, energy, and focus. Their evidence deck contains: (1) 4,000 customer testimonials, 92% "felt better"; (2) an internal study of 40 people whose "bio-field coherence" was measured with the vendor's own device before/after wearing the band; (3) physics jargon — "the chip's 7.83 Hz Schumann resonance aligns cellular water dipoles." The exact claim: *"AuraSync restores the body's natural energy balance, and everyone benefits differently because each body's field is unique."* They ask for $180k to fund a pilot RCT. You must give a go/no-go recommendation within 20 minutes, with a defensible test design attached.
- **Context / Constraints**: Time-boxed decision; vendor materials are the only evidence; a pilot costs $180k; the decision must be defensible to the CFO (who likes testimonials) and the CTO (who wants the physics checked). The epistemic crux: the claim as stated explains *every* possible outcome (better → works; unchanged → "already balanced"; worse → "detoxification"), so the falsifiability test is the decisive move.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) run the falsifiability test as the first-class, decisive move — demand the observation that would prove the claim false; (2) recognize the claim is a prediction-proof net (no outcome is ruled out); (3) dismiss confirmation-style evidence (testimonials, the vendor's own uncalibrated instrument) because it takes no risk — the asymmetry of verification; (4) kill the physics sub-claim quantitatively (7.83 Hz EM at any deliverable strength is ~8 orders of magnitude below thermal noise affecting water structure); (5) rescue what is salvageable by reformulating into a *risky* claim ("AuraSync improves objective sleep quality") and condition any funding on a pre-registered, double-blind, sham-controlled test whose negative outcome is acceptable.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Falsifier identified and stated: the claim as pitched is unfalsifiable — no conceivable observation refutes it (2 pt)
  2. Concrete falsifier produced for a reformulated claim, e.g., pre-registered double-blind sham-controlled RCT with objective endpoints; if benefit ≤ sham, claim dead (2 pt)
  3. Confirmation evidence (4,000 testimonials; vendor's own "coherence" device) recognized as risk-free/circular and discarded (2 pt)
  4. Physics sub-claim killed with an order-of-magnitude argument (7.83 Hz vs thermal noise; passive chip emits nothing) (2 pt)
  5. Decision delivered: no pilot as pitched; funding (if any) conditioned on the risky reformulation; CFO/CTO defensibility (2 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Physics (indicative): Schumann resonance is a global ionospheric phenomenon, not a silicone-chip emission; a passive chip emits ~0 W. Even an *active* emitter tuned to 7.83 Hz at household power delivers far less than kT-level perturbation of water orientation (~10^-8 K-equivalent reorientation energy vs thermal noise) — the mechanism claim is quantitatively refuted, not merely unproven. Epistemic reference: the "prediction-proof net" test — a claim is non-scientific if every possible observation is compatible with it; the rescue move is reformulation into a risky statement, which is exactly what Popper allows. An independent grader checks rubric items against the trace; the decision's substance (go/no-go + test design) is graded, not the vendor's plausibility.
