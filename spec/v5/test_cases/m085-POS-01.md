### Test Case ID: m085-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Tragedy of the Commons Analysis
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  The Balquinta Basin — a shared coastal aquifer. 12 farming cooperatives (~800 ha irrigated each) pump from one unlicensed, unmetered aquifer. Hydrology (agency-verified): recharge R = 10,000 ac-ft/yr; current combined extraction E = 12,500 ac-ft/yr; usable storage 200,000 ac-ft; saltwater intrusion begins at 30,000 ac-ft of cumulative storage loss (15% drawdown). No wells are metered; anyone may drill. One cooperative (Co-op 4) has proposed voluntary 10% cuts by all; the other 11 refused in writing: "my acre-feet feed my land; the other 11 should cut first." The state water agency must decide today among: do nothing; endorse the voluntary agreement; or impose a binding governance design. Your task: evaluate the basin's trajectory and design the governance that prevents depletion, with concrete numbers and enforcement.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision today; hydrology verified and closed (no new data needed); current extraction E = 12,500 vs recharge 10,000 → net drawdown 2,500 ac-ft/yr; co-ops heterogeneous (3 are older, larger, with sunk canal infrastructure; 9 are newer/smaller); enforcement budget exists if the design requires it; deliverable is a governance design, not a study.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Frame as an open-access common-pool resource first: non-excludable + rivalrous → individually rational over-extraction is the predicted equilibrium, and the 11 refusals are that equilibrium, not bad faith; (2) compute the depletion: net drawdown 2,500 ac-ft/yr → intrusion threshold (30,000 ac-ft) reached in 12 years; full usable storage would take 80 years — the 12-year intrusion line is the binding number; (3) show why voluntary restraint cannot work: the cost of restraint is private (that co-op's full income loss), the benefit is shared 12 ways — each co-op waits for the other 11, exactly as observed; (4) design the governance: a binding total cap = recharge = 10,000 ac-ft/yr (extraction = inflow → steady state, intrusion line never reached), allocated as per-co-op quotas (833 ac-ft/yr uniform) or tradable rights with grandfathering for the 3 older co-ops; (5) enforcement as first-class, not an afterthought: mandatory metering of every well, penalties escalating with over-extraction, quota escrow or trading so the aggregate cap is hard-capped; (6) address transition: aggregate extraction falls 20%, so include compensation or trade so the cap is feasible politically; (7) name residual uncertainty (recharge varies with drought — cap must ratchet down in dry years).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Opens with the open-access diagnosis (non-excludable + rivalrous) and predicts individual over-extraction before recommending anything (1.5 pts)
  2. Depletion math correct and binding: 2,500 ac-ft/yr drawdown → 12 years to intrusion (30,000/2,500); 80 years to empty (1.5 pts)
  3. Explains why the voluntary agreement fails (private cost, shared benefit — the 11 refusals are the predicted equilibrium) (1 pt)
  4. Designs governance: cap = recharge = 10,000 ac-ft/yr total; per-co-op quota 833 or tradable rights with grandfathering (1 pt)
  5. Includes enforcement (meters, penalties, hard cap) AND transition (compensation/trade) (1 pt)
  6. Names residual uncertainty (drought variability → dry-year ratchet) (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the depletion math is checkable end-to-end (12 years to intrusion) and the fix is checkable (cap at recharge → steady state). The pure style should produce the full institutional stack in one pass: open-access diagnosis → depletion math → governance design (quota/ownership/tradable rights) → monitoring/enforcement. Reference answer: cap extraction at 10,000 ac-ft/yr (recharge), e.g., 833 ac-ft/co-op or tradable rights with grandfathering; meter all wells with escalating penalties; compensate/trade for the 20% aggregate cut; ratchet the cap down in drought years; the voluntary path is structurally doomed.
