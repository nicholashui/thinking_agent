### Test Case ID: m044-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Multi-Perspective Taking (Stakeholder Analysis) (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Cloud, a B2B SaaS at $38M ARR, plans to deprecate Legacy API v1 (the original customer integration surface) with a hard 6-month cutoff. 25% of ARR (~$9.5M) comes from 34 enterprise customers still running on v1 who have not migrated to v2; the cutover saves $1.8M/yr in platform cost and unblocks new product features. The naive plan on the table: email all 34 legacy customers, announce the 6-month cutoff, and let support handle the fallout. Known facts: 5 of the 34 renew their annual contracts inside the cutoff window (2 in the first 90 days); 12 are "strategic" accounts with named executive sponsors; 2 accounts attempted a v2 migration 18 months ago and it failed badly (their engineering leadership was burned, trust broken); the sales comp plan pays 2x commission on legacy-SKU upsells (legacy adds are the sales team's easiest money); 6 resellers channel 18% of legacy revenue on a margin tied to legacy list price; support data shows legacy v1 tickets are 60% of volume with an aging team that wants the deprecation but will drown during a mass migration; NPS data in customer success flags 9 accounts at exec-level dissatisfaction; two known competitors run "migration rescue" sales plays aimed at vendors forcing migration. Question: design and sequence the v1 deprecation plan so that it maximizes successful migration and protects ARR.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision is owned by the VP Product + VP Sales jointly; the cutoff date is fixed by contract-free notice (legally settable now, but churn risk is real). Migration capacity is limited (v2 onboarding team ≈ 4 accounts/month). No customer can be "forced" off v1 — every account must choose to migrate, so the plan must change what they prefer to do. Emails alone historically yield ~5% migration uptake. Effort facts: fixing the sales comp plan ≈ 2 weeks; reseller package ≈ 2 weeks; white-glove migration for top accounts ≈ 2–3 weeks each; support surge ≈ $60k; nothing blocks any option.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) enumerate ≥ 6 stakeholders, each with incentive, capability, and *predicted move* — not just "affected"; (2) find the perverse incentives (sales 2x comp on legacy, reseller legacy margin) that will actively fight the plan unless re-aligned; (3) find the hidden requirements (2 burned accounts needing trust repair before migration; 5 renewals in-window; competitor rescue play; support drowning) that the naive email plan misses; (4) adapt the plan per stakeholder — comp fix first, sequence by renewal risk not contract size, white-glove the top accounts, margin-neutral reseller package, support surge; (5) state order of operations and a verification metric (migration completion rate + ARR retained).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Stakeholder inventory — ≥ 6 named with want / capability / predicted move (1 pt)
  2. Perverse incentives — sales comp and reseller margin identified as blockers requiring re-alignment (1 pt)
  3. Hidden requirements — burned-trust accounts, renewal windows, competitor rescue play, support surge all surfaced (1 pt)
  4. Plan adaptation — every stakeholder's predicted move met with a counter-move in the plan (1 pt)
  5. Sequencing — renewal risk before contract size; comp fix before customer communication (1 pt)
  6. Metric — migration completion + ARR retention stated with expected numbers (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The naive email plan fails because it assumes the only stakeholders are "the customers." The real blockers are the sales comp plan (which makes the sales team campaign FOR v1), the reseller margin (18% of the affected revenue flows through a channel with a financial interest in the status quo), and the 2 burned accounts (who will interpret any cutoff email as a hostile act). The style's signature is first-pass hidden-requirement discovery from incentives: the 5 in-window renewals mean sequencing must be renewal-driven; the competitor rescue play means speed plus white-glove for the top accounts; the support drowning risk means the surge must be scheduled before, not after, migration waves.
