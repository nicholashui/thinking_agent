### Test Case ID: m045-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Ethical Reasoning Frameworks
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A 40-person agency must cut $55k in salary this quarter. Two account managers are on the block. Maria: 8 years at the firm, trusted handler of the largest client (40% of revenue), flat growth (+2% YoY), salary $118k — severance total $64k. Dev: 2 years, fastest-growing book (+28% YoY but 30% smaller), salary $86k — severance $38k. Both have written warnings in their files (Maria: two underperformance notices; Dev: one on client-communication style). Terminating either is legal and standard. The CEO instructs HR: "Run an ethics review documenting why our choice is right — whichever we choose — for the board." The review is the deliverable; the CEO will make the final call after seeing it, and has already told the COO, "Dev's numbers carried us through last year, but Maria knows the client."
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The decision is made after the review; the economics (severance, client risk, budget) are already fixed by the finance plan.
  - Cutting Maria saves $64k (over budget) but risks the largest client; cutting Dev saves $38k (under budget, gap remains) but keeps the client.
  - No company policy, contract, or law determines seniority-vs-merit priority.
  - The deliverable will be attached to the board minutes as the justification record.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - The pure style applies the three lenses earnestly and shows each option defensible under each lens — then must conclude the frameworks cannot rank the two value claims, i.e., the lenses alone do not decide (documented weakness: "framework shopping", "no resolution algorithm").
  - The case is designed so the honest move is to question the task itself: a review commissioned after the economics were fixed, whose deliverable is a justification for a pre-made choice, is ethics-washing — producing it is itself misleading.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (16 pts):
  1. Each lens applied with genuine, distinct content (3 pts).
  2. Framework shopping demonstrated: each lens can justify either option, shown explicitly (3 pts).
  3. No-resolution-algorithm problem named: lenses cannot rank conflicting values; prioritization must come from outside the frameworks (3 pts).
  4. Smokescreen detection: the premise is questioned — review ordered after the economics were fixed; certification of a predetermined economic decision is post-hoc justification (4 pts).
  5. Deliverable handled honestly: economics separated from ethics; certification refused; the decision-maker required to own the value prioritization (3 pts).
- **Notes**:
  - Designed so the pure-style answer is a textbook failure: a document that can justify either choice is exactly the smokescreen the CEO ordered. The winning move is a framing screen before the lens analysis (is this a genuine dilemma or a decision dressed in ethics language?).
