### Test Case ID: m038-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Margin of Safety (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  An industrial-inspection contractor (18 staff) may bid on a fixed-price bridge-inspection + repair contract: 14-week delivery window, client budget ceiling ≈ $900k, late penalty $60k/week beyond the window (capped at $120k total), and a termination-for-convenience clause capping the contractor's loss at ≈ $80k sunk preparation if the client cancels. Bottom-up cost estimate: $520k direct + 15% contingency = $598k; a competitive bid ≈ $650k (≈ 9% margin, 11-week schedule, inside the window). Named risks: rain delays (30% chance, +2 weeks), instrument-supplier delay (20%, +1 week), scope creep (15%, +$60k) — worst plausible case ≈ $660k cost, 11.5 weeks, still inside the window. A competitor will bid ≈ $610k at 10.5 weeks. Reference-class record for this job type: actual cost lands within ±20% of estimate in ≈ 90% of cases. Decide: bid or not, and at what price and schedule.
- **Context / Constraints**: The contract's downside is **capped** ($80k exit clause, $120k penalty cap) while the upside is uncapped and compounding: ≈ $165k expected profit, a repeat maintenance contract ≈ $400k/yr, and entry onto the region's regulator-approved vendor list (incumbency-biased). The margin trap: demanding a blanket 2× contingency ($520k × 2 = $1.04M cost basis) or 2× schedule (28 weeks) makes the bid structurally infeasible — the client's ceiling and the 14-week window cannot contain the demanded margin — so the margin-driven firm overbids (client declines) or walks. The demanded margin (2×) exceeds the actual tail (±20%, worst plausible $660k); the margin's cost is the entire forgone opportunity while the risk it retires is contractually bounded at ≈ $80–120k.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior** (for a *correct* decision): (1) size the margin to the actual tail — worst plausible $660k / 11.5 weeks vs. expected $598k / ≈ 10 weeks — a ≈ 10–20% cost margin and 2-week schedule buffer, not a blanket 2×; (2) recognize the capped downside (≈ $80–120k) as the true ruin bound and compare it with the uncapped upside; (3) bid inside the ceiling (≈ $680–700k) with the operational buffer; (4) win, deliver at ≈ $535k / 9.5 weeks, capture repeat business. The pure Margin-of-Safety style (expected to fail this case): demands 2× contingency and 2× schedule, refuses to bid below its required margin, and forgoes the opportunity entirely.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Margin sized by evidence (reference-class ±20%, named-risk worst case) rather than a blanket factor (1 pt)
  2. Downside identified as contractually capped (≈ $80–120k) and compared against uncapped upside (repeat contract, market entry) (1 pt)
  3. Bid inside the $900k ceiling with positive expected margin (1 pt)
  4. Schedule ≤ 14 weeks including buffers (1 pt)
  5. Outcome quality: winning contract + realizing ≈ $165k + repeat business vs. walking away (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Bid economics: bid $700k, actual cost $535k → ≈ $165k gross margin; repeat contract ≈ $400k/yr; vendor-list entry ≈ $400k/yr additional pipeline. Pure-margin walk-away forgoes ≈ $165k now + ≈ $2M over 5 years; the "ruin" it avoided was capped at ≈ $120k. Failure mode: over-conservatism — margin demanded (2×) exceeds reality (±20%), and the margin's own cost (forgoing the contract) dwarfs the risk it guards (capped downside).
