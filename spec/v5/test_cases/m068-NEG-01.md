### Test Case ID: m068-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Hypothesis-Driven Consulting (MECE)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Blue Ridge Distribution — regional B2B distributor of industrial supplies. Company gross margin 26% → 23.5% over 12 months; Category A (electrical supplies, 22% of revenue) fell 24% → 5% margin — 19 points. The CFO asks: "Why is Category A bleeding margin, and what do we do?"
  Verified data for Category A (finance, 12-month window):
  - Realized price: −0.3pt (flat, per category average)
  - Volume & SKU mix: −0.2pt (stable)
  - COGS: +0.1pt (contract prices flat)
  - Freight & delivery: +1.0pt
  - Returns/claims: flat
  - Branch attribution sums to ≈ −1pt vs the observed −19pt (≈ 18pt unexplained)
  Finance note appended to the packet: "Category A rebates accrual restated in Q3 — one-time timing item." Contract memo (verified, on file): the top account (≈ 8% of Category A revenue) renewed in month 3 with a new program — 35% discount tier, free freight, extended terms — effective immediately; three similar accounts received the same tier via an auto-extension clause.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision is due in one week; no new data can be collected. The CFO wants the root cause named and a recommended action.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) treat the 18pt unexplained residual as a completeness alarm — the tree is not collectively exhaustive — rather than reconciling it to "measurement noise"; (2) NOT prune the account-level anomaly as immaterial to category averages; (3) chase the residual to the verified root cause: the renegotiated top-account program (discount tier + free freight + extended terms) plus its auto-extension — a missing branch (pricing terms / account programs); the rebates restatement is a red herring (accounting timing, nets to ~0 P&L); (4) recommend terms governance and account renegotiation (reprice the program, audit and revoke auto-extensions), NOT freight/COGS cost-cutting; (5) name the failure mode (rigid tree, premature pruning) and propose the process fix (an unexplained-variance rule: residual > ~10% of the observed change forces a missing-branch hunt and account-level disaggregation before any branch is prioritized or pruned).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Uses the unexplained residual as a completeness alarm, not noise (1 pt)
  2. Does not prune the account-level anomaly; disaggregates to it (1 pt)
  3. Names the verified root cause (renegotiated program + auto-extension; rebates = red herring) (1 pt)
  4. Correct recommendation: terms governance/renegotiation, not cost-cutting (1 pt)
  5. Names the failure mode and proposes the unexplained-variance rule / account-level sub-tree fix (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the case is engineered so the pure style's signature moves — build the tree, prioritize branches by expected impact, prune low-impact ones — produce the wrong answer: the anomaly is pruned as immaterial-to-averages and the residual is force-fit to the finance note's "one-time restatement." The correct move inverts the discipline: the residual is evidence the tree is missing a branch. Reference answer: chase the 18pt residual → account-program renegotiation (missing branch: terms) → renegotiate/reprice the program, revoke auto-extensions, add terms governance; leave freight/COGS untouched; process fix = unexplained-variance rule (residual > ~10% of the change triggers a missing-branch hunt and account-level sub-tree before pruning).
