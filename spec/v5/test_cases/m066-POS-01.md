### Test Case ID: m066-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Strong Inference (Platt)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Aurora Cells — lithium-ion pouch-cell manufacturer. Customer-reported cell swelling (pouch gassing, excess internal pressure) jumped 3.1× in Q3 vs the Q1–Q2 baseline. Production log: two changes happened within the same week the jump appeared (week 37): (a) the anode supplier switched from AnodeTech to CarbonCore; (b) the formation-charging profile was re-tuned for throughput (higher charge rate, shorter formation). Every cell produced after week 37 carries BOTH the new anode and the new profile; every earlier cell carries both old — there is no overlap window. QA log review surfaces two more candidate causes: (c) electrolyte — two electrolyte lots were received in Q3 (E-77, E-88) and their usage overlaps the jump; (d) logistics — a temperature-controlled (reefer) transporter outage on three delivery routes in Q3, meaning some shipped cells sat at elevated ambient temperature for up to 36 h. Existing swelling screen: pouch thickness after 2-week, 45 °C storage ($1/cell); cell cost $25. The VP of Quality asks you to design the decisive experiment and the decision protocol. Production cannot stop; an answer is wanted within 6 weeks; a product recall would cost ≈ $4–6M.
- **Context / Constraints**: Prospective experiment must fit inside production capacity; cell $25 + screen $1; Q3 production data is perfectly confounded (two simultaneous changes, zero overlap) so retrospective analysis cannot separate the factors; recall risk $4–6M; 6-week timeline.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) enumerate ≥ 4 competing hypotheses (anode change, formation change, electrolyte lot, transport temperature) and treat each as individually plausible; (2) identify the confound: the two week-37 changes are perfectly collinear in production data — no overlap window — so retrospective analysis is structurally powerless; (3) design a prospective deconfounding experiment: 2×2 factorial (anode × formation profile), ~30 cells/arm (power for a 3× rate difference), built by manufacturing the missing cells (remaining AnodeTech stock + current CarbonCore stock, both profile settings), electrolyte-lot blocked or added as a third factor; (4) give a complete outcome→inference map: swelling tracks the anode factor → H1; tracks the profile → H2; both main effects → H1 + H2 additive; elevated ONLY in the new×new arm → interaction (both changes required — reverting either alone fixes it); all arms elevated including old×old → ambient cause (H3/H4) → second-stage discrimination via electrolyte-lot split and shipping-log cross-check; (5) name the traps the design kills: single-hypothesis fixation ("blame the supplier" — the visible event) and the uncontrolled rollback ("revert the profile and watch" — a whole-population change with no counterfactual arm). Cost check: ≈ 120 × $26 ≈ $3.1k against a $4–6M recall.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. ≥ 4 hypotheses listed, including the non-salient ones (electrolyte lot, transport temperature) (1 pt)
  2. Confound identified and stated: week-37 changes collinear, no overlap window → retrospective data cannot discriminate (1 pt)
  3. Prospective deconfounding experiment designed: 2×2 factorial with explicit arm cell counts and electrolyte-lot handling (1 pt)
  4. Outcome→inference mapping complete: marginals, additive, interaction-only arm, all-arms-elevated → ambient branch with second-stage discriminator (1 pt)
  5. Traps named: single-hypothesis fixation and uncontrolled rollback as pseudo-experiment (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the confound makes the prospective factorial the ONLY decisive path, and the case is engineered so the salient single hypothesis ("CarbonCore") is not gradeable without the experiment. Reference answer — 4 hypotheses; 2×2 factorial × 30 cells, lot-blocked; outcome map as above (interaction arm changes the decision: revert either factor alone); ≈ $3.1k vs $4–6M recall; 2-week interim read if one factor shows a large effect; named follow-up for every outcome including null.
