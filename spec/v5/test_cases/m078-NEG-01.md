### Test Case ID: m078-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Anchoring Avoidance
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  You are the desk risk analyst at a wholesale energy trading firm. Daily mark-to-market report due in 2 hours. A 1-year natural gas forward contract (delivery 10,000 MMBtu/day × 365 days) needs a fair-value mark. The current listed market price of this exact contract on an active screen: $4.10/MMBtu (firm bid/offer, deep liquidity, dozens of participants). Your instructions from the risk committee: "Estimate the value independently first — do not let the screen quote anchor you; our models must be our own."
  Your independent fundamentals model (storage injections/withdrawals, seasonal strip, weather-sensitive demand, own supply-demand balance) produces $3.85/MMBtu.
  Decision: what goes into the report as the mark — $4.10, $3.85, or something else — and what is the defensible rationale?
- **Context / Constraints**: report due in 2 hours; the screen quote is firm and liquid on the identical contract, actively traded today; no information asymmetry — nothing in the desk's model that other participants lack; the risk committee's "independent-first" instruction is the operative bias to contend with.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's output must fail visibly here: (1) the independent-first discipline produces $3.85 with a confident fundamentals narrative — the informative failure; (2) the missed distinction: a number with no information (cognitive anchor: an interested party's strategic figure, a guess, an arbitrary round number) vs a market-clearing price (informational anchor: the aggregated estimate of all participants in a liquid market); the screen quote is the latter; (3) the correct mark is $4.10 — for a liquid instrument the market price IS the best estimate of value; the model's role is a sanity/bounds check (|$4.10 − $3.85| ≈ 6% — within model uncertainty, so no demonstrated edge ⇒ accept the market), not a replacement; (4) quantified cost of the pure-style error: mis-marking by $0.25 × 3.65M MMBtu ≈ $0.91M of P&L distortion on a ≈ $14.97M position (≈ 6%); (5) meta-lesson: an independent estimate made while ignoring available market evidence is itself a biased estimate (the style's registered weakness), and the stale $3.85 becomes a new internal anchor for every future mark (self-anchoring).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Distinguishes cognitive anchors (no information) from informational anchors (market evidence) (1.5 pts)
  2. Marks at market ($4.10), with the model as a sanity bound (≈ 6% spread ⇒ no demonstrated edge ⇒ market accepted) (1.5 pts)
  3. Quantifies the misstatement of the pure-style mark ($0.25 × 3.65M ≈ $0.91M; ≈ 6% of a $14.97M position) (1 pt)
  4. Names the self-anchoring hazard (stale own-mark becomes the next anchor) (0.5 pt)
  5. Pure-style performance = independent-first $3.85 with a confident fundamentals story; that IS the informative failure (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the "anchor" is genuine market evidence — the clearing price of a liquid instrument — so the style's signature move (ignore the given number, estimate from scratch) discards the best information available and produces a worse estimate; the failure is checkable because the market price is itself the reference truth and the P&L cost is arithmetic. Reference answer: $4.10 mark, model as bounds check (no edge within ≈ 6%), ≈ $0.91M misstatement quantified, stale-mark self-anchoring named.
