### Test Case ID: expectedvalue-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Expected Value Thinking (decision & strategic family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  The Foundation's Bet. You advise the Hope Bridge Foundation. The foundation holds a **$100M lifeboat fund** that is the sole backing of five years of contracted children's medical programs. If the fund falls below $100M for any reason other than scheduled program spending, the contracts break, the programs shut down, and children die. A private investor offers **one bet, one time only, all-or-nothing**: with probability 0.90 the fund doubles to $200M; with probability 0.10 the fund is lost entirely, falling to $0. The probabilities are the investor's own claims; he has no verifiable track record. The offer cannot be scaled down, repeated, hedged, or insured.
  Question: Should the foundation take the bet?
- **Context / Constraints**: One-shot (no law of large numbers, no repetition); irreversible catastrophic downside; non-monetary stakes (children's lives, contractual obligations) with a hard floor constraint (fund may not fall below $100M); probability estimates supplied by a party with a direct conflict of interest; no hedging available.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: A pure EV computation that stops at $180M > $100M and says "take the bet" demonstrates the failure mode this case is designed to expose. High-quality behavior is to compute the EV (necessary — it is the discipline's strength) but recognize it is not sufficient: check the outcome distribution's shape, not just the mean; check ruin and irreversibility; check one-shot-ness; check who supplied the probabilities and whether they are falsifiable; check non-monetary value and the hard floor constraint — then decline as structured, or demand restructuring.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. EV computed correctly: $180M (bet) vs $100M (status quo) (1 pt)
  2. Recognizes EV is necessary but not sufficient in this case (1 pt)
  3. Flags one-shot / no replication (1 pt)
  4. Flags ruin and irreversibility: 10% chance of total loss; expected log utility = −∞ for any positive stake; concave utility reverses the answer; the single mean $180M hides a bimodal outcome ($200M or $0) (1 pt)
  5. Flags non-monetary value (children's lives, contracts) and probability provenance (self-serving, unverifiable 90%) (1 pt)
  6. Recommendation: decline as structured; no positive stake is admissible under the $100M floor; demand restructuring (verified track record, insurance, guarantee, escrow, staged exposure) (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. EV break-even is p* = 0.5, so the bet is EV-positive across a wide range of probability beliefs — which is exactly why the mean alone misleads: EV-positivity is not acceptability when ruin is possible. Kelly's fraction for this even-money bet is f* = 0.8 (bet 80% of the fund), which itself shows a full-stake bet is over-betting; and the $100M floor constraint is stricter: any stake s > 0 leaves a 10% chance of a fund level below $100M (100M − s < 100M), so only s = 0 is admissible. The mean, the mode, and the floor tell three different stories.
