### Test Case ID: m060-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Base Rate Avoidance in Hiring
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Northwind Field Services — 40 field-service technicians across 6 regions, expanding into 8 new territories; 12 technicians must be hired within 8 months (high-volume hiring). Current process: resume screen (years of field experience), one 30-minute unstructured interview with the ops lead ("do I get a good feel?"), one reference call, then a gut call. Costly symptoms: 45% annual turnover; each bad hire costs ≈ $18k (callbacks, re-dispatch, lost accounts); each hire costs ≈ $6k to train. The ops director asks you to design the selection process for the technician role. Assessment budget ≈ $40 per applicant. The org ran a local validation study on 120 prior hires:
    - Work sample (2-hour simulated service call: triage, diagnose, repair plan + written report) — validity r = 0.52, cost $22/applicant
    - Structured interview (45 min, scored rubric, identical questions) — r = 0.47, cost $8
    - Unstructured interview (30 min, conversational) — r = 0.21 (org-calibrated), cost $4
    - Reference check (phone, 15 min) — r = 0.17, cost $2
    - Years of field experience (resume screen) — r = 0.13, cost $0
  Deliverable: a complete selection-process design — signal ranking, scoring/weighting scheme, decision rule, and a cost-vs-expected-return justification against the old process.
- **Context / Constraints**: Budget cap $40/applicant. High volume: ~100 applicants for 12 hires. Bad-hire cost dominates the economics. The old process's de facto composite validity ≈ 0.28 (unstructured interview + experience + references, weighted by conversational impact).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) rank the five signals by validity, citing the numbers; (2) weight the composite so the work sample dominates (≥ 45% of score) with the structured interview second, weighting justified by validity, not convenience; (3) remove the unstructured interview from scoring (reclassified as information-gathering only, or dropped); (4) demote references and years-of-experience to pass/fail screens (red-flag checks) rather than score drivers; (5) state an explicit decision rule (composite threshold ≥ 70/100, mechanical tie-break on the work sample, documented override path); (6) quantify expected uplift — new-process expected validity ≈ 0.50 vs old ≈ 0.28, translated into reduced bad hires and dollars against the $40/applicant budget; (7) name the failure the design eliminates: the charismatic-but-unproductive hire (interview illusion).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Validity ranking correct and explicit with numbers (1 pt)
  2. Work sample top-weighted ≥ 45% of composite (1 pt)
  3. Unstructured interview excluded from scoring (1 pt)
  4. References/experience demoted to screens, not drivers (1 pt)
  5. Explicit decision rule with threshold + tie-break + documented override path (1 pt)
  6. Cost-benefit quantified: validity uplift vs old process in dollar terms (0.5 pt)
  7. Interview-illusion failure named as the design target (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the case is engineered so the base-rate answer is uniquely good — validity data is supplied, volume is high (base rates matter at volume), and the old process's weakness (personality-weighted interview) is documented. Reference answer — weights ≈ work sample 50% / structured interview 30% / references 10% / experience 10% (screens only, no score) / unstructured 0%; threshold ≥ 70 with work-sample tie-break; expected composite validity ≈ 0.50 vs old 0.28 → bad-hire rate roughly halves, avoiding ≈ 2–3 bad hires of 12 ≈ $36–54k against ≈ $3.4k assessment cost. Override path: only for superior specific evidence (e.g., authenticated job-identical artifacts), documented and logged — a deviation without evidence is how the gut re-enters.
