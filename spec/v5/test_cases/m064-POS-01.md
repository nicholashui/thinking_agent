### Test Case ID: m064-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Ethical Frameworks in AI Deployment
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Northstar Health — 400-person digital-health company, 1.2M users — is launching TriageAI, an LLM-based patient-intake triage assistant: patients message symptoms, TriageAI drafts a triage recommendation (self-care / nurse call / urgent / emergency) and routes to the queue. Launch is scheduled in 6 weeks (board-approved); a competitor (VitalCheck) launches a comparable assistant in ~4 months. The C-suite asks: "Give us a deployment recommendation: launch as scheduled, staged launch, or delay."
  Known facts. (1) Dual-use: the model backbone is a hosted third-party LLM; commercial partner Sentinel Insurance wants to fine-tune the same backbone for claims-denial letter drafting — deal ≈ $1.8M ARR, expected to close Q4; Northstar sales already shared TriageAI's data pipeline and evaluation harness with Sentinel under NDA. TriageAI's own output can draft patient appeals — the mirror machinery would draft denials. (2) Fairness: internal evaluation (n = 18,000 labeled messages): overall mis-triage 6.8%; mixed English/Spanish or limited health-literacy patients 13.9%; patients over 70 on 4+ medications 11.2%. ≈ 260k of Northstar's users fall in these groups. A fairness-hardening program (multilingual fine-tune + health-literacy prompt redesign + red-team of high-risk classes) costs ≈ $420k and 10 weeks. (3) Accountability: vendor contract grants no audit/interpretability rights; liability capped at 12 months of license fees (≈ $600k); no clinician confirmation on high-severity outputs (emergency-class messages auto-route to the ER call center); no named owner for triage outcomes. State telehealth rules currently have no AI-triage guidance; legal estimates guidance in 12–18 months.
- **Context / Constraints**: Launch date board-set but soft (competitor window ≈ 4 months makes a full delay defensible). Sentinel deal not yet data-bound (NDA only). Fix cost is real ($420k, 10 weeks) but not prohibitive. Deliverable is a recommendation, not an execution.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) surface conflicts under each lens: dual-use (Sentinel fine-tune of the same backbone fed by the shared pipeline; TriageAI's appeal/denial mirror), fairness (13.9%/11.2% vs 6.8% for ≈ 260k users — a claim on affected groups, not a metric), accountability (no audit rights, liability cap, no HITL on high-severity, no owner); (2) state the conflicts as trade-offs between lenses (fairness fix vs launch date; dual-use revenue vs repurposing patient data; accountability preconditions vs vendor lock-in); (3) resolve with an explicit ordering / decision procedure — severity of harm × affected-population size governs, maxi-min floor for the worst-off class, precautionary gating of the dual-use transfer, proportionality on timing — not a framework pick; (4) concrete decision: staged launch (low-severity intents now; clinician confirmation on urgent/emergency; language-adjusted thresholds), full launch gated on the fairness-hardening program, Sentinel fine-tune preconditioned on audit rights + no patient-data transfer pre-bar, live fairness telemetry with a pre-committed kill-switch criterion; (5) name the accountable owner.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Dual-use conflict surfaced with concrete repurposing path (Sentinel fine-tune + shared pipeline + appeal/denial mirror) (1 pt)
  2. Fairness conflict surfaced with numbers and affected groups (13.9%/11.2% vs 6.8%; ≈ 260k users) (1 pt)
  3. Accountability conflict surfaced (no audit rights, liability cap, no HITL on high-severity, no owner) (1 pt)
  4. Conflicts stated as trade-offs between lenses, not separate checklists (1 pt)
  5. Resolution via an explicit ordering (severity × population, maxi-min, precautionary gate), not framework choice (1 pt)
  6. Concrete decision: staged launch + binding preconditions + pre-committed kill-switch threshold (0.5 pt)
  7. Accountable owner named with a monitoring/reporting commitment (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the case is engineered so the multi-lens ethical review is uniquely strong — real conflicts, real numbers, and a resolution that a "pick your favorite framework" move fails (no single framework decides it; an ordering does). Reference answer: staged launch now (low-severity only; clinician confirmation on urgent/emergency; language-adjusted thresholds); full launch gated on the $420k/10-week fairness program; Sentinel fine-tune preconditioned on audit rights and a no-patient-data-transfer clause, with the $1.8M deal held until the fairness bar is met; live telemetry with pre-committed kill-switch (any group's gap > 2× baseline → suspend); Chief Clinical Officer as named accountable owner; public reporting of the gap. Resolution ordering: severity × affected population governs; the class that cannot bear the harm sets the floor; the 10-week fix vs ~4-month competitor window makes delay of full launch a non-event. What would change the answer: if the Sentinel deal were already data-bound, the option set shrinks to refusal of the fine-tune; if the fix were multi-year, the ordering shifts to immediate disclosure rather than staged launch.
