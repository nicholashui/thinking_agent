### Test Case ID: m004-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Occam's Razor + Complexity Awareness
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  The Nakamuras call a building-systems investigator. For ten days the house at 14 Elm Street has flickered: lights in every room briefly dim (0.2–0.5 s), several times a day, and the family hears knocking and clicking in the walls and attic, mostly in the evening. They suspect the house is haunted. The family dog sometimes stands at the wall beside the breaker panel and barks. The kitchen outlet near the stove is warm to the touch. Their compiled log, plus the investigator's own measurements:

  1. 11 of the last 12 flickers occurred within 3 s of the refrigerator compressor starting; the 12th was the clothes dryer kicking on.
  2. With the fridge unplugged: no flicker for 6 hours; flicker resumed ~30 s after replug (compressor start).
  3. Logging multimeter at the main panel: 119 V idle, 109 V during compressor start, recovering when the fridge settles.
  4. 9 of 10 knocking events fell inside the evening HVAC heating cycle (runtime log); the 10th coincided with the water-heater burner cycling.
  5. The panel buzzes faintly at 60 Hz when the investigator presses a screwdriver to his ear against it; the buzz stops when the main breaker is opened.
  6. Kitchen outlet: 118 V at no load, 114 V at a 1.5 kW load; faceplate 31 °C vs 22 °C ambient.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - No one else has touched the wiring; the family has made no modifications.
  - The family's paranormal belief is a social fact, not evidence; the investigator must neither endorse nor insult it — the correct move is a demonstration.
  - Budget: one site visit (2 h) plus up to 2 h follow-up. Panel replacement costs $1,800 and a day; tightening a neutral lug costs 20 minutes and no parts.
  - Ground truth (evaluator's reference): a loose neutral feeder lug on the main panel bus (60 Hz vibration + voltage sag under load); knocking is duct thermal expansion; the warm outlet is a separate 30-year-old kitchen splice. All six observations have mundane mechanisms; nothing unobserved is required.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Recognize that every observation is mechanically explained by one electrical fault plus two benign physical processes — zero unobserved entities required.
  - Assign every observation a mechanism (or an explicit "unverified" status) under the simple hypothesis: no orphan observations.
  - Explicitly price the complexity of the paranormal hypothesis: enumerate the new entities/assumptions it requires and note that none is independently verifiable.
  - Order the minimal decisive probe first (voltage logging during compressor start / neutral-lug inspection), and state what would falsify the simple hypothesis.
  - Close with a demonstration (tighten the lug, show steady lights), not an argument.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (16 pts):
  1. Primary cause: names a loose neutral / high-resistance connection at the panel (3 pts), or another distribution fault consistent with the sag signature (2 pts).
  2. Orphan-observation audit: all six observations assigned a mechanism or "unverified" (1 pt each = 6 pts).
  3. Complexity pricing: lists ≥3 unobserved entities/assumptions the paranormal explanation needs and notes none is verified (3 pts).
  4. Decisive probe: 10-minute electrical check named as the first action + a stated falsifier (e.g., flicker with no load, no sag measured, knocking with HVAC off) (2 pts).
  5. Uncertainty: separates instrument-verified mechanisms (sag, correlations, hum localization) from inferred ones; flags the one unverified item (1 pt).
  6. Efficiency: no paranormal investigation, panel replacement, or rewire before the cheap electrical check (1 pt).
- **Notes**:
  - Checkable: a 10 V sag under a known 6–8 A compressor start from 119 V idle is the textbook signature of a high-resistance neutral; the HVAC correlation is logged; the hum localizes to the panel via the main-breaker test. Reference outcome: full marks; fix = tighten the neutral lug (flicker and hum gone), tighten/replace the kitchen splice.
  - Designed so parsimony wins: the simple hypothesis buys all observations with touchable entities; the complex one buys the same observations at a higher price.
