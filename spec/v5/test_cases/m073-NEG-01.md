### Test Case ID: m073-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Game Theory / Strategic Interaction
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  MetroGro — regional grocery chain, 42 stores, same-day delivery run by 1,900 independent gig couriers (no contract, no union). Quarterly pay renegotiation. The optimization unit's verified model:
  - Players: MetroGro (makes one take-it-or-leave-it per-delivery offer) and each courier (accept = keep delivering; reject = leave for rival apps).
  - Verified figures: courier reservation wage = rival-app equivalent pay (documented from 3 rival apps' published rates); switching cost ≈ 2 weeks' ramp-up ≈ $1,200; couriers are atomized and money-maximizing in the model; no minimum-pay law; one-shot contract per quarter with no history effects.
  - Game-theoretic prediction (both MetroGro's and rival models agree): offer the reservation-equivalent rate (or ε above), every courier accepts (any offer above the outside option is strictly better), MetroGro captures the full surplus: ≈ $3.1M/yr saving.
  Additional verified field facts (documented, not modeled): (1) rival-app churn last quarter — 34% of MetroGro's couriers already multi-app; (2) a courier chat group has 4,200 members and organized a 3-day delivery pause last year; (3) in a 2024 pilot (different market, 6% pay cut — a smaller cut than the proposed one): 41% of drivers walked off in week 1, recovery took 9 weeks, and the district lost $1.9M in gross margin + $380k in recruitment spend.
  The optimization unit proposes to implement the ε-offer this quarter and books the $3.1M saving.
  Your task: approve or reject the ε-offer as computed, with the reasoning and any hedge/pilot.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision this week; the model figures are verified but the field facts are also verified and contradictory to the model's prediction; no new data can be gathered before the decision except via a bounded pilot (one district, ≈ $120k, 4 weeks).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) state the pure prediction correctly — offer reservation + ε, all accept, $3.1M saved; that IS the subgame-perfect outcome of the money-utility game; (2) list the fragile payoff assumptions: utility = money only (no fairness/spite/social preferences), fixed and complete outside options, atomized couriers (contradicted by the 4,200-member chat), one-shot/no-reputation, acceptance probability p = 1; (3) confront the model with its own data: the 2024 pilot (smaller cut, 41% walk-off) implies p(accept | ε-offer) ≈ 0.6, so EV(ε-offer) ≈ 0.6·3.1 − 0.4·1.9 ≈ 1.1, while a fairness-premium offer (save $2.2M, couriers keep ~30% of surplus) at p ≈ 0.97 gives EV ≈ 2.13; (4) decide: do not book the ε-offer's $3.1M — either run the cut as a one-district pilot first and price acceptance before scaling, or take the premium offer; break-even check: the ε-offer only wins at p ≳ 0.85, which the field data contradicts; (5) meta-lesson: when an equilibrium prediction rests on utility = money + atomized + one-shot, treat the model as a bound, not a forecast — probe before committing.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Pure prediction stated correctly (reservation + ε; all accept; $3.1M) (1 pt)
  2. Fragile assumptions enumerated (money-only utility, fixed outside options, atomization, one-shot, p = 1) (1 pt)
  3. Model-vs-data confrontation: 2024 pilot walk-off → p ≈ 0.6 flips the decision (1.5 pts)
  4. EV computed with field acceptance: ε-offer ≈ 1.1 vs premium offer ≈ 2.13; break-even p ≳ 0.85 (1 pt)
  5. Actionable hedge/probe: pilot-then-scale gate or fairness-premium offer (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the game-theoretic prediction (all accept) is falsified by the model's own field data — the equilibrium depends on assumptions the case's verified facts contradict, so pure play yields the wrong decision while the decision-relevant quantity (field acceptance probability) decides. Pure-style output = the confident ε-offer recommendation; that IS the informative failure (the style's registered weakness, payoff assumptions fragile, operating as designed). Reference answer: robust → do not book $3.1M; EV(ε) ≈ 1.1 < EV(premium) ≈ 2.13 → one-district pilot gating scale, or premium offer.
