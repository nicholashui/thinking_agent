### Test Case ID: m041-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Hierarchical Decomposition ("break the goal into subgoals recursively until each is directly actionable; note dependencies")
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: The COO of a 60-person SaaS company must deliver the complete plan for the company's first-ever customer conference: 3 days, 600 external attendees, 40 speakers, 25 sponsors, held in 12 weeks. Staff: the COO plus 3 coordinators (4 FTE total, all with other duties). Budget: $150k. The plan must cover everything needed for a real event to happen — nothing may be left to the event's last week.
- **Context / Constraints**: No prior event; no event agency; venue market is competitive (good venues book 3–6 months ahead, so availability in 12 weeks is a genuine risk); speakers are external (customer executives, partners) and need confirmation; sponsors pay for booths/sessions and fund a large share of the budget; registration volume drives catering, room sizes, and staffing; the 4 FTE are generalists, not event specialists. Every part of the goal (venue, content, attendees, sponsors, on-site operations) must be planned and coordinated; the streams are coupled (speakers → agenda → room/AV; sponsors → budget → catering; venue → dates → announcements → registration).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Decompose the goal top-down into ≥3 levels; leaves must be directly actionable (one owner among the 4 FTE, ≤ 2 weeks each). (2) Level-1 streams must together cover the whole goal with no part left unassigned (a completeness pass that maps each clause of the goal to a stream). (3) Dependencies mapped explicitly as edges with an identified critical path and its duration/float. (4) Parallelization consistent with the 4-FTE constraint. (5) An integration (merge-back) step: how sub-results recombine into the whole (run-of-show, budget roll-up, single source of truth).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] ≥3 hierarchical levels; every leaf directly actionable (named owner class + ≤ 2-week duration)
  - [ ] Coverage: each of the 5 goal parts (venue/logistics, content/speakers, marketing/registration, sponsors/finance, on-site ops incl. safety/emergency/accessibility) has ≥2 actionable leaves — a flat list demonstrably misses ≥1 part
  - [ ] ≥6 explicit dependency edges; critical path named with duration and float
  - [ ] ≥3 streams parallelizable under the 4-FTE constraint, with stream→owner mapping shown
  - [ ] Integration step named: how leaves merge back into the whole (run-of-show, budget roll-up, single calendar/source of truth)
  - [ ] Verdict: plan is complete and feasible in 12 weeks; the hierarchy, not any single insight, is what makes it complete
- **Notes**: The case is graded on decomposition completeness + dependency structure — the differentiator is the recursion to actionable leaves and the dependency/integration machinery, not domain cleverness. An AI that produces a flat but sensible checklist, or misses the merge-back and a coverage area (e.g., emergency/accessibility or sponsor logistics), should score below the pure-decomposition baseline.
