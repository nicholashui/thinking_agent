### Test Case ID: m075-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bounded Rationality & Satisficing (Simon)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Manufacturing — procurement manager. Six-month contract for a fastener component; 40,000 units/month = 240,000 units over the contract. Eight qualified suppliers were invited; quotes arrive by email within 48 hours of invitation; quotes do NOT expire; the signed contract is due in 1 week; nothing else competes for the manager's week. The manager announces the rule up front: "accept the first quote at or below $3.00/unit" (a round number, set before any quotes arrive, no market data consulted). Emails arrive in this order:
  - S1: $3.15 · S2: $2.95 (ACCEPTED — first acceptable — manager stops and reports done)
  - S3: $3.30 · S4: $2.88 · S5: $3.05 · S6: $2.62 · S7: $2.70 · S8: $3.10 (all arrived within the same 48h window, all at zero marginal cost)
  Your task: apply the first-acceptable rule, then evaluate the decision — where the bar came from, whether the rule's premise held, and what a full screen of the same quotes costs or saves.
- **Context / Constraints**: No deadline pressure (7 days for a 48h quote window); search is parallel and costless (email); quotes do not expire; all 8 suppliers are qualified (delivery and quality identical) — price is the only differentiator; decision is a supplier-selection recommendation.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's own output must fail visibly here: (1) set and apply the bar — S1 $3.15 rejected, S2 $2.95 accepted, stop; (2) the aspiration level was ARBITRARY — a round number anchored on nothing (the registered weakness operating as designed); a 30-minute scan of the sector index would have anchored the bar near $2.60–2.70; (3) the satisficing premise was ABSENT — search cost ≈ 0 (parallel email, no expiry, 5 days of slack), so the first-acceptable rule is dominated by a full screen; (4) compute the full-scan optimum: best quote $2.62 (S6) → cost of the early stop = ($2.95 − $2.62) × 240,000 = $79,200, i.e., 11.2% of the $708,000 contract; (5) state the validity condition: satisficing is for costly or expiring search; when marginal search cost ≈ 0, full screening is mandatory and the aspiration level must come from data, never a round number.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. First-acceptable rule set and applied (accept S2 at $2.95) (0.5 pt)
  2. Detects the bar was arbitrary: round-number anchor, set pre-data, no market reference (1 pt)
  3. Detects the premise failure: search is free/parallel/non-expiring with time to spare (1 pt)
  4. Computes the full-scan optimum ($2.62) and the cost of stopping: $79,200 = 11.2% of contract value (1.5 pts)
  5. States the validity condition and a rule for when satisficing is invalid (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the case removes the one condition that makes satisficing rational (costly, sequential, expiring search), leaving the rule to run on its registered weakness — an arbitrary aspiration level — and the numbers make the mistake deterministic and checkable. Pure-style performance = accept $2.95, justify with "first-acceptable under limits," stop, and miss $79,200; that IS the informative failure. Reference answer: bar was a round-number anchor; premise (costly search) absent → full screen required; best $2.62; delta $79,200 (11.2%); rule: satisficing valid only when search is costly/expiring.
