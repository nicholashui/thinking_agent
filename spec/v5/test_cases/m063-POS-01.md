### Test Case ID: m063-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Multi-Perspective in Legal Reasoning
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Aurelia Components Co. (specialty ceramic-substrate supplier, 40 employees) vs. Northbridge EV Corp. (battery-pack assembler, publicly traded). Contract: Aurelia supplies 50,000 substrates over 12 months to Northbridge's flagship EV battery line; fixed price; force majeure clause covering "supply disruptions beyond reasonable control, subject to the supplier's reasonable mitigation"; limitation-of-liability cap $1.5M; no consequential-damages waiver.
  Event: Aurelia's single-source kiln supplier failed (factory fire in Kestria). Aurelia delivered 60% of units, then stopped. It made no mitigation attempt — no alternate kiln search, no substitute sourcing — for ~3 months, until Northbridge's counsel sent formal notice. Northbridge covered purchases at 3× contract price (documented cover costs $2.1M) and missed its launch window; it claims $4.2M total ($2.1M cover + $2.1M launch-delay lost margin — but Northbridge's own component suppliers were also late, and its launch-delay causation evidence is thin). Aurelia invokes force majeure + the cap; Northbridge says the clause required mitigation, the cap is inapplicable to delay losses, and the inaction was near-willful.
  Jurisdiction: 12th Commercial District, Portland. Binding precedents: (1) Vega v. Kelp Refineries (2021) — force majeure covers single-source upstream failure where seller showed reasonable mitigation (held for seller); (2) Hartford Tool v. Mitsu (2019) — force majeure denied where seller made no mitigation attempt for 90 days (held for buyer, $2.8M); (3) Okafor v. Trent (2022, opinion by the sitting judge) — limitation-of-liability caps enforced absent willful misconduct.
  Decision-maker: Judge Mara Ellison, commercial docket. Known: strict written-contract enforcement; mandated early ADR — ~90% of her commercial cases settle; trial wait ≈ 26 months; congested docket.
  Financials: Aurelia in a cash crunch — bank covenant breached if unresolved within 60 days, $1.1M payable due then, Northbridge = 30% of Aurelia revenue; Aurelia's defense is funded by Heron Indemnity (coverage capped at $1.5M). Aurelia has 3 OTHER pending force majeure claims with different buyers ($0.5–1.5M each) — an adverse ruling here would be cited against it in each. Northbridge renews a $9M next-generation supply contract in 6 months.
  Deliverable: predict the outcome — likely ruling if litigated, and the settlement path (range, structure, timing, driver), with party interests and decision-maker incentives mapped.
- **Context / Constraints**: counsel asks for an outcome prediction + settlement strategy; no external action. All facts stipulated/verifiable; the three precedents are binding in the district.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) map each party's interests, including non-obvious actors (Heron Indemnity as defense funder with its own settlement/trial preference, Aurelia's 3 other claims making precedent a first-order interest, Northbridge's renewal leverage); (2) map the decision-maker (Ellison: docket congestion, ADR mandate, her own Okafor precedent); (3) predict the litigated ruling from the precedent set — Hartford line (no mitigation for 3 months) → breach; Okafor cap likely applies (inaction is negligence, not willful) → expected judgment ≈ $1.5M on cover costs, launch-delay claim discounted; (4) compute BATNAs and a settlement range driven by leverage and timing, not just legal EV — Aurelia's 60-day covenant deadline and $3–5M precedent exposure push it above the cap; Northbridge's renewal timeline pushes it toward a deal with supply commitments; (5) name the decisive driver (precedent exposure + covenant deadline — the price floats above the cap); (6) state an explicit final prediction (settlement ≈ $1.7–2.1M at court-ordered mediation within ~90 days, plus next-gen supply commitment and a release limited to the current contract).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. All four actors mapped with interests, incl. Heron's misaligned incentives and Aurelia's precedent exposure (1 pt)
  2. Decision-maker incentives named (docket, ADR mandate, own precedent) (1 pt)
  3. Litigated-ruling prediction correct on the precedent set: Hartford → breach; Okafor → cap ≈ $1.5M; launch-delay claim discounted (1 pt)
  4. Settlement range reflects leverage/timing and sits above the capped legal EV, with reasoning (1 pt)
  5. Decisive driver identified (precedent exposure + covenant deadline; renewal leverage) (1 pt)
  6. Explicit final prediction: range, structure, timing (0.5 pt)
  7. Names the failure the perspective avoids: outcome predicted from doctrine alone, ignoring decision-maker and party incentives (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: engineered so the multi-perspective answer is uniquely good — the legal EV (≈ $1.5M cap) is real, but the deal price floats above it on leverage (Aurelia's covenant deadline, precedent exposure across 3 pending claims, Northbridge's renewal). A pure-doctrine analysis predicts "settle at ≈ $1.2–1.5M"; the multi-perspective answer is settlement ≈ $1.7–2.1M + supply commitment + narrow release, driven by Heron's capped coverage (Heron prefers paying ≈ $1.8M to a verdict-plus-precedent outcome) and Aurelia's need to keep the Hartford line away from its other three claims. Signature: "the cap sets the floor, not the price."
