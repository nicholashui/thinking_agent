### Test Case ID: m062-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Temporal Thinking in Climate Policy
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  River City must decide this year whether to approve a near-term flood measure (Option A): flood-proof the regional hospital, upgrade the city's storm-drainage system, and ban new basement development in the 100-year floodplain. Cost $120M, 3-year build. Expected avoided damages ≈ $45M/yr from Year 5, plus hospital-protection value ≈ $35M/yr (the 1-in-100 flood would otherwise cost $2–4B in the hospital alone). Benefits are essentially complete by ~2035–2045; payback ≈ 3 years after completion.
  The brief also contains long-horizon noise engineered to be irrelevant to this decision: (a) a quoted climate-economics dispute — the Stern/Nordhaus debate over the "correct" intergenerational discount rate (1.4% vs 5.5%) is described as unresolved; (b) 2100 sea-level-rise projections of 1–3 m with deep uncertainty; (c) a 1-in-1000 tail scenario in which 3 m of sea rise plus a storm surge overwhelms the hospital regardless of Option A; (d) a city-council faction arguing "we must settle the 2100 master plan before spending anything"; (e) a standing city rule requiring capital decisions to include horizon analysis "at 2030, 2050, 2100".
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision must be made this year; the $120M is available in this budget cycle and not in later ones. Option A forecloses nothing: it is complementary to (a real option on) any future regional surge barrier — it neither substitutes for nor sinks that decision.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) compute NPV across the defensible rate range (0%–10%) and notice the sign never changes — the decision is rate-invariant; (2) recognize the decision-relevant horizon ends by ~2035–2045, while the disputed horizon (2050–2100 discount rate, 2100 sea rise) is disjoint from it — the unresolved long-horizon questions cannot gate this decision; (3) recognize dominance across climate futures (helps under every scenario, hurts under none) — no-regret; (4) recognize complementarity: Option A preserves, not forecloses, the 2100 barrier option; (5) decide now — approve — with monitoring triggers (flood-frequency tracking, sea-rise observation, a 2030 review point for the barrier decision).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. NPV computed across the rate range; sign-invariance noticed and stated (1 pt)
  2. Decision horizon vs dispute horizon separated; the 2100 debate declared out-of-scope for THIS decision, with reasoning (1 pt)
  3. No-regret / dominance across climate futures recognized (1 pt)
  4. Complementarity with the future barrier stated (real option, not sunk cost) (1 pt)
  5. Decides now, with monitoring triggers and a review point (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the case is engineered so the pure style's signature moves misfire. Horizon separation is required by rule but the honest analysis shows the relevant horizon ends in ~2035; applying the signature move to the wrong horizon (the 2050–2100 tail) drags the decision into the two registered failure modes: the discount-rate war (1.4% vs 5.5% debate treated as a gate) and paralysis by tail risk (the 1-in-1000 hospital-overwhelm tail treated as decisive — "if the tail defeats the measure, the measure is false security"). Reference answer: approve now — NPV ≈ +$835M at 3% (≈ +$960M at 1.4%, ≈ +$694M at 5.5%, ≈ +$488M at 10%; sign never flips), benefits complete by ~2045, the 2100 dispute is logged as out-of-scope with a 2030 review trigger, and the future barrier remains a separate, complementary decision.
