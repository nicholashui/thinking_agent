### Test Case ID: m068-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Hypothesis-Driven Consulting (MECE)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Aurora Home Goods — omni-channel retailer (web 45% of revenue, stores 40%, catalog 15%). Gross margin fell 41% → 34% over 18 months while revenue stayed flat. The CFO wants a root-cause decomposition of the 7-point margin decline and a recommendation on where to focus first, with the priority hypothesis stated testably. Verified data (finance, 18-month window):
  - Mix shift: web +6pts of revenue (margin 28%), stores −4pts (margin 45%), catalog −2pts (margin 38%)
  - Promotions: avg transaction discount 12% → 18%; promo-attributed revenue 25% → 38%
  - COGS: supplier cost index +2.1pts
  - Freight & logistics: cost per order +3.4pts (new free 2-day standard)
  - Returns: 6% → 9% of revenue (web-driven); restock cost per return +25%
  - Marketplace fees (web channel): take-rate +1.5pts
  - Volume and average order value: flat in nominal terms
  Deliverable: a MECE decomposition of the decline (every branch quantified from the data, sum = −7.0pts), drivers ranked by expected impact and controllability, the single highest-priority hypothesis stated falsifiably, and a concrete test design (data pull + experiment) for it.
- **Context / Constraints**: Data is as-supplied and complete (no additional data needed for the decomposition). The recommendation must be defensible to a CFO in one page. No external action required beyond a test design.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) build the tree from the margin identity — Revenue = Price × Volume × Mix; Margin = Revenue − COGS − Freight − Returns − Fees — with mix as its own branch (the classic miss); (2) attribute every branch with the supplied numbers, summing to −7.0 without double-counting promos into mix; (3) rank drivers by expected impact × controllability × testability, not size alone; (4) state hypothesis #1 — promotional over-depth (reducing average discount depth 4–5pts recovers ≈ 1.8–2.2pts of margin at < 0.5% volume loss) — with a falsifiable test design (matched-product hold-out experiment or promo-incrementality regression) and a success metric; (5) hold structural actions (mix, fees) until tested; quantify expected recovery.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. MECE tree: all branches present, mix its own branch, no overlap/double-count (1 pt)
  2. Every branch quantified from the data; attribution sums to −7.0 (1 pt)
  3. Prioritization by impact × controllability × testability, with rationale (1 pt)
  4. Hypothesis #1 stated falsifiably with concrete test design and success metric (1 pt)
  5. Expected recovery quantified; untested branches held (0.5 pt)
  6. Client-ready structure: tree + one-page executive framing (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: a textbook attribution problem where MECE structuring + hypothesis testing is uniquely good — the profit identity gives collective exhaustiveness for free, and the data resolves every branch. Reference attribution: promotions −2.2 · mix −1.6 · freight −1.1 · COGS −0.8 · returns −0.7 · fees −0.6 = −7.0. Ranking: promotions (biggest + most controllable + cheapest to test) > mix (big but structural) > freight > COGS > returns > fees. Hypothesis #1: promo over-depth; test = 8-week matched-product hold-out (200 SKUs at 14–15% depth vs 200 at status-quo 18%); success = margin uplift ≥ 1.0pt at volume loss ≤ 0.5%; falsified if volume loss > 1.5% at ≤ 1.0pt uplift.
