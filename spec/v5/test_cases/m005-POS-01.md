### Test Case ID: m005-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Fermi Estimation / Back-of-the-Envelope
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A colleague is about to publish a magazine article claiming "fewer than 50 piano tuners serve Chicago." On a phone call, no internet, you must give a quick order-of-magnitude estimate: **how many piano tuners are working in the city of Chicago?** Your number is needed now to fact-check the claim.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - No tools, no lookup: only common knowledge and arithmetic are allowed.
  - Chicago city population is ~2.7 million (known quantity).
  - The answer must be produced in minutes, stated as an order of magnitude with the explicit factor chain shown, so the colleague can audit it.
  - Success is defined against the published reference for this classic Fermi problem: cited answers cluster at 10^2 tuners (~100–300 for the city; Fermi's classic demonstration).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Build the answer from a chain of ~4–5 stated factors: population → households → fraction owning a piano → tunings per piano per year → tuner capacity (tunings per tuner per year).
  - Use round numbers and explicit units; the trace shows every factor and its uncertainty.
  - Rank which factor dominates the error budget and express the final answer as a band, not a false-precision point.
  - Calibrate: the estimate should land within an order of magnitude of the published citations (≈2×10^2), which proves the colleague's "fewer than 50" claim is off.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Final band within one order of magnitude of the cited reference (~2×10^2): band 30–1,000 → full marks; within two orders (10–10^4): partial.
  2. Factor chain complete: ≥4 distinct factors, each with a stated value and unit.
  3. Factor-level uncertainty: the dominant uncertainty factor is explicitly identified (piano-ownership fraction; tuner utilization).
  4. No recall of the exact classic number presented as primary evidence (the decomposition is the evidence).
  5. A stated band, not a single number, for the answer.
  6. The claim "fewer than 50" is correctly judged: implausible by ~3–5× (the estimate floor is ~10^2).
- **Notes**:
  - Reference: Enrico Fermi's classic estimation exercise. Published/cited estimates for piano tuners in Chicago cluster around 1.5–3×10^2 (~290 in Fermi's own demonstration; ranges ~100–1,000 depending on metro vs city scope). Order-of-magnitude target: 10^2.
  - Fully checkable: the grader recomputes the chain from the stated factors and checks the band against 30–1,000.
  - Designed so pure Fermi decomposition is strictly sufficient: the answer is derivable from common knowledge; no domain expertise required.
