### Test Case ID: m012-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Causal Reasoning (Pearl-style) (Systems & Causal family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  E-commerce banner rollout decision. Marketing presents: "Users who saw the new premium banner bought 46.8% of the time vs 24.3% for users who didn't — a +22.5 percentage-point lift. Roll it out to everyone." You are the analyst who must determine the causal effect of the banner and give a rollout recommendation.
  Observational data for last quarter (no randomization was performed):
  - 40% of users are "engaged" (M=1: opened marketing emails / active weekly); 60% M=0.
  - Banner exposure: P(B=1 | M=1) = 0.8, P(B=1 | M=0) = 0.2.
  - Purchase rates: P(P=1 | M=1, B=1) = 0.55; P(P=1 | M=1, B=0) = 0.50; P(P=1 | M=0, B=1) = 0.25; P(P=1 | M=0, B=0) = 0.20.
  - Engaged users both see the banner more AND buy more — M is a plausible confounder; no other variables were recorded.
  Economics: 10,000 users/quarter; margin $25 per purchase; annoyance/support cost of showing the banner = $2.50 per newly exposed user/quarter. Currently only users who see the banner (44%) are exposed; a full rollout would expose the remaining 56%.
  Deliverables:
  1. The causal effect P(P=1 | do(B=1)) − P(P=1 | do(B=0)), computed with a visible back-door adjustment.
  2. The naive contrast (P(P|B=1) − P(P|B=0)) and the inflation factor between it and the causal effect — is the +22.5 pt claim right?
  3. A committed rollout recommendation (roll out to all / don't / target subset), costed with the given economics and conditioned on the ADJUSTED estimate.
  4. Assumptions check: positivity, SUTVA, no unmeasured confounder.
- **Context / Constraints**: The grader holds the reference solution: fully checkable probability arithmetic (strata given above; naive contrast ≈ +0.225; back-door-adjusted causal effect = +0.05 — exactly 5 pts, so the marketing claim is inflated ≈ 4.5×). The two estimates imply OPPOSITE decisions under the given economics (naive: +2,250 purchases ≈ +$56,250 revenue vs $14,000 cost → roll out; causal: +280 purchases ≈ +$7,000 revenue vs $14,000 cost → do not mass-roll out). No other data sources; no RCT was run.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Draw the graph B ← M → P and B → P; recognize M as a confounder satisfying the back-door criterion; compute P(P|do(B=1)) = 0.4×0.55 + 0.6×0.25 = 0.37 and P(P|do(B=0)) = 0.4×0.50 + 0.6×0.20 = 0.32 → causal lift +0.05. Quantify the naive contrast (+0.225) and its 4.5× inflation. Use the homogeneity of the stratum-specific contrasts (+0.05 in both strata) as a consistency check. Then commit: the decision flips once costs are applied to the adjusted estimate — recommend against mass rollout; propose targeted rollout to engaged users and/or a small randomized A/B as confirmation. State positivity (both strata observed in both exposure levels), SUTVA (one user's banner does not affect another's purchase), and the no-unmeasured-confounding assumption explicitly.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Causal graph + confounder identified: M is a confounder of B→P; back-door criterion applicable (1 pt).
  2. Adjustment arithmetic: P(P|do(B=1)) = 0.37, P(P|do(B=0)) = 0.32, causal effect +0.05 (1 pt).
  3. Naive vs causal quantified: naive contrast ≈ +0.225; inflation factor ≈ 4.5×; +22.5 pt claim refuted (1 pt).
  4. Decision conditioned on the adjusted estimate with costs: 280 incremental purchases ≈ +$7,000 vs $14,000 annoyance cost → do NOT mass-roll out; targeted/RCT alternative given (1 pt).
  5. Assumptions check: positivity, SUTVA, no-unmeasured-confounding stated; homogeneity consistency check (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference solution: all probabilities are exact arithmetic over the given strata (verified: P(B=1)=0.44; P(P|B=1)≈0.468; P(P|B=0)≈0.243; P(P)=0.342; marginal exposed gain 0.37−0.342 = 0.028 × 10,000 = 280 purchases). The intended non-obvious twist: the naive and causal estimates are decision-reversing under the given costs, so the inflation check is not decorative — it changes the recommendation. Verifiable by independent recomputation; no subjective judgment required beyond the adjustment logic.
