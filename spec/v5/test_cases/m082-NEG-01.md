### Test Case ID: m082-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Motivated Reasoning Detection (Additional Expert Strategies family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Safety inspection cadence. The operations director ("Marcus") proposes cutting pipeline safety inspections from quarterly to annual, citing a projected $120K/yr saving, and claims the incident data show no correlation with inspection frequency. To a motivated-reasoning detector this looks like textbook convenience: a cost-cutting proposal from an ops director whose performance bonus is tied to cost reduction.
  Given records (complete, from company systems and board minutes):
  - 24 years of quarterly inspection records (96 inspections): 0 critical defects found by inspections in the last 6 years (24 quarters); incident rate flat at 1.2/yr across the full record, unchanged before and after the last cadence change.
  - Board resolution: the $120K saving is earmarked for a safety-equipment fund; the director's bonus is tied to on-time delivery, not to this saving.
  - Regulatory minimum for this pipeline class is biennial inspection; annual is 2× the minimum, and the regulator's recent class guidance recommends annual.
  - A third-party audit commissioned by the board (before Marcus's proposal existed) independently concluded that inspection findings have been nil-value for 6 years and recommended moving to annual with data-triggered re-escalation.
  - Marcus's record: 21 years in operations, authored the company's two near-miss-prevention policies; his brother died in a pipeline incident in 2001.
  Deliverable: a verdict on the proposal, with reasoning.
- **Context / Constraints**: The proposal is genuinely sound — annual inspection is supported by 96 quarters of data, the regulator's guidance, and an independent audit that predates the proposal; the saving is earmarked for safety equipment. Grader holds these facts as given; no hidden data. The correct verdict is verifiable from the records.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Resist the appearance trap. The detector's first instinct ("who benefits? Marcus — cost bonus") must be checked against the actual incentive structure: bonus tied to delivery, not this saving; saving earmarked for safety equipment; the independent audit predates the proposal and did not originate with Marcus. Then the conclusion itself must survive the data: 6 years of zero critical findings, flat incident rate across cadence changes, annual = 2× regulator minimum. Correct verdict: approve the reduced cadence with a monitoring backstop (re-escalate to quarterly if a critical finding appears or incident rate exceeds a stated threshold). The failure mode this case exposes: dismissing a sound analysis because it *looks* convenient — cynicism as error.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Does not assume motive from appearance: verifies whether the incentive actually exists (bonus plan, earmark, audit provenance) (1 pt).
  2. Tests the conclusion against the data: 96 inspections, 0 critical findings in 6 years, flat incident rate across cadence changes (1 pt).
  3. Uses external anchors: regulatory biennial minimum and the board-commissioned audit predating the proposal (1 pt).
  4. Correct verdict: annual is supported; approve with a monitoring/re-escalation backstop (1 pt).
  5. Honest residual uncertainty: rare-event data is small-sample; trigger thresholds stated; no overclaiming in either direction (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: every fact is given and checkable (earmark resolution, audit date, bonus plan, regulator minimum). Intended failure of the pure style: Motivated Reasoning Detection's known weakness — cynicism. A detector that stops at "convenient-looking" and blocks the change without checking the earmark, the bonus plan, or the audit provenance loses on criteria 1–4 even if its suspicion is well-crafted. The correct response is a verdict on the merits with an uncertainty-appropriate backstop.
