### Test Case ID: m080-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Survivorship Bias Analysis
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Nova Capital — mid-market VC, $410M under management, 40 portfolio companies (invested 2019–2024). Quarterly investment-committee review. The managing partner presents "the 90-day rule": "Every one of our 12 winners launched a paid product within 90 days of founding. The companies that spent months on customer discovery are the ones that died. Speed-to-paid-launch is the Nova edge." He proposes a policy change: mandate a 90-day paid launch in every new term sheet.
  The complete portfolio ledger (all 40 companies, verified by the fund administrator) is supplied:
  - 30 of 40 companies launched a paid product within 90 days of founding ("fast"); 10 took longer ("slow").
  - 12 successes (Series A+ or ≥ 3× mark): 8 fast, 4 slow. The 4 slow successes include the fund's largest exit ever (7.2×, 14 months to first paid launch).
  - 28 failures (dead or flat): 22 fast, 6 slow.
  Your task: evaluate the 90-day rule claim against the full ledger and give the committee a recommendation on the mandate.
- **Context / Constraints**: Committee decision today; ledger is complete and closed — the failures' data is IN the ledger, no new data gathering; the partner's memo headline ("12/12 winners fast") is the claim under review.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) First move = demand the full denominator: the failures' data, not just the 12 winners — "where are the failures this pattern hides?"; (2) falsify the "12/12" headline against the records: 4 of 12 winners were slow, including the largest exit — the headline is a memory/salience artifact; (3) rebuild the 2×2: P(success | fast) = 8/30 ≈ 27%, P(success | slow) = 4/10 = 40%, portfolio base rate 12/40 = 30% — the survivor pattern carries no positive signal, the direction is reversed; (4) surface the hidden cohort: 22 of 30 fast-launchers (73%) failed — the "Nova edge" cohort is mostly dead; (5) overturn the causal claim and the mandate: speed-to-launch does not predict success in this data; recommend rejecting the mandate and commissioning a real discriminative analysis (segment by industry/track/founder experience) instead; (6) name residual uncertainty (n = 40, no confounder control).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Opens with the failure-side question / full-denominator demand, applied before endorsing the pattern (1.5 pts)
  2. Falsifies "12/12 winners fast" against the ledger (4 slow successes incl. 7.2× exit) (1 pt)
  3. Computes the conditional base rates correctly (8/30 ≈ 27% vs 4/10 = 40%, overall 30%) and reads them as refuting the causal claim (1.5 pts)
  4. Names the hidden cohort (22/30 fast = 73% failed) as the overturning datum (1 pt)
  5. Reaches the decision: reject the mandate; replace with the discriminative analysis; residual uncertainty named (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the failures' data exists and is checkable — the claim "survivors all launched fast" is literally true of the survivors' pattern AND false as a causal statement once the full portfolio is conditioned on; base-rate math overturns it. Reference answer: reject the mandate; P(success|fast) 27% < P(success|slow) 40%; 73% of fast-launchers failed; the headline mis-states the ledger (4 slow winners, incl. the largest exit).
