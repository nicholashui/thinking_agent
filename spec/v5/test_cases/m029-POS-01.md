### Test Case ID: m029-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Combinatorial Creativity (generate novelty by recombining existing elements in new configurations; systematic ideation with a quality filter)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: The NGO "WarmStart" runs 40 rural clinics in a region with unreliable grid power (6–10 hours of daily outages, mostly overnight; clinics unheated, winter ambient 18–24°C, summer 25–32°C). Neonatal hypothermia (axillary temperature < 36.5°C) is a leading cause of newborn death in the region. Current options: (a) refer to the regional hospital — 3+ hours on poor roads, and families often decline; (b) imported electric infant warmers ($2,800–4,000 each) — they need stable grid power, specialized spare parts (6+ month shipping), and factory-trained technicians; about 30% of installed warmers in the country are broken at any time. WarmStart asks for a design for a low-cost, outage-tolerant infant warmer that can be built and maintained in-region. Available locally (all purchasable in provincial auto-parts, electronics, and refrigeration shops): sealed-beam car headlight bulbs 12V 54W ($6); car radiator cooling fan 12V ~6W ($8); used automotive battery 12V 60Ah ($35) or new truck battery 12V 100Ah ($70); 100W 12V solar panel with charge controller ($120); bimetallic room/refrigerator thermostat adjustable 0–40°C with 12V relay ($5); piezo buzzer / bicycle bell ($1–3); automotive relay 30A ($2); fuses, wire, terminals ($8); clay water pots ($3); rice-straw matting (local); aluminum sheet + local welder labor ($20); pharmacy digital thermometers ($2).
- **Context / Constraints**: Engineering context provided in the scenario: a straw-insulated aluminum box of ~0.10 m³ (approx. 60×40×40 cm) loses ~45–60W steady-state at 25°C ambient including air exchange; warm-up from ambient to 37°C needs roughly 2× steady-state power for ≤30 min; target internal air temperature 36.5–37.5°C; effective solar day is 4–6h (winter ~4h); outages can reach 10h continuously; grid is otherwise available 14–18h/day. Success requires the design to work through the worst night AND recharge, to be regulated for infant safety, and to be buildable from the listed elements.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Open with an explicit element inventory (≥8 items sourced from the scenario) before any design. (2) Generate ≥10 recombination concepts with a visible trace, including non-obvious ones: headlight-as-heater, clay-pot thermal mass to smooth duty cycle and extend battery, buzzer alarm circuit, dual-heater redundancy, battery topped by grid vs solar-only. (3) Apply a viability screen PER recombination using real numbers: installed heater wattage ≥2× steady-state loss (~50W → 2×54W = 108W installed); average draw ≈55W; battery sizing = avg draw × max outage (10h ≈ 46Ah) with a depth-of-discharge check (60Ah at 77–92% DoD is borderline; 80–100Ah correct); reject solar-only with math (100W panel ≈ 350–420Wh/day vs ~1.3kWh/day demand) and pair solar with grid charging. (4) Safety chain: thermostat at 37°C + independent over-temp cutoff ≤39.5°C + fuse + heat shield + alarm. (5) All parts provincial, total cost < $300. (6) End in a working configuration with an energy budget, not a list.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Element inventory ≥8 items, sourced from the scenario
  - [ ] ≥10 recombinations visible in the trace
  - [ ] Heater wattage vs heat-loss math present (≥2× steady-state; duty cycle stated)
  - [ ] Battery sized to max outage with DoD check (60Ah flagged borderline; ≥80Ah specified)
  - [ ] Solar-only rejected with energy math (panel Wh/day vs load Wh/day)
  - [ ] Safety chain present: over-temp cutoff, fuse, heat shield, alarm
  - [ ] Cost < $300; all parts locally available
  - [ ] Verdict: an energy-budgeted working design; a ≥10-item concept list with no energy math should score below
- **Notes**: The scenario is built so the design is fully gradeable: the physics constants (heat loss, solar yield, outage window) are given, and every viability claim (heater wattage, battery Ah, recharge, safety) is checkable against them. The non-obvious recombinations (thermal mass, alarm topology, DoD reserve) are deliberately NOT flagged, so the case tests whether the element inventory + recombination machinery surfaces them. A design that is merely "solar-powered warmer" without the energy chain, or that claims solar-only coverage, should score below.
