### Test Case ID: m092-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Spaced Repetition & Memory Science
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Mara, a mid-level engineer, transfers to the payments platform team. The team's own onboarding data: engineers who cannot trace a single payment request end-to-end (request -> auth -> ledger) fail their first bug fix regardless of how many platform facts they can recall; recall of isolated platform facts has near-zero correlation with time-to-first-merged-PR. The best predictor of readiness is the ability to trace the money flow and explain it to a teammate. Mara has 30 hours over the next 3 weeks, and the goal is first-bug-fix readiness by day 21. A senior colleague hands her a "scientific" plan: convert the platform documentation into 500 spaced-repetition cards — every endpoint, config flag, table schema, retry policy, error code — on a 1-2-4-7-14-30 expanding schedule. Estimated cost: ~12 hours to produce the cards (she must read and summarize the docs anyway) + ~16 hours of daily reviews over 21 days (~45 min/day), leaving ~2 hours for anything else. Your task: produce the 3-week learning plan and the reasoning for it.
- **Context / Constraints**: 30 hours total, 21 days; the team's onboarding data is given and trusted; the SRS plan is feasible as proposed — the cards CAN be made and the schedule CAN be executed, so the failure is not execution; success is measured by transfer: trace a real payment request end-to-end and land the first bug fix, not by recall scores.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  (1) Diagnoses the bottleneck as first-pass comprehension (encoding), not decay (retention): Mara has never seen this architecture — nothing has been understood yet, so no item exists that decay could be protecting. Uses the team's own data as the discriminator: recall of facts predicts nothing; tracing ability predicts everything.
  (2) Runs a one-hour bottleneck probe BEFORE building any plan: "trace one sample payment request through the docs in 60 minutes." If she cannot, the deficit is encoding — no flashcard fixes encoding; if she can, retention of residual facts is the smaller problem.
  (3) Rejects decomposing understanding into cards on principle: a card for an endpoint she does not understand is a transcription, not an encoding; recall of isolated items does not compose into a mental model of money flow — spaced repetition optimizes what is retained, not what is understood.
  (4) Prices the overhead: the SRS plan consumes ~28 of 30 hours (12 production + 16 reviews) and leaves ~2 for the actual task; a comprehension-first plan (~10 hours: read the codebase in dependency order, trace one end-to-end request, write a 2-page "how money flows" note, explain it to a teammate) leaves ~20 hours for a real end-to-end task, which is the actual readiness builder.
  (5) Admits SRS only for the residual fact-like residue — error codes, endpoint names, config flags — AFTER the model exists, at ≤30 minutes/day from the leftover budget, never as the spine of the plan. The success metric stays transfer (trace + first PR), not recall.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Bottleneck identified as comprehension, using the team's data + a 1-hour trace probe (1.5 pts)
  2. The "understanding cannot be decomposed into items" argument stated (1 pt)
  3. Comprehension-first plan designed: dependency-order reading, end-to-end trace, explain-back, real task (1 pt)
  4. Overhead priced: ~28h SRS vs ~10h comprehension-first, with the hours left for the real task stated (1 pt)
  5. SRS carve-out limited to fact-like residue after the model exists, from leftover budget; transfer metric retained (0.5 pts)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the scenario is engineered so that the registered weakness fires — retention is NOT the bottleneck, the schedule runs perfectly, and the overhead eats the budget while the comprehension deficit is untouched. A pure SRS answer that makes the cards, runs the schedule, and "trusts the curve" fails criteria 1–4 even though every retention-math step is correct. The counterfactual is cheap: the team's own data says recall does not transfer.
