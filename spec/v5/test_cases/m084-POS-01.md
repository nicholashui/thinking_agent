### Test Case ID: m084-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Collective Action & Coordination
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Verano Valley irrigation district — 12 farms draw from one canal. Each season the canal must be dredged and the headgate repaired, or it silts up and delivers no water at all. A dredge shift costs any farmer 1 unit (a day of labor or hired help). If the canal is serviced by 8 or more shifts, it works for the season and every farm earns 3 units of crop value — including farms that contributed nothing. If fewer than 8 shifts happen, the canal fails and every farm earns 0. All 12 know these numbers; each season's servicing decision is effectively one-shot; no farmer can be forced to work and the district has no enforcement staff. Last season 3 shifts were contributed and the canal failed; the season before, 5 shifts and it failed. The council's working theory is "these farmers are lazy."
  Your task: explain to the district council why the canal keeps failing — it is not laziness — and design the fix: what institution, rule, or mechanism would reliably get the canal serviced, with the specific design details that make it work.
- **Context / Constraints**: n = 12, one-shot per season; the failure pattern repeats (3 shifts, then 5); the council can adopt any rule it can verify cheaply (the farms sit on one canal; neighbors see each other's work); no budget for hired enforcement or subsidies; deliverable = diagnosis + institution design with the mechanism that makes contribution individually rational.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Diagnose the structure, not the character: a threshold public good — serviceable at 8 of 12 contributions; any single farmer's shift is pivotal only when exactly 7 others have committed; in the one-shot game the contribution equilibrium is severe under-provision (each farmer rationally free-rides if 8 others work, and rationally declines if fewer will — the shift is wasted either way); the observed 3 and 5 shifts are the equilibrium expressing itself, not apathy; (2) compute the efficiency gap: 8 shifts cost 8 against 36 units of benefit (12 × 3) — the district forgoes 36 per failed season; (3) design the institution with the mechanism that restores individual rationality: contingent access / excludability (water shut off to farms with unworked, unexcused shifts — converts a public good into a club good and makes a shift the price of entry), cheap endogenous monitoring (neighbors on one canal observe every shift), graduated sanctions (first miss: warning + access probation; repeat: exclusion); (4) anticipate the institution's own failure modes: measurement disputes (define "shift" as a logged board unit), sanction credibility (sequence delivery to compliant farms first, making exclusion self-enforcing), collusion (cross-reporting by all 12); (5) land the decision: adopt the contingent-access rule.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Correct structure diagnosis: threshold public good (8/12), one-shot free-rider equilibrium = under-provision, observed failures are payoff structure, not culture (1.5 pts)
  2. Correct efficiency math: 8 shifts (cost 8) vs 36 benefit; the gap named (1 pt)
  3. Mechanism design makes contribution individually rational: excludability / contingent access or equivalent selective incentive; monitoring cheap and feasible; graduated sanctions (1.5 pts)
  4. Institution failure modes anticipated and at least one closed concretely (measurement, sanction credibility, collusion) (1 pt)
  5. Clear decision: adopt the institution (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the payoff numbers make the free-rider structure unambiguous (threshold public good), so the answer is not "motivate people" or "just ask" — it is mechanism design; the fix must change the payoff so contribution is a best response, and must name how the institution polices itself. Reference answer: threshold public good at 8/12; one-shot equilibrium = under-provision, so the observed failures ARE the equilibrium; fix = contingent access (water closed to non-contributors), peer monitoring, graduated sanctions, with at least one failure mode closed (board-log definition, compliant-first sequencing); adopt the rule.
