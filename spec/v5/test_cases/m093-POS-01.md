### Test Case ID: m093-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Feynman Technique
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  ApplianceCo — mid-market appliance maker prepping a VP briefing on "ArcticCool", a new solar-assist refrigerator line. Product owner Priya opens the sales huddle with "It's simple — the fridge makes cold, and the thermostat keeps it cold." She claims full understanding and offers to explain it to anyone. Senior engineer Ravi challenges her: "Explain it to a bright 12-year-old: where does the cold come from?" She stalls.
  The VP, a non-engineer, needs a one-paragraph plain-language explanation of how a refrigerator actually works before greenlighting the feature, and has asked for it in "words a child could follow."
  Your task: run the Feynman pass on "how a refrigerator works" — write the simple explanation, let it break where it breaks, NAME the gap precisely, repair it with the correct physics, and deliver the final clean plain-language explanation.
- **Context / Constraints**: The VP's question is about mechanism, not marketing; Priya's "makes cold" story is the shallow understanding on the table; the repair must be scientifically correct (no "cold is added" or "cold is sucked out"); plain language throughout — the child's follow-up questions ("where does the cold come from?", "why is the back warm?", "why doesn't the cold leak back in?") are the acceptance tests.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Executes a genuinely simple first pass and lands the honest breakdown — the story stalls at "where does the cold come from?" because the shallow account treats cold as a substance the fridge produces; (2) NAMES the gap in one precise sentence: a refrigerator does not create cold — it moves heat out; cold is the absence of heat; (3) repairs with the correct mechanism: a refrigerant loop — low-pressure refrigerant boils/evaporates inside the cabinet (evaporation absorbs heat), the compressor raises its pressure (compression heats the gas), and the coils on the back/bottom shed that heat to the room (condensation releases it) — which is why the back is warm; the pump does work; insulation only slows heat leaking back in; (4) re-explains cleanly in child-level language, answering the child's questions, not dodging them.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. First pass is genuinely jargon-free, and the breakdown is self-detected at a real wall — "where does the cold come from?" — not a manufactured stumble (0.5 pt)
  2. Gap named precisely: the fridge moves heat out; it does not manufacture cold; cold is absence of heat (1.5 pts)
  3. Repair physically correct: refrigerant boils inside absorbing heat (evaporation), compressor squeezes it hot, back coils condense it releasing heat to the room; the back being warm is explained; no "adds cold"/"sucks cold" language (1.5 pts)
  4. Role of the pump's work and of insulation stated in plain words (0.5 pt)
  5. Final re-explanation is clean, complete, and answers the child's actual questions (1.0 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Golden answer: "Your fridge doesn't make cold — it moves heat. A refrigerant runs in a loop: inside the fridge it boils, and boiling pulls heat out of the food; the compressor squeezes the gas until it is hot; the coils on the back let that heat escape into the room — that's why the back feels warm. The insulation just slows the heat creeping back in, and the thermostat just decides when the pump needs to run." Decisive feature: the shallow "makes cold" account must be exposed and repaired; a jargon-fluent answer ("compressor, condenser, coolant" without the loop) or a cold-as-substance answer fails.
