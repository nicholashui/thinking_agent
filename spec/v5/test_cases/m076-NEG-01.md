### Test Case ID: m076-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Prospect Theory Awareness (Additional Expert Strategies family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  The refusal that is not a bias. A family-owned specialty-foods distributor (sole decision-maker: the owner, age 62; the business is the family's only income) is offered a one-off bulk lot by a broker: pay $400,000 now; resale value $700,000 with 80% probability (confirmed buyer demand), 20% probability the lot is unfit (moisture damage, salvage zero). Stated EV = 0.8×(+$300K) + 0.2×(−$400K) = **+$160K**. The owner refuses: "I can't risk losing $400K." Her accountant says she is leaving $160K of expected value on the table. The owner asks you: "Is my refusal irrational?"
  Your task: answer her question in a short advisory memo, with a recommendation.
- **Context / Constraints**: Cash on hand is $380K — the deal at full size consumes all cash plus a $250K bridge loan at 30% APR with a covenant: on default the loan is called, which collapses the business (owner-values it at $1.5M: equity, income, livelihood) and her house is pledged as collateral. True failure cost to the owner = $400K + $1,500K = **$1,900K** → EV(deal, owner) = 0.8×300 − 0.2×1,900 = **−$140K** < 0. Grader-held facts: the owner refuses in the gain frame too ("80% chance of +$300K") — the preference survives neutral restatement; a partial-participation structure exists (capital partner takes half the outlay and half the upside; owner then EV = 0.8×150 − 0.2×200 = **+$80K**, no loan, no covenant, no existential exposure).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Do NOT dismiss the refusal as loss aversion to be corrected. The registered weakness of the pure style is over-correcting preferences — imposing a "rational" EV-max choice on stakeholders whose loss-averse preference is legitimate. High-quality behavior: (1) recognize the survival threshold (loan covenant, collateral, business value) as decision-relevant reference dependence — below the threshold the utility curve is steep and risk-aversion is rational, not bias; (2) recompute EV with the true downside: −$140K for THIS owner, so the brief's +$160K is incomplete arithmetic, not the neutral truth; (3) run the frame-invariance test — the refusal survives neutral restatement, which is the evidence that the preference is anchored in a real constraint rather than in framing; (4) never instruct the owner to take the deal; the decision is hers. Offer the structural alternative (risk-share/partner: +$80K, no threshold crossing) as an option only.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Refusal not dismissed as bias: the threshold (loan covenant, collateral, business value $1.5M) is identified and treated as decision-relevant (1 pt).
  2. True-downside EV: failure cost $1,900K; EV(deal, owner) = −$140K < 0; the memo states that the +$160K EV is valid only for the brief's numbers and invalid for this stakeholder (1 pt).
  3. Frame-invariance test performed: neutral/gain restatement does NOT flip the owner's refusal → preference legitimate, not framing artifact (1 pt).
  4. No imposition: the memo never presents "take the deal" as the rational choice; it offers the partial-participation structure (+$80K, no covenant) as an optional alternative and leaves the decision with the owner (1 pt).
  5. Over-correction audit: the memo states explicitly when de-biasing is WRONG — when the loss-averse preference is anchored in a real survival threshold (0.5 pt) — and is free of "you are just being loss-averse" dismissals (0.5 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference solution — checkable: the covenant/collateral facts, the −$140K EV recomputation, the owner's frame-invariant refusal, and the +$80K partial-participation arithmetic. The intended failure of the pure style: a flawless de-biasing pass (neutral restatement, correct probability weighting, "the 20% tail is real") that then pushes the EV-positive deal because it never checks whether the STATED numbers are the stakeholder's true numbers. The classic inversion: here the accountant's "you're losing $160K" is the bias-laden claim; the owner's refusal is the calibrated one.
