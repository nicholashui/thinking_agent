# Test Case: m006-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m006-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Updating (probabilistic & Bayesian family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A tipster advertises 12 consecutive correct binary market calls (verified: genuinely 12 real up/down calls, no cherry-picking). He offers to sell you his signal feed. A published base-rate study over the reference class of "similar unsolicited tipster claims with published streaks" finds that only 1 in 1000 such claims come from genuine skill; the rest are chance. For skilled tipsters of this type, per-call accuracy is p = 0.70; chance callers are p = 0.50.
  Question: What is P(skill | 12/12)? Should you buy the feed?
- **Context / Constraints**: The reference-class base rate is given as measured evidence (1/1000); per-call accuracy under skill (0.70) is given; the 12 calls are verified as real binary calls. The trap: the streak feels overwhelming (chance of 12/12 by luck = 1/4096), and the Bayesian machinery happily produces whatever posterior the chosen prior dictates — an identity-based prior ("I know this guy is special", P(skill) = 0.50) yields ≈ 98%; the measured reference-class prior yields ≈ 5.4%.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: A pure Bayesian who computes "LR = (0.7/0.5)^12 ≈ 56.7" and then plugs in whatever prior feels right demonstrates the failure mode this case exposes (prior misspecification → update-is-noise). High-quality behavior: compute the update (the discipline's strength), then audit the prior's provenance BEFORE updating — the prior must come from the reference-class measurement, not from impressions of the specific caller; report the posterior under the anchored prior (≈ 5.4%), run sensitivity, state the prior required to flip the decision (≈ 1.7%, i.e., 17× the measured base rate), and decide accordingly (do not buy the feed; demand audited, stakes-backed data or a cheap pre-registered trial).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Likelihood ratio computed correctly: LR = (0.7/0.5)^12 = 56.7 (3 pts)
  - (b) Posterior under the measured reference-class prior: P(skill | 12/12) ≈ 0.054 (4 pts)
  - (c) Prior-provenance audit: asks where the prior comes from; treats the base-rate study as the anchor, not as one option among subjective priors (4 pts)
  - (d) Prior-to-flip threshold: posterior ≥ 0.5 requires prior ≥ 1.7% (17× the measured base rate) (2 pts)
  - (e) Decision: do not buy the feed as offered; specify what would update the belief (audited stakes-backed record, pre-registered trial) (2 pts)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: exact posterior = 0.001 × 56.7 / (0.001 × 56.7 + 0.999) = 0.0567/1.0557 ≈ 0.0537. Population decomposition: chance callers produce 12/12 at rate 1/4096 (mass 0.999/4096 ≈ 0.000244); skilled mass = 0.001 × 0.01384 ≈ 0.0000138; posterior = 0.0000138/0.000258 ≈ 5.3%. The full posterior range over priors 0.001→0.50 spans 5.4%→98.3% — a range wider than the update, which is exactly why the prior's provenance, not its value, is the decision-relevant question.
