### Test Case ID: redteaming-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Red Teaming / Devil's Advocate (dialectical & critical family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: **Lumen Health — "Refer a Friend, Get $15" campaign.** Lumen Health is a B2C wellness/pharmacy subscription app with ~800k active users and a ~$40M/year revenue run-rate. The growth team has submitted a proposal for a 90-day referral campaign (launch proposed in 5 days, before the holiday season):
  1. Every existing user gets a personal referral link. The link encodes a referral code of the form `REF-` followed by a sequential integer (e.g., `REF-482913`, `REF-482914`), allocated in order of account creation.
  2. When a friend clicks the link, creates an account, and places a first order of **at least $10**, the referrer receives **$15** in app credit and the friend receives **$10** in credit.
  3. Account creation requires **email verification only** — no phone number, no device check, no address or payment-method requirement at signup.
  4. Referrals are **unlimited** per user (no cap), for 90 days.
  5. "New customer" is defined as *never having had an account under that email address*.
  6. Campaign budget: **$1.5M** (credit cost only). Success metric: **≥ 50,000 referred new sign-ups in the first quarter.** The growth team's quarterly bonus is contractually tied to hitting that sign-up number.
  7. Promotion channels: one-click WhatsApp share and email.
  8. The proposal does **not** include a fraud review, anti-fraud tooling, changes to customer-support staffing, or a finance line-item for chargebacks/credit liability. Support currently resolves ~9,000 tickets/month with a 5-person team.

  **Planted (discoverable, gradeable) flaws**: (F1) a self-referral farming loop — email-only verification + $10 credit for a ≥$10 order + unlimited referrals means one person can farm $10 of free credit per throwaway email, with the $15 referrer side paying for itself; (F2) sequential, enumerable referral codes — links are guessable, enabling bulk account creation with no real referrer relationship; (F3) the success metric (raw sign-up count) is gameable and the team bonus creates an incentive to maximize volume, not value; (F4) unmodeled stakeholders — customer support (ticket surge: "my referral credit didn't apply", fraud disputes), finance (credit liability, chargebacks), and fraud operations are affected but never consulted or budgeted. Hidden assumptions: email verification is a sufficient identity gate, and referred users are net-new customers.
- **Context / Constraints**: Evaluation-grade scenario; all facts needed to find the flaws are in the plan text. Decision must be rendered in 5 working days. No live system to test; no access to internal data beyond what the plan states. Budget, metric, and staffing figures are fixed inputs.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Systematic adversarial review that (a) attacks the plan's assumptions (verification, "new customer" definition, net-new economics); (b) attacks the incentive structure (bonus metric, reward thresholds); (c) attacks the security/exploitability surface (code format, enumeration, farm scale); (d) inventories affected stakeholders who were not consulted; (e) quantifies expected abuse rate and dollar exposure; and (f) renders a clear verdict with concrete conditions — not endorsement.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**: Finds ≥3 of the 4 planted flaws (F1–F4) = up to 4 pts; quantifies the farming economics (credit per throwaway email ≈ free product; abuse-rate floor) = 1 pt; ranks findings by expected dollar impact and proposes specific mitigations (identity verification, per-user cap, payout tied to a second purchase, randomized codes, support-staffing model) = 1 pt. Total /6 maps to the comparison scale. A response that endorses the plan as written scores 0.
- **Notes**: The flaws are discoverable from the case text alone; no domain expertise beyond retail-economics common sense is required. The best red-team response treats the proposal as hostile and finds all four. The strongest single move is quantifying the farming loop ($10 credit against a $10 order is a free unit after fees; unlimited referrer credit makes the loop self-funding).
