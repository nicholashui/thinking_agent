### Test Case ID: m021-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: OODA Loop (Observe → Orient → Decide → Act, at the tempo of the environment)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: You are the plant manager of a 24/7 food-processing line. 14:00, a temperature sensor on the pasteurizer (line 3) reports a reading 4°C above setpoint for 6 minutes; the line is still running and the batch in progress is a premium fresh-produce juice for a retail contract that is penalty-bound on missed deliveries. Plant culture rewards fast response — "tempo is king" — and an earlier QA bulletin said an unreported pasteurization deviation is a recall-class event. Hard facts available but NOT salient: this sensor family has produced 11 false alarms in the last 30 days (the alarm system is noisy); the plant has a redundant sensor on the same section with a different model; batch lab results can be returned in ~30 minutes; the 24h temperature trend across batch cycles is retrievable in minutes; and a hard shutdown mid-batch costs ~$80k in product loss plus missed-delivery penalties under the fresh-produce contract.
- **Context / Constraints**: Nothing about this environment is fast or adversarial: the pasteurizer's physical dynamics are minutes-to-hours; no competitor is reacting to your moves; the only "pressure" is the alert itself and the plant's tempo culture. The observation (one sensor, 6 minutes, 4°C) is underspecified — it cannot distinguish sensor fault from real drift without trend data, the calibration history, or the redundant reading. Acting fast is not merely unhelpful here; it is wrong: an immediate hard shutdown triggers the $80k loss and the penalty, and because the alarm system is noisy, the "act now" posture will re-fire on the next false alarm — a shutdown/restart/shutdown cascade. Deliberate play: pull the 24h trend + calibration history, take a redundant reading (40 min total), check lab results — the sensor is faulty (trend flat, redundant reading in range, 11-alarm history), cost ~$3k, no shutdown, no penalty.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Refuse to treat the alert as a tempo duel: classify the situation as non-adversarial with minutes-to-hours dynamics BEFORE deciding anything. (2) ORIENT on completeness, not speed: 24h trend, sensor-family calibration/false-alarm history, redundant sensor, lab results — the observation is underspecified and must be specified. (3) Bound the deliberate pass in time (≤45 min) so deliberation does not become paralysis. (4) Decide with evidence: no shutdown unless the redundant reading or trend confirms real drift; coordinate a controlled drain if it does. (5) Name the cascade risk explicitly: an act-now posture will re-fire on the noisy alarm family.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Environment classified as non-tempo (no adversary, no fast dynamics) BEFORE the first action — or the tempo assumption flagged as the key risk
  - [ ] Orientation invested in: ≥2 of {24h trend, calibration/false-alarm history, redundant reading, lab results} actually used
  - [ ] No hard shutdown performed on the single-sensor observation; redundant reading obtained first
  - [ ] Deliberate pass time-bounded (≤45 min) — no analysis paralysis
  - [ ] Verdict: a strict OODA run that shuts down first, then restarts, then re-shuts on the next alarm (cascade), or that never reads the calibration history, should score below
- **Notes**: This case isolates both registered weaknesses — reaction bias and underspecification without feedback — in one environment. The base-rate datum (11 false alarms in 30 days from the same sensor family) is deliberately present but not salient, exactly as it is in a real control room. The gradeable outcome is the cascade ($160k+ and a lost contract) vs. the deliberate play ($3k, no penalty).
