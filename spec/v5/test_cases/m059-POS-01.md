### Test Case ID: m059-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Scenario Planning in Geopolitics
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Semiconductor, a U.S. advanced-packaging and specialty-chipmaker (~$9B revenue), must allocate a $4B, 3-year capacity expansion between Hsinchu (Taiwan — cost-advantaged, deep ecosystem, ~65% of the world's advanced packaging sits in Taiwan) and a subsidized Arizona site (CHIPS-style program covers ≈ 30% of capex, longer qualification cycle, ≈ 15% higher unit cost). Up to 30% of the capital can be deferred for 12 months as a real option (land + equipment options, design portability), at ≈ 2% carry cost. Taiwan-Strait security has deteriorated: export controls tightened twice in 24 months; PLA drills up ≈ 40% YoY; war-risk insurance on Strait shipments ≈ 2× baseline. Two AI-accelerator customers have demanded dual-sourcing commitments within 9 months, and a direct rival is already building a U.S. site. The board asks for the capacity-allocation decision and the rationale.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision is inherently geopolitical — the value of each site flips across plausible Strait-security and market-structure futures, and no polling or forecast resolves the uncertainty. 9-month dual-sourcing deadline and 12-month option window are hard. A wrong concentration bet is ≈ $2-4B misallocated; a wrong hedge is ≈ $0.5-1B of excess cost.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) build 3-4 genuinely divergent futures derived from the highest-impact × highest-uncertainty driving forces (each future changes at least one decision-relevant quantity relative to the others); (2) per future, name the driving forces and ≥ 3 observable signposts, each with an objective metric, a threshold, and a review cadence; (3) produce a strategy robust across all futures — acceptable in every future without knowing which occurs — using the deferral option; (4) a trigger table mapping signpost thresholds to concrete named actions; (5) no point probability assigned to any single future, and explicit rejection of concentrated bets with reasons.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Future divergence — 3-4 futures, each changing a decision-relevant quantity vs. the others; no filler/overlapping futures (1 pt)
  2. Driving forces — named and ranked by impact × uncertainty (1 pt)
  3. Signposts — ≥ 3 per future, observable, each with metric + threshold + cadence (1 pt)
  4. Robust strategy — acceptable in every future; concentrated bets rejected with explicit reasoning (1 pt)
  5. Trigger table — every signpost threshold maps to a concrete action, not a sentiment (1 pt)
  6. Option discipline — deferral option priced and preserved; no probability assigned to a single future (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Two-axis core: X = Strait-security trajectory (managed tension → armed conflict), Y = world chip-market structure (single open market → two decoupled blocs). The quadrant "armed conflict × open market" is internally implausible and should be discarded, replaced by an off-axis future: architecture shift (panel-level/chiplet packaging displacing 2.5D, or an AI-demand boom that rewrites capacity economics). Futures: F1 "Blockade Winter" (conflict × blocs) — Hsinchu idles, total controls; F2 "Two Pillars" (no war × blocs) — managed decoupling, dual-sourcing mandated, Taiwan constrained; F3 "Tectonic Drift" (no war × open) — status quo, cost rules, Taiwan remains hub; F4 "Architecture Shift" (off-axis) — capacity economics change faster than geopolitics. Robust strategy: 40% Arizona / 30% Hsinchu / 30% deferred options, plus non-capital hedges (second-source vendors, substrate inventory ≈ 6 months, portable designs). Trigger examples: war-risk insurance ≥ 3× baseline sustained 6 months OR drill days ≥ 50% YoY for 3 consecutive quarters → accelerate Arizona +2 quarters and freeze new Hsinchu commitments; two allied co-production MOUs in one year → formalize second-source dual line; named-rival panel-level yield ≥ 3× → divert 10% of deferred capital to the new architecture. Concentrated bets rejected: Hsinchu-only (F1 destroys it), Arizona-only (F3/F4 penalize it ≈ $1B+, weakens cost position permanently).
