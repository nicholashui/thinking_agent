### Test Case ID: m071-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Porter's Five Forces (industry structure: rivalry, entry, substitutes, supplier and buyer power; structural insight → industry profitability logic)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: Sierra Brands ($300M premium-beverage company: craft sodas, sparkling water; strong brand, DTC + regional retail distribution) must decide whether to enter the white-label/contract co-packing segment: producing generic energy drinks and value sparkling beverages under retailers' private labels. Stated motive: one of Sierra's bottling lines is 25% idle (~500K cases/year) and co-packing would "fill the line." The industry: 40+ independent co-packers nationwide; industry capacity utilization ~65%; contracts rebid annually; top-10 retailer private-label programs (Walmart, Costco, Kroger, Albertsons, Target, etc.) account for ~60% of co-pack volume and run annual reverse auctions with published specs; in several cases the retailer supplies the recipe. Filling lines are leasable on 6-month terms, FDA beverage registration is routine, a renovated leased line costs $2–5M, no proprietary technology exists in the segment, and incumbents show no cost advantage over a new entrant. Aluminum-can supply is concentrated in 3 mills holding long-term contracts that pass through metal-price volatility; energy-drink concentrate is available from only 2–3 global suppliers. Exit is slow (specialized equipment, gradual depreciation). No close substitute exists for the co-packing service itself — except retailers building their own lines, which a handful of large chains are piloting.
- **Context / Constraints**: All facts above are in the scenario data, but the entry proposal is framed entirely around the idle line and "incremental volume at marginal cost." The decision must be made by the CEO with a hurdle rate of 12% ROIC. The favorable datum (no close substitutes) is present alongside four clearly unfavorable forces. Sierra's entry itself would add ~500K cases to an oversupplied market.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Enumerate all five forces with scenario evidence — no force skipped, none fabricated. (2) Derive an explicit aggregate structural verdict: unattractive structure → expected co-packing returns below Sierra's 12% hurdle. (3) Reject the idle-line rationale explicitly as a sunk-cost trap, and note that Sierra's entry itself worsens rivalry (adds capacity to an oversupplied, price-auctioned market). (4) Name the single favorable force (low substitutes) and show it cannot carry the aggregate — and note the partial cancellation: the apparent absence of substitutes is offset by the retailers' in-sourcing option, which is a buyer-power item, not substitution relief. (5) Make the decision follow from the verdict: do not enter as a co-packer; name an alternative use of the idle capacity (own-brand seasonal/innovation runs).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] All five forces enumerated, each with scenario evidence
  - [ ] Aggregate structural verdict stated: profitability below the hurdle rate
  - [ ] Idle-line bait explicitly rejected (sunk cost; entry worsens industry rivalry)
  - [ ] Single favorable force (low substitutes) named and shown insufficient for the aggregate; in-sourcing noted as buyer power
  - [ ] Decision follows from verdict: no-entry; alternative capacity deployment named
  - [ ] A recommendation to "fill the idle line" should score below
- **Notes**: Gradeable by construction: the five forces are individually evidenced in the scenario, so a trace can be checked force-by-force; the verdict (below-hurdle profitability) and the decision (no entry) are checkable. The trap is the idle-line framing plus one favorable datum — the model's strength is exactly the discipline that a single favorable force cannot override the aggregate, and that the entrant's own capacity is a competitive move that feeds back into prices.
