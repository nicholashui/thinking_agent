### Test Case ID: m090-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Minimum Viable Experiment (Build-Measure-Learn)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Atlas Analytics — B2B analytics SaaS for mid-market logistics firms; $2.8M ARR, 300 customers, 800 named executive contacts in CRM. The product VP presents "Project Prism": a native iOS + Android mobile analytics app. Proposal: 12 months, $450K, 4 of the 12 engineers, displacing 2 roadmap features. Rationale: (1) a 200-respondent survey shows 71% of executives "want mobile access to dashboards"; (2) competitor WaveMetrics launched a mobile app and sales reps report "losing deals to 'we need mobile' objections." The VP recommends starting the 12-month build immediately.
  Feasible cheap probes before commitment: (a) fake-door test — 2 weeks, ~$2K (3 engineer-days): show the 800 execs a "Mobile dashboard — get early access" gate in-app + in the weekly email; measure unique execs who click and join the waitlist; (b) concierge — 3 weeks, ~$12K (2 engineers): 20 accounts get a daily SMS/email digest of 3 key metrics + link to a read-only mobile-optimized view built on the existing API; measure weekly-active-exec rate.
  Your task: give the VP a decision rule for Prism — commit to the 12-month build, kill it, or run something first, with the reasoning.
- **Context / Constraints**: The 800 execs are reachable today via the existing in-app/email channel; the survey and the competitor anecdote are the only existing "evidence"; the decision is now, but no external clock forces it — the cost of waiting is bounded by the probe duration.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Restates the core assumption as a behavioral, falsifiable claim — "executives at current accounts will actively USE a mobile analytics channel weekly" — and downgrades the existing evidence: a 71% stated-intent survey is opinion, not behavior, and the WaveMetrics "lost deals" is secondhand hearsay without funnel data; (2) designs the smallest experiment that produces a behavioral signal on THAT assumption: the fake-door test first (2 weeks, ~$2K, <0.5% of the build cost), and a concierge as the graduated second loop only if the fake-door passes; (3) pre-commits kill/continue thresholds BEFORE data collection — fake-door: ≥120 waitlist signups of 800 (15%) or ≥20% click-through; concierge: ≥40% weekly-active execs over 3 weeks — and a miss kills Prism, reallocating the $450K; (4) explicitly declares what the probes do NOT test (retention beyond 3 weeks, platform lock-in, expansion revenue) and names the second learning loop (instrumented live beta, retention cohort) that is earned only after both gates pass; (5) gates the 12-month/$450K build on the combined thresholds and prices the option: kill-now costs $2K vs committing $450K against a stated-intent base rate that is known not to be behavior.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Core assumption reframed as behavioral WILL-USE; survey and competitor anecdote explicitly downgraded as non-behavioral (1 pt)
  2. Smallest valid experiment designed: fake-door first, concierge as graduated loop, costs/duration an order of magnitude below the build ($2K/$12K vs $450K; 2-5 weeks vs 12 months) (1.5 pts)
  3. Kill/continue thresholds pre-committed before data collection, with kill-path (reallocate the $450K) stated (1 pt)
  4. What-is-not-tested declared (retention/lock-in/expansion) + second-loop condition spelled (1 pt)
  5. The build is gated on the results, and the experiment is priced as an option vs the commitment (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the existing "evidence" is stated preference and hearsay — the smallest validating experiment must be behavioral and cheap, and the commitment must be gated on pre-committed thresholds; a survey-based or build-first answer fails. Reference answer: run the fake-door now (2 wks, $2K, ≥120/800 or ≥20% CTR) → if pass, concierge (3 wks, $12K, ≥40% weekly-active) → build only if both gates pass; kill on a miss; declare what is untested and earn the second loop.
