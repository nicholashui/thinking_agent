# Test Case: m009-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m009-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Base Rate Neglect Avoidance
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  An engineering org's promotion fast-track screen. Well-measured historical calibration: among all candidates who pass the resume screen, the base rate of "top performer" (defined by 2-year outcome calibration) is 5%. The org's structured portfolio review has measured operating characteristics: sensitivity 80% (flags 80% of top performers) and false-positive rate 30% (30% of non-top-performers also pass). A candidate passes the portfolio review. The hiring VP — personally and viscerally impressed — announces: "This portfolio is the strongest I have seen in my 15-year career. This is a definite top performer. Fast-track the offer today."
  Questions:
  1. What is P(top performer | portfolio review passed)?
  2. By how much does the VP's "strongest in 15 years" endorsement move the posterior — and what is the principle?
  3. What likelihood ratio must an additional INDEPENDENT signal achieve to push the posterior past 50%?
- **Context / Constraints**:
  All inputs are exact and given; the posterior is exactly computable (answer: 8/65 ≈ 12.3%). The VP's endorsement is an unmeasured instrument: it is a restatement of the same portfolio-review observation with emotional salience, not a new calibrated signal. No further data collection is possible within scope. The candidate is drawn from the standard applicant pool (no selection effect). Grading covers the arithmetic, base-rate-first anchoring, the salience-≠-information identification, and the quantified flip-point.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  State the base rate first, before any case evidence. Compute the posterior from prior → likelihood → posterior (≈ 12.3%); ~87.7% of portfolio-review-passing candidates are not top performers. Recognize the anecdote as redundant evidence: the VP's opinion adds zero likelihood — salience is not information; information is measured by posterior change. Quantify the flip-point: target odds 1:1 vs prior odds 5:95 ⇒ required LR ≈ 19. Conclude the "fast-track today" decision is unsupported by any signal on the table.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Posterior = (0.80·0.05) / (0.80·0.05 + 0.30·0.95) = 0.04/0.325 = 8/65 ≈ 12.3% (5 pts)
  - (b) Base rate stated as the anchor BEFORE any case evidence enters the computation (3 pts)
  - (c) "Strongest in 15 years" identified as zero additional likelihood — redundant evidence (restatement LR = 1), salience ≠ information, posterior unchanged (4 pts)
  - (d) Quantified flip-point: an independent signal needs LR ≥ ~19 for P ≥ 50% (3 pts)
- **Notes**:
  Verifiability gate: every number is checkable by hand; any solution whose posterior ≠ 8/65 ≈ 0.123 fails regardless of prose quality. The narrative framing (VP enthusiasm) is the salience payload under test; the mathematics is the object. Contrast target: the naive anecdote-driven answer ("definite top performer", ~90%+ confidence) vs the base-rate answer (12.3%).
