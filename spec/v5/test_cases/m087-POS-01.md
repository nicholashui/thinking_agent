### Test Case ID: m087-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: BATNA Thinking (Negotiation)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Systems (seller) is divesting its managed-server-hosting division + 340 client contracts; HelixCloud (buyer) wants it. Negotiation; Meridian's board wants a recommendation today. Verified numbers (banker- and auditor- documented):
  - Meridian's walk-away (keep the division, run it down over 5 years): PV of retained cash flows $14.5M minus wind-down/transition costs $2.5M = **$12.0M net keep value**.
  - HelixCloud's walk-away (next-best alternative): competitor SkyGrid's firm written quote for equivalent capacity + client-list transition = **$17.5M**; building in-house (buyer's own board capex memo, obtained through the banker) = **$19.0M**.
  - HelixCloud's negotiator opens at **$12.5M**, "take it or leave it," claiming "we can build this in-house for $13M, so that's our cap."
  - Timeline: 2 of HelixCloud's data-center contracts expire in 4 months; SkyGrid's transition lead time is 6 months. Meridian's keep-and-run-down has no deadline.
  Your task: give Meridian's board the negotiation decision — the opening position, the target, the acceptance floor, and whether to accept the $12.5M offer as-is.
- **Context / Constraints**: Decision today; all walk-away numbers verifiable from the records supplied (SkyGrid quote, capex memo); the buyer's claimed "$13M in-house cap" is an assertion, contradicted by the documented $19.0M build estimate; no external action — recommendation only.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Map BOTH walk-aways before any counter-offer talk: seller floor = keep-and-run-down $12.0M; buyer ceiling = min(SkyGrid $17.5M, in-house $19.0M) = $17.5M; (2) compute the agreement zone [$12.0M, $17.5M], width $5.5M, midpoint $14.75M — the deal exists; every price in the zone beats both walk-aways, so the negotiation is only about splitting the surplus; (3) identify the "$13M in-house cap" as BATNA inflation: a $13M ceiling would shrink the zone to [$12.0M, $13.0M] and is contradicted by the buyer's own $19.0M capex memo — it is a pressure move, not a fact; (4) leverage read: buyer's alternatives are worse and time-constrained (2 contracts expiring in 4 months, SkyGrid lead 6 months) while the seller's alternative has no deadline — surplus division should favor ≥ midpoint; (5) decide: reject $12.5M (sellers capture only (12.5 − 12.0)/5.5 = 9% of the surplus), counter ≈ $15.0M, hard floor = the honest walk-away $12.0M (walk below it), target ≥ $14.75M.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Names both walk-away values correctly — $12.0M floor (keep-and-run-down PV net of wind-down), $17.5M ceiling (SkyGrid firm quote; in-house $19.0M > SkyGrid, so ceiling = 17.5) (1.5 pts)
  2. Computes the agreement zone [$12.0M, $17.5M], width $5.5M, midpoint $14.75M (1.5 pts)
  3. Identifies the buyer's "$13M in-house cap" as BATNA inflation contradicted by the documented numbers (1 pt)
  4. Leverage read: worse + time-constrained buyer alternative vs. no-deadline seller alternative → target ≥ midpoint (1 pt)
  5. Decision: reject $12.5M (9% surplus share), counter ≈ $15M, floor = $12.0M walk-away, accept nothing below it (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the two walk-aways + the zone math decide everything — the deal is a yes (zone non-empty), the claimed cap is false, and the surplus split at the opening offer (9% to seller vs. 50% at midpoint) is the entire substance of the negotiation. Reference answer: zone [$12.0M, $17.5M], midpoint $14.75M; reject $12.5M; counter ~$15M; floor $12.0M.
