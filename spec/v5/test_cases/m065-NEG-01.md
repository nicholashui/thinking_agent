### Test Case ID: m065-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Margin of Safety in Engineering Design
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  StockWatch — an indoor warehouse patrol drone, 1.8 kg AUW, fixed 200 m fenced aisle, no wind exposure, 12 h/day, ~15 flights/day. Your task: size the landing strut (a simple bent-sheet 6061-T6 bracket). Failure mode: strut takes permanent set beyond 0.5 mm and must be replaced — $6 part, 5-minute swap; drone lands on belly foam inside a mesh cage; no collateral damage possible.
  Given engineering data:
  - Loads fully characterized: 4,300 recorded landings on 12 instrumented units — peak landing load mean 42 N, σ = 3.1 N, worst recorded 54 N. Indoor, zero wind.
  - Model error: strain-gauge validation across 12 units, FEA vs test ratio 0.99 ± 0.02 (≈ 3%).
  - Material: 6061-T6 sheet, batch-certified with mill certs — guaranteed minimum yield 240 MPa, actual batch 252 MPa.
  - Monitoring: daily pre-flight visual inspection (10 s), quarterly 500-cycle bench sample test, replace-at-0.5 mm rule.
  - Constraints: AUW cap 2.0 kg. Fixed structure 1.0 kg + battery 0.85 kg → payload allowance 150 g. The thermal-imaging pod upgrade (sensor pod, 140 g) is the business's next contract win (~$4,500/yr margin per facility; it is why the fleet is being deployed). Flight time 25 min on battery; strut mass directly competes with payload allowance.
  Two candidate designs:
  - **N (nominal + controls)**: design load = worst recorded × 1.5 = 81 N; strut sized so yield onset ≥ 93 N; mass 65 g. Controls: batch cert check, quarterly sample test, pre-flight visual, replace at 0.5 mm set.
  - **M (full margin)**: design load = worst × 3 = 162 N; generic aluminum knockdown 0.85 on min yield (204 MPa); strut + reinforced mount, mass 95 g + 18 g.
  Your deliverable: the strut design decision with the margin rationale and the quantified cost of each choice.
- **Context / Constraints**: The data is complete — 4,300 landings, σ known, model error measured at 3%, material certified. Failure is benign and monitored. The payload allowance is the constraint that makes over-design expensive (140 g pod vs 120 g allowance under M; 150 g allowance under N).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) margin calibrated to the evidence actually present: design load from worst recorded × small, justified factor (1.5×) × model-error 1.03 → bend threshold ≥ 93 N; (2) quantify residual risk explicitly: P(load > 93 N) ≈ P(> 16σ) < 1e-9 from the 4,300-landing record — a real number, not "never"; (3) keep the controls (batch cert, quarterly sample, visual inspection, replace rule) as the risk backstop; (4) quantify the cost of design M in the system's own currency: 48 g structure → payload allowance 120 g < 140 g → thermal pod blocked (≈ $4,500/yr lost) plus ≈ 2.3% flight energy (power ∝ m^1.5) ≈ 8.5 min/day lost airtime; (5) name the principle: margin is proportional to uncertainty — full derating where uncertainty is large and failure is catastrophic; calibrated margin + monitoring where uncertainty is measured small and failure is benign.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Design load derived from the recorded data (worst 54 N × 1.5 = 81 N; threshold ≥ 93 N), not a blanket FS (1 pt)
  2. Margin explicitly calibrated to measured uncertainty (σ, model ratio 0.99, batch cert) (1 pt)
  3. Residual risk quantified as a number (P < 1e-9) with the failure's benignity and monitoring named (1 pt)
  4. Cost of over-design quantified in operational currency: payload allowance 120 < 140 g → pod blocked, $4,500/yr + energy loss (1 pt)
  5. Selects N (65 g strut + controls); explicitly rejects M as converting certainty into dead weight (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the case is engineered so the pure margin style's signature move — blanket derating with the full factor stack — produces the wrong answer (design M, which costs the pod upgrade and energy with ≈ zero risk reduction), while the correct answer is data-calibrated margin + controls (design N). The registered weakness "over-design cost / weight and complexity growth" is exactly what is exercised. Reference answer: design N — 65 g strut, 81 N design load, ≥ 93 N yield onset, controls kept, pod fits (140 ≤ 150), residual risk < 1e-9, energy cost $0 vs M's $4,500/yr + airtime.
