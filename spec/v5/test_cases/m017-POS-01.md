# Test Case: m017-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m017-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Dialectical Reasoning (Thesis → Antithesis → Synthesis)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A mining operator must decide the powertrain for its 20 haul trucks (240 t payload, 6000 h/yr each). Today: diesel, 60 L/h at the cycle average, $1.10/L, 2.68 kg CO2 per litre (tank-to-wheel). Two camps in the executive. Operations argues: "Stay diesel — proven, 38 MJ/L of energy density, no charging infrastructure, refuel in 10 minutes; electrification is a vendor fantasy." Sustainability argues: "Go battery-electric now — zero tailpipe CO2, cheap energy, ~90% drivetrain efficiency vs diesel's 40%; every diesel year is ~19,000 t of CO2 we keep paying for." The fixed 8 km haul ramp (the long sustained climb out of the pit) carries 60% of every truck's cycle energy and can take an overhead trolley line ($1.5M/km); a trolley-assist truck (pantograph on the ramp + 200 kWh battery for the flats) costs $0.35M extra per truck; electric drivetrains save ≈ $0.6M/yr fleet-wide in maintenance on converted trucks. A full battery-electric conversion costs $0.7M/truck plus a $4M charging depot; the +4 t battery cuts payload 8% (240 → 221 t), and 2 h charging per 8 h shift cuts availability 25%; the site substation can serve at most 60% of full-fleet charging peak.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Grid power on the ramp: $0.10/kWh. Diesel 38 MJ/L; engine ~40% tank-to-wheel efficient; electric ~90%.
  - Five trucks also serve pit-floor/auxiliary work (irregular routes, no grid) — genuinely flexible work.
  - No simulation: the decision must be made today on pencil-and-paper numbers.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - State the strongest diesel case and the strongest full-BEV case; identify the axis that separates them (where fixed infrastructure is acceptable: rigid high-energy route vs flexible distributed work); synthesize by partitioning along that axis — trolley-battery on the ramp (60% of energy) + diesel retained for flexible work; verify with arithmetic; state a falsifiable boundary (grid capacity or operating horizon) where the synthesis collapses back into the either/or.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Strongest thesis (diesel) AND strongest antithesis (full-BEV), both with their best quantitative case (3 pts)
  - (b) Conflict axis identified: fixed-route/high-energy vs flexible/work — the disagreement is about *where fixed infrastructure is acceptable*, not diesel-vs-electric per se (2 pts)
  - (c) Genuine synthesis: partition along the axis — 15 trolley-battery trucks on the haul route, 5 diesel retained for flexible work; BOTH core interests (cheap zero-tailpipe energy; infrastructure-free flexibility) present in the final design (4 pts)
  - (d) Quantified verification: fleet fuel 7.2M L/yr = $7.92M/yr = 19,300 t CO2; ramp share 60% → 4.32M L, $4.75M, 11,600 t cut; grid draw ≈ 20 GWh ≈ $2.0M → net ≈ $3.3M/yr → payback ≈ 5 yr; full-BEV ≈ $34M with payload/availability penalties → worse on both numbers (4 pts)
  - (e) Boundary condition named, not papered over: if grid capacity < 60% of ramp demand or the operation's horizon < 6 yr, the synthesis collapses back to diesel (2 pts)
- **Notes**:
  - Positive-case design: the conflict is real (energy density/flexibility vs emissions/energy cost) but resolvable by partitioning along the axis that generates it. A binary picker fails both ways (diesel pays $7.9M/yr and 19,300 t forever; full-BEV ≈ $34M and infeasible under the substation limit); a naive average ("parallel hybrid") captures neither interest fully. The synthesis is gradeable: (c) requires an explicit element serving each camp, and all of (d) is hand-checkable. Evaluator reference: 7.2M L; $7.92M; 19,300 t; 4.32M L; $4.75M; 11,600 t; 20.3 GWh ≈ 20 GWh; net $3.3M; payback 17.25/3.3 ≈ 5.2 yr; full-BEV 14 + 4 + 10 + 6 = $34M.
