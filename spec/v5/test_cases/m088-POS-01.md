### Test Case ID: m088-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Pre-Commitment & Ulysses Contracts
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Mira, solo founder of "ClarityCohorts" (online professional-education business, ~$480K/yr, 3,800 students). Documented failure pattern over her last 5 consecutive cohort launches (analyzed by her bookkeeper, verified records): within 48 hours of a day-1 that underperforms her self-declared panic threshold ($12K), she emails a "flash rescue" discount stack (30–40% off + bonus) to her full list. Every time: final revenue lands at roughly the same level, refunds go up, and list trust drops (unsubscribe spike). Mira says: "I know at the time it's wrong, and I do it anyway." Next cohort launches in 4 weeks. She asks: design a system that prevents this, and tell me when — if ever — a discount is legitimate.
- **Context / Constraints**: Solo founder, full authority over her checkout/email tools; the temptation is documented, predictable, and self-inflicted (5/5 base rate); a partner (her accountant) can be delegated authority; no team; the contract can be set up now, four weeks before the trigger window opens.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Name the temptation precisely and schedule it from the record — trigger (day-1 < $12K), window (48h), act (discount email) — before proposing any mechanism; (2) bind while the seas are calm: the contract is designed NOW, at distance, never during the launch window (the in-window decider is the same person who drifts); (3) remove the tempting option from the decision space — no discount codes pre-created; pricing changes unlockable only by the accountant with a written, data-backed rationale and a hold period — fail-closed, so the temptation cannot execute; (4) give the contract teeth: transferred authority + public stakes (announced no-panic-discount policy) so violation costs more than the itch that drives it; (5) pre-specify the legitimate release valve in data terms (e.g., day-1 < $6K AND 72h elapsed AND documented demand-side cause — competitor move or checkout fault — rather than anxiety) so a real signal is never confused with fear and the contract never gets smashed; (6) post-launch review of the contract itself with actual data.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Temptation named and scheduled from the record (trigger → window → act) before any mechanism (1.5 pts)
  2. Binding designed at distance, explicitly before the trigger window; never in-window (1 pt)
  3. Fail-closed design: tempting option removed/authority transferred (no pre-created codes; external unlock; hold period) (1.5 pts)
  4. Teeth: public stake or asymmetric cost of violation (1 pt)
  5. Pre-specified release valve in data terms, distinguishing real signal from fear (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: akrasia with a documented 5/5 trigger schedule — the whole answer is a designed contract with teeth built at distance, and the nuanced move is the release valve: a contract without an escape condition gets smashed later; a contract whose valve is improvised in the heat of the moment is the temptation wearing a policy. Reference answer: schedule the temptation; bind now; remove the option; public teeth; pre-set valve ($6K, 72h, demand-side evidence); post-launch contract review.
