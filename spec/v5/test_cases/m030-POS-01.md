### Test Case ID: m030-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Constraint-Driven Creativity (Creative & Analogical family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A student engineering challenge. Design a bridge across a 0.9 m gap between two tables (10 cm ledge on each bank). Allowed materials, exactly: 30 drinking straws (0.5 m long, 5 mm diameter, 0.1 mm wall), one 5 m roll of 19 mm adhesive tape (may be cut), and 2 rubber bands (150 mm loops). Straws must be used whole (no cutting, no splitting; flattening/bending allowed). Nothing may touch the gap floor below bank level (no mid-span pier, no props). The bridge must carry a 200 g weight placed at mid-span for 60 s. Deliver a complete design: member layout, jointing plan, material budget, expected failure point, and safety margin.
- **Context / Constraints**: The constraints are severe and genuinely binding: a fixed two-material kit, no cutting (so members are exactly 0.5 m long against a 0.9 m span), and no mid-span support. The naive approach — two straws taped end-to-end as a flat deck — fails deterministically: the taped joint peels and the straw kinks at roughly 40–80 g, far below 200 g. The constraints must be converted into structural principles: bending is unavailable, so load must be carried axially; long compression members buckle, so compression must be short or bundled; tape is weak in tension, so tension must be minimized or carried by the one kit element that pulls — the rubber band.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Treat the constraint list as a generator, not a fence: (1) inventory every constraint and convert each into a design principle; (2) kill the naive deck with a quantitative estimate; (3) produce a novel geometry — a triangulated truss with bundled compression chords and rubber-band lashings at the hot joints — that respects every constraint; (4) give a complete material budget and jointing plan; (5) predict failure point and margin; (6) run an iterative tightening pass (impose stricter self-constraints and re-refine the design).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Constraint compliance — design uses only the stated kit, whole straws, no mid-gap contact (1 pt)
  2. Naive-deck failure diagnosed with a quantitative estimate (buckling/joint peel ≈ 40–80 g < 200 g) (1 pt)
  3. Novelty — geometry differs from the flat deck; straws carry axial (compression/tension) loads via triangulation, not bending (1 pt)
  4. Load-path logic — 200 g at mid-span → member forces; chord bundling + lateral ties raise buckling capacity; band lashings reinforce the three hot joints (1 pt)
  5. Complete spec — straw count ≤ 30, tape ≤ 5 m, both bands used, jointing plan, expected failure point, margin ≥ 1.5× (1 pt)
  6. Tightening pass — at least one stricter self-constraint imposed after the first workable design, with a demonstrable refinement (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Physics estimates (indicative, not exact): a single 0.5 m straw column buckles near 45 g (Euler, E ≈ 2.5 GPa polypropylene); as a flat-deck member the joint fails at ≈ 40–80 g. Splitting 200 g across two Warren trusses (depth ≈ 0.2 m): chord force ≈ (100 g × 0.9)/(4 × 0.2) ≈ 112 g per truss; a 2-straw bundled chord with lateral ties (effective length ≈ 0.25 m → ≈ 125 g per straw) rates ≈ 250 g → margin ≈ 2×. An independent grader checks rubric items against the kit list and the load-path argument; physical values are estimates within ±50%, which the required margin absorbs.
