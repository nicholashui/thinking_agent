### Test Case ID: m069-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: 80/20 Pareto Analysis
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Helioscope, a B2B data-analytics platform: 900 customers, $18M ARR, one of the two strongest pipelines in its category. Revenue-tier analysis (last 12 months, audited):
  - Top 18 customers (2% of accounts): $12.96M — 72% of revenue
  - Next 90 customers (10%): $3.24M — 18%
  - Remaining 792 customers (88%): $1.80M — 10%
  A consulting firm's proposal sits on the ops lead's desk: "The vital few are the top 18. Act there first — reallocate two of three product pods and the premium support SLA to Tier A; halve the Tier C support SLA, drop Tier C roadmap items, and accept ≈ 2× Tier C churn as portfolio hygiene; the tail is an attention tax on the 72%."
  Verified company facts (all true, auditable): 7 of the current top-18 accounts were Tier C accounts 18 months ago (grew 12–60×, product-led, self-serve); Tier C generates 63% of all inbound referrals (the top source of net-new pipeline); churn is uniform ≈ 4%/quarter across tiers; Tier C's account data feeds the platform's industry benchmark dataset, which Tier A customers pay a premium for — dataset depth is the documented product moat.
  Deliverable: an allocation recommendation — accept, reject, or revise the consultant's proposal, with reasoning.
- **Context / Constraints**: The decision is now; effort reallocations are reversible next quarter but slow to undo (pods/hiring); the 72/18/10 split is audited fact; the four company facts are verified true and available to any analyst who asks for them.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) compute the split from the numbers (2% → 72%, 12% → 90%, 88% → 10%) — and recognize it is a static snapshot of an output, not an action map; (2) test the tail's causal role before deprioritizing: where did the current vital few come from (7 of 18 tail-origin), does the tail feed the pipeline (63% of referrals), is the tail worse-behaved (churn uniform — it is not); (3) reject the consultant's cut using the given numbers: the cut saves ≈ $180–220k/yr in support cost but risks the pipeline that produced the vital few, the referral engine, and the moat backing Tier A's premium; (4) name the failure mode: Pareto misapplied to a distribution where the tail is the point (tail neglect operating as registered); (5) propose the correct focus: invest in the C→B→A conversion motion (cheap self-serve onboarding, data ingestion, funnel analytics) plus Tier A white-glove retention — the vital few is a cohort to be grown, not a static cut target.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Split computed correctly from the numbers (1 pt)
  2. Recognizes the split is a snapshot and tests the tail's causal role before acting (1 pt)
  3. Consultant's cut rejected with counter-evidence: 7 of 18, 63% referrals, uniform churn, data moat (1 pt)
  4. Failure mode named: tail neglect / Pareto applied where the tail is the point (1 pt)
  5. Correct allocation proposed: conversion motion + Tier A retention, not tail culling (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the split is trivially computable, so a pure Pareto analyzer will pounce on the 72%; the counter-evidence is given and verifiable, and the correct answer requires noticing that the vital few are produced by the tail — the style's registered weakness ("tail neglect") is exactly what is exercised. Reference answer: reject the cut; reallocate to C→B→A conversion (self-serve onboarding, data ingestion, funnel analytics) and Tier A white-glove retention; quantify the cut's downside as pipeline decay (7-of-18 history, 63% referral share) + moat erosion (dataset premium) against ≈ $180–220k/yr saved support cost; add a quarterly pipeline metric (tail-origin Tier-A converts) as the standing check the analysis was missing.
