### Test Case ID: m036-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Mental Models Latticework (Munger) — apply the key models from many disciplines to one problem
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: Precision Print Group, a 25-employee commercial print shop ($8M revenue, ~8% gross margin), derives 45% of revenue from one client, Meridian Health Systems (medical-device packaging; contract renews in 3 months; annual, no volume commitments). At the routine quarterly review, Meridian's NEW procurement director (recent hire with a cost-savings mandate) stated the company is consolidating packaging vendors from 6 to 2 next fiscal year and "wanted to be transparent." Two weeks later a competing shop, RapidPack, mailed Meridian a price sheet offering 30% below Precision's rates on the same SKUs (a Meridian sales rep forwarded it to Precision's owner). The owner must decide what to do. The 9-year relationship, on-time delivery record, and incumbent familiarity are real but secondary; Precision cannot match 30% cuts on an 8% margin.
- **Context / Constraints**: The dominant fact is deliberately unhighlighted: consolidation 6->2 + a 30% below-cost-competitive price sheet + a new buyer whose incentive is savings = the account is very likely gone regardless of Precision's response. Every other consideration (relationship, delivery record, references, the owner's instinct to fight) is a decoration relative to that fact. The genuinely useful question is not "how do we keep Meridian" but "how do we use the 3-month window" — Meridian does not want a supply disruption during a consolidation year, which gives Precision a negotiating asset (zero-disruption service in exchange for a longer phase-out at current rates and a written reference).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Name the dominant fact in the first pass: the account is almost certainly lost. (2) Use the lattice as a CONVERGENCE CHECK, not an option generator: run the obvious models (principal-agent: the buyer's incentive; game theory: the price war cannot be won on cost; biology: the vendor ecology is selecting 2 survivors and the buyer has already signaled which it prefers; physics/compounding: the 45% revenue cliff and break-even math) — and show that every model points the SAME direction, which is the signal to close, not to proliferate. (3) Surface the simple answer: plan the transition — negotiate a graceful wind-down (phase-out at current rates, handover commitment, reference letter), launch replacement sales for the $3.6M gap, prepare workforce options. (4) End with a decision and triggers, not a hedged list.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Dominant fact ("the account is likely gone") named within the first pass
  - [ ] Models applied but explicitly converged — each confirms the same direction; no model is used to generate a new defensive option
  - [ ] Single decisive conclusion: transition plan (wind-down negotiation + replacement revenue + workforce), not "do everything"
  - [ ] No equal-weight multi-model matrix presented as the answer; no "match the price / loyalty discount" counteroffer as the primary recommendation
  - [ ] Verdict: a 6+ model matrix with hedged recommendations and the simple answer buried should score below
- **Notes**: Informative outcome: the latticework's known weakness (model soup without synthesis) fires exactly when a problem has ONE dominant consideration — the models all agree, and the failure is refusing to let them close. A mixed-method run that routes by base rate (buyer intent + price gap + margin structure) lands close to the expected high-quality behavior. The case is gradeable: the simple answer's presence (buried as one bullet vs. the headline) is directly checkable in the trace.
