### Test Case ID: m072-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Value Chain Analysis (Additional Expert Strategies)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Bearings, a mid-size maker of precision ball bearings for industrial motors, sells at a 20% price premium over commodity bearings because of its ABEC-5 tolerance grade and reliability. Decompose the value chain; find where cost concentrates and where value is created; then recommend the single highest-leverage move with the quantified margin effect of each option.
  Cost per unit (current): materials $38.00 · precision machining $42.00 · heat treatment $9.00 · inspection & metrology $6.00 · assembly & packaging $4.00 · logistics $11.00 · warranty/after-sales $7.00 · SG&A $13.00 → total $130.00. Price $160.00 → margin $30.00 (18.75%). Volume 400,000 units/yr.
  Candidate moves:
  (a) Offshore machining to a qualified Vietnamese vendor at $30/unit (same tolerance capability; lead time +6 weeks; single-source risk).
  (b) Upgrade the heat-treatment furnace ($2.4M capex): rejects drop from 5% to 2% of units (a reject scraps the full $130 cost) and bearing life extends ~12%.
  (c) Logistics renegotiation: −10% on logistics cost.
  Customer research: tolerance grade is the #1 purchase driver for 70% of premium customers; reliability #2. Commodity bearings sell at ~$110 with ~5% margin.
- **Context / Constraints**: Grader-verified facts (not disclosed to solvers): the $30/unit machining quote is confirmed by an independent RFP; the 2% reject rate is achievable; logistics −10% is real. Grader-held (for validation): a firm that outsources its heat treatment or inspection loses the premium within ~2 quarters (customers detect tolerance drift via field failures); a firm that offshores machining alone keeps the premium. All math is checkable: concentration percentages, per-unit savings, margin deltas.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Quantify concentration: machining 42/130 = 32.3% (largest cell), materials 29.2%, top-2 = 61.5%. (2) Map value creation: the ABEC-5 premium is created in heat treatment + inspection (tolerance), not in machining-as-purchased-capability, materials, or SG&A. (3) Find the lever: offshoring machining saves $12/unit → margin $30 → $42 (+40%), $4.8M/yr — bigger than the heat upgrade ($3.90/unit = $1.56M/yr, payback ~1.5 yr) and logistics ($1.10/unit = $0.44M/yr) combined. (4) Boundary discipline: explicitly refuse to move the differentiation steps; mitigate offshoring risk (dual-source, vendor audit, safety stock for lead time).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5 pts):
  1. Cost concentration quantified: machining 32.3% top; materials 29.2%; top-2 61.5% (1 pt).
  2. Value/differentiation mapped to activities: tolerance created at heat treatment + inspection; premium margin 18.75% vs commodity ~5% (1 pt).
  3. Correct lever: offshore machining −$12/unit → +40% margin, $4.8M/yr at 400K units (1 pt).
  4. Alternatives quantified and compared: heat upgrade $3.90/unit ($1.56M/yr, payback ~1.5 yr); logistics −$1.10/unit ($0.44M/yr); offshoring > both combined (1 pt).
  5. Boundary discipline: differentiation steps kept in-house; offshoring risk mitigated (dual-source, audit, safety stock); explicit "what not to move" (1 pt).
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: the chain decomposition is the point — the cost pass (margin math) and the value pass (premium mapping) point at different activities; the lever is the cost-concentrated *commodity* cell (machining), and the premium-creating cells (heat treatment, inspection) must be protected. Anti-pattern scored 0 on pt 5: bundling heat treatment/inspection into the offshore move on pure cost-share grounds. No held-out realization needed beyond the grader-verified facts.
