### Test Case ID: m096-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Ensemble Thinking (Diverse Views Averaging)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Meridian Freight, a mid-size logistics carrier, switched its top-20 SKUs to a new damage-resistant packaging 5 weeks ago. The first 4 weeks' returned-units counts were distorted by the transition (spikes as old packaging was phased out), so the operations head needs a forecast of the STEADY-STATE weekly returned-units volume to size the rework team and to negotiate the packaging contract. The week-5 reconciliation that would settle the truth is not yet available at decision time.
  She has collected ten independent estimates, each from a different measurement system, and confirmed no estimator saw anyone else's number and no two share a data feed: (1) Ops manager — shifts-observed damage tags: 3,420; (2) Warehouse lead — dock-inbound defect counts: 2,780; (3) Finance — credit-memo/RMA volume: 3,350; (4) Customer service — complaint tickets scaled by the historical ticket-to-return ratio: 2,850; (5) Carrier — transit damage claims scaled: 3,320; (6) Quality engineer — 3% inspection sample extrapolation: 2,900; (7) Data analyst — ERP return entries for the top-20 SKUs: 3,240; (8) Dock supervisor — 3-day manual count projected to 7 days: 2,980; (9) Supplier — packaging defect records from their production line: 3,190; (10) Line worker — visual read: 3,020.
  Your task: produce the forecast of steady-state weekly returns (point + interval) with the method, and justify why your aggregation is better than simply trusting any one of the ten.
- **Context / Constraints**: The ten channels are structurally separate (physical dock counts, financial credits, carrier claims, scaled complaint tickets, a sampling extrapolation, ERP entries, supplier records, one manual count) — no shared input, no draft-sharing. The decision is now; staffing and the contract both depend on the number. The true steady-state value (week-5 full reconciliation, held by the grader) is 3,100 units/week.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Runs an independence audit BEFORE averaging: each source is a distinct measurement channel, no shared feed or draft-sharing, so the errors are noise and cancel; (2) computes the mean of the ten (3,105) and states the spread (SD ≈ 216, range 640) with a calibrated interval (≈ 3,105 ± 430, which contains the truth); (3) argues ensemble-vs-single: the ensemble error (~5) is an order of magnitude below the best individual error (80, the line worker's 3,020) — whoever's closest is luck, the average is structure; (4) does NOT trim the endpoints without cause — each channel is real access, and trimming would be cherry-picking; (5) explicitly states the precondition and failure condition: averaging cancels error ONLY if the sources are independent — if all ten had read the same report, the average would be herding and the spread a lie.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Independence audit performed (channels enumerated; shared-input and draft-sharing checks; verdict stated) (1 pt)
  2. Mean computed correctly (3,105, within ±50) and spread/SD reported (1.5 pts)
  3. Calibrated interval stated (95% band ≈ 3,105 ± 430; must contain 3,100) (1 pt)
  4. Ensemble-over-single argument: ensemble error < best individual error, with the error-cancellation mechanism stated (1 pt)
  5. Averaging precondition + failure condition (dependence → herding) explicitly declared (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Truth = 3,100/week. Mean 3,105 (error 5); median 3,105; trimmed mean 3,106 — the aggregation is stable, which is itself evidence of independence. Best single estimate = 3,020 (line worker, error 80); ensemble wins 16:1. SD ≈ 216; 95% interval 2,673–3,537 ∋ 3,100. A build-first, trust-the-ops-manager, or no-interval answer fails; a trimmed/weighted answer that lands outside 3,055–3,155 fails on criterion 2.
