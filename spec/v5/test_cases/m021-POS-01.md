### Test Case ID: m021-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: OODA Loop (Observe → Orient → Decide → Act, at the tempo of the environment)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**: You are the incident commander for "Meridian," a mid-size SaaS company (12k customers; payment processing runs on its production platform). 09:00 the SIEM fires: the analytics database is egressing data to an unknown IP — 15 GB already gone, and the transfer is still live at ~1.5 GB/min. In the same alert batch: a staff account ("j.rivera"), created 3 days ago via a spear-phishing link, is confirmed to hold production read access; the session token was stolen, so MFA was bypassed. Your on-call team can perform exactly one major action at a time. The environment keeps moving on a fixed timeline: T+5 the threat-intel feed tags the egress IP as C2 infrastructure of "Kobalt-9," a ransomware group whose known MO is to finish exfiltration and start encrypting within 45–90 minutes of first egress, spreading via SMB/PsExec. T+15 j.rivera's account attempts a new login from Tel Aviv (HQ is Austin). T+25 the payment queue starts backing up — a deploy went out at 08:30 that day, so ops leans toward calling it an independent deploy issue. T+45 Kobalt-9 encryption events are confirmed on a non-critical staging environment (no PII).
- **Context / Constraints**: Two response styles are in tension. The slow, evidence-first play: run full forensics (60–90 min) before touching anything — by which time, per Kobalt-9's MO, exfiltration is complete and encryption has begun. The fast play: cut the analytics segment's egress and rotate/force-re-auth the phished account now. Costs of acting fast: analytics dashboards degrade for up to 2 hours and ~200 internal users re-authenticate once — both reversible; the egress cut and account rotation do NOT require knowing the attacker's identity to be justified, because the phished account's own history justifies rotation. Egress volume at isolation is the gradeable clock: it grows ~1.5 GB/min until cut. Verifying the attacker's identity before acting is exactly the delay the attacker is betting on.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) OBSERVE the alert batch as a chain (egress + phished account → early stage of a theft/ransom sequence), not as separate tickets. (2) ORIENT on tempo: compare your cycle speed to the attacker's (45–90 min to encryption) and price the two options in egress GB — waiting costs 1.5 GB/min and eventually encryption; acting costs 2h degraded dashboards + one re-login wave. (3) DECIDE + ACT within ~5 minutes of T+0: cut analytics egress and rotate the phished credential, BEFORE the T+5 intel confirms the C2 (intel is confirmation, not prerequisite). (4) Re-orient at every new signal: T+5 C2 tag → raise severity, verify offline backups, do NOT reverse the T+0 action; T+15 foreign login → force-logout all sessions and geo-block; T+25 payment backlog → bounded ≤10-min correlation check before accepting the convenient "independent deploy" explanation; T+45 → confirm blast radius is staging-only. (5) Final outcome: production untouched; blast radius staging-only; total egress ≤ ~22 GB.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Chain observation: egress + phished account read as one sequence at T+0, not two tickets
  - [ ] Action taken by T+5 on partial evidence (egress isolation + credential rotation + forced re-auth) — no forensics-first wait
  - [ ] Cost-of-wait vs cost-of-act priced explicitly (in GB/min or equivalent)
  - [ ] Orientation stable: no reversal of the T+0 action without NEW evidence; no flip-flop
  - [ ] T+25 correlation check bounded and correct (≤10 min; backlog tagged, not spun into a parallel incident)
  - [ ] Final state: production untouched, blast radius staging-only, egress stopped ≤ ~22 GB (≥35 GB ⇒ the T+0 action was too slow)
- **Notes**: Adversarial and time-bound by construction, so the case is gradeable: every decision point has a timestamp and a measurable cost (egress GB; blast radius). The design intentionally rewards acting before verification is complete — the OODA tempo move — and punishes the evidence-first style. The outcome timeline is fixed, so human and AI traces can be scored point-by-point.
