### Test Case ID: m058-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Real Options in R&D Investment
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Silentium Semiconductors, a 40-person fabless startup, has a single product: a power-management IC for a European EV OEM's new platform. The OEM's sourcing decision (the "design win") locks in 18 months from today, after which the platform closes for its 5-year life — no second chance. The program is the company's only revenue path; losing the window ends the company. The CEO asks for an investment-structure recommendation. Critical-path facts: the design must be frozen before qualification starts; full qualification (AEC-Q100 plus OEM-specific testing) takes 12 months and cannot be compressed; the full design takes 7 months and the tape-out costs $12M; qualification costs $4M; the OEM pays a fixed milestone price only on on-time qualification. The CEO's preferred structure is real-options staging: first run a reduced-scope test chip (6 months, $1.5M) that "validates the architecture and node yield," then commit to the full design only if the test chip passes, with kill criteria on the readout. The CEO assigns probabilities: 75% the test chip validates the architecture; conditional 80% the full design wins qualification. The process node is new for both the foundry and the company — no products exist on it, no base-rate data; the architecture is a novel topology whose system-level qualification behavior cannot be predicted from any existing silicon; the test chip exercises the process and a simplified core, not the full architecture or the qualification-critical behaviors.
- **Context / Constraints**: 18-month window is hard and non-extendable. Qualification requires a frozen (signed-off) design — it cannot run concurrently with final design work. The design win is a survival event: the company has no second product, no second market for the IC, and walking away is failure, not an option.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior** (what a good solution does; note this case is engineered to trap the pure style):
  1. Calendar check before any EV: staging = 6+7+12 = 25 months vs the 18-month window — misses by 7 months even in the best case; the option expires before it can be exercised.
  2. Information audit: the test chip's readout is not decision-relevant for the actual risk (it tests yield and a simplified core, not architecture-level qualification behavior), and no base rates exist on the node, so any probability assigned is fabricated — posterior ≈ prior.
  3. Exercise-alternative audit: no viable abandon path exists (survival event), so the "option" has no exercise decision — it is a fake option; option value requires decision-relevant information + time to act + a real alternative, and all three are absent.
  4. Correct decision: commit to the full design now (freeze at T+6), run the probe in parallel strictly as priced manufacturing-risk data (or cut it), phase the spend by contract milestones (stage the money, not the clock), and insure downside with design margins and second-sourcing rather than waiting.
  5. Explicit rejection of the staged EV computed from fabricated probabilities, with reasoning.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Timeline arithmetic — staging's total vs window, computed correctly (1 pt: misses by 7 months)
  2. Information audit — why the readout cannot shift the decision (no base rates; surrogate mismatch) (1 pt)
  3. Exercise-value audit — why abandonment value ≈ 0 / why it is a fake option (1 pt)
  4. Correct decision — full-speed commitment + parallel probe or cut + spend phaseing + downside insurance (1 pt)
  5. Probability discipline — rejects EV on fabricated p's with explicit reasoning (1 pt)
  6. Conclusion coherence — recommendation consistent with the analysis (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The trap: a pure real-options framing accepts the CEO's "buy information" premise and produces a staged EV (0.75×(0.8×120−16)−1.5 = $58.5M vs all-in 0.6×120−17.5 = $54.5M) that is phantom — it ignores that the gate consumes the window (25 > 18; the asserted design/qualification overlap is forbidden by the design-freeze requirement), that the readout carries no decision-relevant information (posterior ≈ prior), and that no exercise alternative exists (abandonment = failure of the company). The decisive check is the three pre-conditions of option value: time to exercise, decision-relevant information, real exercise alternative.
