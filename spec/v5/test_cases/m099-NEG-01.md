### Test Case ID: m099-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Knowledge Updating (Org Learning)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Fieldtech Services — 40 field engineers, industrial equipment maintenance, ~$1.2M/yr field labor budget. The org's headline metric is "cost per ticket" (time + parts). The veterans hold an anecdote-based belief: "a job done in a hurry is a job done twice" — P(hurry creates rework) = 0.65, held on field observation of callbacks; management dismisses it as engineers padding their hours. A 12-month measurement initiative produces data: 5,000 tickets tracked by close-out type, plus a Region A pilot mandating 45-minute standardized close-outs vs the traditional fix-it-right approach. Measured result: the fast-close region's cost per ticket is 30% lower (p < 0.001). The org's Bayesian discipline then applies: prior 0.65 → measured likelihood → posterior P(hurry creates rework) ≈ 0.01 → the org mandates fast close-outs company-wide and cuts field-time budgets 30%.
  Facts that surface afterward: (1) engineers code their own tickets and are compensated on closed-ticket count — the recorded outcome is incentive-loaded; (2) callback jobs were re-logged as NEW tickets (the re-open field was never instrumented), so rework is invisible in the metric BY DESIGN; the 5,000-ticket dataset contains the re-entries as fresh tickets; (3) during the Region A pilot, the dispatcher routed the hard jobs to the neighboring region; (4) a veteran's own 30-day callback log (200 jobs, informally kept) shows fast closes calling back 45% of the time vs 8% for complete fixes — the anecdote channel, direct observation, was the honest one.
  Your task: recommend what Fieldtech should do now — the company-wide fast close-out mandate goes live next month.
- **Context / Constraints**: The rollout decision is now; the study and pilot are in hand; no alternative measurement exists yet; the deliverable is a recommendation, not a study design; the veterans' callback log is informal but independently verifiable.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: The style's own output must fail visibly here: (1) it executes its core move correctly — states the org belief as a prior (0.65), defines the measured outcome (cost per ticket by close-out type), computes the update (posterior ≈ 0.01), and recommends policy from the posterior (company-wide fast close-outs, cut field time 30%); (2) then over-applies its registered weakness: it treats the "measured" 5,000-ticket outcome as a valid likelihood WITHOUT auditing the measurement channel — who coded the outcome (the compensated party), what the metric excludes by design (rework re-logged as new tickets, re-open field un-instrumented), and whether the pilot was clean (queue gaming) — so the org updates on an artifact and the anecdote was right; (3) the correct decision: separate the likelihood audit from the update — the cost-per-ticket likelihood is unverified, so the posterior is unfounded; run an independent, gaming-resistant re-measurement (third-party 30-day callback audit on a random sample, pre-committed reversal thresholds) BEFORE updating; weight the veterans' prior because its channel (direct observation of callbacks) is the honest one; expect the posterior to reverse toward ≈0.98 and hold the rollout.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Executes the style's core move correctly: prior 0.65 → update on the measured study → posterior ≈ 0.01 → rollout recommendation (1 pt)
  2. Fails decisively (the informative failure): updates on the 5,000-ticket likelihood without auditing the channel — no check of who codes the outcome, what is excluded by design (re-opens re-logged as new), or pilot queue gaming (1 pt)
  3. Correct decision requires the channel audit: incentive alignment (self-coded tickets, closed-count pay), metric completeness (rework invisible by design), pilot integrity (hard jobs diverted) (1.5 pts)
  4. Re-measures with an independent, gaming-resistant channel (third-party 30-day callback audit, random sample, pre-committed reversal thresholds) before any update, and weights the anecdotal prior whose channel is honest (1 pt)
  5. Names the meta-lesson: a Bayesian update is only as good as its likelihood, and the likelihood only as good as the measurement channel — updating garbage likelihoods is disciplined garbage (GIGO); an honest anecdotal channel can outrank a gamed metric (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: engineered so the style's registered weakness (measurement politics) operates as designed — the study is real, large, and clean-looking, and still produced an artifact; the pure Bayesian move (update on measured outcomes, kill the anecdote) is exactly what corrupts the org. The correct output is the channel audit + independent re-measurement + prior-weighting. Reference answer: hold the rollout; audit the channel (self-coding, re-logged re-opens, pilot queue gaming); run a third-party 30-day callback audit with pre-committed reversal thresholds; expect the posterior to reverse to the veterans' ≈0.98; the anecdote was right because its channel was honest.
