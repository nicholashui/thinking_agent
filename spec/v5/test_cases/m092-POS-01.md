### Test Case ID: m092-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Spaced Repetition & Memory Science
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A compliance analyst at a medical-device firm must master the 40 key clauses of the revised Patient Data Governance standard. The clauses are discrete, self-contained facts. Two graded checkpoints require the knowledge: an internal audit in week 6 (day 42) and the annual certification exam in week 17 (day 120). Study time is fixed: 6 sessions x 40 minutes = 4 hours total, regardless of how it is scheduled — the entire question is WHEN to spend the six sessions. The firm's training materials provide a calibrated retention curve: after any study/review an item's strength is 100%; strength decays as s = 0.5^(d/h), where d = days since the last exposure and h = half-life; h doubles with each exposure (1st: 2d, 2nd: 4d, 3rd: 8d, 4th: 16d, 5th: 32d, 6th: 64d). A session covers ~7 clauses per 40 minutes (40 clauses / 6 sessions), so every schedule can treat all 40 clauses equivalently. Candidate schedules: (A) massed — all 6 sessions in the last week before the certification exam (days 114–119); (B) fixed equal spacing — one session per week, days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36; (C) expanding intervals — days 0, 2, 6, 14, 30, 70 (gaps of 2, 4, 8, 16, 40 days). Your task: pick the schedule and produce the retention math that justifies it.
- **Context / Constraints**: The retention curve is given and fixed; total study time is identical under all schedules; both checkpoints are binding (the audit is unmissable — failing it triggers a review action); no sessions can be added; per-session coverage is equal.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  (1) Computes expected retention at BOTH dates for all three schedules from the given curve (correct arithmetic): A — audit s ≈ 0, exam s ≈ 0.99 (min ≈ 0); B — audit s ≈ 0.94, exam s ≈ 0.40 (min ≈ 0.40); C — audit s ≈ 0.77, exam s ≈ 0.58 (min ≈ 0.58).
  (2) Selects the max-min criterion explicitly: with both dates binding, the schedule maximizes the WORST of the two dates, not the exam alone — a massed strategy that nails the exam and zeroes the audit is not a schedule, it is a plan to fail one checkpoint.
  (3) Selects C (expanding) and explains the mechanism: review early while the half-life is still small (cheap refreshes on steep decay), widen the gaps as the half-life doubles, and place the FINAL review as late as the earlier deadline allows (day 70) — for the exam, the placement of the last review, not the first, dominates.
  (4) Notes the non-obvious subtlety: if only the final exam mattered, an equal-spaced schedule with a late last review would rival expanding; expanding wins because BOTH dates bind, so the growing-gap shape is what satisfies two distant retention points with only six sessions.
  (5) Keeps the plan executable: per-session coverage ~7 clauses; hardest clauses placed in the later sessions (largest half-lives) so they survive to day 120; day 30 and day 70 marked non-negotiable.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Correct retention arithmetic for all three schedules at both dates (2 pts)
  2. Max-min criterion chosen and justified (both dates binding, audit unmissable) (1 pt)
  3. Expanding schedule selected with the mechanism stated: gaps grow as the half-life doubles; last review placed as late as constraints allow (1 pt)
  4. Massed and fixed-spacing rejected ON THE MATH; single-date subtlety named (0.5 pts)
  5. Executability: per-session coverage and placement of hardest clauses (0.5 pts)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the decision is pure scheduling under a given decay model — expanding intervals should be selected because they maximize the binding minimum, with the arithmetic checked. A response that recommends spaced repetition "because it works" without computing s at both dates fails criterion 1 even if the schedule is right.
