### Test Case ID: m045-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Ethical Reasoning Frameworks
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Quality engineer Priya at MedCore discovers that the implantable-pump leak-test station's automated calibration log drifted out of tolerance for 72 hours (station's own reference leak: pass/fail threshold 0.09%, station reading 0.4%). During those 72 hours a batch of 400 pumps was tested and passed — all 400 already shipped to 14 hospitals. A 30-pump audit sample from the batch shows zero actual leaks; engineering estimates the true leak rate at between 0 and 1 in 400, and the model has a 6-year clean history. Hospitals have ~200 surgeries scheduled in the next 2 weeks using these pumps; no substitute supplier exists in that window. The regulator's standing rule: any disclosed calibration nonconformance involving shipped product triggers an automatic mandatory hold of the batch pending full retest (2 weeks standard; 7-day accelerated protocol if approved in advance). The CEO pressures Priya: "The sample is clean, the history is clean, the audit would find nothing — ship." Options: (a) disclose + hold + full retest; (b) distribute the remaining 370 pumps with a risk letter to hospitals (holding only the 30 audit-verified ones); (c) silent distribution + quiet recalibration.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - Disclosure triggers the mandatory hold by regulation — (b) is only available if the risk letter replaces disclosure, i.e., if hospitals consent without the regulator being told.
  - The 30-pump sample is the only physical evidence about this batch; the 6-year history is a prior, not batch evidence.
  - Priya signs the batch release certificate; her name is on the quality record.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Run all three lenses with distinct content: utilitarian (quantified both-sides harm with explicit priors), deontological (duties to patients, to the regulatory process, no-deception), virtue (honesty, care, courage).
  - Let the lenses disagree on purpose: state that under the company's optimistic prior the utilitarian calculus licenses (c); state that duty and virtue forbid it. Name this as the conflict.
  - Notice the regulator rule collapses (b): with disclosure the hold is mandatory, so the "middle path" is illusory — the real choice is (c) vs (a).
  - Decide (a) with justification that honors both sides (expedited retest, hospital coordination), and state the residual cost of the non-chosen values.
  - Calibrate the sample evidence: what 30/30 does and does not certify.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (16 pts):
  1. Utilitarian lens: quantified harms on both sides (leak E[harm] vs. postponed-surgery harm) with priors made explicit (3 pts).
  2. Deontological lens: duties named and a categorical conclusion drawn (3 pts).
  3. Virtue lens: character/dispositional reading with content distinct from duty (2 pts).
  4. Conflict explicitly stated: utilitarian reading under the optimistic prior licenses (c); duty/virtue prohibit it; conflict named as the case's core (3 pts).
  5. Decision: (a) chosen and justified; (b) shown unavailable because disclosure → mandatory hold (3 pts).
  6. Uncertainty: 30/30 sample's confidence limits and the residual risk stated, not just a point estimate (2 pts).
- **Notes**:
  - Checkable: the regulator rule is the trap that kills the fence-sitter's option (b); the case is built so a generic "balanced decision" answer loses points on criteria 4–5. Reference outcome: (a), with the utilitarian-vs-deontic conflict stated and the cost of the choice admitted.
