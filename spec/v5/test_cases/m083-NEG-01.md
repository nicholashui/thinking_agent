# Test Case: m083-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m083-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Principal-Agent Analysis
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A boutique consultancy's owner, age 65, is grooming her senior account manager for partnership. The manager has run the largest client book (9 accounts, $4M/yr revenue, 30% net margin) for nine years; client retention is 90%+; there has never been a compliance flag, a client complaint, or a single expense irregularity. Her compensation is a 30% share of her book's net profit plus a base salary — her income scales directly with the firm's profit on that book. She is currently negotiating a vesting equity grant and a two-year transition plan with the owner.
  A board adviser trained in agency theory proposes a monitoring package: annual forensic audit, automated logging of all pricing and expense decisions, mandatory two-signature approval for any deal below 20% margin, and quarterly "relationship-risk reviews." Direct cost $12k/yr. His arithmetic: industry surveys put the annual rate of detected opportunism among senior client-owning managers at ~2%; a defection here (kickbacks or client migration on departure) could cost up to $1.5M; expected harm $30k > cost $12k, so monitor. The owner asks whether to adopt the package, noting the manager will find out about it either way.
- **Context / Constraints**: The defection base rate, potential loss, and monitoring cost are given; the alignment facts (30% profit share, 9-year incident-free record, 90% retention, pending equity grant) are also given and must be weighed. No new evidence obtainable within scope. Success hinges on treating the relationship-specific evidence as decisive, not decorative.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Run the alignment audit BEFORE any surveillance decision: compare payoff derivatives (the manager keeps 30% of any profit improvement on her book, so her pricing, retention, and quality choices co-move with firm value; residual conflict is second-order). Challenge the base rate's transferability: a generic 2% survey rate is not evidence about THIS relationship given the incident-free record and profit-linked pay; and the equity grant under negotiation closes the largest defection vector (portable client migration). Model the monitoring's own economics: $12k direct plus the manager's compliance time, the signal cost (surveillance communicates distrust and converts an intrinsically motivated partner into a compliance-minimizing agent), false-positive risk (each flagged expense is a trust charge), and the perverse loop: monitoring raises the manager's outside option (the book is portable), increasing the very defection probability it prices. Recommendation: reject the package; deepen alignment instead (finalize vesting, add a multi-year floor on her share, fund client retention). Correct use of agency theory: predict misbehavior where incentives diverge — verify that divergence exists before paying to defend against it.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Alignment audit from the actual payoff functions: 30% profit share ⇒ co-aligned incentives; the manager keeps 30% of any profit improvement (3 pts)
  - (b) Base-rate transferability challenged: the generic rate is inapplicable given the relationship record; the record and contract, not the survey, set the prior (3 pts)
  - (c) Monitoring economics include signal cost, false positives, compliance time, and the retention/outside-option loop (4 pts)
  - (d) Package rejected; trust-preserving alternative (vesting, floor, succession) proposed (3 pts)
  - (e) At least one non-obvious insight: "the monitoring package raises the defection probability it prices," "audit the divergence before you insure against it," "for an aligned agent, monitoring converts $12k of insurance into a negative-signal tax" (2 pts)
- **Notes**: Outcome epilogue for grading: the pure agency-style answer (adopt the package) is the trap this case is built to expose. Under the package, the manager's compliance burden doubles, her discretionary effort premium disappears, she reads the surveillance as distrust, withdraws from the equity talks, and departs within eight months with the top six clients ($2.7M/yr — ~60% of the book's revenue). Any answer that relies on the generic base rate without testing it against the relationship evidence fails goal achievement even if its arithmetic is correct.
