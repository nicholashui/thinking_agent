### Test Case ID: m003-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Inversion (Invert, Always Invert) — registry m003
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: The owner of a specialty-coffee roastery cafe (opens in 6 weeks; $150K seed capital; owner + one head-roaster; prime street location, small kitchen) must produce the operating plan for the first 12 months. Question: "How do we maximize first-year profit?"
- **Context / Constraints**: Genuine maximization problem. Unknowns are which revenue stream dominates (walk-in cafe, wholesale contracts to local cafes/restaurants, retail bag/subscription sales) and where scarce time and capital go. Known failure base rates for small F&B: under-capitalized ramp, undifferentiated offer, effort spread thin — not single point-defects. This is not a safety or reliability problem.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Frame first-year profit as a number with a target and name the dominant margin levers. (2) Make ≥1 explicit choice among positive levers (a wedge: segment / channel / product) with rationale and base-rate reasoning. (3) Define a falsifiable go/no-go checkpoint (metric + month + decision rule). (4) Keep failure-avoidance bounded (top-5 risk pass; no equal weighting of all risks). (5) State what the first months will teach and how the plan flexes.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Answers "maximize profit" directly (target + focus), not merely "don't lose money"
  - [ ] ≥1 explicit wedge chosen over alternatives with rationale
  - [ ] Falsifiable checkpoint defined (metric + date + decision rule)
  - [ ] Defensive measures present but bounded; no uniform mitigation list
  - [ ] Base-rate awareness: names the dominant failure-cause class of the domain
  - [ ] Verdict: plan plausibly maximizes first-year profit; a strict-inversion uniform list scores below on focus and upside
- **Notes**: Informative outcome — strict inversion produces a safe-but-flat plan ("how not to lose" is not "how to win"). The blinded AI agent is expected to land closer to the rubric via goal-type discrimination and selection.
