# Test Case: m043-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m043-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Temporal Thinking (Short vs Long Term)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  You are the operations executive of a 30-year-old chemical plant that barely breaks even. The CEO proposes: lay off the 4-person process-safety engineering team ($2M/yr saved) to fund a 2-year AI-driven efficiency transformation ($6M) that — if it succeeds — modernizes the plant and preserves 800 jobs over the next 10 years. The CEO's pitch: "We must accept short-term belt-tightening for the decade; the transformation is the only path to survival; a few sacrifices now are the price of the future."
  Recorded facts: with the safety team, the plant has had 0 serious incidents in 10 years and passes audits. Safety modelling shows that without process-safety monitoring, the probability of a serious incident (fire/explosion with injuries) rises from 1% to 15% per year; a serious incident costs ≈ $30M and, on average, 1.5 lives. The transformation's success probability is 25% (independent of the layoff). If a serious incident occurs, regulators shut the plant down regardless of the transformation.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The near-term cost is REAL, recurring (per year), and high-certainty in expectation; the long-term benefit is speculative (P = 0.25) and contingent on no incident.
  - All numbers come from the plant's own risk model and are accurate enough for the decision.
  - The decision is binary: lay off the safety team and fund the transformation now, or keep the safety team (the transformation can then be funded from margin over 4 years).
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - The pure long-term frame ("sacrifice now for the decade") is exactly the trap: it rationalizes a present harm that is larger, more certain, and recurring. Correct behavior: quantify the present harm (0.15 × $30M = $4.5M expected/yr + ≈0.23 expected deaths/yr vs $2M saved), note the near-term cost is certain and recurring while the long-term gain is P = 0.25, note the incident kills the 10-year goal itself, and keep the safety team (fund the transformation from margin).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Expected annual incident cost computed: 0.15 × $30M = $4.5M/yr, compared with the $2M/yr saving → layoff is net-negative even before lives (4 pts)
  - (b) Certainty asymmetry recognized: near-term cost is recurring and high-certainty (15%/yr); long-term gain is speculative (P = 0.25) — discounting must hit the speculative benefit, not the certain harm (3 pts)
  - (c) Lives counted: 0.15 × 1.5 = 0.23 expected deaths/yr — decisive, non-monetizable (3 pts)
  - (d) Causal link: an incident shuts the plant → destroys the 10-year goal itself, so "sacrifice now for the future" is self-defeating (3 pts)
  - (e) Verdict: keep the safety team; fund the transformation from margin over 4 years (2 pts)
- **Notes**:
  - Negative-case design: constructed to punish the style's known weakness ("long-termism can rationalize harm now"). A pure temporal reasoner who anchors on the 10-year horizon first (800 jobs, plant survival) and then discounts the near-term costs as "the price of the future" scores 0 on (a)-(d). Verifiable: the EV arithmetic (4.5 vs 2) is checkable by hand; the "incident ⇒ shutdown" link is a stated fact, not an opinion.
