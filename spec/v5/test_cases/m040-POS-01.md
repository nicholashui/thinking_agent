### Test Case ID: m040-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Leverage Points Identification (Additional High-Value family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Cascade Infra — a 40-engineer platform organization at a SaaS company — has missed its 99.9% reliability SLO for six straight quarters (running ≈ 99.55%). On-call firefighting consumes 55–60% of engineering capacity; 8 engineers left in 12 months. Two prior initiatives failed: (1) the org hired 18 engineers over two years — the SLO did not move; the newcomers were absorbed into on-call rotations and onboarding; (2) a process initiative (stricter change review, more documentation) slowed release cadence without moving the SLO. The board's new proposal: hire 25 more engineers plus weekly executive progress reviews. Meanwhile, 18+ months of incident data (tagged by change class, never aggregated) show ≈ 43% of all pages trace to five recurring change classes (config pushes, load-balancer misconfig, feature-flag mishandling, index changes, dependency upgrades). Aggregating and analyzing the data is a ~2-week pipeline effort; a canary/staged-rollout policy for the five classes is a few weeks of policy plus small tooling. Question: identify the highest-leverage intervention and rank the candidate intervention points by leverage (change per effort).
- **Context / Constraints**: The system is a reinforcing loop — page → context switch → hasty change → new page — and the board's frame is linear capacity ("more people"). The two failed initiatives are natural experiments: capacity and process both failed to move the SLO. Effort facts: 25 hires ≈ $4.5M/yr with a 6–12 month lag; feedback pipeline ≈ 2 weeks; canary rule ≈ 3 weeks; exec reviews ≈ 1 hr/wk each. No external constraint blocks any candidate.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) map the system and name the reinforcing firefighting loop, explaining why headcount feeds rather than breaks it; (2) enumerate ≥ 5 intervention points positioned on the leverage scale (parameters/numbers → delays → negative-feedback strength → information flows → rules → goals); (3) rank each by change-per-effort with explicit effort and expected effect; (4) select the information-flow lever (incident → change-class aggregation) plus the rules lever (canary for the five classes) and justify why the board's hire + reviews proposal is low leverage; (5) quantify expected system behavior change (incident rate, on-call %, SLO horizon) and name the verification metric.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. System framing — reinforcing firefighting loop named; headcount failure explained via the loop (1 pt)
  2. Leverage inventory — ≥ 5 intervention points positioned on the leverage scale (1 pt)
  3. Ranking — every candidate scored with effort and expected effect; ranking consistent with change-per-effort (1 pt)
  4. Chosen point — information flow + rules (canary) selected; board proposal explicitly ranked low with reasons (1 pt)
  5. Quantified expectation — incident/on-call/SLO impact stated with verification metric and falsifier (1 pt)
  6. Composition — how the pipeline feeds the rule (the two levers compose) (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The non-obviousness is deliberate: the visible bottleneck is capacity, but the leverage sits in the missing information flow and the rules. 43% of pages in five classes × ~70% canary catch ≈ 30% incident reduction → on-call 55–60% → ≈ 40%, SLO ≥ 99.9% within two quarters, zero new hires. Effort math: hires $4.5M/yr + lag with effect ≈ 0 (natural experiment #1); process with effect ≈ 0 (natural experiment #2); pipeline ≈ 2 weeks (enables targeting); canary ≈ 3 weeks (direct cut). Change-per-effort of pipeline + canary is ~2 orders of magnitude above hiring. On the Meadows scale, hiring is a parameters/numbers intervention (the lowest-leverage zone); information flows (6) and rules (5) are the correct zone.
