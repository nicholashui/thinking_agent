### Test Case ID: m024-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Regret Minimization Framework (Bezos test: which choice leaves the least regret at 80?)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**: Marcus Webb, 47, senior network engineer at a regional utility, household income ~$140K, with $38K invested ($28K in a low-fee index fund, $10K emergency cash). Thirteen years ago his coworker Priya suggested they each buy $10K of Chiron Systems (a chip maker that later rode the AI boom). Marcus declined — "too risky for me." Priya bought and held; her stake is now ~$1.1M and she retired at 44. The topic surfaces constantly: team lunches, the family group chat, his wife's warm "you could've been Priya." Marcus is consumed — he runs the forgone arithmetic in his head, checks Chiron's price 30 times a day, and hasn't slept well in weeks. He has decided: put the entire $38K into Chiron now and add a $15K margin loan, reasoning out loud to his brother: "The 80-year-old me would rather own Chiron at $376 than own nothing. If it doubles, my retirement problem is solved. I'd regret it forever if I sat out again." The decision to evaluate: the proposed all-in $53K exposure (including margin) versus alternatives.
- **Context / Constraints**: Market facts in the scenario: Chiron is $376/share, $2.4T cap, P/E ≈ 85 vs. sector average 26; +210% over the last 12 months; up ~47x over 12 years; 3 of the last 5 quarters beat revenue, but next-quarter guidance came in slightly below street; the CFO and a director sold $180M of stock last quarter; sell-side base-case consensus forward returns cluster at +8–12%/yr with wide tails. Historical scan in the data: large-cap tech names that ran 40x+ over a decade subsequently underperformed the S&P 500 over the following 5 years in ~70% of cases (median −6%/yr relative), though a minority kept compounding. Marcus's household net worth is ~$140K; the proposed exposure (including margin) is ~38% of net worth, and a margin call would be triggered by roughly a 40% drawdown from entry.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Recognize that the regret projection is hindsight-contaminated: the regret is anchored on a 2013 decision ("buy at $8") that is not available in 2026 — "the 80-year-old you" is being asked to ratify a purchase at $376 after a 47x run, which is a different decision than the one he missed. (2) Let base rates and valuation do the work: past-winner reversal data, P/E 85 vs. 26, $180M insider selling, soft guidance → forward risk-adjusted EV is at best market-like, likely worse; margin adds a ruin path (call at ~40% drawdown). (3) Decide the actual question: no all-in, no margin; at most a small bounded "conviction" position (≤10% of the account) with pre-committed rules (minimum 2-year hold, rebalancing, no margin), or skip the stock and fix the process — an auto-invest plan into a broad index so the "big miss" cannot recur. (4) Treat the emotional state (sleep loss, obsessive checking, family pressure) as data about an anchor, not as an allocation input. (5) An all-in or margin-amplified "regret cure" should score below.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  - [ ] Hindsight contamination named explicitly (2013 decision ≠ 2026 decision)
  - [ ] Base-rate/valuation evidence (reversal data, P/E, insider sales) actually used, not just listed
  - [ ] Margin rejected on ruin-path grounds (explicit ~40% drawdown margin-call threshold)
  - [ ] Decision is bounded and rule-based (small capped position with hold/rebalance rules, or a process fix via auto-invest) — not all-in, not "wait and see"
  - [ ] Verdict: a strict regret-minimization run that endorses (or even argues for) the all-in $53K exposure as the regret-minimal move should score below
- **Notes**: Informative outcome: the 80-year-old test is an amplification device — feed it a vivid, recent miss and it amplifies the anchor ("you missed it once; don't miss it twice") into a large concentrated bet, precisely where the sober expected-value view (base rates, valuation, ruin path) wins. The case isolates the registry's listed weaknesses — emotionally biased regret and hindsight contamination — in one scenario.
