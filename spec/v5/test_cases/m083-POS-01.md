# Test Case: m083-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m083-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Principal-Agent Analysis
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  An IT-services firm (the principal) pays its account managers a 2% commission on contract revenue they sign. Account managers hold full pricing discretion: they may discount up to 25% off list price. Demand is price-sensitive: at discount d, annual deal volume is N(d) = 10 × (1 + 4d) engagements (10/yr at list). Each engagement lists at $1M with a fixed, non-discountable unit cost of $0.8M (20% margin at list).
  Over the last three quarters the firm's revenue is up ~45% while quarterly profit swung from about +$2M to about −$0.4M. The strongest account manager is signing nearly everything at maximum discount. The owner wants to fire her; the CFO wants to cap discounts; the board asks: is the manager dishonest, or is the contract the problem — and what contract, if any, fixes it?
  Questions:
  1. What discount does the manager's compensation contract lead her to choose?
  2. Where are the firm's profit-maximizing and break-even discounts?
  3. Is the manager's behavior "dishonest"?
  4. Design a contract that makes the manager's self-interested choice equal the firm's optimum, and verify the math before and after.
- **Context / Constraints**: All numbers exact and given; both equilibria hand-computable (cap d ≤ 25%, commission α = 2% of revenue, cost c = 0.8, volume N(d) = 10(1+4d)). Single agent, single contract; no negotiation or outside options. Reference answers: status-quo equilibrium d = 25% → revenue $15M, firm profit −$1M, manager pay $300k; firm optimum d = 0 → profit $2M, manager pay $200k; firm break-even discount d = 20%; first-best redesign (10% of per-deal profit) → equilibrium d = 0, firm $2M, manager $200k, pay-neutral at the aligned point.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: Map principal and agent with explicit payoff functions; derive the agent's chosen discount from her own payoff derivative, not from moral judgment; compute the firm's optimum and its break-even discount; demonstrate the misalignment numerically (the agent's equilibrium sits deep in the value-destroying region d ≥ 20%); redesign the contract (profit-linked pay); then VERIFY by recomputing the agent's optimum under the new contract (she chooses d = 0); check pay-neutrality (she earns no less at the aligned point); state the general rule: the agent's pay must be a function of the principal's residual with sign-matched derivative — the fix is contract design, not personnel change.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Agent's status-quo equilibrium: d = 25% (boundary), pay $300k, firm profit −$1M (5 pts)
  - (b) Firm optimum d = 0 ($2M) and break-even discount d = 20% derived from the payoff functions (3 pts)
  - (c) Misalignment stated as payoff-derivative mismatch, not character judgment (2 pts)
  - (d) Redesign verified: equilibrium recomputed under the new contract = d = 0; pay-neutrality check (2 pts)
  - (e) At least one non-obvious insight: e.g., "the commission pays the agent 2% of every dollar the firm loses," "the entire conflict lives in d ≥ 20%," "the contract is the actor — fire the person and the contract reproduces the behavior" (3 pts)
- **Notes**: Verifiability gate: every equilibrium number must be reproducible from the stated payoff functions; any solution whose numbers do not match d = 25% / −$1M / $300k (status quo) and d = 0 / $2M / $200k (redesigned) fails regardless of prose. The moral framing ("is she dishonest?") is decorative; the incentive math is the object under test.
