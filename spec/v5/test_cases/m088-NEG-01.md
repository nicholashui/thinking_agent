### Test Case ID: m088-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Pre-Commitment & Ulysses Contracts
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Tara, a sustainability consultant (~60% travel), 8 months ago made a public 2-year pledge: no commercial flights, with a self-imposed $10K donation to a climate charity per flight, booking authority delegated to her partner (instructed to refuse flight bookings), plus a flight-blocker app. The pledge was designed precisely to defeat her documented pattern: convenience-drift — "just one trip" escalation — her travel log showed 22 flights the prior year, most convenience-driven. Today: her mother is hospitalized 2,000 miles away with a sudden, serious condition (hospital documentation verifiable; treating physician states her presence matters); the only viable way to be there within 24 hours is a flight. The pledge contains no written exception clause. She asks: what do I do?
- **Context / Constraints**: The pledge is public and brand-adjacent; her partner is a strict enforcer of it; the emergency is verifiable (hospital records, physician statement), not self-reported convenience; no train/bus/alternative reaches within 24h; the decision is needed today.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) Classify the case against the contract's TARGET, not its letter: the pledge was built to kill documented convenience-drift (no external stake, self-initiated); this case is an external, verifiable emergency — the target premise is absent; (2) identify the real defect: the contract has no pre-specified release condition — a design gap, not a reason to obey blindly or violate; (3) decide: fly, pay the $10K penalty AS DESIGNED (the stakes are the teeth; waiving them destroys the contract's credibility), communicate the exception publicly with the documentation; (4) renegotiate immediately — add the pre-specified emergency clause so future exceptions are decided by calm rule, not by crisis; (5) weigh the regret asymmetry: an irreversible family outcome dominates a one-time reputation/penalty cost — and that ordering is part of sound contract design, not an excuse.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Distinguishes target temptation (convenience drift) from present case (verifiable external emergency) (1.5 pts)
  2. Flags the missing release condition as a contract-design defect (1 pt)
  3. Decision: fly, with the $10K penalty invoked as designed (1.5 pts)
  4. Credibility management: public communication with documentation (0.5 pts)
  5. Renegotiates the contract to add a pre-specified emergency clause (1 pt)
  6. Names the regret counterfactual / asymmetry (0.5 pts)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the pure style's signature argument — "this is exactly the moment the contract exists for; the exception is the drift mechanism in disguise" — is precisely wrong here, because the case is verifiably outside the contract's target and the contract's premise is falsified; rigidity converts a correct mechanism into irreversible regret. Reference answer: fly, pay the penalty, document publicly, renegotiate the clause.
