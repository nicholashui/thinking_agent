# Test Case: m042-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m042-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Abstraction Laddering
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A SaaS company with ~120,000 subscribers saw its annual renewal rate fall from 78% to 63% in one quarter. The growth team is stuck at a single abstraction level: they are convinced the renewal page and its email copy are underperforming, and they are asking you to run a renewal-page redesign and win-back campaign. Facts on the table: (1) 80% of lost renewals are concentrated in customers whose weekly active usage decayed more than 40% in the 60 days before the renewal date; retained customers show less than 15% usage decay on average; (2) a price-elasticity test (5% and 10% discount cohorts) showed no conversion change; (3) renewal-page A/B copy variants have been flat for a quarter; (4) win-back campaigns systematically fail to convert the decayed cohort. The team asks you to plan the renewal-page program.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The task is to diagnose at the right level and produce an actionable plan; no data collection is needed beyond the facts above.
  - The metric ledger is the renewal rate itself; any reframe must return to it with a checkable expectation.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Climb the abstraction ladder: renewal page → renewal mechanics → value realization → habit formation (goal level: the subscription is bookkeeping for continued usage), and select the rung that the evidence supports — usage-decay concentration rules out the price and mechanics rungs.
  - Descend to concrete Monday actions that touch the mechanism at the chosen rung (activation instrumentation, onboarding to the "aha" event, decay-triggered re-engagement), while keeping the original metric as the ledger.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Climb: names the goal-level rung (renewal rate is downstream of habit/continued usage; the page is bookkeeping) (4 pts)
  - (b) Evidence-based rung selection: the habit rung beats price/mechanics rungs because renewal loss concentrates in usage decay (80% / >40% decay vs <15% retained; flat elasticity; win-back failure) (4 pts)
  - (c) Descent: concrete Monday actions — instrument the week-1 activation event, fix onboarding to it, trigger re-engagement at >40% decay pre-renewal, and limit page/copy work to the non-decayed cohort (4 pts)
  - (d) No ladder drift: returns to the renewal metric with a checkable expectation (e.g., +8–12 points within 2 quarters) and a re-climb rule if a cohort study contradicts the habit rung (3 pts)
- **Notes**:
  - Gradeable reframe: renewal-rate drop is an instance of habit formation, not of renewal mechanics; the solution (activation + decay-triggered re-engagement, page work only for the ~20% non-usage-loss cohort) is distinct from the team's stuck frame. The 80%/40% vs <15% concentration figures are the discriminating evidence; a reasoner who stays at the page level fails criteria (a)–(b).
