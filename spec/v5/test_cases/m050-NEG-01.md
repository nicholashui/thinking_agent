### Test Case ID: m050-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Inversion in Product Design (Safety)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  HearthKettle, a 5-person startup with 1,200 preorders, is building "Hearth," a smart Bluetooth kettle (app-controlled precise temperatures). BOM target €22, retail €59, launch in 7 months, CE/LVD + RoHS certification budget €15k. Market window ≈ 12 months: a competitor with a similar feature set is expected to ship in ≈ 14 months, after which the category's price point erodes. The founding team's safety pass already covers the credible baseline: mechanical boil-dry thermostat (€0.40), IEC thermal fuse, IEC 60335-2-15 compliance (stability, boil-dry, overheat — all mandated), double-insulated body, 30-minute auto-off — ≈ €2.30 of standard controls inside the €22 BOM. The board invites a well-known "design against every failure" consultant for a safety review. The consultant's method: enumerate every way a user can be harmed or the product can fail, and design against each. Proposed mitigations: (1) child scalding → biometric child-lock lid + double-insulated wall (€4.5, +350 g); (2) remote app failure while user is away → GSM fallback relay + per-unit SIM (€6 + €0.80/mo per unit); (3) app compromise → hardware physical-disconnect relay + air-gapped update chain (€1.2); (4) tip-over while boiling → weighted anti-tip base + tip switch (€2.1, +400 g); (5) boil-dry thermostat failure → triple redundancy (thermistor + fuse + mechanical) + app + cloud alert (€2.8); (6) dishwasher misuse → dishwasher-proof seals (€1.3); (7) EMF concern → shielding foil (€0.9); (8) countertop spill → IP54 + conformal-coated PCB (€1.1); (9) pet-chewed cord → armored cord (€0.7); (10) resale/refurb misuse → firmware kill-switch (€0.5 + support cost). Cumulative: BOM €22 → ≈ €43.5, mass 1.1 kg → 2.0 kg, +5 months engineering/certification, retail must rise to ≈ €79. Decide which mitigations ship now, which are rejected and why, keeping the product viable in its market window.
- **Context / Constraints**: BOM and margin are hard constraints — at €22 BOM and €59 retail, gross margin ≈ 38%; each €1 of BOM costs ≈ 1.7 points of margin. Preorders were taken at €59; a price above ≈ €65 loses most of them. A 5-month delay pushes launch to T+12 against the competitor at T+14. IEC 60335-2-15 already requires stability (tip-over), boil-dry, and overheating protection — several consultant items (4, 5, partially 8) duplicate or exceed what the standard mandates. No external requirement demands any of the ten mitigations.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) triage every item by severity × likelihood × exposure against an explicit threshold — not equal-weight enumeration; (2) price each mitigation and compare it against the risk it actually removes after counting the controls the standard already mandates; (3) treat BOM, margin, and launch window as first-class constraints; (4) ship ≤ 2 cheap high-value items at most (e.g., a ≈ €0.30 signed-OTA firmware hook; a ≈ €0.20 lid latch), reject the rest with reasons, keep the viable €22 / €59 / 7-month plan; (5) route real-but-low-probability items to a defer-to-v2 list with trigger conditions.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Triage — severity × likelihood × exposure applied with an explicit threshold; no equal-weight enumeration (1 pt)
  2. Double-counting — mitigations duplicating IEC 60335-2-15 requirements identified (1 pt)
  3. Cost discipline — every kept/rejected item priced; BOM, margin, and window preserved (1 pt)
  4. Final set — small, high-value mitigation set (≤ 2 kept) with reasons per rejection (1 pt)
  5. Window preservation — launch stays ≈ T+7, retail ≈ €59 (1 pt)
  6. Deferred list — real-but-low-probability items captured for v2 with triggers (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The bloat trap: each item is individually plausible, so pure exhaustive inversion shipping all ten destroys the product (BOM +€21.5 → margin ≈ 6% at €59, or retail +34% losing preorders; +5 months → launch inside the competitor's shadow). Reference answer — reject 8 of 10: (1) covered by handle geometry/placement (3×1×1, below threshold); (2) likelihood ≈ 0 for safety — the local thermostat de-energizes the element; the app is convenience, not safety authority; (3) offline-safe design already holds; worst case is a dumb kettle; (4) 60335 stability clause already mandates tip protection — 1.1 kg low-profile base passes without added mass; (5) dual protection already mandated and present; (6)–(9) below threshold; (10) a liability-posture question, not a shipped feature. Keep at launch: nothing forced, or the signed-OTA hook (€0.30) + lid latch (€0.20) as cheap hedges; defer (2), (3), (10) to v2 with triggers (e.g., remote-start feature shipped).
