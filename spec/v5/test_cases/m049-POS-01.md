### Test Case ID: m049-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: First Principles in Business Strategy (Domain-Specialized family)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  TerraPet, a DTC premium pet-food subscription: ARPU $60/month, gross margin 65% (≈ $39 contribution per subscriber-month). Current monthly churn 20% → average lifetime 5 months → LTV ≈ $195. The board has read the D2C growth playbook (public comparables spend 30–35% of revenue on paid acquisition; peer CAC ≈ $150; peer churn 6–8%) and proposes: scale paid acquisition to a $150 CAC target, add a 30% first-order discount (≈ $18 per acquisition), goal 3× subscribers in 12 months. The company's own 2024–25 cohort data (12,000 subscribers) shows churn stable at ≈ 20% across every acquisition channel, and 65% of all churn occurs in months 1–3 (onboarding/engagement failures, not price). Question: derive the strategy from unit economics and customer fundamentals — accept or reject the board's plan, and derive what the strategy should be.
- **Context / Constraints**: No new product launches planned; the category norms in the playbook are not a substitute for the company's own cohort physics. Budget: ≤ $1.5M incremental spend. A retention program (engagement redesign: onboarding box, reorder reminders, flavor-rotation plan) costs ≈ $400k and plausibly moves months-1–3 churn from 20% → 8% within two quarters (assumption to be validated). Monthly cohort churn measurement exists.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) derive retention physics from the cohort curve before any acquisition discussion; (2) compute the board plan's unit economics under TerraPet's OWN retention — CAC $150 + promo $18 vs LTV $195 → ≈ +$27 per customer before fixed overhead ≈ breakeven — and show the peer math (LTV/CAC ≈ 3.7) is a property of the peers' churn, not of TerraPet's; (3) name retention, not acquisition, as the binding constraint (65% of churn in months 1–3); (4) derive the acquisition budget rule from LTV and a margin target: CAC ceiling pre-fix ≈ $195 (breakeven), post-fix (churn 8%, LTV ≈ $487) ≈ $120 at 4:1; (5) reject the copycat playbook with the math; (6) quantified expectation + verification metric + falsifier.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. Retention physics derived first, from cohort data (churn → lifetime → LTV) (1 pt)
  2. Board plan's unit economics computed under TerraPet's retention: $195 − $150 − $18 ≈ $27/customer before ≈ $15–20 fixed overhead → ≈ breakeven; scaling multiplies ≈ zero (1 pt)
  3. Peer-math dependence exposed: the benchmark's LTV/CAC ≈ 3.7 requires peer churn 7% (lifetime ≈ 14 months, LTV ≈ $557); under 20% churn the same CAC is 1.3× LTV (1 pt)
  4. Binding constraint named: retention (churn concentrated in months 1–3; stable across channels) (1 pt)
  5. Derived acquisition rule: CAC ceiling pre-fix ≈ $195 (breakeven), post-fix ≈ $120 (4:1) with LTV ≈ $487 at churn 8%; promotion eliminated (−$18) (1 pt)
  6. Verification metric + falsifier: monthly cohort churn < 12% within 3 quarters; quarterly CAC/LTV tracked (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Checkable math: LTV = $39 × (1/0.20) = $195. Board plan per customer: $195 − $150 − $18 = +$27 before fixed overhead → ≈ breakeven; the 30% discount also depresses month-1 revenue. Peer math: churn 7% → lifetime ≈ 14.3 months → LTV ≈ $557 → $557 − $150 = +$407: the benchmark's profitability is a property of the peers' retention physics, not of TerraPet's. Derived strategy: ≈ $400k retention program (churn 20% → 8% → lifetime 12.5 months → LTV ≈ $487), then scale acquisition at CAC ≤ $120 (4:1) with no promo → customer-level contribution ≈ $368 vs ≈ $27 today; 3× subscriber growth only after the churn gate. Success requires deriving from the cohort curve, not from industry norms.
