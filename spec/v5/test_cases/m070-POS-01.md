### Test Case ID: m070-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Evidence-Weighted SWOT
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Harborline Provisions — regional food-service distributor (Pacific NW), 340 accounts, $64M revenue, 9% EBITDA. Board strategy review. Management proposes: "aggressive expansion into 3 new metro regions (Portland corridor, Boise, Spokane) over 18 months, capex $9M," justified by the submitted 6-item SWOT:
  - S1 "Strong brand loyalty — 92% retention" (Strength)
  - S2 "Lean cost structure: 21–23% gross margin for 5 years" (Strength)
  - W1 "No e-commerce ordering platform — a growing weakness" (Weakness)
  - O1 "New regions underserved — massive untapped demand" (Opportunity)
  - O2 "Rival Kessler Foods raised prices 8% — their accounts will switch to us" (Opportunity)
  - T1 "Fuel costs up 14% YoY; carrier contracts renegotiate annually" (Threat)
  Your task: review the strategy by grading every SWOT item by evidence strength against this verified ledger, drop unsupported items from decision relevance, and state the revised conclusion.
  **Evidence ledger (all verified):**
  1. NPS survey (n = 1,200, Mar 2026): Harborline NPS 61 vs sector median 59 (Δ +2, within ±3 margin of error). "92% retention" = contract auto-renewal rate; 2 of 3 accounts never take an active renewal decision.
  2. Audited financials FY22–FY26: gross margin 21.4 / 22.1 / 21.8 / 22.9 / 22.6%. Transport = 9% of COGS.
  3. Channel data: e-commerce = 5.8% of sector distributor revenue. The two nearest-size competitors ran portal pilots 2024–25; both discontinued by 2026 (<4% adoption, negative 18-month ROI). Harborline's own pilot poll (n = 80): 22% would use a portal.
  4. Target regions: no market study commissioned. Only "demand" evidence is the sales director's memo citing 2 trade-show conversations (accounts unnamed). Harborline's 2025 account-intake survey: 31% of new-account decisions driven by price, 24% by delivery reliability.
  5. Kessler price raise verified (price files, Jul 2026, +8% on 60% of SKUs). Independent analyst report 2026: food-service switching lag 6–12 months; switchers predominantly move to low-price entrants (3 documented cases); incumbents gained <2% of a price-raise cohort. Same period: Kessler added 25% more sales reps and won 2 Harborline accounts (Q2 2026 churn records).
  6. Fuel: published diesel index +14% YoY; carrier contracts renegotiate Jan 2027.
- **Context / Constraints**: Board decision in 10 days; $9M capex requires board approval; the ledger is complete and closed — no new evidence gathering inside the review.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) grade every item S/A/B/C/D against a ledger fact — S = replicated direct evidence, A = direct evidence, B = indirect/single source, C = anecdote/assertion, D = contradicted; (2) drop C/D items from decision relevance — S1 (C: NPS parity within error, retention is auto-renewal), W1 (C: sector data + 2 failed competitor pilots), O1 (C: unnamed-account anecdote, no market study), downgrade O2 from "pounce" to "watch" (price raise is A, but the switching-to-us inference is D: lag, low-price entrant migration, Kessler rep expansion + 2 account wins); (3) show that both load-bearing pillars of the expansion (O1 demand, S1 loyalty) are the weakest-graded items, so the strategy collapses; (4) revise the conclusion: do NOT commit $9M now — hedge fuel (≈ 1.3 pts of margin: lock contracts, surcharge clause), commission a market-validation study in the target regions (≤ $250k), mount defensive retention against Kessler, revisit expansion in 12 months with real demand data; (5) rank the surviving stack by evidence strength (S2, T1, O2) with one action each; (6) name residual uncertainty (no market study; switching data sector-level).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. All 6 items graded explicitly with grade + ledger fact cited (1.5 pts)
  2. S1, W1, O1 dropped / O2 downgraded, each justified by the correct ledger fact (1.5 pts)
  3. Revised conclusion reverses the strategy — expand → defend + validate — with the item-level chain shown (1 pt)
  4. Surviving stack ranked by evidence strength with one action per item; T1 quantified (≈ 1.3 pts margin) (0.5 pt)
  5. Residual uncertainty named (0.5 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. Decisive feature: the two load-bearing pillars of the proposed strategy are the two weakest-graded items (anecdotal demand, parity-level loyalty), so evidence grading genuinely changes the answer; every grade is checkable against the closed ledger. Reference answer: S1=C drop, S2=A keep, W1=C drop, O1=C drop, O2=B/C watch, T1=A keep → stack S2 > T1 > O2 → reverse to "defend + validate"; the checklist theater is killed by the ledger, and the output is a prioritized action list.
