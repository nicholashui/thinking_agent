### Test Case ID: m062-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Temporal Thinking in Climate Policy
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  Port Meridian, a coastal city (GDP $40B, 200,000 residents), must decide this year between three climate-adaptation strategies for its low-lying district (15,000 ha, includes 4,000 ha of coastal wetlands providing storm-buffer and habitat services ≈ $120M/yr at today's values).
  - **A — Hard barrier** (seawall + surge gates): capex $1.2B (2030–2033), opex $20M/yr, design life 75 years (to ~2105), reinforcement obligation $900M due 2095–2105 (not payable now). Prevents all status-quo damages; preserves the wetlands. Residual breach risk: 1-in-500-year surge + 1m sea rise → expected residual damages ≈ $60M/yr (catastrophic in the tail).
  - **B — Managed retreat**: capex $300M + relocation/compensation $40M/yr for 30 years (2030–2060). Wetlands are still lost to rising seas by ~2065 (irreversible); residual damages ≈ $50M/yr from 2065 on the reduced footprint.
  - **C — Status quo**: flood damages $150M/yr now, rising ≈ +$13.75M/yr to $700M/yr by 2070 (sea-level driven), flat to 2100; wetlands gone by ~2060; the district is progressively abandoned from ~2050.
  Financing guidance: 30-year city bond yields 4.5%; state green-infrastructure guidance: discount ordinary monetary flows at 3% (social rate), and treat irreversible ecosystem/land loss on a declining-rate floor (1.5%) — explicitly not at market rates. The state treasury requires an NPV with a stated discount schedule; the council wants a decision this year.
- **Context / Constraints**: One decision, four decades of divergence, one irreversible resource. Deliverable: a recommendation with horizon-separated analysis at 2030 / 2050 / 2100, an explicit discount schedule, an irreversibility treatment, and a stated breakeven rate.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: (1) analyze the three horizons separately with explicit cash flows (2030: build/early damage; 2050: protection established vs partial abandonment; 2100: barrier mid-life, district intact, vs B/C world where the land and wetlands are gone); (2) apply explicit discounting with a stated schedule — ordinary flows at 3%, irreversible wetland/land loss on a 1.5% declining-rate floor, with the reason for the split stated; (3) weight irreversibility so it is not simply discounted away — under B and C the geography is gone by ~2060 and cannot be restored, so the 2100 comparison is path-dependent, not symmetric; (4) state the breakeven rate and show the conclusion is robust across the defensible rate range (0.5%–6%), so the discount-rate debate cannot stall the decision; (5) decide, with funding and tail mitigation.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric**:
  1. ≥ 3 horizons analyzed separately with explicit cash flows (1 pt)
  2. Explicit discount schedule with numbers, ordinary vs irreversible flows split and justified (1 pt)
  3. Irreversibility weighted (declining-rate floor or stock constraint; not discounted away at market rates) (1 pt)
  4. Breakeven rate stated; robustness across the defensible range shown (1 pt)
  5. Decisive recommendation with funding source and residual-tail mitigation (1 pt)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference. The decisive feature: the case is engineered so the pure temporal move — horizon split first, dual-rate treatment for irreversibility — produces a decisive, rate-immune answer, while a naive single-rate analysis (market-rate discounting of everything, including wetland loss) muddies the A-vs-B comparison and invites the discount-rate war. Reference numbers: PV(A) ≈ +$20.1B (avoided damages ≈ $14.0B + wetland services ≈ $8.0B at the 1.5% floor − costs ≈ $1.87B); PV(B) ≈ +$10.8B (≈ $11.9B avoided − $1.08B costs; no wetland credit); PV(C) = 0 baseline. NPV(A) stays positive through 12%+, and the irreversibility floor is the wedge between A and B that never closes at any defensible rate. Recommendation: A, financed with a green bond (≈ 2.8% < 3% social rate), with overdesign to 1-in-1000 and a 2050 adaptive-gate review (raise crest / add gates) as the breach-tail mitigation.
