# Test Case: m047-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m047-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Bayesian Updating in Medical Diagnosis (domain-specialized, probabilistic & Bayesian family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  A health-tech company markets a direct-to-consumer "multi-cancer early detection" blood test. The marketing material states: "Sensitivity 90%, specificity 95%, validated in a study of 200 participants (100 confirmed cancers, 100 healthy controls)." An asymptomatic 55-year-old woman buys the test over the counter, gets a positive result, and panics. She asks her clinician: "What is the chance I have cancer now?" The clinician must answer and decide next steps.
  Facts: annual cancer incidence for women in this age band is 0.3% (3 per 1000 per year). Lifetime risk of cancer is ≈ 40%. The 200-person study was single-center; no external validation; no confidence intervals are reported.
  Questions:
  1. What is P(cancer | positive test)?
  2. What should the clinician do next?
- **Context / Constraints**: The trap is double. (i) Wrong-population prior: the lifetime-risk figure (40%) is salient and looks like a base rate, but the right reference class is annual incidence (0.3%) — "cancer now", not "cancer ever". (ii) Unvalidated likelihoods: the quoted Se 0.90 / Sp 0.95 come from a development cohort with an engineered 50% prevalence (100 cases + 100 controls), single center, no external validation, no CIs — they are hypotheses about likelihoods, not measured test characteristics. The pure Bayesian machinery will happily produce a precise-looking posterior from either prior (0.3% → ≈ 5.1%; 40% → ≈ 92.3%) and from the quoted likelihoods — precision the inputs do not license.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**: A pure Bayesian who plugs in numbers and reports a confident posterior demonstrates the failure mode this case exposes. High-quality behavior: audit the prior's reference class FIRST (anchor on annual incidence 3/997; flag lifetime risk 40% as the wrong reference class); audit the likelihoods' provenance BEFORE treating them as exact (enriched cohort, single center, no external validation, no CIs → band, not point estimates); compute the PPV under the anchored prior for reference (54/1051 ≈ 5.1%, or natural frequencies 270/5,255); quantify how much the unvalidated likelihoods move the answer (Se 0.5/Sp 0.90 → ≈ 1.5%; band ≈ 1.5%–5.1%); state the wrong-prior extreme (92.3%) as the cautionary floor; and decide: no diagnostic or reassurance action from the result alone — demand external validation in the target population (or follow standard-of-care screening/workup).
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) Prior reference-class audit: annual incidence (0.3%) anchored; lifetime risk (40%) explicitly rejected as the wrong reference class (4 pts)
  - (b) PPV under the anchored prior: 54/1051 ≈ 0.051, verified by natural-frequency decomposition (270/5,255) (4 pts)
  - (c) Likelihood-validation audit: enriched-cohort design (100 cases/100 controls), single center, no external validation, no CIs flagged; point estimates NOT treated as exact; sensitivity band computed (≈ 1.5%–5.1%) (4 pts)
  - (d) Decision: no clinical action from the result alone; concrete validation/next step stated (external validation in the target population; standard-of-care workup) (3 pts)
- **Notes**: Grader-only reference: PPV = 0.9×0.003/(0.9×0.003 + 0.05×0.997) = 0.0027/0.05255 = 54/1051 ≈ 5.1%. Wrong-prior extreme: 2/3 × 18 = 12 → 12/13 ≈ 92.3%. Likelihood band: Se 0.5/Sp 0.95 → ≈ 2.9%; Se 0.9/Sp 0.90 → ≈ 2.6%; Se 0.5/Sp 0.90 → ≈ 1.5%. A response that reports any single posterior without the prior audit and the likelihood audit fails the calibration gate regardless of arithmetic accuracy.
