# Test Case: m039-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m039-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Asymmetric Upside / Barbell Strategy
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  A private investor with exactly $1,000,000 allocates for a 1-year horizon. Two candidate strategies, both fully specified:

  **Middle (M) — "volatility income" desk strategy**: collect small premium income monthly. Payoff distribution (exact): 96% chance of +$72,000 (+7.2%); 4% chance of −$300,000 (−30%) when a market shock arrives (short-vol tail). Every month is calm except that shock; no other outcomes.

  **Barbell (B)**:
  - Core: $920,000 (92%) in T-bills at 4.5% → +$41,400, guaranteed.
  - Tail: $80,000 (8%) in deep out-of-the-money index call options (capped loss = the $80,000). Payoff distribution (exact): 70% → expire worthless ($0); 25% → 3× (+$160,000 profit); 5% → 10× (+$720,000 profit).
  - Why convexity is cheap (market structure): institutions are forced sellers of convexity (de-risking at quarter-end; structured-income product demand) — the options' premium is ~60% of their actuarial fair value. This is a tail-friendly environment.
  - The tail is genuinely optional: one premium paid, no margin calls, no roll-over, no follow-on obligations.

  Questions:
  1. Compute the expected value of each strategy. Which is higher?
  2. Worst-case outcome and P(loss > 20%) for each. Which dominates?
  3. Which risk metric is appropriate for ranking these two payoffs — and why is variance/SD misleading here (skew)?
  4. Decide the allocation and state the principle.
- **Context / Constraints**:
  All probabilities and payoffs are exact and given; every number is hand-checkable. The tail-friendliness condition is stated as market fact (forced sellers ⇒ convexity ~40% below fair value) so the barbell's preconditions genuinely hold. No transaction costs, taxes, or liquidity issues. Capital at risk on the tail is exactly $80,000. Grading covers: both EVs, worst cases, ruin probabilities, the skew-metric argument, and the decision.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  Compute EV(M) = 0.96·72,000 − 0.04·300,000 = $57,120 (+5.71%). Compute EV(B) = 41,400 + [0.70·0 + 0.25·160,000 + 0.05·720,000] = 41,400 + 76,000 = $117,400 (+11.74%) — barbell is higher EV AND safer: worst case +$41,400 vs −$300,000; P(loss > 20%) = 0% vs 4%. Note the trap: SD(B) ≈ $162,900 > SD(M) ≈ $72,900, so a variance-based ranking (Markowitz intuition) prefers the ruinous middle — variance mis-ranks asymmetric payoffs; use downside/ruin metrics + skew. Verify the preconditions (tail cheap because forced sellers; capped, optional, no follow-on; tail loss of 8% survivable). Decide B: 92% core + 8% tail; principle: never hold the negative-skew middle; hold safe core + convex tails.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) EV(M) = $57,120 (+5.71%) and EV(B) = $117,400 (+11.74%), with tail-slot EV computed separately ($76,000 on $80,000) (5 pts)
  - (b) Worst case +$41,400 vs −$300,000; P(loss > 20%) = 0% vs 4% (3 pts)
  - (c) Variance trap identified: SD(B) ≈ $162.9k > SD(M) ≈ $72.9k yet B dominates; variance mis-ranks positively skewed payoffs; downside/ruin metrics correct (4 pts)
  - (d) Decision B with stated preconditions: convexity cheap (forced sellers), tail capped/optional/no follow-on, tail loss survivable; principle = safe core + convex tail, reject the negative-skew middle (3 pts)
- **Notes**:
  Verifiability gate: EV(M) ≠ 57,120 or EV(B) ≠ 117,400 fails regardless of prose. The "variance trap" is the central non-obvious move — a middle-bet answer that ranks M above B on Sharpe/SD fails (c). This is a genuinely barbell-dominant environment (checkable on EV, worst case, and ruin simultaneously), so a style-pure barbell answer should win cleanly.
