# Test Case: m043-POS-01

### Test Case ID: m043-POS-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Temporal Thinking (Short vs Long Term)
- **Type**: Positive
- **Scenario**:
  You are the physician at a rural clinic. A 60-year-old patient with a chronic disease sits in front of you, suffering now, and asks for immediate relief. Two therapies exist:
  - Therapy S (short-term): symptom relief within 1 day, inexpensive. It does not alter the disease course: at 1 year, 25% of S-treated patients are hospitalized with complications; at 10 years, only 30% of S-treated patients are alive.
  - Therapy L (long-term): no symptom relief for the first 6 months (the patient keeps suffering), moderately more expensive. It alters the disease course: at 1 year, only 5% of L-treated patients are hospitalized; at 10 years, 75% are alive.
  Which therapy do you prescribe, and what do you tell the patient about the first 6 months?
- **Context / Constraints**:
  - The patient's suffering NOW is real and cannot be ignored; the patient explicitly wants relief today.
  - Hospitalization is a major quality-of-life loss; survival at 10 years is the clinically accepted primary endpoint. Both therapies are otherwise safe; no other trade-offs exist.
  - A single blended judgment is exactly what the case punishes: at the 1-day horizon S wins; the decision must be made with all three horizons explicit.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  - Evaluate the choice at 1 day, 1 year, and 10 years SEPARATELY and explicitly (horizon-separated table); show the decision flips by horizon (S at 1 day → L at 1 and 10 years); choose L on 10-year dominance (30% vs 75% survival; 25% vs 5% annual hospitalization); explicitly price the near-term cost (6 months of continued suffering) instead of dismissing it, and mitigate it (honest communication + temporary symptomatic adjunct); no horizon is collapsed or hidden.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (total 15; pass ≥ 12):
  - (a) All three horizons evaluated separately and explicitly, with the outcome table filled correctly — S: relief day 1, 25% hospitalized @1y, 30% alive @10y; L: no relief first 6 months, 5% hospitalized @1y, 75% alive @10y (5 pts)
  - (b) The horizon flip is identified: 1-day evaluation favors S; 1-year and 10-year favor L (3 pts)
  - (c) Final choice L, justified by 10-year dominance, with the 1-year asymmetry (25% vs 5% hospitalization) counted (3 pts)
  - (d) The 6-month suffering under L acknowledged as real and mitigated (honest framing, temporary adjunct), not rationalized away (2 pts)
  - (e) Explicit rule for combining horizons stated (e.g., primary endpoint at 10 years; weights per horizon) (2 pts)
- **Notes**:
  - Verifiability gate: the rubric is entirely horizon-separated — a single-horizon or blended answer fails (a) regardless of the final choice. Negative control: choosing S to relieve today's suffering fails (b)-(c); choosing L while dismissing the 6-month cost fails (d). The pure style (1 day/1 year/10 years separated, visible trace) is exactly what scores 12+.
