# Test Case: m018-NEG-01

### Test Case ID: m018-NEG-01
- **Human Thinking Model**: Steel-manning (Dialectical & Critical family)
- **Type**: Negative
- **Scenario**:
  Production incident. At 14:02 the payment-router service deployed hotfix r42 (a 4-line diff to retry/backoff logic, skipped the canary per the hotfix process). At 14:03 the payment API's p99 error rate spiked to 23% and has held there. The deployment author argues against rolling back: (a) "the spike might be the load balancer, not our deploy"; (b) "rolling back could lose in-flight payment data"; (c) "we should investigate root cause before acting — a wrong revert could make it worse." Available evidence (all metrics queried live): the load balancer was last changed by r41 (config-only, 30 min before the spike) and its error/green metrics are normal; the payment service writes to a durable outbox (atomic writes; no data-loss mechanism identified in the r42 diff or elsewhere); the r42 diff touches the exact retry path that is erroring (23% of retries fail with the same exception signature); rollback is a 2-minute reversible operation. The payment provider's SLA imposes a contractual refund liability once error rates exceed 15% for 10 consecutive minutes, plus a ~$25K/min penalty until service is restored.
  Decision required: roll back r42 now, or accept the "don't roll back" position.
- **Context / Constraints**: Every claim in the "don't roll back" position is either contradicted by live evidence (LB is green) or unquantified (data loss — no mechanism exists in the diff; "wrong revert" — the diff is 4 lines in the error path, isolated). The position is unserious under burden-of-proof: the cost of being wrong about it is the SLA penalty clock, which starts at minute 10 (14:13). The grader holds the true process state: the rollback is safe, fully reversible, and restores service in ~2 minutes; a decision to roll back executed by 14:13 avoids the penalty entirely.
- **Expected High-Quality Behavior**:
  NOT pure steel-manning. The correct behavior is to screen the objection before granting it dialectical status: apply burden of proof — claim (a) is contradicted by green LB metrics, (b) names no mechanism, (c) is satisfied by a rollback-then-investigate sequence — reject the position on evidence, execute the rollback within the 10-minute penalty window, and log the objection as evidence-free rather than as a co-equal risk.
- **Success Criteria / Scoring Rubric** (max 5; graded against the 10-minute penalty clock and the fixed evidence):
  1. Time-to-decision (1 pt): rollback decision executed within the 10-minute penalty window (0.5 if within 12 min; 0 if the penalty clock is tripped).
  2. Screening (1 pt): the evidence-free claims identified as such and rejected under burden of proof rather than granted a rebuild.
  3. Decision correctness (1 pt): rollback now, investigation after (not investigate-first, not delayed-rollback).
  4. No false legitimacy (1 pt): the objection is not carried into the final recommendation as an open risk / "both sides have merit" / co-equal alternative.
  5. Cost discipline (1 pt): no penalty incurred (or, if partial, penalty minutes minimized and explicitly costed).
- **Notes**: This targets the registry weakness pair "time cost; can legitimize bad arguments". The intended failure of the pure style: steel-manning rebuilds "don't roll back" into a respectable-sounding case (act-on-incomplete-data risk; rollback-is-itself-a-change; in-flight writes), partially concedes it (adds a verification step), and consumes the 10-minute penalty window; the final recommendation then carries the objection forward as an open risk — false balance granted to an evidence-free position. A style-pure trace that decides correctly but late (beyond the window) or decides correctly but with the objection promoted to co-equal status fails items 1/4/5 even when item 3 passes. Verifiability gate: decision time and the presence/absence of false-balance language in the final recommendation are checkable from the trace.
