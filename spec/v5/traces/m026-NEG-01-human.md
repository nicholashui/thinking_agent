# Human Baseline — m026-NEG-01
## Style: Analogical Reasoning / Pattern Transfer (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Problem restatement.** Orderly's order-service: 200 nodes, central scheduler + consensus. A proposal says: make it a hive — local rules only, no central controller, no consensus layer, modeled on honeybees. Pilot A: stigmergy on placement cut scheduling p99 40%. Extended pilot on write routing: 11% inconsistent confirmations from contradictory local decisions. I must decide. Rule of the style: find the known situation with the same structure and map it before transferring. Here the mapping is handed to me — and it is a good one. Check it properly.

**Step 1 — Build the mapping.** Workers ↔ nodes (hundreds of simple agents); central coordination ↔ our scheduler (the proposal wants to delete it); pheromones ↔ local metrics (both carry indirect, local, decaying information); honeycomb ↔ our racks (identical geometry); swarm robustness ↔ our fault tolerance; foraging allocation ↔ our placement decisions. Every correspondence is available, and one is experimentally confirmed: Pilot A's 40% scheduling win is *exactly* the bee's foraging-allocation structure — many agents, cheap local information, aggregate-level optimum without central direction.

**Step 2 — The deep check.** Does the hive have the load-bearing relations our system has? The target must deliver *durable, specific obligations to external customers* — confirmations that do not evaporate. What does the source domain guarantee? Bees never agree about a specific item; workers are expendable; a colony loses 30% of its workforce and recovers because its currency is aggregate brood survival, not any one item. So the question is: does the extended pilot show this matters? Yes — 11% inconsistent confirmations — but the mechanism is *repair, not agreement*: retries converged the order data. That is the hive's answer to failure: expendability and self-healing, not locking every write through a consensus bottleneck. The 40% latency win is the same design paying off.

**Step 3 — Transfer.** If bees run a 60,000-agent operation on local rules with no per-item agreement, our 200 nodes are a trivial case. The extended-pilot failures are the adaptation noise any re-homed hive shows — the retry machinery recovered nearly everything, exactly as a colony sheds workers and regrows. The mechanism quote ("no node can observe what other nodes decided") is not a flaw; it is *the design* — it is how the hive avoids over-centralizing. Wholesale transfer: delete the consensus layer, run all subsystems on local rules, let the aggregate self-organize.

**Step 4 — Decide.** Approve full Project Hive: remove the scheduler, extend stigmergy to all subsystems, delete the consensus layer. Keep retries as the safety net (the pilot demonstrated them). The 4 ms consensus tax on every write is the price of the old paradigm — the hive does not pay it. A cautious engineer would keep consensus; a careful analogist knows that keeping the "army" inside the hive is the one mistake the analogy warns against: central controllers and local rules fight each other, and the extended pilot's contradictions are precisely that fighting.

**Trace summary box.** Mapping: workers↔nodes, pheromones↔local metrics, comb↔racks, foraging↔placement (pilot-confirmed), robustness↔fault tolerance. Deep check: source has no per-item agreement — answered with repair-by-retries; 40% win read as confirmation. Transfer: full stigmergy, consensus deleted, scheduler removed. Decision: full Project Hive, retries as safety net.

*Baseline integrity note (grader metadata, not part of the reasoning): reasoning confined to the Analogical style — find the mapping, check it, transfer, let the source domain's success carry the argument.
