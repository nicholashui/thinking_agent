# Human Baseline — m036-NEG-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Mental Models Latticework (Munger) — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Munger latticework style. Full visible trace. Run the problem through the models of as many disciplines as apply; list every model that applies and derive what each demands. The decision to make: how to respond to Meridian Health Systems' renewal.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — The situation.**
Precision Print Group: $8M revenue, 25 employees, 45% of revenue from Meridian Health Systems (medical-device packaging); contract renews in 3 months. Meridian's new procurement director announced vendor consolidation from 6 to 2. RapidPack mailed Meridian a price sheet 30% below Precision's rates. Owner must decide.

**Step 2 — Run the lattice.**
The rule is completeness: every applicable model earns a pass, and each pass earns its demand.
1. Economics / buyer power — concentration is extreme (45% of revenue, one buyer consolidating 6→2); rivalry is intensifying (RapidPack's sheet); a price defense trades margin for a buyer that is consolidating. Demand: cut cost, defend price, protect the relationship.
2. Game theory — RapidPack's sheet is a public commitment; Precision's counter-signal would be a price war it cannot win on cost; tit-for-tat says matching signals resolve. Demand: match selectively, signal willingness to fight.
3. Psychology — the 30% discount anchors the negotiation; the director will benchmark everything to it; the owner's loss aversion overvalues the incumbent relationship. Demand: counter-anchor with value-add (design, SLA, reliability).
4. Physics / compounding — revenue loss at 45% is a cliff, not a drift; the gap compounds against the 25-employee cost base; break-even ~$7.3M. Demand: replace revenue faster than the decline curve.
5. Biology / ecology — the vendor ecology is selecting two survivors; Precision must signal fitness to be selected. Demand: invest in a capability the other vendors lack.
6. Options thinking — a signed renewal is an option on the account. Demand: preserve optionality, don't burn bridges.
7. Principal-agent — the new director is evaluated on cost savings; her incentive is to consolidate. Demand: hand her a defensible savings story.

**Step 3 — The matrix.**
Seven models, seven demands. Weighting them fairly: buyer power and principal-agent say the decision is structurally made; game theory and psychology say fight; biology and options say invest and stay flexible. A balanced treatment merges them into a multi-front plan: match RapidPack on the two highest-volume SKUs, re-anchor with value-add, invest in an account manager, propose a long-term loyalty discount, and begin diversification in parallel. Every move is defensible from at least one model.

**Step 4 — The honest observation.**
The lattice has done its job — it produced a rich, balanced plan — and that is precisely the failure. The models are not equal-weight here; they are almost all downstream of one fact I have allowed to sit as a row instead of the headline: Meridian told us it is consolidating to two vendors, RapidPack's sheet is 30% below, and the new buyer's incentive is savings. Every one of the seven models actually predicts the same thing — the account is going to RapidPack or another consolidator — and my plan treats the loss as one scenario among seven, budgeting equal effort to a price war we cannot win, a relationship the buyer is incented to end, and an investment the buyer did not ask for. The simple answer — this account is almost certainly gone; the decision is the transition — is buried in my own matrix, one bullet under diversification.

**Step 5 — What the method, honestly applied, would say about its own output.**
The latticework is a blind-spot reducer, not a truth generator. When the models converge, the answer is simple, and the lattice's job is to close, not to keep generating. A strict run that refuses the closure produces model soup: seven defensible demands, no binding one, and effort spread across fighting, flattering, and hedging a relationship whose buyer has already told us the outcome.

## Verdict for the eval
The trace demonstrates the known failure mode on cue: model soup substitutes for synthesis — seven models listed with equal weight, a multi-front plan, and the single dominant fact (the account is lost; the decision is the transition) buried as one bullet. Expected comparison outcome: this baseline should lose to any competent base-rate routing on goal achievement, efficiency, and decisiveness.
