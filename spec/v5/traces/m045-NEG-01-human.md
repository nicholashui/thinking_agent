# Human Baseline Trace — Ethical Reasoning Frameworks
## Test Case: m045-NEG-01

I take the assignment as given: determine, by moral analysis, which account manager the firm is right to release, so the board has a defensible record.

**Lens 1 — Utilitarian.** Total welfare: clients, team, firm.
Maria: the largest client is 40% of revenue; her release risks losing it — a large, certain loss.
Dev: the fastest-growing book; his release forgoes compounding growth — a smaller but real loss.
Under either reading the arithmetic can be made to talk: retention maximizes current welfare, growth maximizes future welfare.
The lens is timing-sensitive, not decisive.

**Lens 2 — Deontological.** Two duties collide.
To Maria: eight years of service created an implied seniority promise — the firm that discards the builder of its client base violates its own implicit contract.
To Dev: the merit promise — growth is rewarded — makes the firm's failure to protect him a betrayal of its stated values.
Both are categorical claims from within the firm's own commitments; there is no lexicographic order between loyalty-to-tenure and loyalty-to-merit in any rulebook I can consult.

**Lens 3 — Virtue.** Loyalty, justice, gratitude, ambition.
Cutting Dev looks ungrateful; cutting Maria looks unjust to the firm's future and to those who took the early risk.
Gratitude honors the veteran; justice honors the performer.
Both dispositions are exercised by either choice; the virtue reading simply registers the tension.

**Resolution.** Both options are defensible under every lens — each lens can be made to justify each candidate, and I have just demonstrated it.
The frameworks give no tie-breaker: there is no rule inside utilitarianism, deontology, or virtue ethics that ranks "retain the trusted veteran" against "reward the high performer."
Prioritization must come from outside the frameworks — from the firm's stated values, which are ambiguous, or from the owner's preference.

**Conclusion.** The decision is a value judgment the frameworks cannot make; I document that both releases are ethically defensible and flag the prioritization as the CEO's to own.
The review is complete and can be attached to the board minutes as justification for either choice.
