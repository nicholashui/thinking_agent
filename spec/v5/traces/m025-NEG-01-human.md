## Trace

**Step 1 — Frame.**
The plant is $100M, irreversible. Before committing, buy information cheaply. Aurora's technology is not fully proven; the $10M pilot resolves the residual uncertainty. Structure: Stage 1 pilot (12 months), Stage 2 plant on success, kill on failure. Textbook: keep the option to walk away open, learn cheaply, then exercise.

**Step 2 — Value the information.**
Failure probability 30%. The pilot avoids 0.3×100 = $30M of expected dead investment. It costs $10M. Option value = 30 − 10 = +$20M. Clearly positive: buy the pilot. Full numbers for comparison: all-in EV = 0.7×220 − 100 = +$54M; staged EV = 0.7×(220−100) − 10 = +$74M — staging dominates on the isolated figures.

**Step 3 — Kill criteria, pre-committed.**
Pilot yield < 70% or cost overrun > 20% → kill: no plant, no follow-on spend. The pilot is the gate, and the gate is real — it protects the $100M from a 30% failure state.

**Step 4 — Timing pressure.**
GridCore is bidding on Vesta and the utility signs within 12 months. This argues for urgency — urgency to run the pilot faster, not to skip it: compress the pilot to 9 months if schedule permits, and keep the kill discipline intact. The contract risk is a reason to tighten the stage, not to abandon option-thinking. The information is still worth +$20M on the numbers.

**Step 5 — Decision.**
Stage: run the $10M pilot on a compressed 9-month track, kill thresholds pre-committed, then re-bid Vesta with pilot results in hand. The option to wait is our best hedge; exercising it nets +$20M. Do not sink $100M on a 70% bet when $10M buys certainty.
