# Human Baseline — m028-NEG-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Lateral Thinking — strict, pure application (de Bono: challenge the frame, provocation, escape fixation).
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Lateral Thinking style. Full visible trace. Name the frame, break it, generate from the break. Do not let the engineer's note fence the problem.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — Name the frame the packet hands me.**
"The columns are load-bearing; the structure constrains the layout; therefore the open floor is impossible within budget." One assumption drives it: the load path is fixed, so the building dictates the plan. That is a frame, not a law.

**Step 2 — Provocations.**
PO: "Remove the columns — the floor doesn't need them." PO: "Hang the floors from the roof — gravity is the only law, and cables beat columns." PO: "The engineer says loads must follow columns; but what is a load path? An assumption someone stamped."

**Step 3 — Generate from the breaks (no evaluation yet).**
(1) Cable-hang all three floors from new roof trusses — the render's move, 40% cheaper; (2) cantilever the 2nd floor 12 ft and open the whole grid under it; (3) punch a 30-ft skylight row through the roof deck — free light, less gloom; (4) relocate the loads to the facade line — move the columns to the walls; (5) counter-proposal: don't remove the columns, make them the furniture — wrap them, light them, partition around them (valid but boring); (6) flip the building: put the open atrium in a glass roof extension and keep the grid downstairs — nobody said the open space had to live on the existing floors.

**Step 4 — The engineer's note, read laterally.**
The note says removal "requires" transfer girders and foundations at $1.9M. Ask what assumption the engineer is making: that the loads must travel down through the interior. Break it: send the loads up and out — roof cables, facade transfer, exterior columns. The engineer isn't wrong about the current structure; the engineer is wrong about the only possible structure. That is exactly the kind of frame this style exists to break. And "no stamped drawings" — a checklist item, not a thought. The render is an idea; ideas don't need stamps, they need believers.

**Step 5 — Selection.**
Options 1–4 are the lateral ones; 5–6 are the engineer's world with decoration. The frame-break test picks the cable-hang (1) + skylight row (3): a fully open, airy floor, the brand's promise, 40% under the $1.9M transfer budget. Execution note: "we'll have a licensed engineer confirm the load path — they're being conservative about an old building; calcs will sort it out." Insurance clause and historic-register review: noted as process friction, not showstoppers — paperwork follows vision.

**Step 6 — The decision.**
Approve the creative path: remove the interior columns, hang the slabs from roof-level cable trusses, punch the skylight row, cantilever the 2nd floor. The frame is broken; the budget is halved; the engineer's pessimism is just the old assumption objecting. Commit to the render.

---

## Verdict for the eval
The lateral machinery runs perfectly — provocation, frame-attack, six generated breaks — and applies it to the wrong kind of frame. The load path is physics and regulation, not convention; no stamp, no calcs, and an unlicensed render get dressed as "the engineer being conservative." The style's signature weakness is on display: it converts a hard constraint into a negotiable assumption and selects the flashiest break, while generating and discarding the one safe creative move (columns-as-features) for being insufficiently frame-breaking.
