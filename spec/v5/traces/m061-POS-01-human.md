# Human Baseline — m061-POS-01
## Style: Opportunity Cost in Personal Finance (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Price the purchase in its real currency first.** Maya sees an $8,000 option. That is not the price. The price is what $8,000 becomes by age 63 at the 7% real rate she assumes for her index portfolio: 1.07^35 ≈ 10.68 → 8,000 × 10.68 ≈ $85,400. At the 4% rule, that is ≈ $3,400 a year of retirement income, for life, starting at 63. The premium package does not cost $8,000 of today's money; it costs $3,400 a year of her 63-year-old self's life.

**Step 2 — Set the bar the spend must clear.** "Daily enjoyment" is a real good — so price it. The honest question: is the package worth $3,400 a year of guaranteed future income, every year, forever? That is a far higher bar than a one-time "I can afford it" — it is a $280/month subscription for life. She would not pay that for a trim. The bar must be named before the spend is judged, or the judgement is just rationalization.

**Step 3 — Kill the all-or-nothing by pricing the pieces.** Marginal analysis: rank the package's components by enjoyment per dollar, then re-price each in compounding units. The cheapest high-utility subset — aftermarket sound, ≈ $1,500 — compounds to 1,500 × 10.68 ≈ $16,000, i.e., ≈ $640/yr for life. Even the "cheap" piece fails the bar. The full package fails it by 5×. There is no subset that clears; therefore there is no subset.

**Step 4 — Where the money goes.** The full $8,000 goes into the index fund inside the retirement account. The account's illiquidity is a feature, not a cost: it locks the compounding in and removes the temptation to convert it back into a trim later.

**Step 5 — Decision.** Base model, no premium package. The deciding rule, applied once: every discretionary dollar is an investment by default; spend only what clears its compounded price. This one does not — and it is the math that says so, not willpower.

**Trace summary box.** 8,000 × 1.07^35 ≈ 85,400 → ≈ 3,400/yr for life (4% rule) → break-even named: enjoyment must be worth $3,400/yr forever → subset pricing: 1,500 → ≈ 16,000, fails too → premium rejected, full $8,000 invested → the compounding math, not self-discipline, decides.
