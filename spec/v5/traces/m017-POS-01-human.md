# Human Baseline Trace — Dialectical Reasoning (Thesis → Antithesis → Synthesis)
## Test Case: m017-POS-01

This is a conflict between two positions that both believe they are right. I will make each side as strong as I can, find the axis of the disagreement, and only then attempt the synthesis. The synthesis is not a compromise; it is a structure that contains both truths.

### Thesis — Diesel, in its strongest form
- Energy density 38 MJ/L; refuel in 10 minutes; no infrastructure — the truck works anywhere on the property, today. The fleet burns 20 × 6000 h × 60 L/h = 7.2M L/yr; at $1.10 that is $7.92M/yr — the price of not being hostage to a grid.
- Core interest of the thesis: unconditional mobility, zero dependency on fixed assets.
- Its honest cost: 19,300 t CO2/yr, and the mine's license to operate is under attack on exactly this line.

### Antithesis — Battery-electric, in its strongest form
- Zero tailpipe CO2; ~90% drivetrain efficiency against diesel's 40%; the ramp climb is precisely where diesel burns most and electric wins most: 60% of all cycle energy, on one fixed 8 km route, at $0.10/kWh.
- Core interest of the antithesis: the cheapest, cleanest energy exactly where energy is spent.
- Its honest cost: 20 × $0.7M + $4M depot = $18M, and then the +4 t battery cuts payload 8%, 2 h charging cuts availability 25%, and the substation serves at most 60% of charging peak. Full electrification is not available today.

### The axis of the conflict
They are not disagreeing about powertrains. They are disagreeing about where fixed infrastructure is acceptable. The ramp is one rigid route carrying 60% of all energy — infrastructure is cheapest exactly where energy is densest. Pit-floor work is irregular, grid-less, flexible — infrastructure there is impossible. The axis is route rigidity, and the two positions live on opposite ends of it.

### Synthesis — partition along the axis
- 15 trucks: trolley-assist (pantograph + 200 kWh battery) on the ramp. Capex: $1.5M/km × 8 km = $12M line + $0.35M × 15 = $5.25M conversions = $17.25M.
- 5 trucks: diesel, retained for the flexible work the grid can never reach.
- The diesel thesis's core interest (unconditional mobility) is preserved where it is actually needed; the electric thesis's core interest (cheap, clean energy) is captured where energy is actually spent. Each camp loses only its overreach, not its truth.

### Verification
- Electrified share: 4.32M L/yr displaced → $4.75M/yr fuel saved; 11,600 t CO2 cut.
- Grid draw: 4.32M L × 38 MJ/L × 0.40 ÷ 0.90 ≈ 20 GWh ≈ $2.0M/yr → net $2.75M + $0.6M maintenance = $3.3M/yr → payback 17.25/3.3 ≈ 5 years.
- Full-BEV for comparison: ≈ $34M with payload and availability penalties and a substation gap — twice the capex, still not whole.
- Boundary condition — the synthesis has limits: if grid capacity < 60% of ramp demand, or the horizon < 6 years, the partition collapses and the honest answer reverts to diesel.

### Conclusion
Trolley-battery on the ramp, diesel in the field: 60% of energy and emissions electrified at ≈ 5-year payback — a structure that contains both truths. And I state where it fails: if the boundary conditions do not hold, this synthesis is fiction and the either/or is real again.
