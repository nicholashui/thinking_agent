# Human Baseline — m089-NEG-01
## Style: Optionality Preservation (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — First move: count the doors.** The CEO's instinct is right to examine before signing. What does signing Nordik close? A 3-year volume commitment — it ties a floor to our orders and locks us to Nordik's terms for three years. What does not signing keep open? Honkoh. The cheaper supplier, in certification talks, could open a materially better economics door. A 22% discount is a bird in hand; Honkoh is potentially two birds.

**Step 2 — The deadline is the tell.** Nordik says "10 days, or the deal goes to competitors." Pressure tactics. A genuine option gives you room; a hard deadline is designed to make you commit before you know. I should not let their calendar force my decision. Options are preserved by refusing to be rushed.

**Step 3 — Price the open door.** Honkoh certification within 12 months: 15%. If it lands, savings beyond Nordik's discount could be 10–15% of our materials bill plus a second certified source — a second source alone is valuable optionality: negotiation leverage, redundancy, protection if Nordik stumbles. 15% × that is a real expected value, and it is strictly increasing while we wait: every month uncommitted, our leverage grows.

**Step 4 — The commitment is the hidden cost.** A volume commitment means we cannot downscale if the new product line slips — we would be paying for minimums on a postponed launch. Signing now converts a flexible future into a fixed obligation. Non-signature keeps volume flexible: we can always sign later if Honkoh falls through — the Nordik door does not actually close, it just gets more expensive. Expensive is not closed.

**Step 5 — Wait, with a holding pattern.** Tell Nordik we will revisit at the 30-day mark; hold the offer window open through negotiation. If they truly walk, the cost is a smaller discount and list-price allocation — the price of keeping every door open. That is the optionality price, and optionality is worth paying for. Keep the Honkoh door, keep the volume flexibility, keep the leverage.

**Step 6 — Decision.** Do not sign. Hold off. Preserve optionality: no binding commitment while a cheaper door is still resolving. Decision is deferred — not made, not closed.

**Trace summary box.** doors counted (signing closes volume-flexibility + locks Nordik terms; not signing keeps Honkoh + leverage + flexibility) → deadline treated as pressure, not fact → Honkoh priced at 15% + second-source value → commitment seen as hidden cost → holding pattern selected → do not sign.
