## Trace

**Step 1 — The question.**
Gridlock Analytics, $40M ARR, NRR 92%, 1,200 accounts. Board proposal: raise list price 25% at renewal for all accounts to fund GridlockAI. CFO model: elasticity −0.3 → +15% revenue. Asked: is the uniform price rise right?

**Step 2 — Economics: incentives and where the decision actually gets made.**
The CFO's curve assumes the renewal decision is the user's. It is not. Sales/CS comp is 100% new-ARR — nobody is paid to retain, so a renewal fight has no owner. And the binding agents are the customers' own buyers: procurement officers evaluated on cost savings. A 25% renewal increase above $50K triggers mandatory competitive tender — in the contract data. For the Legacy block the price decision is a procurement gate, not a price-elasticity curve. Implication: the uniform raise is fiction for 70% of ARR.

**Step 3 — Psychology: endowment, loss aversion, anchors.**
Founder-era accounts hold multi-year prepaid contracts with a grandfather clause — a price raise opens a 90-day no-penalty exit in the customer's hands. Their users have three years of embedded dashboards and anchor on the founders' price. Raising price converts a clause-guaranteed, compounding stream into a decision event the customer can exit for free. Implication: do not disturb Legacy; grandfather it.

**Step 4 — Physics: the rate-of-change constraint.**
GridlockAI needs GPU inference; capacity has a 9-month lead time; today's infrastructure runs at 1.2x load. Revenue cannot ramp faster than the bottleneck carries it: if the price rise lands before capacity, latency degrades, tickets spike, and the CS team — the same constrained resource that would handle renewals — is consumed. Implication: any Scale increase is gated by the capacity ramp, not the marketing calendar.

**Step 5 — Biology: Red Queen and selective pressure.**
VisioData's roadmap shows the same AI feature at a comparable price — parity is coming, so the willingness-to-pay window closes on a clock; that argues for monetizing Scale early. And the base is an ecology: Legacy contains low-fitness accounts (low usage, price-sensitive, support-consuming) that would leave anyway; discount-retaining them keeps weak competitors in the market for the same resources. Selective pressure is free segmentation. Implication: let low-fitness accounts cull at renewal; concentrate retention on high-fitness Scale; price the Scale increase ahead of the parity clock.

**Step 6 — Mathematics: the compounding check.**
Grandfathering Legacy keeps ~$28M at ~92% NRR; a botched raise converts the block to tenders and risks more than the $7M gross it would add. A staged Scale increase on $12M at even +15% adds ~$1.8M/yr, compounding into the AI feature's moat. Expected-value math: the segmented path dominates the uniform path before discounting.

**Step 7 — The intersection.**
The models do not give separate answers; they jointly constrain one: (1) grandfather Legacy at founders' price — no tender-triggering increase; (2) raise Scale at renewal in two anchored steps (prepay discount as the anchor), gated to the GPU ramp; (3) restructure CS comp to retention-weighted before any renewal push, so the increase has an owner; (4) no discount-retention for low-fitness Legacy accounts — let them cull; (5) front-load the Scale increase ahead of VisioData's parity clock, but never faster than capacity.

**Step 8 — The softest number.**
Every implication depends on one unmeasured quantity: the true probability a tender triggers at the $50K threshold, and the real Scale elasticity. The plan is built so the decision is robust to it — grandfathering is right whether the tender probability is high or low. Calibration: measure it in the first quarter; do not let it drive the decision before then.

## Verdict for the eval
The lattice is run deliberately: five models, five binding implications, one intersection — the segmented, staggered, comp-aligned plan. The uniform raise is rejected on model-grounded grounds, not preference; the strongest single-answer temptation (the CFO's elasticity) is defeated by the principal-agent structure the lattice forces into view. All rubric items met.
