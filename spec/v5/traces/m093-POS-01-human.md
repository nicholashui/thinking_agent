**Step 1 — Write the explanation in plain words, on paper, out loud.** "The fridge makes cold, and the thermostat keeps it cold." Clean. Read it back the way a child would hear it: cold is a thing the fridge makes. Fine so far — the sentence sounds complete.

**Step 2 — Read it back like a bright 12-year-old.** "Where does the cold come from?" ... And there it breaks.

**Step 3 — Let the break teach.** The back of the fridge is WARM. If the fridge made cold and put it inside, the outside should feel cold too — heat doesn't pick sides. The sentence "the fridge makes cold" dies exactly at the word "makes."

**Step 4 — Own the breakdown.** I don't actually know where the cold comes from. I know the words — coolant, compressor, condenser — and no mechanism behind them. This is the moment the method exists for: the words were wearing a physics costume.

**Step 5 — Name the gap precisely.** Cold is not a thing the fridge produces — cold is the absence of heat. A refrigerator is a machine that moves heat out of a box and into the room. The child's question proved I couldn't say how. The gap, named: how does a machine move heat out of a box and into the room?

**Step 6 — Repair with the physics.** Boiling absorbs heat — that is the engine. The refrigerant is a fluid that boils at a low temperature when the pressure is low; inside the fridge it sits at low pressure, so it boils, and boiling pulls heat out of the food and the air.

**Step 7 — Follow the loop.** The compressor then squeezes the gas — compression heats it. Outside, in the coils on the back, the hot gas condenses back to liquid, and condensation releases the heat into the room. That is exactly why the back is warm.

**Step 8 — Check the leak and the work.** The pump does work the whole time — it fights the natural direction, because heat always wants to flow back in. Insulation just slows that leak. Nothing is created; everything is moved.

**Step 9 — Re-explain simply and check.** "Your fridge doesn't make cold — it moves heat. A refrigerant runs in a loop: inside the fridge it boils, and boiling pulls heat out of the food; the compressor squeezes the gas until it's hot; the coils on the back let that heat escape into the room — that's why the back feels warm. The insulation just slows the heat creeping back in, and the thermostat just decides when the pump needs to run."

**Step 10 — Run the acceptance test.** Where does the cold come from? — it doesn't; heat is removed. Why is the back warm? — that's where the heat lands. Why doesn't it get cold again? — insulation slows the leak, and the pump keeps re-fighting it. Every question answered without error.

**Step 11 — Recommendation.** Give the VP the re-explained version. Priya's stall was a vocabulary-to-mechanism gap: "makes cold" is the sound of not understanding, and the child's question is the instrument that found it.
