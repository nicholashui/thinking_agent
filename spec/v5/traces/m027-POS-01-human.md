# Human Baseline — m027-POS-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Design Thinking — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Design Thinking style. Full visible trace: Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test → iterate. Do not propose a solution before the empathize/define steps have produced the need from observation data.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — EMPATHIZE. Watch, do not ask.**
The survey said: "we forget, give us a simpler pillbox with bigger print and a louder alarm." I ignore the survey for now and read the 12-home observation field notes and diaries instead. What I actually see: 10 of 12 don't use the pillbox at all. 9 of 12 improvise rituals — flipping the bottle, tick-marks on the label, moving the bottle across the counter. 8 of 12 describe the daily fear as "I can't remember if I already took today's dose — so I skip it to avoid double-dosing." 10 of 12 dread refill week: reconciling 4–7 bottles, matching generics, filling compartments — "an hour of anxiety I do twice a month." 7 of 12 homes have the alarm-based reminder switched OFF. 3 of 12 traveled with loose pills and mixed them.

**Step 2 — DEFINE. The need is not what was said.**
Point of view: a 78-year-old who must manage 5 chronic medications needs to CONFIRM whether today's dose was taken, and needs refill week to be fast and error-free — not to be reminded. The survey's stated need (print size, alarm volume) is contradicted by the evidence: the alarm device already exists and is switched off in 7 of 12 homes; the pillbox exists and is unused by 10 of 12. The design target is dose-state verification and refill-week reconciliation. "Verify, don't remind."

**Step 3 — IDEATE. ≥3 concepts.**
A — Deluxe loud-alarm pillbox, 28 compartments, bigger print (the survey's ask). B — Card-sized weekly strip: 7 cells, one per day, a mechanical latch you click after taking the dose, plus a color dial showing today. C — Caregiver photo-verification app: adult child photographs the morning dose; receives confirmation. D — Refill-week service: pharmacy pre-sorts the week into numbered blisters; the patient just takes the right blister.

**Step 4 — PROTOTYPE + TEST (v1).**
Low-fi v1 of B: paper-backed plastic strip, 7 cells, spring latch, push-dial. Tested with 5 seniors, ages 70–88, in their homes, tasks: "take today's dose, mark it taken, reset for tomorrow." Results: 4 of 5 cannot close the latch — arthritic pinch strength is insufficient; 2 of 5 misread the daily dial; 1 of 5 drops the strip while opening. The caregiver app (C): 3 of 5 seniors refuse to photograph pills ("I won't photograph my medicine"), but the one caregiver in the room loves it. The observation data is doing its job: the failure is in the test, not in the idea.

**Step 5 — ITERATE (v2) + RETEST.**
v2 redesigns from the test failures: oversized thumb tab (a slide, not a pinch), color-coded dial with a single "today" window, one-handed close, larger cells. Retest, same 5 seniors: 5 of 5 complete all tasks. Separate test of the refill blister (D) with 4 seniors: refill time drops from "an hour of sorting" to minutes; 4 of 4 report the sorting-error anxiety "gone."

**Step 6 — FINAL recommendation.**
Ship: taken-state verification strip (v2 mechanics) as the core device, prefilled weekly blisters as the pharmacy service, caregiver photo-check as an optional add-on for the minority who use it. The survey's print/alarm features are demoted explicitly: the evidence says reminder devices get switched off and verification devices get used. The insight came from watching the rituals, not from reading the survey.

---

## Verdict for the eval
The trace demonstrates the method on its home ground: the real need (verify-not-remind, refill reconciliation) came from observation data that contradicted the survey's stated need; the define step overrode the survey with evidence; the v1 prototype test found a genuine usability failure that reasoning alone would not have caught; the v2 spec was written from that test; and the final artifact targets the observed job-to-be-done. All rubric items met.
