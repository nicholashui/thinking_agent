# Human Baseline — m085-POS-01
## Style: Tragedy of the Commons Analysis (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — The question: is this a shared resource heading to overuse?**
Run the two structural tests on the Balquinta aquifer. Rivalry: one co-op's acre-feet leave less for everyone — yes. Excludability: anyone may drill; nothing keeps a co-op out — no. Rivalrous + non-excludable = open-access common-pool resource, and its only stable behavior is over-extraction. This is a structural property, not a comment on anyone's character.

**Step 2 — Quantify the depletion; the binding number is the intrusion line.**
Extraction 12,500 vs recharge 10,000 → net drawdown 2,500 ac-ft/yr. Usable storage 200,000; saltwater intrusion begins at 30,000 ac-ft of cumulative loss → 30,000 / 2,500 = **12 years to intrusion**. Full exhaustion would take 80 years, but the aquifer is dead in 12 — the intrusion line is the clock, and it makes the decision "now."

**Step 3 — The incentive structure explains the 11 refusals, so the voluntary path is falsified.**
The cost of restraint is private and complete (that co-op forgoes the full income of its acre-feet); the benefit of restraint is shared across all 12 (a slightly later intrusion line everyone enjoys). Each co-op therefore maximizes by pumping now and waiting for the other 11 — which is exactly what happened. Co-op 4's voluntary proposal is not morally disappointing; it is structurally doomed, and the design must not rely on it.

**Step 4 — Design the governance: cap at recharge, hard and binding.**
The only sustainable extraction level is the inflow: total cap = 10,000 ac-ft/yr. Allocation: 833 ac-ft per co-op (10,000 / 12), or better, tradable rights with the 3 older co-ops grandfathered at historical share — trade lets water flow to highest value without ever busting the aggregate cap.

**Step 5 — Enforcement is part of the design, not an afterthought.**
Meter every well — no meter, no extraction. Escalating penalties for over-extraction; a cap-and-trade ledger so the aggregate stays hard-capped even when rights change hands. A quota without metering is a suggestion, and a suggestion is what we already have.

**Step 6 — Make the transition feasible.**
The cap cuts aggregate extraction 20%. Fund compensation from license/trade fees so the cut is survivable — otherwise the design fails politically where it could have passed, and the aquifer dies while the lawsuit runs.

**Step 7 — Name the residual uncertainty.**
Recharge varies with drought: the intrusion line moves with inflow, so the cap must ratchet down in dry years. The cap is a schedule, not a fixed number.

**Trace summary box.**
open-access diagnosis (rivalrous + non-excludable) → drawdown 2,500 ac-ft/yr → **12 years to intrusion** (30,000/2,500) → voluntary path falsified by the incentive structure (private cost, shared benefit — the 11 refusals ARE the equilibrium) → governance: cap = recharge = 10,000 total, 833/co-op or tradable rights with grandfathering → metering + penalties + hard cap → transition compensation → drought ratchet.
