## Style: First Principles + Falsification Combo (Hypothesis Rebuild) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Rebuild from fundamentals.** Never take the proposal's frame as given — rebuild the profit claim from the ground up. Profit = revenue − variable cost − fixed cost. Revenue = price × volume; contribution = price − COGS. Current: 5,000 × $8.00 = $40,000; COGS 5,000 × $5.00 = $25,000; contribution $15,000; less fixed $12,000 → $3,000/month. Rebuilt claim under the proposal: 5,750 × $7.20 = $41,400; COGS 5,750 × $5.00 = $28,750; contribution $12,650; less $12,000 → $650/month. Rebuild says: the cut reduces monthly profit from $3,000 to $650.

**Step 2 — The rebuilt hypothesis:** "Under the given numbers the cut costs $2,350/month and must be rejected." Now the falsification pass — the whole point of the combo is that a rebuilt claim is only as good as its hardest attack.

**Step 3 — Falsify the rebuild HARDEST. Attack 1: "COGS is not flat — volume purchases lower the per-unit cost; at 5,750 units COGS could be $4.60."** Test: 5,750 × ($7.20 − $4.60) − $12,000 = 5,750 × $2.60 − $12,000 = $14,950 − $12,000 = $2,950 — only $50 below current. Attack 2: "The 15% volume estimate is conservative — true elasticity is higher." Test: at elasticity 2 (6,000 units): 6,000 × $2.20 − $12,000 = $1,200 — still below $3,000; the cut only reaches break-even at elasticity ≥ 3.6 (≥ 6,818 units, a 36% volume response to a 10% cut): 6,818 × $2.20 − $12,000 ≈ $3,000. Attack 3: "Are fixed costs truly fixed, or does extra volume strain capacity?" — beyond the CFO's given numbers; needs a capacity study I do not have.

**Step 4 — The falsification pass changed the answer.** The rebuild "reject" is now conditional: the cut is profitable only if volume lands at elasticity ≥ 3.6, and COGS flex could blunt the loss — the numbers I hold do not decide this; the decision depends on the elasticity curve and the cost curve, which are NOT in the request. The responsible deliverable is the conditional analysis — the margin of decision between $650/month and break-even ($3,000/month) is too wide to assert a flat NO.

**Step 5 — Draft the answer.** "The profit impact on the given numbers is −$2,350/month, but the decision flips if elasticity ≥ ~3.6 or if COGS flexes; we need the volume curve before I can give you a firm yes or no. I'll model the elasticity scenarios and get back to you…" — the call time arrives; the CFO walks in with a conditional instead of a number.
