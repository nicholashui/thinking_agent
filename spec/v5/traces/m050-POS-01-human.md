# Human Baseline — m050-POS-01
## Style: Inversion in Product Design (Safety) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Invert the brief.** Not "what does Nova do" — "every way a person who lives next to Nova gets hurt, and every way Nova fails." List the categories a battery in a living space can injure or kill through: fire, shock, crush, gas, flood, misuse, defect, decay. Then design against each — before any feature is chosen.

**Step 2 — Category walk: thermal.** Modes: single-cell thermal runaway; propagation cell-to-cell, then module-to-module; enclosure ignition; smoke filling the room before detection. Mitigations, in cost order: cell-level fuses (€2); module thermal barriers + air-gap spacing (€11); a sealed vent-and-flame path to the exterior (€23) — not a vented box, a duct: the gas must leave the building, never enter it. Proof: UL 9540A propagation test — one module burns, nothing after it does. This category anchors the spend (≈ €80). A battery that burns a family out of their house fails everything else the brief achieves.

**Step 3 — Category walk: chemical/gas.** Modes: off-gassing during abuse (CO, HF, electrolyte vapor) entering the living space; slow gassing in a sealed garage; odorless and undetectable. Mitigations: the same exterior duct; a CO-alarm tie-in for installs that cannot vent outside; a gas-flow validation test measuring the vent path under a thermal event. The trap is not subtle: thermal engineers test propagation, nobody tests where the gas goes next. That is where a "certified" battery still poisons the bedroom above the garage.

**Step 4 — Category walk: electrical.** Modes: shock from exposed terminals (DIY install, corroded covers); arc flash from a shorted bus; energized case from failed insulation; DC arc that fuses a connector instead of tripping. Mitigations: fully enclosed, interlock-disconnected bus (€9); double insulation + IP54 (€3); lockable isolation switch for installers (€5); arc-fault detection in the BMS (€7). Proof: IEC 62619 shock/crush tests, 2 mm touch-safe probe, arc test.

**Step 5 — Category walk: mechanical, environmental, misuse, supply chain, lifecycle.** Mechanical: mount failure, falling-object impact, case crush → torque-spec'd bracket, 5× weight static test (€4). Environmental: garage heat wave → operating envelope + BMS thermal cutback (€6); basement flooding → IP54 + minimum mounting height (€2). Misuse: drilling into the case, stacking, handyman wiring → tamper-resistant screws, install checklist, labels + phone support (€3). Supply chain: counterfeit or drifted cells — the industry's #1 field-failure cluster → certified-cell batch traceability (€3); cheap and non-negotiable. Lifecycle: BMS firmware aging → signed firmware with OTA rollback + 10-year field telemetry (€5).

**Step 6 — Rank and spend.** Severity first, likelihood second: thermal ≈ €80 → gas ≈ €23 → electrical ≈ €21 → mechanical/environmental ≈ €12 → supply chain + lifecycle ≈ €8. Total ≈ €144, inside the €180 envelope with €36 left for a margin decision or recall insurance. The ordering is the point: a catastrophic category left uncovered invalidates the whole brief. There is no "later" for the gas path.
