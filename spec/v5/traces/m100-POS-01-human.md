## Style: First Principles + Falsification Combo (Hypothesis Rebuild) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Strip the proposal down to fundamentals.** Demand is 16,800/week = 2,800/day. Capacity is not "vans." Capacity = min(vans, drivers, docks, pick crew) × deliveries per van-day, and the van-day output is the route-planning norm of 50 deliveries (union work rules cap routes at ~10 hours with admin buffer — the norm sits at that edge). Compute each pool: vans 40; drivers 52; docks 4 × (600 min / 8 min) = 300 van-trips/day; pick 60 loads/day = 360/week = 18,000/week. Van-day need: 2,800 ÷ 50 = 56. Docks and pick are not binding. The REBUILD: the binding pool is min(vans 40, drivers 52) — and the trap is right here: the naive "double the vans" assumes vans bind, but drivers bind first — 52 drivers cap running vans at 52 no matter how many vans are bought.

**Step 2 — Rebuild hypothesis (constructed):** "At 16,800/week the binding constraint is the driver pool (52 < 56); Vesper needs 56 vans and 56 drivers — buy 16 vans, hire 4 drivers (~$1.2M), not 40 vans."

**Step 3 — Falsify the rebuild HARDEST. Attack 1, the utilization counter: "40% idle means 40 vans already have the headroom — your rebuild is false; don't spend anything."** Test it: 40 vans at 50/van-day deliver 12,000/week — the 40% idle exists (8.3 trips vs 12.5 possible), but filling it means 70/van-day, which is 11.7 trips ≈ 9.85 hrs with admin buffer — at or over the work-rule edge; not enforceable without OT, which is banned. And even if the norm flexed, idle van-hours are not dispatchable without drivers — only 12 spare drivers exist (52 − 40), capping the fleet at 52 × 50 × 6 = 15,600/week, short of 16,800. The attack is half-right (vans do idle) and rejected where it matters (no dispatchable capacity). Trap identified: utilization reads at the van level dissolve at the pool level.

**Step 4 — Attack 2, the norm-degradation counter: "your 50/van-day norm assumes today's traffic; at 2,800/day the same routes run longer — congestion + admin push the effective norm to ~47."** Test it: 2,800 ÷ 47 ≈ 60 van-days — the rebuild needs 60 vans and 60 drivers, not 56/56. This attack PARTIALLY SUCCEEDS: the plan has no buffer against route-time degradation, the highest-margin risk of the next 6 months. Revise the rebuild: buy 20 vans + hire 8 drivers (~$1.4M), and re-measure the effective norm at the 2,800/day load before the second tranche — a pre-committed review point.

**Step 5 — Attack 3, the union-hour counter: "flex the 10-hr cap."** Fails short-term: the agreement runs 9 months; demand hits in 6. Not a lever in the window.

**Step 6 — Conclusion, falsification-verified.** Buy ~20 vans now + hire ~8 drivers (~$1.4M) — half the $2.8M naive spend. The 40-van plan fails on its own numbers: with 80 vans the fleet still delivers 52 × 50 × 6 = 15,600/week — short of 16,800. Re-measure the route norm at the new load; re-open the driver cap when the contract window arrives.
