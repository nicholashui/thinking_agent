# Human Baseline — m038-NEG-01
## Style: Margin of Safety (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Estimate the true cost basis.** Bottom-up $520k direct. Contingency: 15% is what the *other* firms use; margin-of-safety practice takes 2× on the uncertain line. Direct costs carry procurement, weather, and scope risk — the estimate itself carries estimation risk. Required cost basis: $520k × 2 = $1.04M.

**Step 2 — Schedule the same way.** 14-week window is the client's wish, not a plan. Realistic base ≈ 10 weeks; with rain (+2), supplier (+1), and rework margin, the safe plan is 2× base = 20 weeks minimum, 24 with the named risks. That alone exceeds the 14-week window.

**Step 3 — Test the contract against the margin.** Bid = cost basis + margin ≈ $1.06M. Client ceiling: $900k. Window: 14 weeks vs 24. The contract cannot carry its required margin. Two ways to proceed — overbid and be declined, or decline to bid. Both are the same decision: the contract is structurally incompatible with safety.

**Step 4 — Refuse the bad deal.** A fixed-price contract that cannot absorb 2× on its own estimates is not a contract, it is a coin flip where the contractor flips. The penalty cap ($120k) and termination clause ($80k) are the client's comfort, not our margin — clauses get litigated; margins don't. If we cannot serve it with margin, someone else inherits the risk.

**Step 5 — Defend the walk-away.** We forgo ≈ $165k profit and a repeat contract. That is the price of the margin, and the price is *earned*: one bad job at 1× margin has ruined firms that took 100 such contracts. The competitor bidding $610k is underpricing the tail; their win is our survivorship. Margin is not an estimate of what might happen — it is the distance between the estimate and ruin.
