# Human Baseline — m018-POS-01
## Style: Steel-manning (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Problem restatement.** A memo proposes a 4-month Go rewrite of the 7-year-old Python OMS (16 dev-months to "API parity"), citing spaghetti code, "3 failed" incremental attempts, 2.4 s p99 vs a 300 ms target, and talent attraction. I must decide: approve or reject — and before that, treat the opposing position honestly.

**Step 1 — Set aside the straw-man.** The memo does not engage the incremental position; it dismisses it ("band-aid, tried 3 times and failed"). That is a rhetorical label, not an argument. I will not inherit it. The real position to answer is the one a competent defender of "keep + modernize" would actually make — so I must first build that case from the evidence, at its strongest.

**Step 2 — Rebuild the opponent's case (strongest form).** A skilled defender of the incremental path, given the appendix, would argue at minimum:
1. **Re-derivation cost.** 340 bug-fix commits over 36 months are accumulated domain truth — edge cases discovered the hard way, ~90% of them covered only by manual QA. Reimplementing without tests means re-deriving that truth: ~6–9 dev-months of work the memo's 16-month estimate omits, and the risk concentrates exactly in the behaviors nobody can see. A rewrite silently bets the service on them being re-findable.
2. **The goal is not in the rewrite's scope.** The stated target is 300 ms p99. The order-API p99 is already 410 ms; the 1.8 s term is the batch ETL. "Parity = all API endpoints migrated" — the ETL is not an endpoint. Even a perfect rewrite cannot hit the goal. The memo's headline justification fails its own metric.
3. **The staffing math.** Six Python engineers and one Go engineer (the author) to deliver 16 dev-months of Go in 4 months is a 6-of-7-team retraining problem; the parity date is not credible on the author's own numbers.
4. **The "3 failures" are not approach failures.** A (2019): cancelled by the CEO at day 21 for a reorg. B (2021): cancelled by a new VP at day 30. C (2022): a completed, unshipped spike. None ran to an evaluation milestone. A defender would say the incremental path has never actually been tried — and the honest response is: conceded, that's right.

**Step 3 — Evaluate the rebuilt case.** The strongest defense does not collapse; it reframes. Its deepest point is the untested-behavior risk: *"if 90% of fixes are untested, a 6-week modernization is itself risky"* — a fair objection that cuts both ways. A defender's best move is to demand sequencing: fix the ETL first (the goal-gap driver, isolated, measurable), build the regression harness around the manual-QA behaviors, then refactor with typed contracts. That sequence is exactly what makes the rejection robust: it answers the strongest case on its own terms.

**Step 4 — Decide.** Reject the rewrite. The strongest version of the opposing case wins on three of its four arguments (re-derivation cost, scope-excluded goal, staffing) and on the fourth (attempt history) the memo is simply factually wrong. Adopt: 6-week modernization — parallelize/stream the ETL (kills the 1.8 s term), containerize, regression-test-first around the untested behaviors, typed contracts. ~2 dev-months ≈ 1/8 of 16, reaches the goal, keeps the domain truth.

**Step 5 — Survivor test.** Re-run the defender's best case against the decision: the re-derivation risk is answered (harness built before refactoring, not after); the goal objection is answered (ETL first, measured); the staffing objection is answered (Python-native work); the history objection is conceded and neutralized (we are giving the incremental path its first real attempt). No top-tier objection survives. The rewritten memo's own number (16 dev-months) fails, not the opposition.

**Trace summary box.** Straw-man: "band-aid / failed 3×" named and set aside. Strongest case rebuilt: re-derivation cost (340 fixes, ~6–9 dev-months, ~90% untested), goal-scope failure (ETL 1.8 s of 2.4 s, excluded from parity), staffing (6-of-7 Python), history (2 cancellations + 1 unshipped spike). Decision: reject rewrite; 6-week ETL-first modernization, ~2 dev-months, 300 ms reached. Survivor test: passed — deepest objection answered by sequencing.
