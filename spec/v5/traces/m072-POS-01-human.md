## Style: Value Chain Analysis (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Problem restatement.** Meridian's chain, per unit: materials 38, machining 42, heat treatment 9, inspection 6, assembly 4, logistics 11, warranty 7, SG&A 13 → total 130, price 160, margin 30 (18.75%). Decompose the activity chain; find where cost concentrates and where value is created; then find the lever. Margin is the target: each $1 removed from the chain is +$1 margin at constant price.

**Step 1 — Chain map and cost concentration.** Total 130. Machining 42/130 = **32.3%** — the largest single cell. Materials 38/130 = **29.2%**. Top two = **61.5%** of cost. Logistics 11 = 8.5%; heat treatment 9 = 6.9%; inspection 6 = 4.6%. The cost pass says: value of a dollar saved is highest in machining, then materials; everything else is <10%.

**Step 2 — Value map (where the premium comes from).** The ABEC-5 premium (18.75% margin vs ~5% commodity) is created where tolerance is set and verified: heat treatment (metallurgical consistency) and inspection/metrology. Evidence: 70% of premium customers buy on tolerance. Machining is *purchased* precision — the capability is rentable; the vendor audit is the buy condition, not the differentiator. Materials are commodity. Assembly, logistics, SG&A neither create nor destroy the premium. The chain therefore splits into premium-creating cells (heat + inspection) and volume cells (machining, materials).

**Step 3 — Lever test, quantified.** (a) Offshore machining: 42 → 30 = −$12/unit → margin 30 → 42, **+40%**; at 400K units = **$4.8M/yr**. (b) Furnace upgrade: rejects 5% → 2% saves 3% × 130 = **$3.90/unit = $1.56M/yr** vs $2.4M capex → payback ≈ 1.5 yr; life +12% supports the premium claim. (c) Logistics: −10% = −$1.10/unit = $0.44M/yr. Ranking: offshoring ($12/unit) > heat upgrade ($3.90) + logistics ($1.10) combined. **The lever is machining.**

**Step 4 — Boundary discipline (what NOT to move).** Heat treatment + inspection are the premium's physical home; moving them saves at most ~$1.5M/yr (25% of $15/unit × 400K) while risking the $32/unit price point the tolerance supports — ~$12.8M/yr of premium revenue, an ~8:1 self-inflicted loss. Rejected on value, not on cost. Machining offshoring risk (single-source, +6 weeks lead time) is mitigated: dual-source with the vendor plus one alternative, plant audit before transfer, safety stock for the lead-time gap.

**Step 5 — Recommendation.** Offshore machining now (audited, dual-sourced); fund the furnace upgrade second (payback 1.5 yr, protects the premium); skip the logistics renegotiation (smallest cell).

Lever: machining offshore −$12/unit → margin +40%, $4.8M/yr — larger than heat upgrade + logistics combined. Do not move: heat treatment, inspection. Mitigations: dual-source, audit, safety stock.
