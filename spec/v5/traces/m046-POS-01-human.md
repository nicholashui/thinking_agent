# Human Baseline — m046-POS-01
## Style: First Principles in Software Architecture (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Enumerate the fundamental constraints before any design exists.** (1) Data: two flows, and they must be separated from the start. Location pings: high rate, replaceable content, no money consequence. Assignment/status transitions: low rate, one-of-a-kind, contract-breaking if lost or duplicated. (2) Scale: 8,000 vehicles / 5 s = 1,600 pings/s steady; 1/s burst mode = 12,800/s peak. Assignments: 20/s steady, 200/s peak. (3) Latency: dispatcher sees each ping within 1 s — a customer-visible ETA commit. (4) Failure modes: ask of every write, "what happens if it is lost? if it is duplicated?" Lost location → a position 5 s stale; the vehicle re-pings; nobody notices. Lost or duplicated assignment → the wrong truck dispatched; a contract broken. (5) Change rate: the vehicle protocol is frozen — the location pipeline will never change. The dispatch rules change weekly.

**Step 2 — Derive storage and consistency from the failure-mode answer.** Data whose loss is invisible is ephemeral: a sensor reading, not an event. It wants cheap, fast, batched writes and zero recovery ceremony. Data whose loss is a contract breach is the system of record: it wants transactions, single-owner writes, an audit story. The classification is not "events vs queries" — that is the pattern's vocabulary. It is ephemeral vs durable, decided by the loss-consequence test.

**Step 3 — Derive the shape from scale and latency.** 12,800/s peak against a single Postgres with batched upserts (≈ 20k+ tps) is 1.5–2× headroom: scale is a non-constraint. The binding demand is the 1 s freshness bound, so the dispatcher must sit on an in-memory write-through cache (< 50 ms reads) that the ingest path updates synchronously. Any design that interposes an async hop between ping and cache — a queue, a log, a projection — pays a 10–100 ms tail plus consumer-lag risk against a 1 s bound, and buys replay and durability for data that is replaceable by definition. Derivation: no queue in the ingest path.

**Step 4 — Derive the boundaries from change rate.** Two modules, split by the rate they change, not by the pattern's service taxonomy: the frozen ingest/state core (never changes; hardware contract) and the weekly-changing dispatch-rule module (small, isolated, redeployable alone). Event sourcing/CQRS couples the hot zone to the cold zone: every rule change invalidates projections over the whole stream. Change rate says: keep the rule module small and adjacent, the pipeline untouched.

**Step 5 — Select.** Monolith, or at most two services: Postgres — locations as a plain table with batched upserts (ephemeral), assignments transactional (single-owner writes) — plus an in-memory write-through cache serving the dispatcher. Transactions only on assignment state. ≈ 6 weeks, 2 engineers, vs 3–4 months and 6 engineers for the pattern. The pattern's virtues — durability, ordering, replay — are virtues for the wrong data class; the only durable data in the system (assignments) runs at 20/s and does not need Kafka.

**Step 6 — Expected behavior + metric.** p95 ping→dispatcher < 1 s at 12,800/s peak; assignment loss = 0 (transactional); weekly rule deploy with zero pipeline changes. Falsifier: if p95 exceeds 1 s at peak for two weeks, revisit the cache design, not the ingest path.

**Trace summary box.** Constraints (data, scale, latency, failure mode, change rate) → ephemeral vs durable by loss-consequence → scale a non-constraint, latency binding → no async hop → boundaries by change rate → monolith + Postgres + write-through cache; 6 weeks/2 engineers vs 3–4 months/6.

The signature move is the loss-consequence test deciding ephemeral-vs-durable before any pattern vocabulary enters.*
