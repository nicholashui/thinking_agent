# Human Baseline — m062-NEG-01
## Style: Temporal Thinking in Climate Policy (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Establish the horizons, as the standing rule requires.** 2030: the measure is built; avoided damages begin. 2050: benefits are mature. 2100: here the real accounting happens — 1–3 m of sea rise, the 1-in-1000 surge that overwhelms the hospital, the unresolved question of what the regional barrier strategy is. A city rule demands the analysis at all three horizons, and the 2100 horizon is where this decision's honesty has to live.

**Step 2 — The rate question must be settled first.** NPV of Option A: at 1.4% (Stern) ≈ +960M; at 3% ≈ +835M; at 5.5% (Nordhaus) ≈ +694M. The sign is stable, but the magnitudes are not the point — the point is that no legitimate intergenerational analysis can just *pick* a rate. Discounting is the moral decision in this problem, the one that decides how much the 2050 and 2100 generations count. If the field cannot agree on 1.4% vs 5.5%, adopting 3% today is a political choice pretending to be arithmetic. I cannot in good conscience recommend a spend that presumes a settled rate.

**Step 3 — Now the tail.** At 1-in-1000, with 3 m of sea rise and a storm surge, the hospital is overwhelmed anyway. The measure protects against the 1-in-100 flood and quietly invites the city to believe it is protected against the climate — against the century's real problem. The drainage and the flood-proofing will be presented as the answer, and the 1-in-1000 will arrive as a surprise that was modeled and then ignored. False security is worse than declared vulnerability: it is risk mislabeled as safety.

**Step 4 — What the honest analysis requires.** The correct temporal structure is: (a) a 2100 regional master plan (barrier + retreat + land-use) resolved first, with the intergenerational rate settled by the full council with public deliberation; (b) only then does Option A's role inside that plan — as interim or as theater — become decidable. A near-term spend that predates the settlement of the long-term question is a decision in the wrong order: the long horizon is the frame, and the near-term measure is the content.

**Step 5 — Verdict.** No decision on Option A this year. Return to council: resolve the 2100 master plan and the discount-rate question first; fund only a monitoring program (flood-frequency tracking, sea-rise observation) meanwhile. The tail is the binding constraint, and the rate is unresolved — approving $120M in this state is intellectual impatience wearing the mask of pragmatism.
