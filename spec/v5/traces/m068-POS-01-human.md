# Human Baseline — m068-POS-01
## Style: Hypothesis-Driven Consulting (MECE) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Start from the identity, not the story.** Margin is an equation before it is a narrative. Decompose gross margin: Margin = Revenue − COGS − Freight − Returns − Fees; Revenue = Price × Volume × Mix. The tree is MECE by construction: every point of a 7-point decline must land in exactly one branch — price, volume, mix, COGS, freight, returns, fees. No overlap, no gap. Mix is its own branch: shifting share from stores (45% margin) to web (28%) loses margin with no price or cost changing — the most commonly missed branch.

**Step 2 — Attribute from the data; the tree must close.** Promotions −2.2 (discount depth 12→18%, promo share 25→38% — with mix held constant). Mix −1.6 (web +6, stores −4, catalog −2). Freight −1.1 (cost per order +3.4). COGS −0.8 (index +2.1). Returns −0.7 (6→9%, restock +25%). Fees −0.6 (take-rate +1.5). Sum: −2.2 − 1.6 − 1.1 − 0.8 − 0.7 − 0.6 = −7.0. The tree closes against the observed decline — attribution is only complete when the arithmetic reconciles.

**Step 3 — Prioritize by impact × controllability × testability.** Size alone is a trap: mix is second-biggest (−1.6) but structural — channel share takes quarters to move. Promotions rank #1 on all three axes: biggest (−2.2), fully controllable (we set the depth), cheapest to test (hold-out products, 8 weeks). Freight is the second quick win; mix and fees are sequenced after, not ignored.

**Step 4 — State the hypothesis to test first, falsifiably.** H1: promotional over-depth — reducing average discount depth 4–5pts recovers ≈ 1.8–2.2pts of margin at < 0.5% volume loss. Test: 8-week matched-product experiment — 200 SKUs at 14–15% depth vs 200 at status-quo 18%, same channels, same weeks; success = margin uplift ≥ 1.0pt at volume loss ≤ 0.5%; falsified if volume loss > 1.5% at ≤ 1.0pt uplift.

**Step 5 — Hold the untested branches.** Freight renegotiation waits for the promo answer (discount depth changes shipment economics); mix and fees are contractual/structural. Sequence: test promos now, freight next, mix as strategy.

**Step 6 — Client-ready framing.** One tree, one ranked list, one experiment, one number: "7 points lost: 2.2 to discounting, 1.6 to channel mix, 1.1 to freight, 0.8 to cost inflation, 0.7 to returns, 0.6 to fees. We test the biggest and most controllable driver first — discount depth. Eight weeks, matched products, a number at the end."

**Trace summary box.** margin identity → 6 branches quantified, mix as its own branch, sum = −7.0 → ranked by impact × controllability × testability → H1 = promo over-depth, falsifiable 8-week matched-product test → untested branches held → one-page CFO story.
