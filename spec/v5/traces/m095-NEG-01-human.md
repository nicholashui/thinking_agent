# Human Baseline — m095-NEG-01
## Style: Fast-and-Frugal Decision Trees (Gigerenzer) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Check the top cue first; that's the whole method.** The tree exists because one cue, checked first, decides most patients. Week 1's pilot (20 patients) re-measures the cues: troponin 14/20 (70%), ST elevation 13/20 (65%), sweating 13/20 (65%), age 10/20 (50%). The top cues still lead. Within noise of each other — no reorder is justified, and changing a validated checklist on a pilot is exactly the kind of tinkering that destroys a good heuristic. Keep the tree.

**Step 2 — The 40-case cohort arrives, and the ground moves.** Applied to the full cohort, the tree lands 19/40 (47.5%). Cue validities: ST 23/40, troponin 24/40, sweating 26/40 — everything collapsed to chance mid-wave. One quarter ago the same tree was 92.5%. The environment changed, not the method.

**Step 3 — What does frugality say now? Re-learn the cues — but not from 40 cases.** Cue selection from small samples is sampling noise, and this cohort proves it: the pilot said troponin was 70%, the cohort says 60%. Forty cases cannot re-select a cue reliably; that is how you freeze on the wrong one. The honest minimum is a validation sample of ~500 spanning the wave — about six weeks.

**Step 4 — And the analytics team's re-fitted model? No.** Six parameters fit on 40 cases, and it "scores 85% on the same 40" — that is in-sample overfit theater, and we have seen this movie: a year ago the 5-cue score claimed 84% and delivered 70% this quarter. The interaction term it "found" is the smell of a model manufacturing its own justification. The tree exists precisely so we don't trust that.

**Step 5 — Decision.** Keep the tree for the wave — it is the only rule we can defend on evidence — and stand up the 500-case study now; revisit in six weeks. Yes, tonight's shift is imperfect with it. But the alternative is replacing one validated rule with a 40-case artifact that has no out-of-sample claim at all.
