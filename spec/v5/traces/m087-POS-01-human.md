## Style: BATNA Thinking (Negotiation) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 0 — Stance: numbers before postures.** Negotiations are won or lost before anyone makes an offer — in the walk-away arithmetic. I write the two alternatives down first, then the zone they define, then everything else is just the split.

**Step 1 — My walk-away, before anything else.** If this deal dies, what do I have? Keep the division and run it down: $14.5M of retained cash flows, less $2.5M of wind-down and transition costs → my alternative is worth **$12.0M**. That is the number under my chair; every offer gets measured against it. Below $12.0M, walking is not a threat, it is arithmetic.

**Step 2 — Their walk-away, never their word for it.** HelixCloud's alternative if this dies: buy from SkyGrid — firm quote, **$17.5M** — or build in-house at $19.0M per their own capex memo. Their real alternative is the cheaper one: $17.5M. That is their ceiling: anything we offer below $17.5M is better for them than their alternative, so they are not going anywhere.

**Step 3 — The zone.** [My floor $12.0M, their ceiling $17.5M] → agreement zone width **$5.5M**, midpoint **$14.75M**. A deal exists. And here is the truth about zones: every price inside the zone beats both walk-aways, for both sides. The negotiation is not about whether we deal — it is about who takes how much of the $5.5M surplus.

**Step 4 — Their "$13M in-house cap" is leverage, not a fact.** A $13M cap would compress the zone to $1M wide — but their own capex memo prices the build at $19.0M, and SkyGrid sits at $17.5M. A buyer who claims a $13M ceiling while facing $17.5M elsewhere is negotiating against themselves; the claim shrinks THEIR zone, not mine. It is a pressure move. I do not argue with it; I do not believe it.

**Step 5 — Leverage.** Their contracts expire in 4 months; SkyGrid needs 6 to transition. They have a hard clock. My run-down has no clock — I can wait out a deal that beats $12.0M. They need this more than I do, and their alternative is worse than my alternative. That asymmetry is worth real dollars — it is why the midpoint is a floor for my target, not a ceiling.

**Step 6 — Decision.** Reject $12.5M: at their opening I capture (12.5 − 12.0)/5.5 = **9% of the surplus**; they take 91%. That is not a negotiation, that is a gift. Counter at **$15.0M** (above the midpoint, justified by my no-clock leverage and their 4-month deadline). Hard floor: **$12.0M** — walk below it and invoke the run-down; that is what the floor is for. In the room: "your cap is your build memo, not $13M — we are at $15M, and our floor is the division running on." Expect the close around $14.5–15.5M, and anything ≥ $14.75M is a good week.
