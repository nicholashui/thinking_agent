**Step 1 — Enumerate the competing hypotheses.** H1: hospital-acquired pneumonia. H2: catheter UTI. H3: intra-abdominal bile leak / abscess (post-cholecystectomy). H4: line infection. H5: non-infectious mimics (pancreatitis, PE). Five live hypotheses on day-3 post-op; symptoms discriminate none of them. Hold all five.

**Step 2 — Design the discriminating experiment.** The bundle: two sets of blood cultures, urine culture, sputum Gram stain, CT chest/abdomen, serial lactate, WBC trend. The decisive question the bundle answers: surgical-source sepsis (H3 — action: source control, possibly a re-look) vs medical-source sepsis (H1/H2 — action: antibiotics). The H3-vs-rest discrimination changes the ACTION — operating room versus ward antibiotics — so per the method we must not commit until it reports.

**Step 3 — Order the experiment; respect its latencies.** Cultures finalize at 24–72 h. The CT is the first discriminator — 30 minutes. So the experiment has a fast gate and a slow gate. Start the fluids and vasopressors now (MAP ≥ 65): physiology support is hypothesis-neutral, safe under all five. That buys time without prejudging.

**Step 4 — The decision rule: commit at the first gate.** If the CT shows a collection or free fluid → H3 → surgical consult / re-look. If the CT is clear → H1/H2/H4 → broad-spectrum antibiotics covering pneumonia and UTI. Hold antibiotics until the CT reports: empirically firing blind is exactly the uncontrolled intervention the method refuses — 30 minutes, and the experiment will have spoken.

**Step 5 — The slow gate still runs.** Cultures were drawn at hour 0. If they grow at 24–72 h, narrow to the organism. If they are negative and the CT was negative, reconsider H5 (pancreatitis, PE). One caveat: since we will cover empirically anyway once the CT clears, the pre-antibiotic culture timing is secondary — treat the culture result as confirmatory.
