# AI Thinking Agent — Trace — m044-NEG-01
**Run metadata:** single deterministic run; blinded (no human-model information provided); task = decide the future of a money-losing division; external action = none (decision recommendation only).

## Stage 0 — META-CONTROL
- **Context:** regional logistics, $210M/yr; Rural Route division = 10% of revenue, −18% margin, −$2.2M/yr and growing; consumes 40% of fleet budget; 2 urban clients (30% of revenue) threatening to leave. **Hard deadlines:** budget in 2 weeks; fleet master lease take-or-pay renewal in 60 days — signing commits fleet size for 3 years. **Stakes:** high. **Effort:** E3. **Route:** complicated, time-critical — not consensus-eligible; the calendar is a constraint, not a preference. **Safety:** none (no physical risk). Proceed.

## Stage 1 — WHAT: Frame the Problem
- **Frame:** this is a *decision with a hard deadline and a single decision right* — the owner-CEO must answer the Rural Route go/no-go within 2 weeks; the 60-day take-or-pay clause means "decide later" IS a decision (re-commitment by default). Impacted parties are numerous; decision rights are not distributed. **Gate:** decision is the owner's; my deliverable is the recommendation with the transition design. Pass.

## Stage 2 — WHY: Diagnose and Model
- **Economics:** division loses $2.2M/yr with 3 years of growing losses; the urban-client threat (30% of revenue ≈ 3x the route) converts the division's cost into an enterprise risk — keeping the route cross-subsidized endangers 3x more revenue than it earns. **Deadline math:** a 6-month study is impossible — by the time it reports, the 60-day take-or-pay signature has re-committed the fleet and the decision is made by default. **Stakeholders bounded by relevance:** employees (120 jobs — transition design), 3 fully dependent towns (continuity design), union (transition consultation), the son/division manager (sentiment; his advocacy must not decide), urban clients (service restoration), board (budget). Regulators, press, and 120 households are affected but hold no decision-relevant lever inside the 2-week window — noted, not weighted. **Decision right:** owner alone; no stakeholder has veto.

## Stage 3 — HOW: Generate, Test, and Select
- **Alternatives:** A keep-and-cross-subsidize (sentimentally attractive; commits the fleet at take-or-pay in 60 days, keeps −$2.2M/yr, and risks the urban clients' 30% — rejected on arithmetic, not feeling) · B task-force study (6 months > 60-day lock-in — infeasible by construction; rejected) · C shut down with a 90-day structured transition (severance $1.1M, outplacement, partnership handoff so the 3 dependent towns keep daily service; no fleet re-commitment; urban clients get a service-restoration plan) · D same-window rescue sale (untested in 60 days — tracked as a parallel option, not the primary).
- **Verify + select:** C passes all gates: decided within the window, within the owner's authority, transition designed before the announcement, economics versus the 30%-of-revenue threat, no veto anywhere. A fails economics + lock-in; B fails the calendar; D fails certainty. **Empathy placement check:** the human cost (120 jobs, 3 towns) is real and is addressed in implementation — severance, outplacement, town continuity — but the go/no-go is an economic-deadline call; empathy informs the landing, not the launch.
- **Premortem:** transition slips past the 2-week budget → ship the budget with a conditional line and the take-or-pay rejection signed immediately; union resistance → consultation window opened day 1 with the economics brief.

## Stage 4 — DO
- External action: none (owner executes); deliverable = decision memo: (1) decision: exit Rural Route within the 2-week window; reject the take-or-pay signature (documented); (2) 90-day transition: severance + outplacement for 120, partnership handoff talks for the 3 dependent towns, service-credit plan for urban clients; (3) board budget line: one-time $1.1M severance + transition costs vs −$2.2M/yr ongoing; (4) son: division manager reassigned to urban service restoration with a stated rationale. Verification: take-or-pay declined in 60 days; urban clients retained; transition complete in 90 days.

## Stage 5 — REVIEW
- **AAR:** the decisive moves were (a) reading the take-or-pay clause as the binding deadline — it converts "we need more time" into "the default decision is re-commitment," and (b) separating decision right (owner) from impact (everyone else), which let empathy be spent on the transition instead of being forced to answer the go/no-go. Gap: the son's sentiment and family dynamics were noted but not fully weighed as implementation risk — a succession/communication plan should be named in the memo.

## Decision Packet
- **Conclusion:** exit Rural Route within the 2-week window; reject the take-or-pay signature; execute a 90-day transition (severance, outplacement, partnership handoff for the 3 dependent towns, urban-client service restoration); no task-force delay and no cross-subsidy. **Status:** SOLVED (recommendation; execution requires the owner's signature — external authorization noted, not executed).
- **Assumptions:** urban-client threat is credible (30% of revenue); take-or-pay cannot be renegotiated inside 60 days; partnership handoff is achievable in 90 days.
- **Evidence:** 3 years of growing losses (−$2.2M/yr), 40% fleet-budget share, urban-client defection threat, 60-day take-or-pay clause, budget deadline.
- **Alternatives:** A keep-and-cross-subsidize (rejected — arithmetic) · B 6-month task force (rejected — impossible before lock-in) · C exit with transition (selected) · D rescue sale (parallel, untested).
- **Uncertainty:** rescue-sale viability (parallel track), transition cost drift (reserve 15%), urban-client retention (service plan).
- **Risks:** union/employee response (day-1 consultation + economics brief), son/family dynamics (succession + communication plan), dependent-town fallout (handoff talks before announcement), take-or-pay slip (sign rejection immediately, tracked to 60-day date).
