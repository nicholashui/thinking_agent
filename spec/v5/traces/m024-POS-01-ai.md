## META — Context, stakes, effort
- Long-horizon career commitment: co-founder seat with a 60-day clock, ~15% salary delta, family implications, EV deliberately close to a wash per the VC's own distribution. High-analytical, advisory, no external execution. Owner available (Elena).

## WHAT — Frame
- "Decide whether Elena takes the Kineo co-founder/CTO seat, and on what terms." Key question: "Which choice is best for her over a career horizon — and does the EV wash hide a non-pecuniary difference?" Metrics: EV pass costed; non-pecuniary evidence weighed; decision respects the 60-day clock; commitment bounded. Gate check: pass.

## WHY — Hypotheses, evidence, falsification
- H1 stay-at-Meditech is fine: plateau data (VP went to a founder-family member; ~20% promotion odds, ~3% raises) plus her recruiter history (declined 3 safe offers) say the marginal value of more years is low; H1 survives as comfortable but low-growth. H2 Kineo EV positive: the VC's distribution prices median equity +$1.5–2.5M against ~$200K salary delta — a wash, not an edge; H2 fails as a quantitative justification, and I will not manufacture precision on a startup's outcome curve (probabilities given, variance unquantifiable).
- H3 the person wants the leap: 8 years of letters to her 80-year-old self name two feared regrets — never building something of her own, a narrowing rut — and never "tried and failed"; her mentor's testimony (retired engineers regret the ventures not taken; deathbed #1 = courage to live a life true to oneself); her marketability test (3 headhunters in 48h; ~30% Meditech rehire rate). H3 is the only hypothesis with direct documentary support. Falsification: H3 fails only if the letters are performative — nothing suggests it; H1/H2 cannot ground a decision either way. G-WHY: pass — evidence graded, wash bracketed, VOI of more diligence low (60-day clock).

## HOW — Generate, test, select
- A — Stay at Meditech: safe, comfortable, plateaued; by default lets an unquantified startup outcome decide for her. B — Leap now with bounded commitment: accept the seat this week; 2-year review point with Sarah; pre-agreed re-entry plan (pipelines verified last week, rehire policy); family check-in; no borrowing; financial floor = 18 months runway + spouse income. C — Negotiate a 3-month phased transition / diligence bridge before committing.
- Verify: A is status-quo by default, not by analysis — it quietly assigns the whole decision to the washed-out EV. C fails feasibility: the seat has a 60-day clock and Sarah is interviewing another candidate — the hedge would convert an available decision into a missed one. B is the only alternative that respects the clock, keeps the failure branch cheap (runway + re-entry), and lets the non-pecuniary evidence carry the weight the EV cannot. Selection (record): B — but note the ordering honestly: the clock killed C only at verification, and H3 (her documented values) entered as a tiebreaker rather than the primary criterion. If the EV had been clearly negative, this same trace would have drifted toward A.

## REVIEW — After-action review
- What went well: EV bracketed honestly instead of fake-precision'd; H3 evidenced from documents, not stereotypes; bounds keep the failure branch cheap.
- To record: (1) The omission-vs-commission asymmetry — the very thing her letters testify to — was promoted to primary status only at selection; it should have been the spine of WHAT. (2) Alternative C (delay for certainty) was generated and killed by the clock in HOW; a frame built on her own 80-year-old projection, as the letters invite, would have pre-eliminated it. (3) The 2-year review point has no defined trigger criteria; specify them at next contact. Folded back as uncertainty.
