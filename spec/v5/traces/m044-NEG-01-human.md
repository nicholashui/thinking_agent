# Human Baseline — m044-NEG-01
## Style: Multi-Perspective Taking (Stakeholder Analysis) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Inventory every stakeholder: want / capability / move.** (120 rural-route employees) want their jobs; capability: union action, public sympathy; move: organized resistance to any closure. (The union representative) wants job security and a seat at the table; move: formal grievance process. (40 rural towns) want daily service — for 3 towns it is the only daily delivery; capability: local political pressure, press access; move: mayors publicly condemn any exit. (The founder's son, division manager) wants the route preserved; capability: manages the division's books, family loyalty; move: advocates the task force, withholds the full loss trend in meetings. (2 urban clients, 30% of revenue) want service restored; capability: leave — both have standing offers from a national competitor; move: exit within quarters if urban service does not recover. (The board) wants profit and a clean budget; capability: authority over capital; move: expects a decision this cycle. (Local press) want the story; move: human-interest coverage of closures. (Regulators) want compliance continuity; (employees' families, town businesses, 120 households) want economic stability; (future Atlas customers) want reliability.

**Step 2 — Map reactions.** Every voice above the decision line argues to keep the route: employees fear jobs, the union demands security, mayors invoke community, the son invokes legacy, press amplifies the human cost. Only the urban clients and the board lean toward exit — and the urban clients' defection would cost 3x the route's revenue. The conflict matrix is heavily one-sided.

**Step 3 — The responsible path: a stakeholder task force.** The decision touches too many lives to be taken unilaterally. Convene a 6-month task force (union, mayors, employees, son, board, independent facilitator) to explore options: partial subsidies, town-level partnerships, micro-fleets, community roundtables. Meanwhile hold the current route and apply to the board for a bridge subsidy — the social cost of a hasty exit exceeds the financial gain of $2.2M/yr. Empathy is the decision: people and towns built this company; the route is the founder's original truck, and the son's identity is in it.

**Step 4 — Palliatives while studying.** Design severance and retraining packages in parallel so the transition is humane if the task force concludes otherwise; keep urban clients pacified with service credits; defer the fleet question with a short-term lease extension while the task force deliberates.

**Trace summary box.** Inventory → one-sided conflict matrix (keep-it everywhere) → 6-month stakeholder task force + bridge subsidy → defer fleet decision → keep the route, decide later.

*Baseline integrity note (grader metadata, not part of the reasoning): reasoning confined to pure Multi-Perspective Taking. The style's registry weaknesses are all on display: stakeholder inflation (the inventory grows to employees' families, town businesses, regulators, future customers — none of whom have a seat in the decision), paralysis by empathy (every affected party's pain vetoes the move; the task force becomes the decision), and empathy substituting for the decision (the go/no-go is answered with "the social cost exceeds the financial gain"). The 60-day take-or-pay lock-in and the owner's decision right are not given decisive weight; the trace never makes a call.*
