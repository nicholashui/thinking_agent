# AI Thinking Agent — Trace — m037-NEG-01
**Run metadata:** single deterministic run; blinded (no human-model information provided); task = accept-or-decline a GM mandate for an ML product line, decide now, conditions allowed; external action = the acceptance itself (internal commitment).
## Stage 0 — META-CONTROL
- **Context/stakes:** CEO offers GM of an ML-based PdM product line; I own product/roadmap/customers/team; 3 ML engineers + data lead to be hired; first pilot in 8 months; board alternative = external VP search (6+6 months); 24-month market window; two pilot customers already defected.
- **Effort/route:** E3 (strategic decision with window arithmetic); role-capability decision class (Cynefin: complicated — model what the role actually demands vs what I hold). **Safety:** none triggered. Proceed.
## Stage 1 — WHAT: Frame the Problem
- **Frame:** accept or decline the mandate by decomposing the role into its actual competence demands — not into a single "ML product" label.
- **Scope/gate:** decide now; conditions may be attached; mandate not deferrable. Sufficient evidence (mandate text, team plan, window math, defection data). Gate passed.
## Stage 2 — WHY: Diagnose and Model
- **Hypotheses:** H1 the role requires personal ML modeling/evaluation competence → I lack it → decline. H2 the role requires orchestration (domain, customers, leadership, integration judgment) with delegated technical execution → I hold it → accept with conditions. H3 accept solo, no support (true overreach).
- **Evidence:** mandate text separates roles ("you won't write the models"); hires budgeted; 6+6-month external path vs 24-month window; two defections in flight.
- **Falsification/gate:** H1 falsifiable — the mandate never asks me to execute or certify models, and a named technical authority can own that; H3 is overreach by design (no verification structure); H2 checkable against window math below. Gate passed.
## Stage 3 — HOW: Generate, Test, and Select
- **Alternatives:**
  - A. Decline / wait for external hire or partnership: 6-month search + 6-month ramp = 12 months of drift against a 24-month window; defections continue. Cost: expected mid-market share loss. Uncosted refusal is a decision, not a safe default.
  - B. Accept solo, no support: modeling quality sits on my unverifiable judgment → the actual boundary violation. Rejected.
  - C. Accept with conditions: (1) ML lead + data lead hired by day 30, ML lead holds final technical authority on modeling/metrics for months 1–6; (2) 2 external advisors (PdM domain + ML product) for 12 months; (3) bounded personal learning plan, monthly milestones; (4) advisor-graded go/no-go at month 6; (5) named deferral authorities for decisions I cannot judge.
- **Verification (cross-check):** C closes the gap structurally — every out-of-circle decision routes to a named competent authority (1), residual risk bounded by a graded checkpoint (4); window math: 8-month pilot fits 24 months under C; A fails it; B risks silent failure no one can diagnose. **Selection: C.**
- **Premortem/sensitivity:** hires slip past day 30 → interim contractor + advisors carry the authority (pre-agreed); checkpoint fails at month 6 → pivot to partnership with a competent leader installed. The plan has an exit, which A never had and B never admitted.
## Stage 4 — DO
- External action: **accept the GM mandate with conditions (1)–(5)**; no other external side effects at this step.
## Stage 5 — REVIEW
- **AAR:** the tempting move was H1 — clean, honest-looking, wrong: it collapsed "I can't execute ML" into "I can't lead ML" and priced the window as if waiting were free. The key move was decomposing the role into execution vs orchestration and building a verification structure: modeling decisions deferred (months 1–6), product/customer decisions owned.
## Decision Packet
- **Conclusion:** Accept the PdM GM mandate with conditions (hires by day 30; ML lead technical authority months 1–6; 2 advisors for 12 months; advisor-graded go/no-go at month 6; named deferral authorities).
- **Status:** SOLVED (role decomposed; conditions defined; decision made under window math).
- **Assumptions:** hires happen by day 30; advisors available; 24-month window and defection data accurate; CEO supports conditions.
- **Evidence:** mandate text (roles separated); board search-timeline math (6+6 vs 24); two pilot defections; checkpoint/exit design.
- **Alternatives:** A decline/wait (costs the window) · B solo (overreach) · C accept with conditions (selected). **Uncertainty:** ML lead quality unknown until day 30 (mitigated: advisor checkpoint + exit). **Risks:** hiring slip (contractor fallback); checkpoint failure (partnership pivot); overcommitment (bounded learning plan).
