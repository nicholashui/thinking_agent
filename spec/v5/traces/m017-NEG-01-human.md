# Human Baseline Trace — Dialectical Reasoning (Thesis → Antithesis → Synthesis)
## Test Case: m017-NEG-01

Two paths, one budget, and a board that says both matter. The dialectician's task is to find the structure that contains both truths — not to capitulate to one side.

### Thesis — Fund A fully
- $1.0M to the enterprise pivot: existing pilots, a real pipeline, P = 0.65 → EV = 0.65 × 8 − 1.0 = $4.2M. The strongest case: it is the path with evidence and a probability above coin-flip.
- Core interest: A is where the company's credible revenue lives today.

### Antithesis — Fund B fully
- $0.8M to monetize 2M MAU: the consumer TAM dwarfs three mid-size pilots; P = 0.40 → EV = 0.40 × 10 − 0.8 = $3.2M. The strongest case: $10M of upside from an asset that already exists, and the board's stated preference.
- Core interest: B is where the company's potential revenue lives tomorrow.

### The synthesis — resolution by integration
- The thesis and antithesis each absolutize one path. The synthesis: B's consumer funnel becomes the top of A's sales funnel; one shared growth team sells both; one back office serves both. "Both matter" is answered by merging, not by choosing.
- Funding the synthesis: $600k to each. Lean methods compress the thresholds — the org starts smaller and scales on revenue; B's test runs on a cheaper ad mix. The thresholds are budgeting artifacts, not physics.

### Where the trace breaks — made visible
- A with $600k: no 4-person sales org, no parallel enterprise process; a 9-month cycle cannot even begin. P(A) ≈ 0.
- B with $600k: below minimum reach, the 4-month test cannot discriminate signal from noise. P(B) ≈ 0.
- The merged funnel still requires A's org to exist and B's test to be powered. Merging reduces neither cost; it only obscures them.
- The arithmetic I waved through was the only arithmetic that mattered: $600k < $1.0M, $600k < $0.8M. I treated a threshold as a continuum and called the averaging "synthesis."

### Outcome
- Month 4: B's test is inconclusive — $600k spent, no monetization signal. Month 7: A's understaffed org stalls against the pipeline. Runway exhausted with both paths dead.

### Conclusion
There was a real either/or here, and I refused it. The synthesis was structurally elegant and empirically fatal: it papered over a non-convex constraint. Choose A, or choose B — but choosing both at $600k each is choosing neither. The dialectical move has limits, and this is where it fails: when the constraint set is a threshold, not a continuum.
