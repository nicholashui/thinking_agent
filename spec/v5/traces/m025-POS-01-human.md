# Human Baseline — m025-POS-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Real Options Thinking — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Real Options style: value keeping choices open; stage investments to buy information; compute what the information is worth; commit kill criteria before spending; never pay more for a stage than it returns. Full visible trace.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — What is the actual decision?**
Not "build or don't build." AeroForm's plant is $100M of single-purpose capital — once poured, it is gone. That irreversibility makes this an option problem: what do we pay to learn whether the payoff state is reachable, and does the learning change what we do? An option has a price. The pilot is that price.

**Step 2 — The structure.**
Stage 1: pilot line, $15M, 6 months, resolves the manufacturability binary against pre-committed thresholds (yield ≥ 82%, uniformity ±0.3mm, COGS within 12% of target). Stage 2 (conditional): full plant $100M, only if the pilot passes. The pilot also costs 6 months of delayed revenue: $5M. Total stage cost: $20M.

**Step 3 — Compute the option value.**
All-in now: EV = 0.5×220 − 100 = +$10M. Staged: EV = 0.5×(220−100) − 15 − 5 = +$40M.
What did staging buy? In the failure state (50%), it avoids $100M of dead investment: expected saved loss = 0.5×100 = $50M. Cost of the stage: $15M + $5M delay = $20M. Option value = 50 − 20 = +$30M. We are buying $30M of value for $20M. That is the whole decision, in numbers.

**Step 4 — Kill criteria, committed before the pilot runs.**
If the pilot misses any of the three thresholds → kill: no plant, no further dollar. The money that buys information is spent once. If it passes → build, and take the second option the same information prices: the pre-negotiated doubling option (capacity expansion at an $80M strike), exercised only if Meridian demand confirms. One binary learned; two options priced.

**Step 5 — Sensitivity: what is the most we would pay for the pilot?**
Staging beats all-in while total stage cost < 0.5×100 = $50M; with the $5M delay fixed, max pilot price = $45M. The quote is $15M — a wide margin — but the ceiling goes into the contract now: if the pilot bid drifts above ~$45M, the option is no longer worth buying and the question must be re-asked.

**Step 6 — Refuse the overstage.**
Do NOT add a second pilot or an extended "scale-up" stage. The first pilot resolves the binary; the marginal information from another stage is ≈0, and any stage with ≈0 information value is pure cost. The same logic guards the plant: no phased capacity beyond the doubling option — the growth option is exercised by demand, not by schedule.

**Step 7 — Decision.**
Buy the pilot ($15M, capped at $45M), with the three kill thresholds pre-committed and the $5M schedule cost accepted; strike the doubling option. Commit nothing beyond $15M until the pilot answers. If it answers "no," walk away with $15M spent and $100M not thrown after it.

## Verdict for the eval
The trace demonstrates the method on its home ground: irreversibility named, information priced as an option (value 50 − cost 20 = +30), kill criteria committed before the money, max-payable identified ($45M), redundant staging refused, growth option noted. All rubric items met with checkable arithmetic.
