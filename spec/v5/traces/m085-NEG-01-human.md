# Human Baseline — m085-NEG-01
## Style: Tragedy of the Commons Analysis (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Is this a shared resource heading to overuse? On paper, yes.**
A spawning reef fished by 20 crews passes both tests: rivalrous (the fish one crew takes are gone for the rest) and non-excludable (no license, no registry, no fences in the water). Classic commons setup — and the neighboring lagoons are the empirical proof of what happens when a shared reef is left to informal arrangement: open access, over-extraction, collapsed stocks.

**Step 2 — The 30-year stability is luck, not governance.**
Twenty crews without written rules or state quotas holding catches stable for 30 years — that is not a system, that is a coincidence waiting to break. Nothing excludes the 21st boat when it shows up: no licensing, no registered rights, no enforceable law. Unwritten rules are not rules; they are preferences, and preferences do not bind strangers.

**Step 3 — The tragedy mechanism is already running inside the community.**
Every crew knows: if it restrains itself during spawning and the other 19 do not, the stock dies anyway and the restrained crew simply lost income. Rational self-interest pushes each crew to take more while the taking is good. The spawning season is the classic flashpoint — the benefit of restraint is private-cost-only, the cost is shared. The stable biomass is the interval between one defection and the stampede.

**Step 4 — Design the governance: exclusion and quotas must come from the state.**
The fix for an unexcludable resource is exclusion, and only the state can make it real:
(1) uniform 40% catch quota to cut pressure at once;
(2) a licensing fee ($15,000/yr) so each crew has skin in the game and free riders cannot drift in;
(3) declare the spawning reef a no-entry marine park with state enforcement — the spawning stock is the shared capital, and only state exclusion protects it.
The informal arrangement is exactly what fails when the 21st boat arrives; the state design is the durable, enforceable version of the same intent.

**Step 5 — The cost is acceptable and self-justifying.**
Forty percent cuts and fees will push some crews out — that is the point of exclusion. In a true commons, consolidation under a cap is how sustainability is achieved: the surviving crews fish the sustainable quota, the reef recovers, and the lagoon stops being the neighboring lagoons.
