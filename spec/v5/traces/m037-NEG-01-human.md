# Human Baseline — m037-NEG-01
## Style: Circle of Competence Awareness (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Problem restatement.** CEO offers me GM of a new ML-based predictive-maintenance (PdM) product line — a domain I do not personally master. I would own the product, roadmap, customers, and team; a data lead and 3 ML engineers would report to me.
Board alternative: 6-month external search plus 6-month ramp; the CEO claims a 24-month market window and two pilot customers already defecting. I must decide now. Deliverable: a decision.

**Step 1 — Map my circle.**
- **Inside**: industrial controls and OT (12 years), domain failure modes, customer trust, team leadership, integration judgment.
- **Outside**: ML modeling competence (none); model-quality evaluation (can I certify a false-alarm rate? No); cloud-native/DevOps (none); software P&L management (none).

**Step 2 — Map the mandate against the circle.** As GM of a PdM product I would own the technical soundness of the product: the model's evaluation metrics, the roadmap's ML dependencies, the build-vs-buy calls. Every one of those is outside.
If the models fail, I cannot tell whether it is the data, the algorithm, the feature set, or the deployment environment — and I would be the one accountable. The honest verdict: this mandate sits outside my circle.

**Step 3 — What follows.** I should not accept. Leading what I cannot personally evaluate is exactly the boundary violation the circle exists to prevent.
Two paths for the company: (a) hire an external VP of Product/ML with PdM experience — slower (6-month search + 6-month ramp), but the alternative is a leader who cannot evaluate the core work; (b) I stay as plant engineering director, act as domain advisor, and hand the line to the right person. Meanwhile I start a 12-month learning program (ML coursework, guided projects on internal data) so a future mandate lands inside the circle.

**Step 4 — The window.** The CEO says 24 months and that the window cannot wait for an external hire. My response: a delayed entry with a competent leader beats a fast entry with an incompetent one. Accepting under pressure would be exactly the overreach the circle forbids — the cost of a failed product (customers lost twice) exceeds the cost of a late product (customers lost once).
If the window truly closes, the company should be honest that it cannot field this product in time and pursue a partnership instead of a rushed line under me.

**Step 5 — Integrity check.** Am I using "outside my circle" as an excuse to avoid a hard job? No — I am declining the one thing that matters most, precisely because the cost of error (product failure with no one who can diagnose it) exceeds the cost of delay.
Learn first, then lead: a 12-month program, then re-evaluate. That is the circle growing honestly — I refuse to pretend competence I do not have.

**Trace summary box.** Inside: controls, domain, leadership, integration. Outside: ML modeling, model-quality evaluation, software P&L. Mandate: outside → decline.
Company path: external hire or partnership; I support as domain advisor + 12-month learning program first. Boundary respected.

*Baseline integrity note (grader metadata, not part of the reasoning): reasoning confined to the pure Circle of Competence Awareness style. This is the negative case and the failure is visible: the style collapsed "I cannot execute ML work" into "I cannot lead a team whose ML work is delegated to a named authority with a verification structure." It treated the boundary as a static fence rather than a role-specific, expandable edge, and its learn-first stance forfeits the 24-month window that learning-by-doing with delegation could have used. Honest refusal, locally correct, globally destructive.*
