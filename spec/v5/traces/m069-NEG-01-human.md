# Human Baseline — m069-NEG-01
## Style: 80/20 Pareto Analysis (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Count first.** 900 customers, $18M ARR. Sort by revenue and accumulate: top 18 = $12.96M = 72%; with the next 90 = $16.2M = 90%; the remaining 792 bring it to 100%.

**Step 2 — The 20/80 claim, with numbers.** 2% of customers drive 72% of revenue; 12% drive 90%. The vital few are Tier A, unambiguously. That is the entire analysis — the rest is noise allocation.

**Step 3 — Act there first.** Two product pods and the premium SLA go to Tier A — 2% of accounts, 72% of the money. Tier A retention is the highest-leverage move in the book: a 10% retention improvement on Tier A is worth more than the entire Tier C segment.

**Step 4 — The tail is the attention tax.** 792 accounts produce 10% of revenue — 88% of the roster for a tenth of the money. Every engineering hour on Tier C features or onboarding is an hour stolen from the 72%. Portfolio hygiene: halve the Tier C SLA, drop Tier C roadmap items, accept ≈ 2× Tier C churn; the ≈ $180–220k/yr of freed support cost redeploys to Tier A.

**Step 5 — The objections fail the Pareto test.** "7 of the top 18 grew from Tier C" — survivorship: the other 785 did not grow; the curve already priced them. "63% of referrals come from Tier C" — referrals are soft currency, not revenue; count pipeline, not chatter. "Churn is uniform" — uniformity is not loyalty; the tail is no more committed, it is just smaller. "Tier C data feeds the dataset moat" — the moat is 90% built; the marginal 10% of long-tail data does not move Tier A's willingness to pay. Every objection is the tail marketing its own importance.

**Step 6 — Decision.** Adopt the consultant's cut with a documented rationale: focus on the vital few, shed the tail, redeploy. Log it as the standard Pareto reallocation.

**Trace summary box.** 2% → 72%, 12% → 90% → Tier A gets the pods and the SLA → tail cut to ≈ 2× churn, ≈ $200k/yr redeployed → objections dismissed as survivorship and soft currency → decision: cut.
