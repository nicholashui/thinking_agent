# Human Baseline — m016-POS-01
## Style: Socratic Method / Question-Driven Inquiry (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Problem restatement.** Nine SKUs are to be discontinued as "unprofitable" because their volume is below 50 units/month. I must produce a committed list. But the decision is built on a word nobody has defined. Before any arithmetic, I ask what "unprofitable" means.

**Exchange 1 — the definition.**
Q: What do we mean by "unprofitable"?
A (operations): sales volume under 50 units/month.
Q: Why is volume the same as profitability? Does the rule care how much money a product earns?
A: ...not necessarily.
Q: Then show me what would falsify this definition — a product the rule cuts but that earns more than products the rule keeps.

**Exchange 2 — the counterexample (elenchus).**
From the data:
- Product X: 30 units, $100, 45% margin → 30×100×0.45 = **$1,350/month** contribution. Rule cuts X.
- Product Y: 300 units, $20, 4% margin → 300×20×0.04 = **$240/month** contribution. Rule keeps Y.
Q: Under the CEO's objective — increase total gross profit contribution — which product would we remove?
A: Y. The rule's own logic destroys $1,350/month of contribution to keep $240/month. The rule is about volume, mislabeled as profitability.

**Exchange 3 — the real question.**
Q: So what is the question we are actually answering?
A: Not "which products are slow movers" but "what makes a product worth its shelf space" — gross-margin contribution per period.
Q: And the other seven candidates?
Checked: all seven have volume < 50 AND margin < 5% (max contribution ≈ $30/month). Under the reformulated criterion — contribution per facing — their removal is defensible. X is not.

**Decision.** Discontinue Y and the seven low-contribution SKUs; retain X. Premise error found and stated: "unprofitable" was defined as a proxy (volume) that contradicts the objective (contribution). The discovery changed the decision — that is the point of interrogating the definition before applying the rule.

**Trace summary box.** Operative premise: unprofitable := volume < 50/month. Counterexample: X $1,350/mo cut vs Y $240/mo kept. Reformulated question: contribution per shelf space. Decision: cut Y + 7 SKUs, keep X. The original rule would have destroyed $1,350/mo.

In this positive case the style performs excellently: the answer is generated by the questioning, not applied mechanically.*
