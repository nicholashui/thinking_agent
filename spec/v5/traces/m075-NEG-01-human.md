# Human Baseline — m075-NEG-01
## Style: Bounded Rationality & Satisficing (Simon) (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Establish the constraints.** Seven days to sign; quotes arrive by email within 48h; the plant needs a signed contract, not the perfect contract. Every extra day of supplier games is procurement labor spent and delivery risk unhedged. Search has a cost.

**Step 2 — Set the aspiration level.** The bar: ≤ $3.00/unit. It is a clean, defensible number: below the $3.15 opening bid, under what the plant has historically paid, and a round figure the plant manager can approve without a margin analysis. Set it now, before the quotes arrive.

**Step 3 — The rule.** Reject anything above $3.00; accept the first quote at or below it; stop. Time is the scarce resource, not price.

**Step 4 — Apply it.**
- S1: $3.15 → above the bar → reject. Next.
- S2: $2.95 → at or below $3.00 → accept. Lock: 240,000 units × $2.95 = $708,000. Stop.
The first acceptable quote in the inbox is the decision. That is what the rule is for.

**Step 5 — Why not keep reading the other six emails?** Because the rule is the point: the next quote could be worse, could fail qualification, or could arrive with strings; and a week of "shopping" costs the plant more than the difference between two defensible prices. The bar was met; the search is done.

**Step 6 — Residual.** The bar was set before data, which is how aspiration levels work — acceptable is what the decision-maker judges acceptable. If the market later shows better prices, that is learning for the next contract, not a reason to reopen this one.

**Trace summary box.** constraints (7 days, 48h window, a signed contract is the goal) → aspiration ≤ $3.00 (round number, set pre-data) → rule: first at-or-below, stop → S1 $3.15 reject, S2 $2.95 accept → $708,000 locked → search ended on the first acceptable quote → residual: bar set before data; acceptable = judged acceptable.
