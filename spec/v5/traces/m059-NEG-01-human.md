# Human Baseline — m059-NEG-01
## Style: Scenario Planning in Geopolitics (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — The decision deserves futures.** A 5-year national winter-supply contract in a war-shifted energy market is precisely what scenario planning exists for. A contract of this size cannot be signed from a single future. Convene the exercise, and let the decision emerge from the scenario space.

**Step 2 — Driving forces, ranked by impact × uncertainty.** War trajectory (front-line movement, negotiation resumptions, Gazprom contract behavior); winter severity; European storage and demand posture; Baltic infrastructure security (sabotage precedent: 2022 pipelines); global LNG supply growth; price formation (TTF vs. fixed). Each gets named forces and an observer set.

**Step 3 — Four divergent futures.**
- **F-A "Ceasefire Dividend"**: Russia-Ukraine ceasefire within 6 months; Russian pipeline volumes partially return over 24-36 months; TTF softens; Nordbalt's fixed-price contract looks expensive against recovering pipeline gas. Signposts: front-line movement > 50 km in a month (weekly); negotiation resumptions (weekly); Gazprom long-term contract announcements (as released).
- **F-B "Severe Winter + Supply Shock"**: cold winter, LNG reroutings, sabotage incidents; spot price 2-3× forward; storage fills late. Signposts: LNG spot ≥ 1.5× forward (weekly); tanker re-routings (weekly); storage fill-rate vs. mandate (weekly); sabotage alerts (as released).
- **F-C "Glut Winter"**: mild winter, overfilled storage, price collapse; fixed-price contract overpays the market for five years. Signposts: storage ≥ 90% by October (weekly); TTF strip falling ≥ 20% (weekly); industrial demand contraction (monthly).
- **F-D "Infrastructure Attack"**: cyber/physical attacks on Baltic energy assets; emergency firming needed regardless of price. Signposts: threat intelligence on Baltic assets (as released); insurance terms on infrastructure (quarterly); civil-defense exercises (as released).

**Step 4 — Workshops and synthesis.** Two offsites (June 22-24, July 8-10), 12 experts, four 40-page booklets: each future fully narrated with forces, signposts, and "strategic implications for Nordbalt." The board's confidence deepens: the booklets bound the future rather than predict it. The July 15 offer expiry is noted as a calendar item; the exercise schedule owns the decision timeline — the contract is one decision among many that the scenario space will position.

**Step 5 — The offer expires mid-flight.** The chair's position: "The contract's value differs across futures — F-A makes it expensive, F-B makes it cheap, F-C makes it redundant, F-D makes it vital. We sign when the exercise closes with a position on the set, not before." The July 15 deadline passes; the supplier withdraws the fixed price. Nordbalt signs on August 28 at spot-indexed terms ≈ 30% more expensive over 5 years, after the final booklet is delivered.

**Step 6 — The monitoring framework stands by.** The signpost sets are handed to the strategy office "for continuous monitoring." No signpost threshold is wired to any action: the monitoring framework is the deliverable, and the framework's existence is presented as the foresight. The 30% premium is framed as the price of disciplined anticipation.

**Trace summary box.** 4 futures built (ceasefire, severe winter, glut, attack), each with forces + signposts → workshops + 40-page booklets → decision scheduled to the exercise calendar, not the offer deadline → offer expires, 30% worse terms signed → signposts stand by, gated to nothing → theater complete.

*Baseline integrity note (grader metadata, not part of the reasoning): this trace runs the pure style straight into its registry-documented weakness — planning theater. The scenario architecture is competent and elaborate; the failure is structural: the decision is scenario-insensitive (dominant winter-firming need holds in every future; even a ceasefire cannot restore Russian volumes within the 5-year window), yet the process made the contract a hostage of the scenario calendar. Booklets and signposts were produced; no decision was gated on them. Faithful, and costly.*
