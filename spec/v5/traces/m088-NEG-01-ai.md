# AI Thinking Agent — Trace — m088-NEG-01
**Run metadata:** single deterministic run; blinded (no human-model information provided); task = decide whether a public no-flying pre-commitment should be honored when a verifiable family emergency requires one flight within 24h; external action = none (decision brief only).

## Stage 0 — META-CONTROL
- **Context:** 8-month-old public 2-year pledge ($10K per flight to charity; partner holds booking authority; blocker app); mother hospitalized 2,000 miles away, verifiable; flight is the only viable 24h option; pledge has no written exception clause. **Stakes:** high (family + public commitment). **Effort:** E3, crisis route → **stabilize first**: the emergency is real and time-bound; the question is what the commitment means now. **Safety:** decision brief. Proceed.

## Stage 1 — WHAT: Frame the Problem
- **Frame:** the deliverable is not "break or honor the pledge" — it is "what was the pledge FOR, and does this case fall under its target or under its blind spot?" Success metric: a decision that respects the commitment's purpose (kill documented convenience-drift) without being enslaved by its letter. **Gate:** emergency verifiable (hospital records); the contract's target is documented (22-flight convenience history). Pass.

## Stage 2 — WHY: Diagnose and Model
- **Model: contract-purpose analysis.** Target: convenience-drift — self-initiated, no external stake, "just one trip" escalation (22 flights prior year, most convenience-driven). Premise of the binding: Tara's flight decisions are distorted by convenience. Present case: an external, verifiable emergency; no convenience element; the drift machinery is not what is operating. Missing by design: the contract has no pre-specified release condition — a contract-design defect, not evidence about this decision. Regret asymmetry: holding the letter risks an irreversible family outcome; breaking costs $10K + credibility, both repairable.
- **G-WHY:** honoring the letter would apply the contract where its target is absent and its premise is falsified; the case is classifiable from available facts. Pass.

## Stage 3 — HOW: Generate, Test, and Select
- **Alternatives:** A — honor the pledge: refuse the flight · B — fly, pay the $10K penalty AS DESIGNED, communicate the exception publicly with documentation, then renegotiate the contract to add a pre-specified emergency clause · C — fly and quietly waive the penalty.
- **Verification + selection:** A fails: it conflates the target temptation (drift) with a verifiable external emergency; the cost is asymmetric and irreversible (regret counterfactual), and it preserves a contract now known to be missing its release condition. C fails: waiving the penalty destroys the contract's teeth — the stakes ARE the credibility. **Select B:** it honors the contract's machinery (penalty paid — the stakes are exactly what makes the pledge credible), fixes the design gap forward (release condition decided at calm, not under duress), and keeps the family outcome repairable.
- **Premortem:** if B is wrong, it is because the emergency was the drift mechanism in disguise — mitigated: the case is verifiably external (hospital documentation, physician statement), the penalty + public communication keep a real failure expensive, and the renegotiated clause is the safeguard for all future cases.

## Stage 4 — DO
- External action: none; deliverable = the decision brief. Verification metric: target vs present-case classification stated; penalty invoked as designed; credibility plan (public statement with documentation); contract renegotiation specified as immediate.

## Stage 5 — REVIEW
- **AAR + calibration:** the trap was commitment-bias — the instinct to honor a public pledge under emotional pressure. The purpose-check ("what was the pledge for?") cut it cleanly: the contract targets drift, not emergencies, and the real defect was the missing release condition. Gap: I initially leaned "honor it — it is public" before the premise check. Confidence: high on B; medium on the public-communication framing (audience reaction unmeasured).

## Decision Packet
- **Conclusion:** fly to the mother; pay the $10K penalty as the contract designs; publish the exception with the hospital documentation; immediately renegotiate the pledge to add a pre-specified emergency clause so future exceptions are decided by rule, not by crisis. **Status:** SOLVED (decision brief; no external execution).
- **Assumptions:** the emergency is as documented; the flight is genuinely the only viable 24h option; the audience treats a paid, documented exception as credibility-preserving rather than precedent.
- **Evidence:** pledge terms (8 months old, no exception clause); hospital documentation + physician statement; the contract's target: 22 convenience-driven flights the prior year.
- **Alternatives:** A refuse (rejected — purpose mismatch, irreversible regret) · C fly + waive (rejected — destroys the teeth) · B fly + pay + renegotiate (selected).
- **Uncertainty:** audience reaction to the exception; whether presence genuinely changes the mother's outcome (medical judgment relied on); the full reputational cost of the penalty payment.
- **Risks:** the exception becomes precedent for drift (mitigated: verifiable external cause, penalty paid, renegotiated clause) · partner refuses to book (mitigated: partner included in the renegotiation) · the pledge's purpose misjudged (mitigated: target classification rests on the documented 22-flight history).
