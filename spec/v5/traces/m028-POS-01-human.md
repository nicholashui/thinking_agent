# Human Baseline — m028-POS-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Lateral Thinking — strict, pure application (de Bono: challenge the frame, provocation, escape fixation, generate from the break).
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Lateral Thinking style. Full visible trace. Do not evaluate the problem inside its own frame; first name the frame, then break it. Provocation before analysis.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — Name the frame the problem hands me.**
"The elevators are too slow; complaints will fall only if actual wait time drops; therefore money goes into the machines." Three stacked assumptions: (1) the complaint is about duration, (2) duration is objectively bad, (3) the fix must be hardware.

**Step 2 — Attack each assumption with provocation.**
PO: "The elevators are fine. The waiting is wrong." PO: "Tenants shouldn't notice the wait at all." PO: "Nobody complains in an empty lobby, whatever the speed."

**Step 3 — What breaks if an assumption breaks?**
If the complaint isn't about duration, the metric to move is "the felt experience of waiting" and hardware becomes irrelevant. The data agrees: sensors say 38 seconds actual vs. tenants' claimed 1:55 — a 3x perception gap; complaints even off-peak at under 15s waits. The lobby: blank walls, dead phone zone, people standing staring at doors. Nobody is complaining about a minute; they are complaining about boredom in a dead room.

**Step 4 — Generate options from the break (no evaluation yet).**
(1) Full-length mirrors — the oldest trick; (2) arrival-time displays, so the wait becomes known and finite; (3) seats, greenery, a coffee point; (4) wi-fi/phone boost so the dead zone stops being dead; (5) stair fast-track for floors 2–4; (6) move the doors.

**Step 5 — The archived memo changes everything.**
Nine years ago, in THIS building, a manager put full-length mirrors in the lobby as a temporary experiment: complaints fell sharply for 14 months, sustained, until a renovation removed the mirrors — and the complaints came back. Same lobby, same tenants, proven locally, in writing. That is not a hypothesis; it is a regression we caused.

**Step 6 — The decision, as the break makes it.**
The frame said "$4.2M–$7.8M." The break says: mirrors + arrival displays + seating + wi-fi ≈ $150K, three weeks, zero disruption — and the memo says it works here. Order of operations: run it; measure complaints and sensor waits for 8 weeks against the existing baseline; if the gate fails, the hardware quotes stay on the shelf — modernization becomes a staged fallback, not the plan. And the reframe generalizes: the leasing pitch changes from "faster lifts" to "an elevator experience that isn't a wait."

## Verdict for the eval
Pure lateral on home ground: frame named, frame broken (duration → experience), options generated from the break, buried evidence (perception gap, dead zone, mirror memo) assembled into the solution, and a $7.8M capital question collapsed to a $150K experiment with a measurement gate. The assumption broken — "the fix must reduce actual wait time" — is named, and the solution follows from it. All rubric items met.
