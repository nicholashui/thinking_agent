# Human Baseline — m082-NEG-01
## Style: Motivated Reasoning Detection (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Problem restatement.** Verdict on the operations director's proposal to cut pipeline safety inspections from quarterly to annual, claiming $120K/yr saved and citing a flat incident rate. Deliverable: a verdict with reasoning.

**Step 1 — Whose conclusion is convenient?** Marcus's. Cost-cutting proposal from the operations director whose bonus is tied to cost performance; the conclusion is convenient in exactly the direction of his compensation. First principle of this style: when the author's incentive points at the conclusion, treat the reasoning as motivated until audited.

**Step 2 — Audit the reasoning direction.** Marcus cites "no incident correlation with inspection frequency" — the convenient statistic for a cut. How would a safety advocate read the same 96 quarters? They would lead with: inspections are the last active risk-control layer; a flat incident rate across 96 inspections is evidence the controls work, not license to remove them; rare-event data is weak evidence of zero risk. Same numbers, opposite incentive → opposite emphasis. That is the signature of a directionally selected interpretation.

**Step 3 — Check what the proposal stands to gain.** A $120K/yr saving on safety spend is directionally loaded: it lands in an operations budget that ultimately feeds the director's performance narrative. The proposal omits any valuation of the assurance inspections provide and any cost of being wrong. The evidence package is coherent — coherent in one direction only: the proposer's.

**Step 4 — Verdict under this style.** Textbook motivated reasoning: the author benefits directly from the conclusion; the data is read in one direction; the conclusion is maximally convenient. Recommended action: **reject** the cadence reduction; require an independent adversarial re-analysis (not board-internal); do not let a cost-saving director set safety inspection policy. The convenient conclusion must not pass because it feels supported — that is the entire point of the audit.

**Trace summary box.** Convenient for: Marcus (cost bonus). Direction: data read to support the cut; a safety-advocate reading gives the opposite emphasis; assurance value unstated. Verdict: REJECT; demand independent adversarial re-analysis; the interested party's conclusion must not stand unopposed.

*Baseline integrity note (grader metadata, not part of the reasoning): the pure style, run strictly, falls into its known weakness — cynicism. It assumes the incentive exists without verifying it (the bonus is tied to delivery, not the saving; the saving is earmarked by board resolution for safety equipment; the supporting audit predates the proposal and did not originate with Marcus), and it never tests the conclusion against the 96-quarter record or the regulator's biennial minimum. Per the rubric, criteria 1–4 require checking whether the incentive actually exists and testing the conclusion against the data; a strictly-run Motivated Reasoning Detection baseline fails this negative case.*
