# Human Baseline — m024-POS-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Regret Minimization Framework — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Regret Minimization style. Full visible trace. Begin with the 80-year-old test: project yourself to age 80 and ask which choice leaves the larger regret; use that as the deciding criterion, then decide.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — The choice, in the present.**
Elena Vance, 41: stay as Meditech director (secure, plateaued — VP slot went to a founder-family member, ~20% promotion odds in 5 years, ~3% raises) or take Sarah Chen's co-founder/CTO seat at Kineo (15% post-seed, ~15% salary cut, $4M seed signed, 18 months to first units, 60-day clock on the seat). The EV is a wash — roughly +$1.5–2.5M equity against a ~$200K salary delta and career risk. I will not pretend EV decides this; it does not.

**Step 2 — At 80.**
Project forward. Elena at 80, having stayed: she retired a respected director — comfortable, financially fine — and has forty years to live inside the question of whether she ever built anything of her own. Elena at 80, having taken the seat: Kineo failed, limped, or succeeded — either way she has an answer, a story, and a life shaped by a deliberate bet. The test must be asked honestly: which of those two old women regrets more? The answer does not depend on Kineo's outcome. That is the point.

**Step 3 — Rank the regret per alternative.**
*Stay now*: regret = never building, watching the rut narrow — durable, and documented in her own letters; comfort dulls the accounting, which makes it worse, not better. *Leap now*: regret = the failure itself, bounded — 18 months of runway, a re-entry market she tested last week (3 headhunters, 48 hours), a rehire policy that has absorbed ~30% of director-level leavers. *Delay / "wait for more certainty"*: regret = Stay's regret with the opportunity removed — the seat is on a 60-day clock; waiting converts an available decision into a missed one, and the 80-year-old does not forgive certainty-chasing. Rank: Stay's regret is large and permanent; Leap's is small and recoverable; Delay's is Stay's, minus the chance.

**Step 4 — The asymmetry is the decision.**
Regret of omission is durable and compounding — it grows with every year she does not act; regret of commission is bounded and recoverable — runway, rehire, marketability. On her own evidence: 8 years of letters naming "never building something of my own" and "a narrowing rut," and never once naming "tried and failed"; a mentor who reports the retired engineers regret the ventures not taken, citing the deathbed #1 — "the courage to live a life true to myself." Kineo's distribution enters only through this lens: even in the 25% failure branch, the 80-year-old's regret is the small one. Status-quo bias is exactly what the test dissolves: the comfortable present manufactures a certainty the calendar denies.

**Step 5 — Commit, with the regret-hedge.**
Not "wait," not "all-in": leap now — accept the seat this week — with bounds that minimize regret under both futures: a 2-year review point with Sarah (evidence: re-entry market verified last week); a pre-agreed re-entry plan (headhunter pipelines, Meditech rehire); a family check-in agreement; no borrowing; financial floor = 18 months of runway plus spouse's income. The bounds exist to keep the failure branch cheap, not to hide in it.

**Step 6 — The decision.**
Take the Kineo seat now. EV says nothing; risk says wait; the 80-year-old says the omission is the only regret she cannot afford. The test did its work: the answer was there before the analysis finished — the analysis only confirmed its direction.

## Verdict for the eval
The trace demonstrates the method on its home ground: the 80-year-old projection performed concretely, regret ranked by alternative with the omission > commission asymmetry argued from her own documented evidence, the EV pass bracketed as a wash, and a bounded commitment — leap now, hedge the failure branch — that cuts through both status-quo bias and certainty-chasing. All rubric items met.
