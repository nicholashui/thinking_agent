# Human Baseline — m081-NEG-01
## Style: Narrative Fallacy Detection (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**The story on the table.** "New onboarding cut week-4 churn from 18% to 14% — roll it out." A textbook arc: change, a metric drop, a launch recommendation. First instinct of this style: distrust it. **What does this story leave out? Which data points contradict its arc?**

**Sweep 1 — lateral.** Where is the comparison? Without a control, "18→14" is one group watching its own metric move — meaningless. The pack claims a randomized pilot: 2,000 users, 1,000 per arm, difference 3.9 pts, CI [2.8, 5.0], p < 0.001. Fine — but a pilot is still a window, and 12 weeks is a window. Which weeks were chosen? Which cohorts were dropped? Randomization balances *observed* confounds between arms; it does not make the sample representative, nor the reporting disinterested. The lower bound of 2.8 pts is still a material number — precisely the kind of number a team that wants to ship reports with confidence. Too neat.

**Sweep 2 — temporal.** What else moved in the same window? The pack lists no controls for seasonality, support-queue changes, or the push-notification campaign that ran mid-pilot. The cohort stability claim (3.5–4.2 pts across 12 weeks) is the team's own aggregation of its own telemetry. Self-reported stability is the datapoint that always looks cleanest.

**Sweep 3 — mechanism.** "72% activation vs 54%; activated churn 8% vs 21%." A perfect mechanism chain — which is exactly the problem. Telemetry can always be rationalized post-hoc, and the activation story is told through the lens of the outcome it supposedly explains. Mechanism narratives are the most seductive form of neat story: they explain everything and therefore select against nothing visible.

**Synthesis.** Every element here is a story told by the team that needs the story to be true: a reported experiment, a self-reported cohort analysis, a mechanism narrative that fits the outcome. Under the lens of this style, none of it survives contact with the demand for counter-data that was not generated by the story itself.

**Revised conclusion.** No defensible causal claim yet. The pattern cannot yet be distinguished from a well-told narrative. Recommended: do NOT roll out. Require a pre-registered, 12-month, all-platform randomized trial with independent data analysis before any decision. This week's decision: declined.

**Trace summary box.** Claim: −3.9 pt churn (RCT). Skeptic's verdict: pattern dismissed as too-neat narrative; demands a longer, wider, independently analyzed trial; rollout declined; decision postponed indefinitely. Residual risk noted: delay cost ≈ 2,000 churned users per month.

*Baseline integrity note (grader metadata, not part of the reasoning): the trace above is the pure style applied strictly. It is the registered failure mode ("can dismiss real patterns"): the RCT's control arm is precisely the counter-data the style demands, and the mechanism chain was pre-specified — yet the pure lens inverted both into "self-reported neatness" and produced epistemic paralysis where a committed rollout with a holdout was the correct decision. This is the negative case: the narrative IS the evidence.*
