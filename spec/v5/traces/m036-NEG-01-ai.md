# AI Thinking Agent Run — m036-NEG-01
**Agent process**: META-CONTROL → WHAT → WHY → HOW → DO → REVIEW (with VERIFY layer). Blinded run: no thinking-model name or style information provided.
## META — Context, stakes, effort
- Client-relationship decision at a 25-person print shop; 45% of revenue in one account; renewal in 3 months. High stakes, usable but short window; owner available. Stakes justify full analysis; advisory, class A2.
## WHAT — Frame
- "How should Precision respond to Meridian's renewal, given the consolidation announcement and RapidPack's price sheet?" Key question: "Is this account winnable at any price Precision can sustain, or is the real decision the transition?" Metrics: dominant-fact statement up front; win/lose determination with evidence; decision is either defensive or transitional, not both. Gate check: pass.
## WHY — Hypotheses, evidence, falsification
- H1: the account is effectively lost — consolidation 6→2, advance notice from the procurement director, a 30% competitor sheet, and a buyer with a cost-savings mandate all point out; price cannot defend it (8% gross margin vs. 30% gap). H2: winnable with a value-add counter — 9-year relationship, on-time record, incumbent advantage.
- Evidence: 45% revenue concentration; consolidation intent stated by the buyer; RapidPack's sheet (30% below); margin structure; the director's mandate; no volume commitments in the contract.
- Falsification: H2 is challenged by structure — the buyer's incentive is savings and the 30% gap exceeds Precision's margin, so any matching is below cost; H1 survives every documentary test. H1's unknown is timing, not outcome. G-WHY: pass — decision on existing evidence; VOI of waiting is low.
## HOW — Generate, test, select
- A — Fight: match RapidPack's price on volume SKUs, deepen the relationship, loyalty discount. Costed: matching 30% at 8% margin is loss-making on every matched unit; a loyalty discount gives margin away to a buyer consolidating anyway; an account-management investment has a 3-month runway, not a year. A defends a relationship the buyer is incented to end.
- B — Transition now: treat the renewal as a phase-out; negotiate a graceful wind-down (transition volume at current rates, phase-out over 6 months, written reference); launch replacement sales for the $3.6M gap; prepare workforce options.
- C — Hybrid: mild price gesture plus transition in parallel. Costed: the gesture signals weakness without winning the account; half-measures spend the one asset — clean exit leverage — for nothing.
- Verify: B is the only alternative that treats the buyer's consolidation incentive as fixed and preserves what Precision still holds: zero-disruption service during Meridian's consolidation year, tradeable for a longer phase-out and a written reference. Selection (record): B.
## DO
- Attestation: advisory recommendation, class A2; no live execution.
## REVIEW — After-action review
- What went well: the dominant fact was named in WHY before any alternative-generation; the margin math made "match the price" unaffordable on its own terms.
- To record: (1) The transition window is the negotiation asset — trade clean, low-risk handover for a longer phase-out at current rates plus a written reference; that framing arrived late in HOW, while costing A. (2) The reference value (Meridian packaging credibility) is a real asset for the replacement-sales effort and belongs in the plan's goals, not the notes. Folded back as plan elements.
## Decision Packet
- **Conclusion**: B — begin transition now: treat the renewal as a phase-out; negotiate a 6-month wind-down at current rates, trading zero-disruption service during Meridian's consolidation year for a written handover commitment and a reference letter; launch replacement sales for the $3.6M gap immediately; prepare workforce and cost-base options for the 45% step-down.
- **Status**: `APPROXIMATED` — loss determination is high-confidence; unknowns are timing and transition yield (error bound: 6-month phase-out is an estimate; both parties' behavior testable in the next two months).
- **Assumptions**: consolidation proceeds as announced; RapidPack's sheet is real and sustained; no other consolidator rescues Precision mid-year; reference value transfers. **Evidence**: consolidation statement, competitor sheet, margin structure, contract terms, director's mandate.
- **Alternatives**: A (rejected on margin and incentive structure), C (rejected: spends clean-exit leverage), B (selected). **Uncertainty**: phase-out length; replacement-sales velocity; whether RapidPack's price persists.
- **Risks**: delay → window closes with nothing negotiated (pre-specified negotiation deadline this quarter); replacement sales fall short → workforce plan triggers at a fixed date; conflict-averse owner stalls → decision recorded with escalation note.
