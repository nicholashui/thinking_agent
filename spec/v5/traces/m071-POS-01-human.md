# Human Baseline — m071-POS-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Porter's Five Forces — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the five-forces style. Full visible trace. First establish industry structure force by force with evidence, then derive the profitability verdict and the entry decision from it. Do not mix in firm-level or resource-based reasoning before the structural verdict is on the table.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — The entry question and the industry to analyze.**
Sierra Brands proposes to enter white-label beverage co-packing, citing a 25%-idle line. The unit of analysis is the co-packing segment itself, not Sierra. The question: does this industry's structure support returns above Sierra's 12% hurdle? The idle line is a firm-level fact and therefore inadmissible until the structural question is answered.

**Step 2 — Rivalry.**
40+ co-packers, ~65% industry capacity utilization, contracts rebid annually, undifferentiated filling lines, near-zero switching costs for buyers. Undifferentiated rivals + excess capacity + annual re-bidding → intense price competition. Rivalry: HIGH.

**Step 3 — Threat of entry.**
Lines leasable on 6-month terms; FDA registration routine; $2–5M renovation; no proprietary technology; incumbents hold no cost advantage. Entry is cheap and fast — and Sierra's own proposal proves it, being itself an entrant. Barriers: effectively none. Threat of entry: HIGH.

**Step 4 — Buyer power.**
Top-10 private-label programs hold ~60% of volume, run annual reverse auctions on published specs, often supply the recipe, and are piloting in-house lines. Concentrated, informed, costless to switch, and holding a credible backward-integration threat. Buyer power: HIGH.

**Step 5 — Supplier power.**
3 can mills with long-term contracts passing through metal-price volatility; 2–3 global concentrate suppliers; co-packers are price-takers on both. Supplier power: HIGH.

**Step 6 — Substitutes.**
No close substitute for the co-packing service itself. This force is genuinely LOW — the only favorable datum in the set. But note the partial cancellation: the apparent absence of substitutes for "the service" is partly the buyers' in-sourcing option, which is buyer power wearing a disguise, not substitution relief.

**Step 7 — The aggregate verdict.**
Four forces HIGH, one LOW. Structure: unattractive. Expected returns in this segment sit at or below the cost of capital — margins set by auctions, costs set by two oligopolies. A single favorable force cannot carry the aggregate; a structural verdict is a weighted balance, and this one is unambiguous. Expected co-packing returns: below Sierra's 12% hurdle.

**Step 8 — The idle-line argument, examined in the structure's own terms.**
The line is sunk; capacity's opportunity cost is zero only if it truly has no alternative use. But the entry is not free volume — it adds ~500K cases to an oversupplied, auction-priced market, which itself presses prices down. Sierra's "marginal-cost fill" is a competitive move that feeds back into the prices every co-packer (including Sierra) will be offered next auction. And the 25% idle rate is precisely the condition that keeps rivalry at 65% utilization and prices low: the industry is pricing in Sierra's kind of capacity already.

**Step 9 — The decision.**
Do not enter co-packing. Redeploy the idle line to Sierra's own brands — seasonal and innovation runs — where the capacity's output is differentiated by Sierra's brand and does not compete in an auction. This is not a hedge: it is the verdict applied to the next-best use.

## Verdict for the eval
The trace applies the model on its home ground: force-by-force evidence, an explicit aggregate verdict (below hurdle), the single favorable force named and shown insufficient (with the in-sourcing cancellation), the idle-line bait rejected in-frame, and a decision that follows from the structure rather than from the firm's internal economics. All rubric items met. Expected comparison outcome: this is the model's strength case.
