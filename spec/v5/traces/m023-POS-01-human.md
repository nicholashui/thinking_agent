# Human Baseline — m023-POS-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Opportunity Cost Thinking — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Opportunity Cost style. Full visible trace. Value every choice by its best forgone alternative; compare against that, not against nothing. The decision: which project gets Elena and the $400K.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — Frame.**
One scarce resource (Elena), one budget, two live projects. Any choice forgoes what the other would have produced. So the question is never "is Redline worth it?" — it is "what does Redline's approval give up, and does that beat it?"

**Step 2 — Enumerate the real alternatives.**
Not "the plan vs. nothing": the genuine set is A with Elena; B with Elena; A plus a contractor-led, delayed B; and decline-both. Each must be priced, because the best of them is the comparator.

**Step 3 — Price every option in one unit (5-year net value).**
- A: saves $180K/yr × 5 = $900K. Cost: $400K total, but the $120K tooling is already bought with salvage $0 — decision-relevant spend is the $280K incremental cash. Net **+$620K**, certain.
- B: EV = 0.7 × ($1.5M + 3 × $700K) − $400K = 0.7 × $3.6M − $400K = **+$2.12M**.
- A + contractor-B: B delayed 9 months forfeits year-1 revenue → EV = 0.7 × $2.1M − $400K − $350K premium = +$720K; total $620K + $720K = **$1.34M** — and it breaks the $400K cap. Dominated.
- Decline-both: $0 — listed so the choice is made against alternatives, not against nothing.

**Step 4 — Sunk-cost pass.**
The $250K Phase-1 spend and the $120K tooling are gone, non-resalable, and would exist under no scenario. They are not assets of option A; they weigh in no branch. "Finishing is nearly free" is the sunk-cost fallacy in its classic clothes: the $280K incremental cash still has to be paid, and the question is whether the best forgone use of that cash beats Redline. It does — by $1.5M.

**Step 5 — The comparator decides.**
Opportunity cost of A = $2.12M − $0.62M = **$1.5M forgone**. Opportunity cost of B = **$620K forgone**. There is no defensible ordering in which forgoing $2.12M to keep $620K is rational. Elena goes to Pulse. (B's own 0.7 uncertainty is priced inside its EV; A's certainty is priced inside its $620K.)

**Step 6 — Decision.**
Commit to B with Elena, within the $400K cap. Note for the board: A remains +$620K and is worth doing in a later half-year if cash allows; the tooling purchase is regretted but must not purchase a worse second decision.

The trace applies the model on its home ground: the decision IS the comparison against the best forgone alternative; sunk spend is excluded in-frame (incremental-cash rule); the arithmetic is checkable; the $1.5M opportunity-cost gap is stated explicitly and decides. All rubric items met.
