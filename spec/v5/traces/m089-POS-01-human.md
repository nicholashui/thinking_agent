**Step 1 — First move: count the doors.** Before touching costs, I ask of each option: which future decisions does it keep, which does it close? Option A (Lockstep): closes S1 (EU residency), S2 (multi-carrier), S3 (neutral access) — three doors shut behind me. Option B (middleware): keeps all three. That is the whole frame: A is a strategy of committed ignorance about three known future needs; B is a strategy of staying able to act.

**Step 2 — Count the meta-door.** B keeps a fourth, higher-order option: the vendor decision itself. I don't have to choose my long-term execution partner today; I can choose the *order* of my decisions — pick the layer, defer the partner. A forces the irreversible choice now, under maximum uncertainty.

**Step 3 — Price the doors, because open doors are options, not slogans.** S1: p 0.30 × (€2.4M/yr contract, lost outright under A) ≈ €720K/yr exposure. S2: p 0.25 × $4–6M migration ≈ $1.1M. Option value of keeping the doors ≈ 0.58 × $4.5M ≈ $2.6M of priced downside — and that ignores the S1 contract, which is not a cost but lost revenue.

**Step 4 — Compare against the direct-cost delta.** A's savings: $700K integration + 2 yrs × $540K fee = ≈ $1.78M. The doors are worth ≈ $2.6M+ in downside protection alone. The delta loses. And the delta is bounded and known; the doors are open-ended. Asymmetric: I pay a known $1.78M to keep access to an unbounded set of futures. That is the whole point of optionality — buy the convexity cheap.

**Step 5 — The deferral premium.** B also defers the irreversible step (vendor lock-in) until S1–S3 resolve. Waiting on a decision whose inputs are resolving is not dithering; it is information value. I can flip this: don't ask "which vendor," ask "what must be true before I must know."

**Step 6 — Bound the optionality.** A door kept forever becomes a liability — B must carry an exercise checkpoint: revisit the vendor choice at 12 months, or immediately when any scenario triggers. Optionality is bought, not free: B costs $700K more and launches 3 months later. That is the price of the doors, and it is worth paying.

**Step 7 — Decision.** Choose B, with the checkpoint. $1.78M buys ≈ $2.6M of priced protection plus the €2.4M/yr contract door — and the right to decide later.
