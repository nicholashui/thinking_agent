# Human Baseline — m088-POS-01
## Style: Pre-Commitment & Ulysses Contracts (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — Name the temptation before designing anything.** Mira's problem is not "launches underperform." It is akrasia: a predictable future failure of willpower. The record gives me a schedule, not a mood: day-1 revenue below $12K → within 48 hours → discount email. Five launches, five executions. You cannot out-think a schedule; you can only bind against it.

**Step 2 — Bind while the seas are calm.** The contract is built NOW, four weeks out, when $12K is an abstract number. Anything designed inside the launch window is designed by the same person who discounts — and that person is the enemy of the contract. Ulysses tied himself to the mast while the sirens were out of earshot and sealed the sailors' orders against his own later pleas. The design decision belongs to calm Mira; the window belongs to the contract.

**Step 3 — Remove the tempting option from the decision space.** The cleanest contract is the one with no decision to make:
- No discount codes ever created in the platform. A coupon that does not exist cannot be sent in a panic.
- Pricing authority transferred: the accountant is the only person who can unlock a price change, and only against a written, data-backed rationale.
- Friction: any discount request holds 72 hours. A case made in calm can wait; a fear-made case cannot survive the wait.

**Step 4 — Give the contract teeth and witnesses.** A private rule is a preference in a raincoat. Announce the no-panic-discount policy to her list: the announcement converts the rule into a public stake — violating it now costs reputation, not just revenue. The asymmetry is the mechanism: the itch that drives the violation is one afternoon of anxiety; the cost of breaking the pledge must be heavier than the itch.

**Step 5 — Pre-specify the release valve, because a contract without one gets smashed.** "When IS a discount legitimate?" must be answered in this room, not in week four. Pre-agreed: day-1 below $6K AND 72 hours elapsed AND the written rationale shows a demand-side cause (competitor move, checkout fault) — not a feeling. The valve is pre-set, so the only judgment left in the heat of the moment is reading a condition. That is the smallest decision a panicking mind can be trusted with.

**Step 6 — Review the contract, not just the launch.** After the cohort closes, revisit the thresholds with actual data. The binding that saved launch six may be too tight for launch seven. Adjust at distance, with data, never inside the window.

**Trace summary box.** temptation scheduled (day-1 < $12K → 48h → discount email) → bound now, not in-window → option removed (no codes; accountant-held unlock; 72h hold) → teeth (public stake, asymmetric cost) → release valve pre-set in data terms ($6K, 72h, demand-side evidence) → post-launch contract review with data.
