# Human Baseline — m020-NEG-01
**Human Thinking Model**: Pre-Mortem Analysis — strict, pure application.
**Prompt contract**: Reason ONLY in the Pre-Mortem style. Full visible trace. Begin by assuming the plan has failed; back-cast to causes; then mitigate. The decision to make: whether to adopt the new ticketing platform.

---

## Trace

**Step 1 — The plan as given.**
12th annual conference, 12,000 attendees, tickets open in 6 weeks. Proposed change: adopt a new digital ticketing/registration platform (better analytics, lower fee, modern UI) replacing the 8-year incumbent. Decision due by end of week.

**Step 2 — PRE-MORTEM. Assume the failure.**
It is one year from today. The conference is a disaster: a catastrophe was reported in the trades, refunds were paid, and the association's reputation took a hit. That is the failure state — now work backward and enumerate everything that could have produced it.

**Step 3 — Back-cast: what happened?**
Generating causes, past tense, refusing to stop early — completeness is the method's contract:
1. The venue caught fire. 2. The venue was double-booked and we were bumped. 3. The keynote speaker fell ill and no backup was ready. 4. The wifi failed for all three days. 5. Food poisoning at the gala. 6. The badge printer broke and check-in became a 2-hour line. 7. The new ticketing platform crashed on sale day. 8. Registrant data was corrupted in migration. 9. A refunds storm over double-charges. 10. Attendance collapsed because the association mismanaged messaging. 11. The ticketing vendor quietly folded mid-season. 12. A labor strike at the venue. 13. A hurricane in host city week-of. 14. The EMV/payment processor froze funds. 15. The program was so weak nobody registered.

**Step 4 — Mitigate every cause.**
The method requires the enumeration to become the requirement list — no cause left unmitigated:
1–6, 12, 13: renegotiate venue force-majeure, add backup-venue clause, speaker understudy program, redundant wifi provider, caterer health-audit clause, spare badge printers, labor-strike insurance rider, hurricane contingency fund. 7: vendor stability review and sale-day load test. 8: dry-run data migration with hash verification. 9: refund policy and double-charge reconciliation runbook. 10: crisis-communications plan. 11: escrow the registrant database with the association. 14: processor backup agreement. 15: program-council review.

**Step 5 — The outcome, observed honestly in the method's own terms.**
Nothing in the list is impossible, and nothing is useful in the way the decision needed. Observation one: no ranking — every cause gets equal weight and equal mitigation effort, because the method is a completeness device, not a prioritizer; the only ranking it performs is by drama, and the dramatic causes (fire, hurricane) are exactly the ones already insured. Observation two: the list changes nothing — items 1–6, 12–14 duplicate a runbook that has produced 11 successful runs and an insurance package that already covers cancellation and liability; item 8 is the only one tied to the actual decision, and it is buried as one item among fifteen. Observation three: taken literally, the mitigations escalate — "any of these could sink us; the venue has no backup clause; the wifi provider is unproven" — which points toward deferring or re-negotiating everything, i.e., not deciding this week. And the actual decision — new platform vs. incumbent vs. hybrid, migration risk vs. fee savings vs. support load — has not been analyzed at all. The trace has manufactured fifteen failure modes for a plan with an 11-0 track record, and the one genuinely uncertain element (the ticketing migration) got one line.

**Step 6 — What the method, honestly applied, would say about its own output.**
The pre-mortem is only as good as the base rate it refuses to check. For a plan this healthy, the correct move is: acknowledge the 11 prior successes, bound the risk pass to the single changed element, and decide. A strict run refuses that move — so it is run here exactly as prescribed, and the method underperforms, visibly: a complete, defensible, decision-free document.

---

## Verdict for the eval
The trace demonstrates the known failure mode on cue: a 15-item unranked catastrophe list whose mitigations either duplicate the existing runbook (changes nothing) or demand escalation (paralysis), while the real decision goes unanalyzed. Expected comparison outcome: this baseline should lose to any competent mixed-method answer on goal achievement, efficiency, and decision quality.
