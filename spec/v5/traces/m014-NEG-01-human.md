# Human Baseline — Constraint Theory / Bottleneck Thinking — m014-NEG-01
**Style enforced: Constraint Theory / Bottleneck Thinking (find → exploit → subordinate → elevate → repeat). Full visible trace.**

## 1. Find the constraint
- Serial flow per the org chart: Intake 600 → Adjudication 400 → Payout 450. Demand 420/day. All work flows through adjudication → 420 > 400 → it runs at 105% utilization; every other stage has ≥ 25% slack. The binding constraint is **human adjudication**. Clean and unambiguous — the only stage at or over capacity.
- The mothballed engine is a side channel, not part of the flow: it was decommissioned, so it is not in the system as it exists. Constraint analysis operates on the system as it is, not on hypothetical parallel structures; re-architecting the flow is a different exercise from finding its constraint.
- Demand mix is irrelevant to the identification: whether a claim is high- or low-touch, 420/day still must pass adjudication today. Mix changes the composition of the load on the constraint, not the fact of the constraint. And the roadmap shift to 75% high-touch only INCREASES the load human judgment must carry — it reinforces the diagnosis.

## 2. Exploit
- Protect adjudicators: no interruptions, clean work queues, best-trained staff on the hardest claims first, backlog visible. The drum is the adjudication rate; nothing upstream may starve it.

## 3. Subordinate
- Intake feeds adjudication in steady small batches (no surges); payout must never hold up adjudication output; float QA/clerical headcount to support adjudication during the ramp. Non-constraints exist to serve the constraint.

## 4. Elevate
- The only option that lifts the constraint is expanding human adjudication capacity: **hire 5 adjudicators** (≈ +150 claims/day → capacity ~550/day, $1.2M/yr, 6-month ramp). Hire now — the ramp is long and the backlog (~20/day) compounds into ~2,400 claims and escalating SLA penalties by month 6. Add targeted overtime (+15% for 3 months) as a temporary exploit to slow backlog growth until the hires land.
- Accept the SLA cost of the ramp as the price of elevation; negotiate the client timeline with a hire-in-progress plan.

## 5. Repeat
- After the hires land, re-check: if demand keeps growing (2%/mo), adjudication may bind again at the new capacity — re-run the loop: re-locate the constraint by utilization and WIP, then elevate again if needed.

## Conclusion
Hire 5 adjudicators immediately; protect and subordinate the whole pipeline around human adjudication; absorb the 6-month ramp. The bottleneck is human adjudication and the only effective move is lifting its capacity — every hour spent elsewhere (including on the idle engine) is time the constraint keeps ruling the system.
