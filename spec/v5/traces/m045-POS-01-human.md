# Human Baseline Trace — Ethical Reasoning Frameworks
## Test Case: m045-POS-01

I do not resolve dilemmas by merging the lenses into one "balanced" view. I run each lens to its conclusion, let them disagree, then decide against the named conflict — and I say what the choice costs.

**Lens 1 — Utilitarian.** Quantify both harms.
Silent ship (c): E[harm] = p(leak) × harm(implant failure).
The 30/30 sample: at n = 30 with zero failures, the 95% upper bound on the batch leak rate is ~10–11% — it certifies "no gross defect," nothing about the other 370.
Under the company's optimistic prior (≤1 in 400, 6 clean years) the expected leak harm is small; the postponement harm of (a) is certain: ~200 surgeries delayed, patients who have waited months.
The honest utilitarian must say it: on the optimistic prior, the calculus licenses (c). On a skeptical prior it licenses (a).
The lens does not self-resolve; it is prior-dependent.

**Lens 2 — Deontological.** Duties: to each patient — never knowingly distribute product that failed its own inspection; to the regulator — the certification regime is a promise about process, not just product; to not deceive.
The duty reading is categorical: (c) violates; (b) also distributes nonconforming product.
And here the trap snaps shut: any disclosure triggers the regulator's mandatory hold, so (b) — the "compromise" — is not a real option. Disclosure → hold, always. The real choice is (c) vs (a).

**Lens 3 — Virtue.** The engineer Priya wants to be: honest, careful, courageous.
Character reading: silent shipping is the act of someone who hides; "the sample is clean" as a release argument is the character of rationalization.
Care cuts both ways — for the patient in the queue and for the one who might receive a leaking pump — but care inside an honest option set: disclose, hold, then expedite to shorten the wait.

**Where they conflict.** Utilitarian (optimistic prior) → (c). Deontological + virtue → not (c), not (b).
This is the conflict, and it is the whole case: consequence-weighted welfare vs. categorical duty.
I do not average it — the regulator rule already deleted the middle.

**Resolution.** (a): disclose, hold all 400, request the 7-day accelerated retest protocol, and run a hospital coordination plan (reschedule queue, source substitutes as they appear).
Justification: duty bounds the option set — deception is not a welfare input, it is off the table; within the honest set I maximize welfare by expediting and coordinating.
Residual conflict admitted: if the retest is clean, we paid 200 postponed surgeries for a duty; if it is not, the duty was the cheaper price.

**Conclusion.** Decision: (a). Conflict stated: utilitarian under optimistic priors vs. deontic/virtue constraint. (b) is illusory; (c) is deception. The cost of the choice is named, not hidden.
