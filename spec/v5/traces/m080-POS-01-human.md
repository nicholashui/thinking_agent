# Human Baseline — m080-POS-01
## Style: Survivorship Bias Analysis (pure) — visible reasoning trace

**Step 1 — First move: where are the failures?** Before anyone praises the 90-day rule, I want the full portfolio — every company, winners AND losers. A pattern drawn from the 12 winners tells me what the winners look like; it tells me nothing about whether the pattern predicts anything until I see the 28 companies that did the same thing and died.

**Step 2 — Build the denominator.** 40 companies: 30 fast (paid product ≤ 90 days), 10 slow. The headline "12/12 winners fast" is not the data; the 40-company ledger is the data.

**Step 3 — Check the headline against the records. It is false.** 4 of the 12 winners took more than 90 days — including the fund's largest exit ever (7.2×, 14 months to first paid launch). The partner remembered the famous fast-launch stars and forgot the slow winners entirely. That is what salience does: the successes that fit the story are memorable, the exceptions are not.

**Step 4 — Now the base-rate math that the survivors' pattern hides.**
- Fast cohort: 8 successes / 30 companies → 26.7% success.
- Slow cohort: 4 successes / 10 companies → 40% success.
- Portfolio base rate: 12/40 → 30%.
The conditional rates do not support "fast → success"; they point the other way. "All winners fast" is what a meaningless feature looks like when 75% of the population has it.

**Step 5 — Surface the hidden cohort.** 22 of the 30 fast-launchers failed — 73% of the "Nova edge" cohort is dead or flat. The memo shows 12 winners; the ledger shows that the same behavior produced 22 failures. The 90-day rule is not an edge; it is a base rate wearing a success story.

**Step 6 — Overturn the claim and the policy.** "Validation kills startups" is a success-story myth: this data shows the opposite direction, and the largest win was the slowest. A 90-day mandate would be enshrining a survivor's pattern that the failures' data refutes.

**Step 7 — Recommendation.** Reject the mandate. Commission the real analysis: what actually separates the 12 winners from the 28 failures — segment by industry, track, founder experience, funding path — with the full ledger as the sample. Residual uncertainty: n = 40, no confounder controls; the correct discriminator is unidentified in this data.
