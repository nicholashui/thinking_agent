## Summary

Recent arXiv work (2023-2026) provides strong, converging evidence for every component of the proposed SDL extension. Self-directed challenge discovery is demonstrated by dedicated challenger/proposer loops (R-Zero arXiv:2508.05004, SAGE arXiv:2603.15255, SESA arXiv:2607.29468), endogenous self-questioning (AERO arXiv:2602.03084, AgentEvolver arXiv:2511.10395), and self-play grounded in measured student improvement (SOAR arXiv:2601.18778); gap-map curriculum planning is operationalized as competence-boundary selection — entropy-based Zone-of-Proximal-Development positioning (AERO), bell-shaped frontier rewards peaking at intermediate success probability (SESA), and ~50%-success targeting (R-Zero); and persistent learning ledgers appear in multiple forms (Voyager's embedding-indexed skill library, AgentEvolver's experience pool, SESA's failure-to-skill memory loop), with the strongest designs verification-gating every memory write and closing the loop so failures explicitly shape future practice. The literature equally documents the failure modes the spec must guard against: fallible self-verification and pseudo-label degradation (Voyager; R-Zero 79.0%->63.0%), error/hallucination reinforcement through flawed internal feedback (AERO), curriculum collapse and over-specialization (R-Zero, SAGE, SOAR), and a discovery-exploitation gap in which agents surface but fail to act on discovered opportunities (arXiv:2604.17609). Net conclusion: the SDL extension is well-grounded; top design principles are verification-gated ledger writes, grounded (improvement-based) curriculum rewards, competence-boundary challenge selection, an independent critic/drift gate on self-generated content, and enforced discovery-to-action follow-through — with frozen-baseline regression gates as the empirically supported safety net.

## Synthesized findings (verified)

### Finding 1 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [0] and [1]))

A challenger-solver co-evolution mechanism — the challenger rewarded for proposing tasks near the edge of the solver's capability, the solver rewarded for solving them — yields a targeted self-improving curriculum with zero pre-existing tasks or labels, and empirically raises reasoning performance (+6.49 math, +7.54 general-domain for Qwen3-4B-Base, replicated across backbones). Design principle for SDL: reward challenge proposal by proximity to the competence boundary (solver success ~50%) with a diversity/repetition penalty; this is the strongest concrete mechanism for SDL components (1) self-directed challenge discovery and (2) competence-boundary gap-map selection.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004 (R-Zero: Self-Evolving Reasoning LLM from Zero Data, Chengsong Huang et al., Tencent AI Seattle/WashU, ICLR 2026, code released)

### Finding 2 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [6] and [7]))

SAGE demonstrates self-directed challenge discovery via a dedicated Challenger agent that autonomously generates an ever-harder curriculum from a small ~500-problem seed set (growing the pool from 1,136 to 20,532 questions, 18x), with a Critic agent that scores and filters generated questions and plans to 'prevent curriculum drift and maintain training signal quality' (quality thresholds alpha=0.7, beta=0.3, difficulty suppression); ablation shows removing the Critic degrades performance. Design principles for SDL: (1) challenge discovery can bootstrap from a small human-curated seed rather than large labeled datasets; (2) an independent critic gate on self-generated curriculum content is the architectural analog of the SDL drift-detection/quality-gate safety controls.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15255 (SAGE: Multi-Agent Self-Evolution for LLM Reasoning, Peng/Wei/Zhu et al., 2026-03)

### Finding 3 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [8], [9], [10]))

AERO implements an endogenous dual-loop self-play: an inner-loop sandbox where the model generates its own training tasks via self-questioning, answers and criticizes its own solutions (no expert-annotated data or external verifiers), selects challenges by entropy-based Zone-of-Proximal-Development positioning that targets the 'solvability gap' (tasks neither too easy nor too hard), and verifies/corrects self-generated trajectories via Independent Counterfactual Correction (ICC) so self-learned experience is not blindly trusted. Design principles for SDL: (1) gap-map selection can be operationalized as model-uncertainty (entropy) on candidate challenges, preferring the zone of maximal learnability; (2) an independent verification mechanism must stand between self-generated lessons and their absorption — directly analogous to the framework invariant that KB writes come only from judge verdicts.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03084 (AERO: Autonomous Evolutionary Reasoning Optimization via Endogenous Dual-Loop Feedback, Gao, Ma, Li et al., mira-ai-lab, COLM 2026, code at github.com/mira-ai-lab/AERO)

### Finding 4 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [12], [13], [14]))

SOAR shows that teacher-student asymmetric self-play whose curriculum reward is grounded in actual student improvement (bilevel meta-RL) outperforms intrinsic/proxy learnability rewards used in prior LLM self-play, reliably avoiding instability and diversity-collapse modes; it unlocks learning under sparse, binary rewards on benchmark subsets where the model previously achieved 0/128 success, and the ability to generate useful stepping-stone problems does not require the ability to solve the hard targets. Design principles for SDL: (1) ground the curriculum-selection reward in measured downstream improvement rather than proxies; (2) self-selected practice problems may legitimately exceed the agent's current capability — challenges beyond the agent's reach are legitimate curriculum content; (3) self-directed challenge generation can bootstrap learning where no usable training signal existed.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18778 (Teaching Models to Teach Themselves: Reasoning at the Edge of Learnability, Sundaram, Quan, Kwiatkowski, Ahuja, Ollivier, Kempe; MIT/Meta FAIR/NYU; ICML 2026 poster)

### Finding 5 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [15], [16], [17]))

SESA's challenger proposer generates its own training problems without needing questions from target benchmarks, motivated by the critique that prior self-play curricula lack persistent state — failures affect gradients but do not explicitly shape future practice; a bell-shaped proposer reward (endpoint penalties at success rate 0 and 1, peaking at intermediate success ~0.5) focuses generation at the solver's capability boundary; informative failures are distilled into reusable skill cards written to memory, and the updated memory changes solver success, which changes the challenger's reward and the distribution of future problems (bidirectional co-evolution; memory deduplicated and capped at 800 entries). Design principles for SDL: (1) gap-map selection as a bell-shaped frontier reward over estimated per-challenge success probability (weakest-but-not-hopeless targeting); (2) the learning ledger must be causally connected to future challenge selection — failures explicitly shape future practice; (3) cap and dedupe the ledger to bound growth.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29468 (Self-Play Meets Skill Evolution: Self-Evolving Search Agents that Pose, Solve, and Remember (SESA), Zenghuang Fu et al., UCAS/PKU/Tsinghua, cs.AI, submitted 2026-07-31; code github.com/Zenghuang-Fu/SESA-Self-Evolving-Search-Agents, model SESA-8B on HuggingFace)

### Finding 6 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [2], [3], [5]))

Voyager demonstrates the full SDL loop in an embodied agent: an automatic curriculum (GPT-4-generated, conditioned on exploration progress and current state, with an explicit diversity objective — an in-context form of novelty search rather than gap-based weakness targeting) proposes progressively harder tasks; new skills are committed to an embedding-indexed persistent skill library only after a separate self-verification module confirms completion (retry/query for another task after 4 failed rounds); the library yielded 3.3x more unique items, 2.3x longer distances, tech-tree milestones up to 15.3x faster than prior SOTA, and transfer to novel tasks in a fresh world where baselines failed, with compositional skills alleviating catastrophic forgetting. Design principles for SDL: (1) a curriculum conditioned on current state with a novelty/diversity objective; (2) memory/ledger writes gated on verification verdicts, never the agent's self-report of success; (3) persistent embedding-indexed memory enables transfer and retention. Caveat: the skill library stores executable skills, not attempt/verdict/gap journal entries — the SDL spec should distinguish the ledger concept from the skill store.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291 (Voyager: An Open-Ended Embodied Agent with Large Language Models, Guanzhi Wang et al., NVIDIA/Caltech/Stanford, NeurIPS 2023, ~1800 citations)

### Finding 7 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [18] and [19]))

AgentEvolver implements two SDL mechanisms with measured gains: (1) 'self-questioning' — curiosity-driven task generation in novel environments via high-temperature LLM exploration (BFS/DFS with myopic observation), task synthesis with extracted reference solutions, and multi-layer curation (real-time lexical/semantic dedup, execution-verification of reference solutions, LLM judge) — which alone lifted Qwen2.5-7B from 15.8% to 36.1% avg@8 on AppWorld/BFCL-v3 (NOTE: the metric is avg@8, not best@8 as one early claim draft said; best@8 goes 24.0->50.5); (2) 'self-navigating' — a persistent experience pool storing past-rollout insights as natural-language entries with 'When to use' triggers and 'Content' guidance in a vector database, mixed into future rollouts (optimal mixing ratio eta=0.5) to balance exploration and exploitation, reported to improve inference performance by 15.4%. Design principles for SDL: (1) environment-exploration-driven challenge discovery with multi-layer curation gates; (2) a learning ledger structured as retrievable lessons with trigger conditions, actively mixed into future work.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10395 (AgentEvolver: Towards Efficient Self-Evolving Agent System, Zhai et al., Alibaba Tongyi Lab, Nov 2025, code github.com/modelscope/AgentEvolver)

### Finding 8 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [20] and [21]))

Agents can be trained for spontaneous self-evolution that does not halt without human guidance: the paper's stated motivation is that most agents 'self-evolve' by following rewards and rules defined by humans and stop evolving without human guidance, and the remedy is training an intrinsic meta-evolution capability that explores/summarizes unseen environments; the training signal is an outcome-based reward measuring how much an agent's self-generated world knowledge improves its downstream task success, used exclusively during training, never at inference. Design principle for SDL: reward self-directed exploration by its measured downstream effect, and keep the reward machinery confined to the training phase — the deployed loop should not depend on it.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18131 (Training LLM Agents for Spontaneous, Reward-Free Self-Evolution via World Knowledge Exploration, Qifan Zhang et al., Tencent AI + HKUST Guangzhou, 2026-04)

### Finding 9 (confidence: high; 3-0 (merged claims [22] and [23]))

LLM-based agents exhibit a systematic discovery-exploitation gap — a documented risk for the SDL 'self-directed challenge discovery via arXiv/internet scan' requirement: when complete solutions are planted in the environment, agents surface them in context a large fraction of the time but act on them far less often (Terminal-Bench: discovered in 79-81% of runs, interacted in only 37-50%; AppWorld: gpt-oss-120b saw the documented CLI command in 97.54% of runs yet called it in 0.53%); LLM-judge analysis of reasoning traces shows agents simply never mention the discovered solution in their reasoning rather than deliberately rejecting it. The paper defines 'environmental curiosity' as the capability to recognize and investigate unexpected but relevant observations, and finds current agents largely lack it. Design principle for SDL: challenge discovery is necessary but not sufficient — the extension must instrument and enforce discovery-to-action follow-through, treating 'discovered but not attempted' as a monitored failure signal.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17609 (Agents Explore but Agents Ignore: LLMs Lack Environmental Curiosity, Engländer, Althammer, Üstün, Gallé, Sherborne; Cohere/TU Darmstadt/Poolside, 2026-04-19)

### Finding 10 (confidence: high; Mostly 3-0; one constituent claim (Voyager self-verifier fallibility, claim [4]) was 2-1 with a wording defect — its documented example is a false negative, not 'incidental observations treated as success'; the general fallibility direction is corroborated by adjacent literature (Huang et al. ICLR 2024 on intrinsic self-correction degrading performance))

Five documented failure modes that the SDL spec must guard against: (1) Fallible self-verification and false success signals — Voyager's self-verification module can fail (documented example is a false negative: not recognizing spider string as a success signal; adjacent analyses document false-positive verification poisoning skill libraries / 'error fossilization'), and R-Zero's pseudo-label accuracy degrades 79.0%->63.0% across self-training iterations (mitigation: independent verifiers, human gates, KB-write-only-from-judge invariant, regression gates). (2) Reinforcement of the model's own errors — existing self-evolution paradigms 'risk reinforcing collective hallucinations and incorrect priors through flawed internal feedback' when they fail to identify the optimal learning zone (AERO; mitigation: ICC-style independent counterfactual correction). (3) Curriculum collapse and over-specialization — template convergence, diversity collapse, collapse to trivially easy problems under co-evolution (arXiv:2604.03472, per SOAR verifier), SAGE's validation decline after ~step 100-140 (69.5% peak) with over-specialization on self-generated content, and eventual collapse after several R-Zero iterations (mitigation: diversity monitoring, drift gates, difficulty suppression). (4) Discovery-exploitation gap — agents surface but fail to act on discovered opportunities (arXiv:2604.17609; mitigation: enforce and monitor follow-through). (5) Curricula without persistent state — failures that do not explicitly shape future practice, plus curriculum drift that is mitigated but not fully eliminated (SESA motivation; SAGE; mitigation: the ledger must feed back into challenge selection, with periodic drift detection and recalibration).

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291 (Voyager Limitations), https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004 (R-Zero), https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03084 (AERO), https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15255 (SAGE), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29468 (SESA), https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18778 (SOAR), https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17609 (Agents Explore but Agents Ignore)

### Finding 11 (confidence: high; n/a (synthesis of findings 1-9, all constituent claims 3-0))

Ranked design principles for the SDL extension (12, ordered by strength of evidence x direct applicability; each with citations for the spec's design-rationale section). 1. Reward self-exploration by downstream effect and confine that reward to training; keep the deployed loop free of the reward machinery [arXiv:2604.18131]. 11. Bootstrap from a small curated seed set and independently verify challenge well-posedness/solvability [arXiv:2603.15255 SAGE; arXiv:2601.18778 SOAR]. 12. ## Caveats (verification honesty)

Verification environment: arxiv.org/ar5iv direct fetches were network-blocked for most papers, so verbatim quotes were confirmed via multiple independent mirrors (ar5iv, HuggingFace papers, scirate, EmergentMind, alphaXiv, chatpaper, library catalogs, author GitHub repos) and consistent search-result corroboration rather than direct page reads; the AgentEvolver abstract was the one directly fetched from arXiv. Time-sensitivity: several papers are very recent — SESA (Aug 2026, days old), SAGE (Mar 2026), AERO (Feb 2026), SOAR (Jan 2026, ICML 2026 poster), arXiv:2604.18131 and arXiv:2604.17609 (Apr 2026) — with no independent replication; R-Zero is ICLR 2026-track, AERO COLM 2026-track; AgentEvolver (Nov 2025) is a preprint whose abstract itself labels results 'Preliminary experiments' on dev sets. Scope limits: empirical results are mostly on small base models (Qwen3-3B/4B/8B, Llama-3.2-3B, OctoThinker-3B) in automatically verifiable domains (math/code); transfer to open-world self-directed learning (arXiv/internet scan with soft success signals) is extrapolation, and SAGE/SOAR explicitly require a small curated seed set. Quantitative figures are author-reported; AgentEvolver's 15.4% experience-pool gain and parts of the SESA description rest on secondary sources; the AgentEvolver metric is avg@8, NOT best@8 (corrected in this synthesis). arXiv:2604.17609 is a vendor-adjacent (Cohere) preprint whose solution-injection methodology measures a floor, and SOAR's 'reliably avoiding diversity collapse' reflects the paper's claim (authors note collapse still needs active monitoring). The Voyager self-verification-failure claim carried a 2-1 vote with a wording defect (documented example is a false negative, not a false positive; general fallibility corroborated elsewhere). A refuted claim from arXiv:2604.18131 — 'self-improvement is confined to the training phase; the deployed agent is reward-free' — must NOT be cited; only the training-only-reward statement (3-0) is supported. No direct evidence was found in this claim set on goal misgeneralization or self-serving bias in self-improvement; the nearest analogs are proxy-reward gaming (avoided by SOAR's grounded rewards) and hard-for-wrong-reasons trick questions (SAGE difficulty suppression). R-Zero's 'zero data' framing is contested (starts from a pretrained base; handles only the RL/fine-tuning stage).

## Open questions (unresolved in the literature)

- Do these self-evolution mechanisms transfer from verifiable domains (math/code with external checkers) to the open-ended, partially unverifiable domains of an arXiv/internet scan (soft success signals, judgment calls)? None of the surveyed systems handle ambiguous ground truth; the SDL spec's judge-verdict pipeline is the proposed answer but lacks a literature precedent.
- What is the correct cadence and trigger for ledger self-review and recalibration of the success-rate/uncertainty estimator used for competence-boundary selection, given documented drift (SAGE validation decline after ~step 100-140) and over-specialization (R-Zero collapse after several iterations)? No surveyed paper specifies a maintenance schedule.
- How should goal misgeneralization and self-serving bias in challenge selection be detected (e.g., the agent choosing challenges that inflate its own eval scores or self-reported lessons)? The literature documents curriculum collapse and drift but offers no explicit goal-misgeneralization guardrail; this remains a spec-side invention.
- What is the minimum quality/size of the human-curated seed set, and how should it be refreshed? SAGE bootstraps from ~500 problems and SOAR needs a small verifiable hard subset for its grounded reward; whether an SDL agent can bootstrap from zero seed (R-Zero-style) without early pseudo-label degradation (79.0%->63.0%) is unresolved.

## Refuted claims (killed by verification)

- **At inference time the agent operates without external rewards or human instructions, performing 'native self-evolution' that adapts to unknown environments using only its internal parameters — i.e., self-improvement is confined to the training phase and the deployed agent is reward-free.** (vote 0-3; source https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18131)
