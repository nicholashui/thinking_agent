### HOW — style passes (dual-route, completion contracts)
- Pass S1 (m027 design cycle — full contract): EMPATHIZE — job-to-be-done from watching: confirm whether today's dose is taken + make refill week fast and error-free; observation overrides the survey explicitly. DEFINE — POV: a 78-year-old on 5 meds needs taken-state verification and a refill week that cannot produce sorting errors — "verify, don't remind." IDEATE — A loud-alarm big-print pillbox (the survey's ask — decoy); B 7-cell taken-state strip (mechanical latch + "today" dial); C caregiver photo-verification app; D pharmacy prefilled weekly blisters. PROTOTYPE + TEST — low-fi v1 of B, 5 seniors in-home: 4/5 can't close the latch (arthritic pinch), 2/5 misread the dial, 1/5 drops the strip; C: 3/5 refuse to photograph pills (the caregiver loves it). Failure is real and observed — in the test, not the idea. ITERATE — v2: slide-tab (no pinch), single "today" window, one-handed, larger cells; retest same 5 → 5/5 complete; blister (D) with 4 seniors → sorting anxiety gone (4/4), refill time collapses. HARD-CONSTRAINT CHECK (weakness gate): v2 usable by 80+ without caregiver (core has no app dependence); shippable at 140 stores (no regulatory submission; blister fits pharmacy workflow); concrete form + go/no-go — no drift into theater.
- Pass S2 (m025 real options — dual-route): the pilot is an option, not a commitment: 3-store pilot (~$40K, 8 weeks) buys the option to roll out 140 stores, exercised only if verification adoption ≥ 60% at week 8 and blister error reports < 2%; downside capped at pilot cost, upside open. Survey-led A carries negative option value — it would validate the wrong need.
- Divergence resolution (V1–V3): passes AGREE — need = verify-not-remind; option framing prices the go/no-go; agreement recorded, no calibration pass needed.

### REVIEW — insight pass (S2, packet gate)
- I1: the survey is the interested-party artifact — stated-preference instruments make respondents answer in the language of products they can imagine, so "bigger print, louder alarm" is the market-research echo of the failing reminder category, not the need; the 68% "we forget" measures a symptom, the observation measures the behavior.
- I2: refill week (10/12 "worst hour") is the bigger and cheaper-to-serve pain, and the chain already holds the data (prescriptions, fill dates) to auto-build blisters — the device is the visible anchor, the blister service is the adherence win; the chain's own infrastructure is the service.
### DECISION PACKET
- Conclusion: ship taken-state verification strip (v2 mechanics) + prefilled weekly blister service; caregiver photo-check optional add-on; demote the survey's print/alarm features with observation evidence; go/no-go pilot gate: 3 stores, adoption ≥ 60% at week 8 and blister errors < 2% → 140-store rollout.
- Status: APPROXIMATED — need evidenced from observation; artifact validated at prototype scale (n=5); error bound: small-N test, pilot adoption unmeasured → the pilot gate is the bounding experiment.
- Assumptions: 12-home sample representative of the 140-store senior base; v2 manufacturable at target cost; blister workflow clears chain ops without regulatory submission. Evidence: 12-home observation (10/12, 9/12, 8/12, 10/12, 7/12, 3/12); v1 test 4/5 latch fail; v2 retest 5/5; blister test 4/4.
- Alternatives: A loud-alarm pillbox (rejected — abandoned category); B+D strip + blisters (selected); C app-only (rejected — 3/5 photo-refusal, 80+ without caregiver); do-nothing (rejected).
- Uncertainty: pilot adoption rate; blister margin at 140-store scale; v2 unit cost; novelty decay of the taken-state habit. Risks: pilot fails gate (sunk ~$40K); manufacturing cost erodes margin; 88+ sub-population untested; photo-check layer caregiver-dependent.
